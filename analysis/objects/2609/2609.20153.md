<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
The endomorphism algebra $\mathrm{End}(\1)$ of the unit object $\1$ in a monoidal category $\mathcal C$ will be denoted $\Omega\mathcal C$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
The category $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ has two simple objects, $\1$ and $Y$.  The unit $\1$ is real, and the other simple $Y$ is quaternionic.
    In the Deligne product $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H\boxtimes\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C$, the object $Y\boxtimes\mathbb C$ is not simple, but decomposes into two copies of a unique simple object.
    Of course, this product category is just the complexification of $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$, which is $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C$, and the simple object that appears inside of $Y\boxtimes\mathbb C$ is just the odd line.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A simple object $Y$ in a fusion category $\mathcal C$ over $\mathbb K$ is said to be quasi-invertible if $Y\otimes Y^*\cong n\cdot\1$, for some positive integer $n$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
The object $Y\in \mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb H}$ is self-dual, and satisfies $Y\otimes Y\cong4\cdot\1$, so it is quasi-invertible.
<!-- end example-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:Quasi-invertible rules}
    For any quasi-invertible $Y\in\mathcal C$, the algebra $\mathrm{End}(Y)$ is central simple over $\Omega\mathcal C$, and for any other object $X\in\mathcal C$, 
    \[\mathrm{End}(X\otimes Y)\cong\mathrm{End}(X)\otimes_{\Omega\mathcal C}\mathrm{End}(Y)\,.\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For any object $X\in\mathcal C$, 
    \[\mathrm{End}(X\otimes Y)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(X,X\otimes Y\otimes Y^*)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(X,n\cdot X)\cong n\cdot\mathrm{End}(X)\,,\]
    as vector spaces over $\Omega\mathcal C$.
    In particular, setting $X=\1$ shows that $\mathrm{End}(Y)$ has dimension $n$ over $\Omega\mathcal C$.
    For any pair of objects $A,B\in\mathcal C$, there is an inclusion of algebras
    \[\mathrm{End}(A)\otimes_{\Omega\mathcal C}\mathrm{End}(B)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}(A\otimes B)\,.\]
    For $(A,B)=(Y,Y^*)$, we can use the algebra inclusion to find that
    \begin{gather*}
        \mathrm{End}(Y)\otimes_{\Omega\mathcal C}\mathrm{End}(Y)^{op}\cong\mathrm{End}(Y)\otimes_{\Omega\mathcal C}\mathrm{End}(Y^*)\\
        \hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}(Y\otimes Y^*)\cong\mathrm{End}(n\cdot\1)\cong M_n(\Omega\mathcal C)\,.
    \end{gather*}
    Comparing dimensions, we find that this must be an isomorphism of algebras.
    If $Z(\mathrm{End}(Y))$ were strictly larger than $\Omega\mathcal C$, then $\mathrm{End}(Y)\otimes_{\Omega\mathcal C}\mathrm{End}(Y)^{op}$ would decompose into multiple matrix blocks, from whence it follows that $\mathrm{End}(Y)$ must be central simple over $\Omega\mathcal C$.

    For $(A,B)=(X,Y)$, the inclusion
    \begin{gather*}
        \mathrm{End}(X)\otimes_{\Omega\mathcal C}\mathrm{End}(Y)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}(X\otimes Y)
    \end{gather*}
    must again be an isomorphism, for dimension reasons.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:Complex simples are big}
    If $\Omega\mathcal C=\mathbb R$ and $X$ is a complex simple object, then $\mathrm{FPdim}(X)>\sqrt2$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Rigidity forces $X\otimes X^*\cong 2\cdot\1\oplus V$, for some object $V\in \mathcal C$.
    By Lemma \ref{lem:Quasi-invertible rules}, $V$ must be nonzero, and so $\mathrm{FPdim}(X)^2=2+\mathrm{FPdim}(V)>2$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
For $\mathcal C$ a fusion category over a field $\mathbb K$, with $\mathrm{End}(\1)=\Omega\mathcal C$, the Frobenius-Perron dimension of $\mathcal C$ is the quantity
    \begin{equation}\label{eqn:FPdim formula}
        \mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal C)=\sum_{\substack{X\in\mathcal C,\\ X \text{ simple}}}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X)^2}{\dim_{\Omega\mathcal C}\big(\mathrm{End}(X)\big)}\;.
    \end{equation}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- example-3: body_md -->
When $H$ is a semisimple Hopf algebra over a field $\mathbb K$,
    \[\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb K}(H))=\dim_{\mathbb K}(H)\,.\]
<!-- end example-3 -->
<!-- example-4: body_md -->
The object $Y\in\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ satisfies $Y\otimes Y\cong 4\cdot\1$, and thus has $\mathrm{FPdim}(Y)=2$.
    From this it follows that
    \[\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H)=\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\1)^2}{\dim_{\Omega\mathcal C}(\Omega\mathcal C)}+\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(Y)^2}{\dim_{\Omega\mathcal C}(\mathrm{End}(Y))}\;=\;2\,.\]
<!-- end example-4 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:Each simple contributes at least 1 to FPdim}
    Each summand
    \[\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X)^2}{\dim_{\Omega\mathcal C}(\mathrm{End}(X))}\]
    in Equation \ref{eqn:FPdim formula} is the Frobenius-Perron dimension of the internal object
    \[X^*\otimes_{\mathrm{End}(X)}X\,,\]
    and is therefore an algebraic integer that that is $\geq1$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- example-5: body_md -->
\label{eg:Complex simples contribute more}
    When $\Omega\mathcal C=\mathbb R$ and $X$ is a complex simple, Corollary \ref{cor:Complex simples are big} implies that
    \[\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X)^2}{\dim_{\Omega\mathcal C}(\mathrm{End}(X))}>\frac{2}{2}\;=\;1\,\]
    and so $X$ must contribute strictly more than 1 to $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal C)$.
<!-- end example-5 -->
<!-- example-6: body_md -->
\label{eg:Semion has no braided real forms}
    The semion category $\mathcal Sem$ is the braided category $\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}^{(\omega,\beta)}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)=\langle\1, \sigma\rangle_{\oplus}$, with associator and braiding determined by the formulas 
    \begin{gather*}
        \Big(\omega_{\sigma,\sigma,\sigma}:(\sigma\otimes\sigma)\otimes\sigma\to\sigma(\sigma\otimes\sigma)\Big)=-\mathrm{id}_\sigma\,,\text{ and}\\
        \Big(\beta_{\sigma,\sigma}:\sigma\otimes\sigma\to\sigma\otimes\sigma\Big)=i\cdot\mathrm{id}_\1.
    \end{gather*}
    Any monoidal autoequivalence $T$ of $\mathcal Sem$ must fix all objects, and the monoidal structure $J$ can be assumed to be trivial, because $H^2(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z;\mathbb C^\times)=0$.
    The presence of $i$ in the formula for the braiding implies that any autoequivalence that performs complex conjugation would not be braided, so $\mathcal Sem$ has no braided real forms.
<!-- end example-6 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Typically there is no preferred isomorphism with $H^2$.
    However, if a preferred real form $\mathcal C$ exists, then we can identify that with $0\in H^2(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z;\mathrm{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathrm{id}_\mathcal D))$, and compare all other options for $\mu$ relative to this preferred choice $\mu_\mathcal C$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:Equivalence of pre-metric groups and PBFCs}
    There is an equivalence of categories between pre-metric groups, and the 1-categorical truncation of the 2-category of pointed braided fusion categories, braided functors, and monoidal natural transformations.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:Gauss sum and central charge}
    The Gauss sum $\tau(A,q)$ of a pre-metric group $(A,q)$ is the complex scalar
    \[\tau(A,q)=\sum_{a\in A}q(a)\;.\]
    If $\tau(A,q)\neq0$, the central charge is the normalized Gauss sum
    \[c(A,q):=\frac{\tau(A,q)}{|\tau(A,q)|}\;.\]
    If $\mathcal C=\mathcal C(A,q)$, then we will use the shorthand $\tau(\mathcal C):=\tau(A,q)$ and $c(\mathcal C):=c(A,q)$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- example-7: body_md -->
\label{eg:Introducing TC}
    The toric code is the braided category $\mathrm{TC}=\mathcal Z(\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z))$.
    This category is pointed braided, of the form $\mathcal C((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2,q)$, where $q(a)=-1$ for exactly one $a\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$, and $q(x)=1$ for the other three elements.
    The Gauss sum is $\tau(\mathrm{TC})=2$.
<!-- end example-7 -->
<!-- example-8: body_md -->
\label{eg:Introducing DS}
    The category $\mathcal Z(\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C^\omega(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z))$ is equivalent to $\mathcal Sem\boxtimes\mathcal Sem^{rev}$ (see Example \ref{eg:Semion has no braided real forms}), and is thus referred to as the double-semion category.
    We will denote this category by $\mathrm{DS}$.
    This category is pointed, of the form $\mathcal C((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2,q)$, where $q(\sigma)=i$, $q(\overline\sigma)=-i$, and $q(\sigma\overline\sigma)=1$.
    The Gauss sum is $\tau(\mathrm{DS})=2$.
<!-- end example-8 -->
<!-- example-9: body_md -->
\label{eg:Introducing TF}
    The three fermion category $\mathrm{3F}$ is $\mathcal C((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2,q)$, where $q(a)=-1$ for all three nontrivial elements of $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$.
    The Gauss sum is $\tau(\mathrm{3F})=-2$.
<!-- end example-9 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
For a simple object $X$, the behavior of its self-braiding $\beta_{X,X}:X\otimes X\to X\otimes X$ is called its exchange statistics.
    The self braiding can be complicated in general, but when it is a scalar multiple of the identity, there are special names.
    When $\beta_{X,X}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes X}$, we say that $X$ has bosonic exchange statistics, or that $X$ is a boson.
    When $\beta_{X,X}=-\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes X}$, we say that $X$ has fermionic exchange statistics, or that $X$ is a fermion.
    The name semion is meant to evoke the idea of having exchange statistics that are `halfway in between' $1$ and $-1$.
    More generally, if $\beta_{X,X}=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes X}$, where $\lambda$ is some other complex phase, $X$ is called an anyon.
    
    For our purposes, real or quaternionic simple objects $X$ will be called fermions or bosons based on the exchange statistics of the unique simple object inside of $X\boxtimes\mathbb C$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- example-10: body_md -->
\leavevmode

        \item The toric code $\mathrm{TC}$ has three bosons and one fermion.
        \item The double semion category $\mathrm{DS}$ has two bosons and two semions.
        \item The three fermion category $\mathrm{3F}$ has (unsurprisingly) three fermions and one boson.
        \item The object $Y$ in $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ is a quaternionic fermion.
    \end{itemize}

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:The only possible complexifications}
    Suppose $\mathcal C$ is a nondegenerately braided fusion category over $\mathbb R$, with $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal C)=4$.
    The complexification $\overline\mathcal C$ of $\mathcal C$ is pointed, and $\tau(\overline\mathcal C)$ is real.
    If $\tau(\overline\mathcal C)>0$, then $\overline\mathcal C$ is a Drinfeld center, either $\mathrm{TC}$ or $\mathrm{DS}$.
    If $\tau(\overline\mathcal C)<0$, then $\overline\mathcal C$ is $\mathrm{3F}$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The first two statements are just a rephrasing of the previous discussion, so it will suffice to prove the other two claims.
    
    If $\tau(\overline\mathcal C)>0$, then by \cite[Proposition A.7]{MR2609644}, $\overline\mathcal C$ has a Lagrangian subgroup, which implies that $\overline\mathcal C$ is a Drinfeld center.
    Any complex category $\mathcal D$ for which $\mathcal Z(\mathcal D)\simeq\overline\mathcal C$ must have $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal D)=2$, which forces $\mathcal D$ to be either $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$ or $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C^\omega(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$.
    The first corresponds to $\mathrm{TC}$, and the second to $\mathrm{DS}$.

    Suppose that $\tau(\overline\mathcal C)<0$.
    If $A=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z=\langle x\rangle$, then $q(x)^2=b(x,x)$ must be a 4th root of unity, and so $q(x^k)=\xi^{k^2}$ for some 8th root of unity $\xi$.
    The Gauss sum is then $\tau(\overline\mathcal C)=1+\xi+\xi^4+\xi$, but this can never be negative.
    If $A=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2=\langle x,y\rangle$, then $q(x)^2=b(x,x)=\pm1$, and the same for $y$.
    Choosing $b(x,y)=\pm1$, $q(x)=i^j$ and $q(y)=i^k$ gives $q(x+y)=b(x,y)q(x)q(y)=\pm i^{j+k}$.
    The Gauss sum is then $\tau(\overline\mathcal C)=1+i^j+i^k\pm i^{j+k}$.
    The only way for this to be negative is if $j=k=2$ and $b(x,y)=-1$.
    This corresponds to $\mathrm{3F}$, and the proof is complete.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:Classification of real forms of TC and 3F}
    The category $\mathrm{TC}$ admits four real forms, which we will denote by $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$, $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$, $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b$, and $\mathrm{TC}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$.
    The category $\mathrm{3F}$ admits three real forms, which we will denote by $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb R$, $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb H$, $\mathrm{3F}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Both $\mathrm{TC}$ and $\mathrm{3F}$ admit a skeletal description (gauge) where all associator coefficients are trivial, and where all braiding coefficients are $\pm1$.
    This implies that the tensorator $J$ of the functor $T$ is classified by an element of (a torsor of) $H^2_{br}((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2;\mathbb C^\times)=0$ (here the coefficients are untwisted).
    It follows that $J$ is unique up to monoidal equivalence, when it exists\footnote{The reader should think of this observation as an invocation of Theorem \ref{thm:Equivalence of pre-metric groups and PBFCs}.
    This cohomological fact is what implies that braided $\mathbb C$-linear equivalences of metric group categories are determined up to isomorphism by the underlying group automorphism.
    In this particular context, complex conjugation doesn't affect the result, because all coefficients are real.}.

    One possible choice of $T$ is just the complex conjugation functor, which admits a trivial tensorator $J$.
    Possible real forms resulting from this choice are the braided category $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R:=\mathcal Z(\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z))$, or the purely real version $3\mathrm{F}_{\mathbb R}$ of $\mathrm{3F}$.
    This shows that the $O_3$ obstruction for this $T$ is trivial for both $\mathrm{TC}$ and $\mathrm{3F}$.
    The different choices of framed real forms that use this choice of $(T,J)$ form a torsor over
    \[H^2(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z;\mathrm{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal D}))\cong H^2(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z;(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2)\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\,.\]
    These classes determine which two simples in the real form are quaternionic, if any.
    
    Since $\mathrm{TC}$ has a unique fermionic simple, exactly two of the cohomology classes described above correspond to real forms where the fermion becomes quaternionic, and hence these two forms contain $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb H}$.
    The autoequivalence that swaps the two bosons permutes these two cohomology classes, and so the resulting categories are braided equivalent to one another, and we will denote them by $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$.
    The trivial cohomology class corresponds to the form $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$, while the remaining class corresponds to the form where the fermion is real.
    This last class will be denoted $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b$, because both quaternionic simples are bosonic.

    In the $\mathrm{3F}$ case, all three fermions are in the same orbit under braided autoequivalences, and so all of the nontrivial cohomology classes produce equivalent braided categories, and we will denote the resulting real form by $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb H$.
    The trivial cohomology class causes no Brauer-twistings, and so all four simple objects are real, and we will call this class $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb R$.

    The other option for $T$ is a functor that swaps two simple objects. 
    Note that $\mathrm{3F}$ has other braided autoequivalences, but swapping is the only $T$ that will have order two, up to conjugacy and isomorphism.
    For $\mathrm{3F}$, it doesn't matter which two fermions are swapped, but in the case of $\mathrm{TC}$, a braided functor must fix the unique fermion, so $T$ must swap the two bosons with each other.

    It was already shown in \cite[Appendix A]{MR4934638} that $\mathrm{TC}$ has a real form corresponding to the swap functor, and this form contains a copy of $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb H}$.
    This category has exactly one simple of each type, so we will call it a \emph{triumvirate} and denote it by $\mathrm{TC}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$.
    For both $\mathrm{TC}$ and $\mathrm{3F}$, the swapping action makes the $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ module $\mathrm{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathrm{id}_\mathcal D)\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$ acyclic, so in particular, the real form corresponding to the swap functor is unique, assuming it exists.

    We have found all the real forms of $\mathrm{TC}$, so we will focus on $\mathrm{3F}$ for the rest of the proof.
    Let the fermions be $a$, $b$, and $ab$.
    The functor $T$ will swap $a$ and $b$.
    We can choose a gauge where the braiding is given by
    \[\beta_{a^ib^j,a^kb^\ell}=(-1)^{ik+i\ell+j\ell}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{a^{i+k}b^{j+\ell}}\,.\]
    For the tensorator $J$, we can use the formula
    \[J_{a^ib^j,a^kb^\ell}=(-1)^{jk}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{a^{j+\ell}b^{i+k}}\,.\]
    Recall that if this choice works, then it is unique up to coboundaries.
    This is a valid tensorator, since it is a 2-cocycle and the associators are trivial.
    This choice is braided, as can be seen by the commutativity of the diagram below:
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             
    \[\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
    	{T(a^ib^j)\otimes T(a^kb^\ell)} \&\& {T(a^ib^j\otimes a^kb^\ell)} \\
    	{T(a^kb^\ell)\otimes T(a^ib^j)} \&\& {T(a^kb^\ell\otimes a^ib^j)}
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{jk}}", from=1-1, to=1-3]
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{j\ell+jk+ik}}"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{ik+i\ell+j\ell}}", from=1-3, to=2-3]
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{\ell i}}", from=2-1, to=2-3]
    \end{tikzcd}\;\;.\]

    The next piece of information we need is a monoidal natural isomorphism $\mu:T^2\to\mathrm{id}$.
    The underlying functor of $T^2$ is the identity, and the tensorator is
    \[J^2_{a^ib^j,a^kb^\ell}=(-1)^{i\ell+jk}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{a^{i+k}b^{j+\ell}}\,.\]
    The map
    \[\mu_{a^ib^j}=(-1)^{ij}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{a^ib^j}\]
    provides the desired natural isomorphism, because $\delta\mu=J^2$.
    This $\mu$ provides two isomorphisms from $T^3$ to $T$: $\mu\circ\mathrm{id}_T$ and $\mathrm{id}_T\circ\mu$.
    The obstruction $O_3$ is determined by the ratio of the corresponding scalars.
    It follows that
    \[O_3(1,1,1)=\frac{(-1)^{ji}}{(-1)^{ij}}=1\,,\]
    so the obstruction vanishes, and a real form exists.
    The swapping action creates a complex object $(a\oplus b)$, and the fact that $\mu(ab)=-1\cdot\mathrm{id}_{ab}$ means that $ab$ becomes quaternionic in the real form.
    With one real, one complex, and one quaternionic object, this is another triumvirate category, and we denote it by $\mathrm{3F}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$.

    In summary, there were two options for $T$: all objects are fixed, or two objects are swapped.
    There was never any choice for $J$.
    For $\mathrm{TC}$, when $T$ fixes objects, there were 4 options for $\mu$ that resulted in $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$, $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$($\times2$), and $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b$, respectively.
    For $\mathrm{3F}$, when $T$ fixes objects, there were 4 options for $\mu$ that resulted in $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb R$, and $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb H^f$($\times3$), respectively.
    For both $\mathrm{TC}$ and $\mathrm{3F}$, when $T$ swaps two objects, the unique choice for $\mu$ produces $\mathrm{TC}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$ and $\mathrm{3F}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$, respectively.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
In the above proof, the scalar $\mu(ab)=-1\cdot\mathrm{id}_{ab}$ can be seen as a generalization of the of the Frobenius-Schur indicator.
    This anomaly is precisely what causes $ab$ to become quaternionic in the real form.
    Any attempt to endow $ab$ with an equivariant structure $T(ab)\to ab$ will lead to the equation $|\lambda|^2=\lambda T(\lambda)=\mu(ab)=-1$.
    To get around this, take the direct sum $ab\oplus ab$, and define the equivariant structure to be any $2\times2$ matrix $M$ such that $M\circ T(M)=-I$.
    This works, and the resulting equivariant morphisms come from the copy of $\mathbb H$ inside of $M_2(\mathbb R)$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:DS has a unique real form}
    The double semion category $\mathrm{DS}=\mathcal Z(\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C^\omega(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z))$ has exactly one equivalence class of real form.
    This category $\mathrm{DS}_\mathbb R$ is $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$-graded, with trivial component $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)=\langle\1,\sigma\overline\sigma\rangle_{\oplus}$, and nontrivial component $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C=\langle(\sigma\oplus\overline\sigma)\rangle_\oplus$.
    The real object $\sigma\overline\sigma$ is bosonic.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
As we saw in Example \ref{eg:Semion has no braided real forms}, the category $\mathcal Sem$ has no braided real forms, because a conjugating functor replaces the $i$-braiding with a $-i$-braiding.
    In the double semion category, there is an object $\overline\sigma$, coming from $\mathcal Sem^{rev}$, that does have such a $-i$-braiding.
    Thus, in order for $T$ to be braided, it must swap $\sigma$ and $\overline\sigma$.
    The monoidal structures $J$ that we can equip $T$ with form a torsor over $H^2((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2;\mathbb C^\times)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ (untwisted).
    A representative cocycle for this nontrivial cohomology class is $J_{\sigma^i\overline\sigma^j,\sigma^k\overline\sigma^\ell}=(-1)^{i\ell}\cdot\mathrm{id}$.
    When this cocycle is placed on the (non-conjugating) identity functor, it does not satisfy the braiding condition, and it follows that for any braided functor $(T,J)$, the tensorator $J$ is unique up to cohomology\footnote{Unlike the previous cases in Proposition \ref{prop:Classification of real forms of TC and 3F}, the braiding coefficients are no longer real, and so this must be checked directly.}.

    For $T$ that conjugates scalars and swaps $\sigma$ and $\overline\sigma$, the trivial tensorator $J=\mathrm{id}$ is braided, and so this must be the unique choice.
    Since the tensorator is trivial, $T^2=\mathrm{id}$ on the nose, and we can choose $\mu$ to be the identity natural transformation.
    Strictness of composition, together with $\mu=\mathrm{id}$ allows us to easily verify that $O_3=0$, so a real form does exist.
    As in the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:Classification of real forms of TC and 3F}, the swapping action of $T$ makes $\mathrm{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathrm{id}_\mathrm{DS})$ acyclic, and so $H^2(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z;\mathrm{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathrm{id}_\mathrm{DS}))=0$, and the real form is unique.

    The real form can be constructed as the equivariantization $\mathrm{DS}_\mathbb R=\mathrm{DS}^{\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z}$.
    The objects $\1$ and $\sigma\overline\sigma$ are easily seen to admit equivariant structures coming from identity maps, and the object $\sigma\oplus\overline\sigma$ also admits an equivariant structure via swapping.
    Galois equivariant endomorphisms of $\1$ and $\sigma\overline\sigma$ must be real, and endomorphisms of $(\sigma\oplus\overline\sigma)$ must be of the form $\lambda\oplus\overline\lambda$.
    The braiding is induced from $\mathrm{DS}$, and so $\sigma\overline\sigma$ remains bosonic.

<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
Given a symmetric tensor category $\mathcal E$ over $\mathbb R$, a minimal nondegenerate extension of $\mathcal E$ is a fully faithful braided embedding $i_{\mathcal C}:\mathcal E\hookrightarrow \mathcal C$.

    The identity is given by the canonical embedding $i_\mathcal Z:\mathcal E\hookrightarrow\mathcal Z(\mathcal E)$, and the inverse is given by reversing the braiding.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:Don't worry about the embeddings}
    For $\mathbb D=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb D=\mathbb H$, every embedding $i:\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb D\hookrightarrow\mathcal Z(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb D)$ lies in the same $\mathcal Mext$ equivalence class as the canonical embedding $i_\mathcal Z:\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb D\hookrightarrow\mathcal Z(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb D)$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Note that any autoequivalence $F$ of $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ must fix all objects.
    Functoriality implies that $F$ acts by algebra automorphisms on $\mathrm{End}(Y)\cong\mathbb H$.
    By the Skolem-Noether theorem, all such automorphisms are inner, so we can assume that $F$ acts by conjugation by $h\in\mathbb H^\times$.
    This implies that $\mu_Y:=h\cdot\mathrm{id}_Y$ defines a natural isomorphism from $F$ to the identity functor.
    
    Any monoidal structure $J$ on the identity functor must be a natural isomorphism, and this means that on $Y$ it must commute with all quaternions.
    In other words, $J$ can only take on real values.
    Since a generic $F$ is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor by $h$, transport of structure reveals that the possible $J$ on $F$ must also take on real values.
    In this way, we can assume that the underlying functor is the identity, and values for $J$ are determined by cocycles in $H^2(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z;\mathbb R^\times)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$.
    It follows that in our current computation, the argument for $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ reduces to the related computation for $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R$.

    Suppose then, that $i:\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R\hookrightarrow\mathcal Z(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R)$ is a fully faithful braided embedding.
    Since the nontrivial object $f\in\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R$ is fermionic, it must map to the unique fermionic object $ab\in\mathcal Z(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R)=\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$.
    Since $\1$ must also map to $\1$, the underlying functor is unique.
    
    There are two possible tensor structures $J$ that can be put on $i$ and they have representatives $J_{f,f}=\mathrm{id}_\1$ and $J_{f,f}=-\mathrm{id}_\1$.
    The fist corresponds to the canonical inclusion, so assume that $J$ is given by this second formula.
    Define a functor $(T,K)$, where $T$ is the functor that swaps the bosons $a\leftrightarrow b$, and the tensor structure is
    \[K_{a^ib^j,a^kb^\ell}=(-1)^{ik+i\ell+j\ell}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{a^{a+k}b^{j+\ell}}\,.\]
    One gauge for $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$ has the braiding given by the formula
    \[\beta_{a^ib^j,a^kb^\ell}=(-1)^{i\ell}\,.\]
    The braiding coherence square is given by the diagram below.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     
    \[\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
    	{T(a^ib^j)\otimes T(a^kb^\ell)} \&\& {T(a^ib^j\otimes a^kb^\ell)} \\
    	{T(a^kb^\ell)\otimes T(a^ib^j)} \&\& {T(a^kb^\ell\otimes a^ib^j)}
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{ik+i\ell+jl}}", from=1-1, to=1-3]
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{jk}}"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{i\ell}}", from=1-3, to=2-3]
    	\arrow["{(-1)^{ik+kj+\ell j}}", from=2-1, to=2-3]
    \end{tikzcd}\]
    This commutes, so the functor $(T,K)$ is braided.
    Now notice that $K_{ab,ab}=(-1)\cdot\mathrm{id}_{\1}$, which matches the tensorator $J_{f,f}=(-1)\cdot\mathrm{id}_\1$.
    It follows that $(T,K)\circ(i,J)=i_\mathcal Z$, so the two embeddings represent the same class in $\mathcal Mext(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R)$.

\label{thm:Mext(sVec_R) is Klein-four}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Minimality of $\mathcal C$ implies that $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal C)=4$, and so $\mathcal C$ must be contained in the list in Theorem \ref{thm:List of all ndBFCs over R of dim=4}.
    Of this list, the only categories that extend $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R$ are those listed in the statement of the theorem.
    All of these categories are $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$-graded, and the objects in the nontrivial components braid nontrivially with $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R$, so these are minimal nondegenerate extensions.

    Consider two of these categories, say $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal D$.
    Their product is the category of local modules $\mathcal C\boxdot\mathcal D=(\mathcal C\boxtimes\mathcal D)_A^0$.
    Because all of these extensions are $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$-graded, the category of all modules can be decomposed using the gradings $\mathcal C=\mathcal C_0\oplus\mathcal C_1$ and $\mathcal D=\mathcal D_0\oplus\mathcal D_1$ to give
    \[(\mathcal C\boxtimes\mathcal D)_A\simeq\mathcal C\boxtimes_{\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}}\mathcal D\simeq\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^1\mathcal C_{i}\boxtimes_{\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}}\mathcal D_j\simeq \mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}\oplus\mathcal C_1\oplus\mathcal D_1\oplus\mathcal C_1\boxtimes_{\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}}\mathcal D_1\,.\]
    By minimality, the objects in $\mathcal C_1$ and $\mathcal D_1$ only contain objects that braid nontrivially with the fermion in $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}$, and this implies that these objects are not local modules for $A$.
    The objects that remain already constitute a subcategory with $\mathrm{FPdim}=4$, so the underlying category of the product of extensions is
    \[\mathcal C\boxdot\mathcal D\,:=\;(\mathcal C\boxtimes\mathcal D)_A^0\simeq \mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}\oplus\mathcal C_1\boxtimes_{\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R}}\mathcal D_1\,.\]
    Although this is not a complete description of the braiding, we can still deduce relations in $\mathcal Mext(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R})$ by leveraging our knowledge of the product in $\mathcal Pic(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb R})$.

    The nontrivial component of $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b$ and of $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb H$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb H)\boxtimes\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R$.
    This is just the inclusion of the Brauer group into $\mathcal Br\mathcal Pic(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R)$ that was described in \cite{sanford2026puttingbrauerbrauerpicard}, which happens to land in $\mathcal Pic(\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R)$.
    Since this module has order 2, the minimal extensions $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b\boxdot\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b$, $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b\boxdot\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb H$, and $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb H\boxdot\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb H$ must have only real simple objects.

    We also know that the $\mathrm{3F}$ categories have central charge $-1$.
    By Lemma \ref{lem:Central charge for MNEs}, it follows that the product of two $\mathrm{3F}$ categories must be a $\mathrm{TC}$ category.
    Combining this with the previous observation, we find that $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$ must be the unit element, and the group is generated by $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^b$ and $\mathrm{3F}_\mathbb R$, with $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$ fusion rules.

    The analysis thus far explains the effect of $\boxdot$ on the underlying categories.
    What about the embeddings?
    Suppose that $i_k:\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R\hookrightarrow\mathcal C$ for $k=1,2$ are two MNEs with the same underlying category.
    The product $\boxdot$ produces an embedding $i:\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R\hookrightarrow\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$, because all possible $\mathcal C$ have order two.
    By Lemma \ref{lem:Don't worry about the embeddings}, this resulting embedding must correspond to the identity element in $\mathcal Mext(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R)$.
    But this means that all embeddings for $\mathcal C$ are inverse to each other, so all embeddings are inverse to themselves, and therefore all embeddings for a given category $\mathcal C$ are equivalent.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:Centers of CG cats are real forms of C_0}
    For any fusion category $\mathcal C$ over $\mathbb R$ that is faithfully Galois-graded, $\mathcal C=\mathcal C_0\oplus\mathcal C_1$, $\mathcal Z(\mathcal C)$ must be a real form of $\mathcal Z(\mathcal C_0)$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The complexification of a complex, faithfully Galois-graded fusion category must be indecomposable multifusion.
    Because $\mathbb C\otimes_\mathbb R\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$, every simple object $X$ splits into two simple objects $pX$ and $qX$ upon complexification, and we can write $p=p\1$ and $q=q\1$.

    \[\mathrm{id}_p\otimes (\mathrm{id}_X\boxtimes\lambda)=\mathrm{id}_p\otimes (\lambda\mathrm{id}_X\boxtimes1)\hspace{3mm}\text{and}\hspace{3mm}\mathrm{id}_q\otimes (\mathrm{id}_X\boxtimes\lambda)=\mathrm{id}_q\otimes (\overline\lambda\mathrm{id}_X\boxtimes1)\;.\]
    It follows that the matrix summands of $\overline\mathcal C$ are
    \[\overline\mathcal C\simeq\begin{bmatrix}
        p\overline{\mathcal C_0}p & p\overline{\mathcal C_1}q \\
        q\overline{\mathcal C_1}p & q\overline{\mathcal C_0}q
    \end{bmatrix}\,.\]
    The center of any indecomposable multifusion category over $\mathbb C$ is braided equivalent to the center of any of its diagonal summands.
    Clearly $\mathcal C_0\simeq p\overline{\mathcal C_0}p$ as complex fusion categories, so the center of $\mathcal C_0$ must be equivalent to $\mathcal Z(\overline\mathcal C)\simeq\overline{\mathcal Z(\mathcal C)}$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:Grothendieck ring isomorphism}
    Let $\mathcal C$ be a pivotal fusion category over $\mathbb R$.
    If $\Omega\mathcal C=\mathbb R$, then the internal character $\mathrm{ch}:\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)\to\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal C)$ is an isomorphism from the real Grothendieck ring.
    If $\Omega\mathcal C\cong\mathbb C$, then $\mathrm{ch}:\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb C,\text{Gal}}(\mathcal C)\rightarrow\mathrm{CF}(\mathcal C)$ is an isomorphism from a version of the complex Grothendieck ring, where Galois nontrivial simple objects $X\in\mathcal C$ correspond to generators with the property that $[X]\lambda=\overline\lambda[X]$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The underlying object of the algebra $FR(\1)$ is
    \[FR(\1)\cong\bigoplus_{X}X\otimes_{\mathrm{End}(X)}X^*\,.\]
    The relative tensor products come from the fact that $R$ can be constructed using an end, and the decomposition into a direct sum is a consequence of $\mathcal C$ being semisimple.

    Since Shimizu proves injectivity of $\mathrm{ch}$ more generally, for finite tensor and not just fusion categories, his arguments also use the end formulation, so they apply to our setting.
    In order to prove surjectivity, he specializes to fusion categories in Example 4.4 \emph{loc. cit.}
    The key point is that $\mathcal C(X\otimes_{\mathrm{End}(X)}X^*,\1)$ remains one dimensional in our case, and so the map is an isomorphism.

    The morphism $\mathrm{ch}(X\otimes Y)=\mathrm{ch}(X)\mathrm{ch}(Y)$ factors through a map
    \[X\otimes Y\otimes Y^*\otimes X^*\to\1\,.\]
    Scaling $\mathrm{ch}(Y)$ by a complex number $\lambda$ precomposes this map with $\mathrm{id}_X\otimes \lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}_{Y\otimes Y^*\otimes X^*}$.
    If $X$ is Galois nontrivial, then $\mathrm{id}_X\otimes \lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}_Y=\overline{\lambda}\cdot\mathrm{id}_X\otimes \mathrm{id}_Y$, and this produces the desired Galois action.

\label{eg:Conjugating Ising}
    The category $\mathcal C=\mathcal C_{\overline{\mathbb C}}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,\chi)$ is a non-split version of a Tambara-Yamagami category that was introduced in \cite{MR5003359}.
    This category has $\Omega\mathcal C\cong\mathbb C$, and the simple object $m$ is Galois nontrivial.
    The fusion rules are
    \begin{gather*}
        a\otimes a\cong\1\,,\\
        a\otimes m\cong m\cong m\otimes a\,,\\
        m\otimes m\cong 1\oplus a\,.
    \end{gather*}
    This category admits a pivotal structure, so Proposition  \ref{prop:Grothendieck ring isomorphism} applies.
    To compute in the complex-Galois Grothendieck ring, we will omit brackets for the generators.
    The equation $a^2=1$ tells us that we can build a projection $p=\frac12(1+a)$.
    Writing $1=p+q$, we find that these are central idempotents, so the algebra splits into the images of $p$ and $q$.
    Since $qm=0$ and $qa=-q$, the image of $q$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb C$.
    
    Since $pa=p$, the image of $p$ is generated by $p$ and $pm=m$.
    The fusion rules then imply that
    \[m^2=(1+a)=2p\,.\]
    Thus the element $(\frac{1}{\sqrt2})\cdot m$ squares to $p$ and conjugates scalars.
    This subalgebra can easily be identified as $M_2(\mathbb R)$.
    
    It now follows from Proposition \ref{prop:Grothendieck ring isomorphism} that $\mathrm{End}(R(\1))\cong M_2(\mathbb R)\oplus\mathbb C$.
    Therefore, $\mathcal Z(\mathcal C)$ contains at least one real simple, and one complex simple.
    Since $\mathcal C$ has a Galois nontrivial object, $\Omega\mathcal Z\mathcal C=\mathbb R$.
    We know that $R(\1)$ contains the unit, because it's an algebra, so the $M_2(\mathbb R)$ summand means that this real simple that we've found is the unit object in $\mathcal Z(\mathcal C)$.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:List of all C with dim(ZC)=4}
    Every fusion category $C$ over $\mathbb R$, with the property that $\Omega\mathcal Z\mathcal C=\mathbb R$ and $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal Z(\mathcal C))=4$ must be one of the following categories:
    \begin{enumerate}[(I),leftmargin=50mm, labelsep=5mm, itemsep=1mm]
        \item $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$
        \item $\mathcal C_\mathbb H(1,\chi,-1/2)=:\mathcal Q_-$
        \item $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb R^\omega(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$
        \item $\mathcal C_\mathbb H(1,\chi,+1/2)=:\mathcal Q_+$
        \item $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C\big((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2_{\text{Gal}}\big)$
        \item $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C^\alpha\big((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2_{\text{Gal}}\big)$
        \item $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C\big((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)_{\text{Gal}}\big)$
        \item $\mathcal C_{\overline\mathbb C}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,\chi)$
        \item $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C^\omega\big((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2_{\text{Gal}}\big)$
        \item $\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C^{\alpha\omega}\big((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2_{\text{Gal}}\big)$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Here, $\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb K}^\varphi(G)$ denotes $G$-graded vector spaces over $\mathbb K$, with associator given by the cocycle $\varphi\in Z^3(G;\mathbb C^\times)$.
    The subscript Gal indicates that there is a Galois nontrivial simple object, and that $\varphi$, if present, has twisted $\mathbb C^\times$ coefficients.
    The absence of $\varphi$ indicates the trivial cocycle.
    The categories in items (II), (IV), and (VIII) are non-split Tambara-Yamagami categories, whose classification and notation can be found in \cite{MR5003359}.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal C$ be one such category, and suppose at first that $\Omega\mathcal C=\mathbb R$.
    It follows that $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal C)=2$, so Proposition \ref{prop:Each simple contributes at least 1 to FPdim} implies that each of these categories contains exactly two simple objects, one of which is the unit $\1$.
    The other simple, say $X$, must be self-dual, and must satisfy
    \[1\;=\;\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X)^2}{\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathrm{End}(X)}\,.\]
    By Example \ref{eg:Complex simples contribute more}, this object $X$ cannot be complex.
    Since $X$ must either be real or quaternionic, $\mathrm{FPdim}(X)$ must be either $1$ or $2$, respectively.
    If $\mathrm{FPdim}(X)=1$, then $X$ is invertible, and different choices of associator give categories (I) and (III).
    If $\mathrm{FPdim}(X)=2$, then $X$ is quasi-invertible, and different choices of associator give categories (II) and (IV).

    Now suppose that $\Omega\mathcal C=\mathbb C$.
    Since $\Omega\mathcal Z\mathcal C=\mathbb R$, $\mathcal C$ must have Galois nontrivial objects, and it follows from \cite[Theorem 4.9]{MR4806973} that $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal C)=4$.
    Galois nontriviality induces a $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$-grading $\mathcal C\simeq\mathcal C_0\oplus\mathcal C_1$, and this implies that $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal C_i)=2$ for each component.
    Since all simples in $\mathcal C_0$ are Galois trivial, $\mathcal C_0$ must be fusion over $\mathbb C$.
    There are only two possibilities: $\mathcal C_0=\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$, and $\mathcal C_0=\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}^\omega(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$.
    The Galois-grading implies that $\mathcal C_1$ must be an invertible bimodule for $\mathcal C_0$.
    
    When $\mathcal C_0=\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$, there are two possible bimodules: $\mathcal C_1\simeq\mathcal C_0$, and $\mathcal C_1\simeq\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}$.
    When $\mathcal C_1\simeq\mathcal C_0$, the category will be graded by a group of order 4, and this produces categories (V), (VI), and (VII).
    
    Note that twisted coefficients give $H^3((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2_\text{Gal};\mathbb C^\times_\sim)=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$.
    The four cocycles in $H^3((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2_\text{Gal};\mathbb C^\times_\sim)$ correspond to categories (V), (VI), (IX), and (X).
    The cocycle $\alpha$ becomes trivial when restricted to $\mathcal C_0$, and $\omega$ restricts to be nontrivial.

    When $\mathcal C_0=\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$ and $\mathcal C_1\simeq\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb C$, the category must have Tambara-Yamagami fusion rules, and the classification of \cite[Theorem 7.1]{MR5003359} shows that (VIII) is the only possibility.

    In the case where $\mathcal C_0=\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}^\omega(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$, the only invertible bimodule is the trivial bimodule, and so $\mathcal C_1\simeq\mathcal C_0$.
    The group $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ has no cocycles that restrict to become $\omega$.
    In fact, conjugating coefficients imply that $H^3(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z;\mathbb C^\times_\sim)=1$.
    Thus, assuming nontrivial associator, the only possibility is a $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$-grading, and these correspond to categories (IX) and (X) that we already found.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:The centers of (I)-(IV)}\leavevmode
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The center of (I) is $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$.
        \item The center of (II) is $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$.
        \item The center of (III) and (IV) is $\mathrm{DS}_\mathbb R$.
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
When the category is pointed, and all the associators are trivial as in case (I), half-braidings are determined by characters.
    The trivial and sign character for $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ can equally be equipped to both invertible objects, so all objects in the center are real, and this is $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$.

    The category (II) is the underlying category of $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$.
    Theorem \ref{thm:Mext(sVec_H) is Klein-four} already formally identified the unit element of $\mathcal Mext(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H)$ as $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$, but the unit element of the Mext group is always the Drinfeld center, so $\mathcal Z(\mathcal Q_-)=\mathcal Z(\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H)=\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$.

    The categories (III) and (IV) are the two real forms of $\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb C}^\omega(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)$, whose center is $\mathrm{DS}$.
    Proposition \ref{prop:DS has a unique real form} implies that the center of each of these categories must be $\mathrm{DS}_\mathbb R$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:Centers of (V)-(VIII)}\leavevmode
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The center of (V) is $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$.
        \item The center of (VI) and of (VII) is $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$.
        \item The center of (VIII) is $\mathrm{TC}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$.
        \item The center of (IX) and of (X) is $\mathrm{DS}_\mathbb R$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Lemma \ref{lem:Centers of CG cats are real forms of C_0} tells us that centers of (V)-(VIII) will be real forms of $\mathrm{TC}$, and that the centers of (IX) and (X) must be $\mathrm{DS}_\mathbb R$, by Proposition \ref{prop:DS has a unique real form}.

    Using proposition \ref{prop:Grothendieck ring isomorphism}, the complex-Galois Grothendieck ring of (V) is easily seen to be $M_2(\mathbb R)\oplus M_2(\mathbb R)$.
    Since $FR(\1)\cong 4\cdot\1$, it follows that the unit object in (V) admits two nonisomorphic half-braidings.
    These half-braidings correspond to characters for $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, determined by how the object braids across the Galois trivial simple object $a$.
    The object $a$ can be equipped with such characters as well, and this establishes the existence of four distinct real simples, so the center must be $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb R$.

    Let us write $a$ for the generator of $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ in case (VII).
    The Grothendieck ring contains a central involution $a^2$, so the projections $p=\tfrac12(1+a^2)$ and $q=\tfrac12(1-a^2)$ decompose the algebra into two blocks.
    On the $p$ block, $(pa)^2=p$ and $pa$ conjugates scalars, so this block is isomorphic to $M_2(\mathbb R)$.
    On the $q$ block, $(qa)^2=-q$ and $qa$ conjugates scalars, so this block is isomorphic to $\mathbb H$.
    Since $FR(\1)\cong 4\cdot\1$, we have found real and quaternionic half-braidings that live over $\1$.
    The quaternionic half-braiding can be realized as a $90^\circ$ rotation matrix acting on $2\cdot\1$ whenever the object braids over $a$.
    These half-braidings can be ported to live over $a^2$ as well.
    The quaternionic half-braiding on $a^2$ is easily seen to be fermionic, from the description above, and so the center must be $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$.
    It was shown in \cite[Theorem V.6]{wen2026categoricaltimereversalsymmetries} that (VI) and (VII) are Morita equivalent, and so the center of (VI) is also $\mathrm{TC}_\mathbb H^f$.

    Finally in case (VIII), we have already seen in Example \ref{eg:Conjugating Ising} that $\mathrm{End}(R(\1))\cong M_2(\mathbb R)\oplus\mathbb C$.
    By Proposition \ref{prop:Classification of real forms of TC and 3F}, the presence of a complex object implies that the center must be $\mathrm{TC}_{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{ri}}$.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:Nontrivial auto-Witt equivalence for sVec_H}
    The fusion category $\mathcal C_{\overline{\mathbb C}}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,\chi)$ is a non-Morita-trivial $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$-Witt equivalence from $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ to itself.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal E=\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$, and $\mathcal C=\mathcal C_{\overline{\mathbb C}}(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,\chi)$.
    The fact that $\mathcal Z(\mathcal C)$ is a minimal nondegenerate extension of $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ is equivalent to the statement that there is a braided equivalence $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H\simeq\mathcal Z(\mathcal C,\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H)$, over $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$.
    Since $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ is symmetric, we can write this as
    \[\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H\boxtimes_{\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H}\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H^{rev}\simeq\mathcal Z(\mathcal C,\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H),\]
    which is just the statement that $\mathcal C$ (together with the braided equivalence) is an $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$-Witt equivalence from $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$ to itself.

    Nontriviality means that $\mathcal C$ is not Morita equivalent to $\mathbf{s}\hspace{-1pt}\mathrm{Vec}_\mathbb H$.
    This is immediate, because they have inequivalent Drinfeld centers.
