\label{thm1}
    Let \(a/q \in (0,1)\) be fixed and reduced, and let \(\beta \in [0,1)\). Let \(c\) be a positive number less than \(\sqrt{2(1-\beta)}\). Then for sufficiently large \(T\), we have

\label{thm3}
    Let \(a/q \in (0,1)\) be fixed and reduced. Then there exists a positive constant \(C_q>0\), depending only on \(q\), such that, for sufficiently large \(T\), we have
    \[
    \max_{\sqrt{T}\le t\le T}\Big|\zeta\Big(1+\mathrm{i}t,\frac{a}{q}\Big)\Big| \ge \mathrm{e}^\gamma (\log_2 T+\log_3 T) -C_{q}.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{t1-lem1}
    Let \(a\) and \(q\) be fixed. Then the following estimates hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\((\mathrm{\romannumeral 1})\)] For \(X\ge 2\), we have
\begin{align}
    \label{t1-lem1-moment}
    \int_{-X}^{X}\Big|H\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}t\Big)\Big|^2\mathrm{d}t \ll_q X \log X.
\end{align}
\item[\((\mathrm{\romannumeral 2})\)] Let \(\eta \in (0,1/2]\) be fixed. Then uniformly for all \(|t|\ge 1\) and \(\sigma \in [-\eta,1+\eta]\), we have
\begin{align}
    \label{t1-lem1-bound1}
    H(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t) \ll_{q,\eta}(1+|t|)^{(1-\sigma+\eta)/2}.
\end{align}
\item[\((\mathrm{\romannumeral 3})\)] Uniformly for \(|t|\ge 1\), we have
\begin{align}
    \label{t1-lem1-bound2}
    H(1+\mathrm{i}t)\ll_q \log(2+|t|).
\end{align}
\item[\((\mathrm{\romannumeral 4})\)] There exists a function \(G_{a,q}(z)\), holomorphic in a neighborhood of \(0\), such that
\begin{align}
    \label{t1-lem1-Laurent}
    H(1+z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{q}z}+ G_{a,q}(z).
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By \eqref{t1-moduloeq} and the mean-square estimate for the Hurwitz
zeta function due to Rane \cite[Theorem 2]{Rane1980JLMS},
\[\int_1^T \Big| \zeta\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}t,\frac{a}{q}\Big)\Big|^2 \mathrm{d}t \ll_q T\log T.\]
A straightforward calculation yields \eqref{t1-lem1-moment}. For \((\mathrm{\romannumeral 2})\), let \(\chi\) be a character modulo \(q\), and let \(\chi^\ast\) be the primitive character of conductor \(d \mid q\) inducing \(\chi\). If \(d >1\), then
\[L(s,\chi) = L(s,\chi^\ast)\prod_{p \mid q, p \nmid d} \Big(1-\frac{\chi^\ast(p)}{p^s}\Big).\]
Since \(q\) is fixed, the finite Euler product is \(O_{q,\eta}(1)\) uniformly for \(\sigma \in [-\eta,1+\eta]\). By the classical convexity bound for the primitive character (see, for example \cite[Theorem 3]{Rademacher1959MathZ}),
\[L(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t,\chi^\ast)\ll_{q,\eta}(1+|t|)^{(1-\sigma+\eta)/2}.\]
Hence the same estimate holds for \(L(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t,\chi)\). If \(d=1\), then \(\chi=\chi_0\) is the principal character modulo \(q\), and
\[L(s,\chi_0) = \zeta(s)\prod_{p \mid q}\big(1-p^{-s}\big).\]
The corresponding convexity bound for \(\zeta(s)\) therefore gives
\[L(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t,\chi_0)\ll_{q,\eta}(1+|t|)^{(1-\sigma+\eta)/2}.\]
Thus, uniformly for all characters modulo \(q\),
\[L(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t,\chi)\ll_{q,\eta}(1+|t|)^{(1-\sigma+\eta)/2}.\]
Combining this with \eqref{t1-decomposition} proves \eqref{t1-lem1-bound1}. Similarly, the standard estimate, valid for every Dirichlet character \(\chi\) modulo \(q\),
\[L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi)\ll_q\log(2+|t|)\]together with \eqref{t1-decomposition} gives \eqref{t1-lem1-bound2}. Finally, \eqref{t1-lem1-Laurent} follows from the Laurent expansion of \(H(s)\) at \(s=1\). We complete the proof of Lemma \ref{t1-lem1}.

\label{t1-lem2}
Let \(\sigma \in [0,1)\) be fixed and suppose \(z = x+\mathrm{i} y\). Assume that \(F\) is holomorphic in the horizontal strip \(\sigma-2 \le y \le 0\), satisfying
\[\max_{\sigma-2\le y \le 0}|F(x+\mathrm{i} y)|=O\Big(\frac{1}{x^{2} + 1}\Big).\]
Then, for every real \(t \neq 0\),
\[\int_{-\infty}^\infty H(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i} y)H(\sigma-\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i} y)F(y)\mathrm{d} y= q\sum_{\substack{k,\ell \ge 1 \\ k \equiv \ell \equiv a \pmod q}}\frac{\widehat{F}(\log k\ell)}{k^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}t}\ell^{\sigma-\mathrm{i}t}}-P(t),\]
where 
\[P(t)=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{q}}(H(1-2\mathrm{i}t)F(-t-\mathrm{i}(1-\sigma))+H(1+2\mathrm{i}t)F(t-\mathrm{i}(1-\sigma))).\]
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Write \(h(z)=H(z+\mathrm{i}t)H(z-\mathrm{i}t)F(\mathrm{i}\sigma-\mathrm{i} z)\). Then the only poles in \(\sigma \le \operatorname{Re}(z) \le 2\) occur at \(1 \pm \mathrm{i}t\). Let \(Y\) be large. Integrating \(h(z)\) along the rectangle with vertices \(\sigma \pm \mathrm{i} Y\) and \(2 \pm \mathrm{i} Y\), and using the residue theorem, gives
    \[J_1+J_2+J_3+J_4=\mathrm{i} P(t).\]
Here, \(J_1\) and \(J_3\) represent the contributions from the lower and upper horizontal segments of the rectangular contour, running from \(\sigma-\mathrm{i} Y\) to \(2-\mathrm{i} Y\) and from \(2+\mathrm{i} Y\) to \(\sigma+\mathrm{i} Y\), respectively. Similarly, \(J_2\) and \(J_4\) are the contributions from the right and left vertical segments, oriented from \(2-\mathrm{i} Y\) to \(2+\mathrm{i} Y\) and from \(\sigma+\mathrm{i} Y\) to \(\sigma-\mathrm{i} Y\), respectively.

For \(J_2\), we have
\begin{align*}
    J_2 &= \mathrm{i} \int_{-Y}^Y H(2+\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i} y)H(2-\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i} y)F(y-\mathrm{i}(2-\sigma))\mathrm{d} y \\
    & = q\mathrm{i}\sum_{\substack{k,\ell \ge 1 \\ k \equiv \ell \equiv a \pmod q}}\frac{1}{k^{2+\mathrm{i}t}\ell^{2-\mathrm{i}t}} \int_{-Y}^Y \mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i} y \log k\ell}F(y-\mathrm{i}(2-\sigma))\mathrm{d} y.
\end{align*}
Put \(\omega=y-\mathrm{i}(2-\sigma)\). Then
\[J_2 = q\mathrm{i}\sum_{\substack{k,\ell \ge 1 \\ k \equiv \ell \equiv a \pmod q}}\frac{1}{k^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}t}\ell^{\sigma-\mathrm{i}t}} \int_{-Y-\mathrm{i}(2-\sigma)}^{Y-\mathrm{i}(2-\sigma)}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega\log k \ell}F(\omega)\mathrm{d} \omega.\]
Since \(F\) is holomorphic in the strip \(\sigma-2\le \operatorname{Im}\omega\le0\) and satisfies the growth condition, Cauchy’s theorem allows us to shift the line of integration to the real axis. Therefore,
\[\lim_{Y \to \infty}J_2=q\mathrm{i}\sum_{\substack{k,\ell \ge 1 \\ k,\ell \equiv a \pmod q}}\frac{\widehat{F}(\log k\ell)}{k^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}t}\ell^{\sigma-\mathrm{i}t}}.\]
On the other hand, 
\[\lim_{Y \to \infty}(-J_4)=\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^\infty H(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i} y)H(\sigma-\mathrm{i}t+\mathrm{i} y)F(y)\mathrm{d} y.\]

 To estimate \(J_1\), using the growth condition on \(F\) together with \eqref{t1-lem1-bound1}, we have
\begin{align*}
        |J_1| &\ll \frac{1}{Y^2}\Big(\Big(\int_\sigma^{1+\eta}+\int_{1+\eta}^2\Big)|H(x-\mathrm{i} Y+\mathrm{i}t)||H(x-\mathrm{i} Y-\mathrm{i}t)|\mathrm{d} x\Big) \\
        &\ll_\eta \frac{1}{Y^2}\Big(1+ \int_\sigma^{1+\eta}\big(1+Y^{1-x+\eta}\big)\mathrm{d} x\Big) \ll\frac{1}{Y^2} \Big(1+\frac{Y^{1-\sigma+\eta}-1}{\log Y} \Big).
\end{align*}
Taking \(\eta=1/6\), for sufficiently large \(Y\),
    \[J_1 \ll \frac{1}{T^{5/6}\log Y} \to 0.\]
Similarly, we obtain \(J_3 \to 0\) as \(Y \to \infty\). This completes the proof of Lemma \ref{t1-lem2}.

\label{t1-lem3}
    The set \(\mathcal{N}\) defined above satisfies the following properties.
    \begin{enumerate}
\item[\((\mathrm{\romannumeral 1})\)] The map \(m \mapsto u_m m\) from \(\mathcal{M}\) to \(\mathcal{N}\) is injective. In particular, \(|\mathcal{N}| =|\mathcal{M}|\).
\item[\((\mathrm{\romannumeral 2})\)] For every \(x \in \mathcal{N}\), we have 
\begin{align}
    \label{t1-lem3-congruence}
    x \equiv 1 \pmod q.
\end{align}
\item[\((\mathrm{\romannumeral 3})\)] We have
\begin{align}
    \label{t1-lem3-gcdsum-X}
    \frac{S(\mathcal{N})}{|\mathcal{N}|} \ge \frac{1}{q}\frac{S(\mathcal{M})}{|\mathcal{M}|}.
\end{align}
Moreover, the largest prime factor occurring in \(\mathcal{N}\) is at most the maximum of \(q\) and the largest prime factor occurring in \(\mathcal{M}\).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since every prime factor of \(m\in\mathcal{M}\) is larger than \(q\), while every prime factor of \(u_m\) is at most \(q\), the factorization of \(u_m m\) uniquely determines both \(u_m\) and \(m\). Hence the map \(m \mapsto u_m m\) is injective, and therefore \(|\mathcal{N}|=|\mathcal{M}|\). \eqref{t1-lem3-congruence} follows immediately from the definition of \(u_m\). Let \(x=u_m m\) and \(y=u_n n\). Since \((m,n)\mid(x,y)\) and \(\sqrt{u_mu_n}\leq q\), we have
\[ Q(x,y) = \frac{(x,y)}{\sqrt{xy}} \geq \frac1q\frac{(m,n)}{\sqrt{mn}} = \frac{1}{q}Q(m,n).\]
Summing over \(m,n\in\mathcal{M}\) proves the desired estimate for \(S(\mathcal{N})\). Finally, the assertion concerning the prime factors follows directly from the definition of \(\mathcal{N}\). Combining the above arguments, we complete the proof of Lemma \ref{t1-lem3}.

Let \(x,y\in \mathcal{N}\). Then there exist positive integers \(k\) and \(\ell\) such that \(xk=y\ell\), \(k \equiv \ell \equiv a \pmod q\) and 
\begin{align}

\end{align}
Moreover, \(k\) and \(\ell\) may be chosen so that
\begin{align}
    \label{t1-lem4-bound2}

\end{align}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let \(d=(x,y)\). Then put \(x = d x^\prime\) and \(y = d y^\prime\). Since \(x \equiv y\equiv1\pmod q\), we have \((d,q)=1\), and \(x^\prime, y^\prime \equiv d^{-1} \pmod q\). Let \(r_d \in [1,q]\) satisfy \(r_d \equiv ad \pmod q\), and define \(k = y^\prime r_d\) and \(\ell = x^\prime r_d\). Then, 
\[xk=dx^\prime y^\prime r_d = d y^\prime x^\prime r_d = y\ell.\]
Moreover, we have
\[
k \equiv y^\prime r_d \equiv d^{-1} (ad) \equiv a \pmod q
\]
and similarly \(\ell \equiv a \pmod q\). Finally, 
\[\frac{q}{\sqrt{k\ell}} = \frac{q}{r_d\sqrt{x^\prime y^\prime}} = \frac{q}{r_d} \frac{d}{\sqrt{xy}} = \frac{q}{r_d}Q(x,y) \ge Q(x,y),\]
since \(r_d \le q\). This gives \eqref{t1-lem4-bound1}. Also, \eqref{t1-lem4-bound2} follows from
\[k\ell =r_d^2x^\prime y^\prime = r_d^2 \frac{[x,y]}{(x,y)} \le q^2 \frac{[x,y]}{(x,y)}.\]
We now complete the proof of Lemma \ref{t1-lem4}.

     Let \(K\) be defined as above. If \(k\) and \(\ell\) are positive integers satisfying \(k\ell\le T^\varepsilon\), then

     \begin{align}

Since \(|z|^2 = t^2 + 1/4 \asymp 1+ t^2\) and \(|\sin(x+\mathrm{i} y)| \le \mathrm{e}^{|y|}\), we have \(|\sin^2(\varepsilon z \log T)| \le T^\varepsilon\). Substituting this into the definition of \(K\), we obtain
    \[\Big|K\Big(t\pm \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\Big)\Big| \ll_\varepsilon \frac{T^\varepsilon}{(1+t^2)\log T}.\]
    Finally, differentiating the definition of \(K\) and applying the same estimates to the sine and cosine factors gives the corresponding bound for \(K'(t\pm \mathrm{i}/2)\), with at most one additional factor \(\log T\).

<!-- lem-6: body_md -->
\label{t2-lem1}
Let \(\sigma_0>0\) and \(A>1\) be fixed, and put \(N=\lfloor T^A\rfloor\). Then there exists a constant \(\delta=\delta(\sigma_0,A)>0\) such that, uniformly for \(\sigma\ge\sigma_0\), \(1\le |t|\le T\) and \(0 <\alpha \le 1\), we have
\[\zeta(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \frac{1}{(n+\alpha)^s}+\frac{(N+\alpha)^{1-s}}{s-1}+O_{\sigma_0,A}(T^{-\delta}),\]
where \(s=\sigma+\mathrm{i}t\).
<!-- end lem-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For \(z \in \mathbb{C}\) and \(j \ge 0\), set \((z)_0=1\) and \((z)_j = z(z+1)\cdots(z+j-1)\). Moreover, let \(B_j(x)\) be the \(j\)-th Bernoulli polynomial and let \(\widetilde{B}_j(x) = B_j(x-\lfloor x \rfloor)\) be its periodic version. Choose an integer \(M\ge 1\) sufficiently large such that
\[A\sigma_0+2M(A-1)>1.\]
By \cite[Chapter 12, Theorem 12.21]{Apostol1976}, we have
\begin{align}
    \label{t2-lem1-Euler}
    \zeta(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{(n+\alpha)^s}+\frac{(N+\alpha)^{1-s}}{s-1}+\frac{1}{2(N+\alpha)^s}-s\int_N^\infty\frac{\widetilde{B}_1(x)}{(x+\alpha)^{s+1}}\mathrm{d} x.
\end{align}

For \(k\ge 1\), define
\[I_k = \frac{1}{k!}\int_N^\infty\frac{\widetilde{B}_k(x)}{(x+\alpha)^{s+k}}\mathrm{d} x.\]
Since
\[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} x}\Big(\frac{\widetilde{B}_{k+1}(x)}{(k+1)!}\Big) =\frac{\widetilde{B}_k(x)}{k!},\]
integration by parts gives
\begin{align}
    \label{t2-lem1-Ik}
    I_k = -\frac{B_{k+1}}{(k+1)!(N+\alpha)^{s+k}} +(s+k)I_{k+1}.
\end{align}
Iterating \eqref{t2-lem1-Ik}, and using
\[B_{2j+1}=0 \qquad (j\ge 1),\]
we obtain
\[-sI_1 = \sum_{j=1}^M \frac{B_{2j}}{(2j)!}(s)_{2j-1} \frac{1}{(N+\alpha)^{s+2j-1}}+E_M,\]
where
\[E_M := E_M(s,\alpha,N)= - \frac{(s)_{2M+1}}{(2M+1)!}\int_N^\infty\frac{\widetilde{B}_{2M+1}(x)}{(x+\alpha)^{s+2M+1}}\mathrm{d} x.\]
Combining this with \eqref{t2-lem1-Euler} gives
\begin{align}
    \label{t2-lem1-Euler2}
    \zeta(s,\alpha)&=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{(n+\alpha)^s}+\frac{(N+\alpha)^{1-s}}{s-1}
    +\frac{1}{2(N+\alpha)^s} \nonumber \\
    &\quad+\sum_{j=1}^M \frac{B_{2j}(s)_{2j-1}}{(2j)!(N+\alpha)^{s+2j-1}}+E_M.
\end{align}

We now estimate the last three terms in \eqref{t2-lem1-Euler2}. Since \(N+\alpha\asymp T^A\) uniformly for \(0<\alpha\le 1\), we first have
\begin{align}
    \label{t2-lem1-error1}
    \Big|\frac{1}{2(N+\alpha)^s}\Big| \ll T^{-A\sigma} \le T^{-A\sigma_0}.
\end{align}
For \(1\leq j\leq M\), put \(u=2j-1\). Since \(|(s)_u| \ll_u T^u+(1+\sigma)^u\), we obtain
\[|(s)_u|(N+\alpha)^{-\sigma-u} \ll_u T^u(N+\alpha)^{-\sigma-u} +(1+\sigma)^u(N+\alpha)^{-\sigma-u}.\]
The first term on the right-hand side above satisfies
\[T^u(N+\alpha)^{-\sigma-u} \ll T^{-A\sigma_0-u(A-1)}.\]
On the other hand, for the second term, since \(N+\alpha\ge 2\) for sufficiently large \(T\),
\[(1+\sigma)^u(N+\alpha)^{-(\sigma-\sigma_0)} \ll_{u,\sigma_0} 1.\]
Hence,
\[(1+\sigma)^u(N+\alpha)^{-\sigma-u} \ll_{u,\sigma_0} T^{-A\sigma_0-Au}.\]
Combining the above estimates, we get
\begin{align}
    \label{t2-lem1-error2}
    \sum_{j=1}^M \frac{B_{2j}(s)_{2j-1}}{(2j)!(N+\alpha)^{s+2j-1}} \ll_{\sigma_0,A} T^{-A\sigma_0-(A-1)}.
\end{align}
It remains to estimate \(E_M\). The Fourier series of the periodic Bernoulli function gives
\[\big|\widetilde{B}_{2M+1}(x)\big| \le \frac{2(2M+1)!}{(2\pi)^{2M+1}}\zeta(2M+1).\]
Therefore,
\[|E_M|\ll_M |(s)_{2M+1}|(N+\alpha)^{-\sigma-2M}.\]
Using the same argument as above, we obtain
\[|E_M| \ll_{\sigma_0,A} T^{2M+1-A(\sigma_0+2M)} + T^{-A\sigma_0-2MA}.\]
Since
\[2M+1-A(\sigma_0+2M) = -(A\sigma_0+2M(A-1)-1)<0,\]
there exists a constant \(\delta=\delta(\sigma_0,A)>0\), such that
\begin{align}
    \label{t2-lem1-error3}
    |E_M| \ll_{\sigma_0,A} T^{-\delta}.
\end{align}
After decreasing \(\delta\) if necessary, we may assume that \(0<\delta<A\sigma_0\). Substituting \eqref{t2-lem1-error1}, \eqref{t2-lem1-error2} and \eqref{t2-lem1-error3} into \eqref{t2-lem1-Euler2} proves
\[\zeta(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \frac{1}{(n+\alpha)^s}+\frac{(N+\alpha)^{1-s}}{s-1}+O_{\sigma_0,A}(T^{-\delta})\]
and the proof is complete.

<!-- lem-7: body_md -->
\label{t3-lem1}
Let \(q\) be fixed and let \((a,q)=1\). Set \(Y = \exp\big((\log T)^{10}\big)\). Then, uniformly for \(\sqrt{T}\le |t| \le T\), we have
\[\zeta\Big(1+\mathrm{i}t,\frac{a}{q}\Big) = \frac{q^{1+\mathrm{i}t}}{\phi(q)} \sum_{\chi\pmod q} \overline{\chi(a)}L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi;Y)+O_q\Big(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{9}}\Big).\]
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By the truncated Euler product estimate for Dirichlet \(L\)-functions, we have, uniformly for all characters \(\chi\pmod q\),
\[L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi) = L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi;Y) \Big(1+O_q\Big(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{10}}\Big)\Big)\]
whenever \(T^{1/10} \le |t| \le T\); see Dixit and Mahatab \cite[Lemma 3.1]{Dixit2021BAustMS}. In particular, this holds in the range \(\sqrt{T}\le |t| \le T\). Moreover, for fixed \(q\), the following classical upper bound holds uniformly in this range:
\[L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi) \ll_q \log T.\]
It also follows that
\[L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi;Y) \ll_q \log T\]
and
\[L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi)=L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi;Y)+O_q\Big(\frac{1}{(\log T)^9}\Big).\]
Substituting this estimate into \eqref{fundamental-decomposition}, and noting that the number of characters modulo the fixed modulus \(q\) is \(O_q(1)\), we obtain
\[\zeta\Big(1+\mathrm{i}t,\frac{a}{q}\Big) = \frac{q^{1+\mathrm{i}t}}{\phi(q)} \sum_{\chi\pmod q} \overline{\chi(a)}L(1+\mathrm{i}t,\chi;Y)+O_q\Big(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{9}}\Big).\]
This completes the proof.
