\label{ass.KPY.1.1}
We suppose that the claims $\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i \in{\mathbb N}\}$, are represented by non-negative, identically distributed random vectors, with common distribution $F$. The $\{Y_i\,,\;i \in {\mathbb N} \}$ are real-valued,  independent, but not necessarily identically distributed, random variables, and for any $q \geq 0$ we denote
\begin{eqnarray*}
M_q^{(i)} := \mathbf{E}\left[\left( e^{-Y_i}\right)^q \right]\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
for any $i \in {\mathbb N}$.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{exam.KPY.2.1}
Let $A_1$ defined by \eqref{eq.KPY.2.4}, ${\bf X}=(X_1,\,\ldots,\,X_d)^{\top} \stackrel{d}{\sim} F$. We suppose that the marginals $X_i \stackrel{d}{\sim} F_i \in \mathcal{B} \in \{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_D}\}$ and are quasi-asymptotically independent ($QAI$), that means it holds
\begin{eqnarray*}
\lim \dfrac{\mathbf{P}(X_i > x\,,\;X_j > x)}{\overline{F}_i(x) + \overline{F}_j(x)} =0\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
for any $1\leq i \neq j \leq d$. Then $F \in \mathcal{B}_{A_1}$, with $\mathcal{B} \in \{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_D}\}$, respectively.

{\bf Proof.}~
Since the $(X_1,\,\ldots,\,X_d)$ are $QAI$ and non-negative, we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.2.8}
\overline{F}_{A_1}(x) = \mathbf{P}({\bf X} \in x\,A_1) = \mathbf{P}\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^d \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\right) \leq \mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^d \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\right)  \sim \sum_{i=1}^d \mathbf{P}\left( \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\right) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
where at the last step we used \cite[Th. 3.2]{chen:yuen:2009}. From the other hand side, by Bonferroni inequality and taking into account the $QAI$ property we find
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.2.9}
&&\overline{F}_{A_1}(x) = \mathbf{P}\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^d \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\right) \\[2mm] \notag
&& \geq \sum_{i=1}^d \mathbf{P}\left( \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\right) -  \sum_{1 \leq i< j \leq d} \mathbf{P}\left( \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\,,\;\dfrac{X_j}{b_j} > x\right) \sim \sum_{i=1}^d \mathbf{P}\left( \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\right) \,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
If $X_i/b_i \stackrel{d}{\sim} F_i'$, for $i =1,\,\ldots,\,d$, then obviously $F_i' \in \mathcal{B} \in \{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_D} \}$, for any $i =1,\,\ldots,\, d$. So, in combination with the asymptotic relation
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.2.b}
\overline{F}_{A_1}(x) \sim \sum_{i=1}^d \mathbf{P}\left( \dfrac{X_i}{b_i} > x\right)=\sum_{i=1}^d \overline{F}_i'(x)\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent

\label{def.KPY.2.1}
Let $A \in \mathscr{R}$ some fixed set. We say that $\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i \in {\mathbb N}\}$ are quasi asymptotically independent on $A$, symbolically $QAI_A$, if the $\{X_A^{(i)}\,,\;i \in {\mathbb N}\}$ are $QAI$, namely for any $i,\,j \in {\mathbb N}$ with $i\neq j$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.2.12}
\lim \dfrac{\mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)} > x\,,\;X_A^{(j)} > x)}{\mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)} > x)+\mathbf{P}(X_A^{(j)} > x)} = 0\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem.KPY.2.1}
In the framework of one-dimensional set up, $QAI$ has been studied in several papers, and is satisfied by many commonly used copulas, see for example \cite{chen:yuen:2009}, \cite{li:2013}, \cite{cheng:2014} among others. From Definition \ref{def.KPY.2.1}, we can see that if $\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i \in {\mathbb N}\}$ are independent, then they are also $QAI_A$, for any $A \in \mathscr{R}$. In \cite[Sec. 2.2]{chen:konstantinides:passalidis:2025}, we obtain two examples of $QAI_A$, where $A= A_1'$.

In the following example we show sufficient conditions for the $QAI_{A_1'}$, in combination with the distributions of the claim vectors in $(\mathcal{C}\cap \mathcal{P_D})_{A_1'}$. For sake of simplicity, we use the set 
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.2.13}
A_1' = \{(x,\,y)\;:\; x\vee y > 1\}\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent  
but the result holds through similar methodology for any $b>0$, instead of $b=1$, as in \eqref{eq.KPY.2.13}.

\label{exam.KPY.2.2}
Let ${\bf X}=(X_1,\,X_2)$ and ${\bf Z}=(Z_1,\,Z_2)$, two non-negative random vectors, with marginal distributions from class $\mathcal{B} \in \{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_D}\}$. We suppose that the $X_1,\,X_2,\,Z_1,\,Z_2$, are widely upper orthant dependent, symbolically $WUOD$, see for examples of this dependence in \cite{wang:wang:gao:2013}, \cite{wang:cheng:2011}. Namely, for the $Y_1,\,Y_2,\,Y_3,\,Y_4$ with $Y_i \in \{X_1,\,X_2,\,Z_1,\,Z_2\}$, for $i =1,\,\ldots,\,4$, with $Y_i \neq Y_j$, for any $1\leq i \neq j \leq 4$, there exists a sequence of positive constants $\{g_U(n)\,,\;n =1,\,\ldots,\,4\}$ such that it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.2.14}
\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^n \{Y_i > x_i\} \right) \leq g_U(n) \prod_{i=1}^n \mathbf{P}(Y_i >x_i)\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
for all $x_i \in {\mathbb R}$, with $i=1,\,\ldots,\,n$. Then the ${\bf X}$ and  ${\bf Z}$ follow distributions from the class $\mathcal{B}_{A_1'}$, with $\mathcal{B} \in \{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_D}\}$ and are $QAI_{A_1'}$.

{\bf Proof.}~
Firstly, it holds that $WUOD \subsetneq QAI$. Indeed, since by \eqref{eq.KPY.2.14}, for any $1\leq i \neq j \leq 4$ we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.2.15} \notag
&&\lim \dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(Y_i > x\,,\;Y_j > x \right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(Y_j > x \right)} \leq g_U(2) \lim \dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(Y_i > x \right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(Y_j > x \right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(Y_j > x \right)} \\[2mm]
&&=g_U(2) \lim \mathbf{P}\left(Y_i > x \right)=0\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
and from \eqref{eq.KPY.2.15} we take immediately that $QAI$ is valid. Hence, from Example \ref{exam.KPY.2.1}, we find that the distributions of ${\bf X}$ and ${\bf Z}$ belong to $\mathcal{B}_{A_1'}$, with $\mathcal{B} \in \{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_D}\}$. Further, by \eqref{eq.KPY.2.b} we obtain

For any $x>0$ we obtain

\label{rem.KPY.3.1}
We have to notice that the assumption $G_i(x\,A) \asymp G_j(x\,A)$, with $i,\,j\; \in {\mathbb N}$ and $i \neq j$, implies that $\overline{G}_A^{(i)}(x) \asymp \overline{G}_A^{(j)}(x)$, where $G_A^{(i)}$ represents the distribution of

Further, the relation \eqref{eq.KPY.3.1} of Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.3.1}  implies the fact that insurance risks dominate on the financial risks, in the sense that the $e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_i)}$ follows distribution with lighter tail than that of $F_A$. \begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.3.2}  
&&\mathbf{E}\left[ \left( e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_i)} \right)^{p_2} \right] = \mathbf{E}\left[ \left( e^{B(\tau_{i-1})-B(\tau_i)-Y_i} \right)^{p_2} \right] \\[2mm] \notag
&& =\mathbf{E}\left[ \left( e^{-Y_i} \right)^{p_2} \right]\,\mathbf{E}\left[ e^{-p_2[B(\tau_i-\tau_{i-1})]} \right] = M_{p_2}^{(i)}\,\mathbf{E}\left[ e^{-p_2[B(w_i)]} \right] \leq \mathbf{E}\left[ e^{w_i\,\phi_B(p_2)} \right] < \infty\,, 
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
where $\{w_i:=\tau_i - \tau_{i-1}\,,\; i \in {\mathbb N}\}$ represents the inter-arrival times between successive claim arrivals. At the pre-last step we used relation \eqref{eq.KPY.1.3} 
and \eqref{eq.KPY.3.1}, while the last step follows by the fact that $\phi_B(\cdot)$ is finite, on any finite point. From \eqref{eq.KPY.3.2} we see that the same would hold and 
for $M_{p_2}^{(i)} < \infty$, namely $\mathbf{E}\left[ \left( e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_i)} \right)^{p_2} \right] < \infty$, however relation \eqref{eq.KPY.3.1} is necessary for the convergence of the infinite randomly weighted sums, that were used in the proof of Theorem \ref{th.KPY.3.1}. The domination of the insurance risks against the financial risks is an assumption that is used in most of the papers on risk theory, although such an assumption was not observed always in actuarial  practice, see \cite{li:tang:2015}, \cite{tang:yang:2019} and \cite{konstantinides:passalidis:2025o}, for some discussions on this opposite case.

\label{rem.KPY.3.2}

\label{th.KPY.3.1}
Let consider the discounted aggregate claims from \eqref{eq.KPY.1.2}. Under Assumptions \ref{ass.KPY.1.1}, \ref{ass.KPY.1.2}, \ref{ass.KPY.3.1}, \ref{ass.KPY.3.2} and if the sequence $\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i \in {\mathbb N}\}$ is $QAI_A$, it holds

&&\mathbf{P}\left( {\bf D}(t) \in x\,A \right) \sim \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta =0\}}  \in x\,A \right) +\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P}\left(\widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta >0\}}  \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm]
&&= \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
uniformly for $t \in \Lambda_T$, for any fixed $T \in \Lambda$.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem.KPY.3.4}
Relation \eqref{eq.KPY.3.7} looks a bit complicated. However, it contains the products of independent random vectors ${\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta =0\}}$ and $\widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta >0\}}$, while later we provide through Corollary \ref{cor.KPY.5.1} some cases, in which the $\widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}$ can be also approached via an independent product. This shows the strong  presence of the multivariate linear single big jump principle, for the discounted aggregate claims, although we are not limited on L\'{e}vy-Renewal environment.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor.KPY.3.1} 
Let consider the discounted aggregate claims in relation \eqref{eq.KPY.1.2}. Under the conditions of Theorem \ref{th.KPY.3.1} with the restrictions $F \in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)$, $G_i \in MRV(\beta,\,Q_i,\,\nu_i)$, with $i \in {\mathbb N}$, and $\alpha,\,\beta \in (0,\,\infty)$, and instead of  \eqref{eq.KPY.3.1}, we assume

M_{\alpha}^{(i)} \vee M_{\beta}^{(i)} \leq 1\,, \qquad (M_{p_1}^{(i)} \vee M_{p_2}^{(i)} ) \bigvee (M_{q_1}^{(i)} \vee M_{q_2}^{(i)}) < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent

\end{eqnarray}\noindent

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem.KPY.3.5}
As in Theorem \eqref{th.KPY.3.1}, here again the estimation in \eqref{eq.KPY.3.9} is in fact uniform with respect to $n \in {\mathbb N}$, see Lemma \ref{lem.KPY.4.4} below. <!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem.KPY.4.1}
Let $A \in \mathscr{R}$ some fixed set. We suppose that Assumptions \ref{ass.KPY.1.1}, \ref{ass.KPY.1.2} hold, the $\{ {\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i \in {\mathbb N} \}$ are $QAI_A$, with $F \in \mathcal{C}_A$, and 
$G_i \in \mathcal{C}_A$, with $i \in {\mathbb N}$, with $G_i(x\,A) \asymp G_j(x\,A)$, $i,\,j \in {\mathbb N}$, $i \neq j$. Let, for any $n \in {\mathbb N}$, there exists some 
\begin{eqnarray*}
p > J_{F_A}^+ \vee  J_{G_A^{(1)}}^+ \,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
such that it holds $M_p^{(i)} < \infty$, for $i = 1,\,\ldots,\,n$.  Then, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.2}
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) 
\sim \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right)  \\[2mm] \notag
&&+  \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}}\in x\,A \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right)\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
uniformly for $t \in  \Lambda_T$, for any fixed $T \in \Lambda$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- head-5: body_md -->
~
At first we shall show that the sequence $\left\{ \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)} \,,\; i \in{\mathbb N} \right\}$ is $QAI_A$. It is enough to show that the sequence $\left\{\widehat{ X}_A^{(i)}\,,\; i \in{\mathbb N} \right\}$ (recall relation \eqref{eq.KPY.3.a}) is $QAI$. Since $F_A \in \mathcal{C}$ and $M_p^{(i)} < \infty$ for any $i \in {\mathbb N} $, in combination with the fact that the sequence $\left\{X_A^{(i)}\,,\; i \in{\mathbb N} \right\}$ is $QAI$, by \cite[Th. 2.2]{li:2013} we obtain that the sequence $\left\{ \widehat{ X}_A^{(i)}=X_A^{(i)}\,e^{-Y_i}\,,\; i \in{\mathbb N} \right\}$ is $QAI$. 

Next, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.4}
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) 
  \\[2mm] \notag
&&=\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right)+\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n}  \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$. We have to show that for some $p > J_{F_A}^+ \vee J_{G_A^{(1)}}^+ $ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.5}
\mathbf{E} \left[ \Theta_i^p(t) \,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \right] < \infty\,, \quad  \mathbf{E} \left[ \widetilde{\Theta}_i^p(t) \,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \right] < \infty\,, 
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$.  

Indeed, for any $t \in  \Lambda_T$ we obtain
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\mathbf{E} \left[ \left(\Theta_i(t) \right)^p\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \right] = \mathbf{E} \left[ \left(\exp\left\{-B(\tau_i ) - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} Y_j\right\}\,{\bf 1}{\{\tau_j \leq t\}}\right)^p\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \right] \\[2mm]
&& =\mathbf{P}(\theta=0)\,\int_0^t \mathbf{E} \left[ \left( e^{-B(s ) - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} Y_j}\,{\bf 1}{\{\tau_j \leq t\}}\right)^p \right]\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \in ds) \\[2mm]
&& \leq \mathbf{E}\left[e^{ -p\, \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} Y_j}\right] \,\int_0^t \mathbf{E} \left[ e^{-p\,B(s ) }\right]\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \in ds) \\[2mm]
&& \leq \mathbf{E}\left[e^{ -p\, (i-1)\,\widehat{Y}}\right] \,\int_0^t e^{s\,\phi_B(p)}\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \in ds) \leq \left\{\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{ -\widehat{Y}}\right)^p\right]\right\}^{i-1} \,\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\tau_i\,\phi_B(p)}\,{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_i \leq T\}} \right] < \infty\,, 
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
where we denote 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\widehat{Y}:= \bigwedge_{j=1}^{i-1} Y_j\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
and at the fourth step we used \eqref{eq.KPY.1.3} and the last step follows from the inequalities $-\infty < \phi_B(p) < \infty$, hence the last expectation is bounded either by $e^{T\,\phi_B(p)}$, when $\phi_B(p) > 0$, or by unity, when $\phi_B(p) \leq 0$. The last relation provides the first inequality of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.5}.

The second inequality of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.5} follows by quite similar way, through integration with respect to $\mathbf{P}(\tau_i + \theta \in ds)$.

From the first relation of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.5}, and since $F \in \mathcal{C}_A$, we find, for  any $t \in  \Lambda_T$, the following statements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The $X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} $ follows a distribution from class $\mathcal{C}$, see \cite[Th.3.4 (ii)]{cline:samorodnitsky:1994} or \cite[Prop. 5.3 (iv)]{leipus:siaulys:konstantinides:2023}. Recall that the $X_A^{(i)}$ and $\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} $ are independent. Hence the distribution of the product  $ {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) $ belongs to class $\mathcal{C}_A$
\item
The sequence $\left\{ {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}\,,\; i \in{\mathbb N} \right\}$ is $QAI_A$, due to \cite[Th. 2.2]{li:2013}, that is valid because of the first relation of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.5}, in combination with $F_A \in \mathcal{C}$.  
\end{enumerate}

Therefore, by \cite[Th. 4.1 (i)]{konstantinides:passalidis:2024g}, we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.6}
\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right) \sim \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right)\,, 
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$.

In a similar way, using the second inequality of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.5}, for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, the sequence 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\left\{ \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}}\,,\; i \in{\mathbb N} \right\}\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
is $QAI_A$, and their distributions belong to class $\mathcal{C}_A$. Applying \cite[Th. 4.1 (i)]{konstantinides:passalidis:2024g} we find that it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.7}
\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}}  \in x\,A \right) \sim \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right)\,, 
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$.

From \eqref{eq.KPY.4.4}, \eqref{eq.KPY.4.6} and \eqref{eq.KPY.4.7}, we conclude that \eqref{eq.KPY.4.2} holds uniformly for $t \in  \Lambda_T$.     
~\hfill \qed

\label{lem.KPY.4.2}
Let $A \in \mathscr{R}$ some fixed set. We suppose that Assumptions \ref{ass.KPY.1.1}, \ref{ass.KPY.1.2}, \ref{ass.KPY.3.1}, \ref{ass.KPY.3.2} are valid.  Then, it holds

\end{eqnarray}\noindent
and 
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.9}
\lim_{N \to \infty} \limsup \sup_{t\in \Lambda_T} \dfrac{\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right)}{\mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(1)}\,\Theta_1(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right) +\mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(1)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_1(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right)} = 0\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for any fixed $T \in  \Lambda$.

<!-- head-6: body_md -->
~
At first, taking into account that $F \in \mathcal{C}_A$, by \eqref{eq.KPY.4.5}, recalling that Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.3.2} is stronger than the moment conditions that we assumed in Lemma \ref{lem.KPY.4.1}, from \cite[Th. 3.3 (iv)]{cline:samorodnitsky:1994} we obtain that it holds

\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for any $i \in {\mathbb N}$ and all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, and similarly, since $G_i \in \mathcal{C}_A$ and $G_i(x\,A) \asymp G_1(x\,A)$, for any $i \in {\mathbb N}$ it holds
 \begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.11}
\mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right)\asymp  \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(1)} \in x\,A \right)\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$. From \eqref{eq.KPY.4.10} is implied that the 
\begin{eqnarray*}
X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
with $i \in {\mathbb N}$ and all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, have Matuszewska index equal to $J_{F_A}^+$, while from \eqref{eq.KPY.4.11} is implied that the 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\widehat{ X}_A^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}}\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
with $i \in {\mathbb N}$ and all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, have Matuszewska index equal to $J_{G_A^{(1)}}^+$.

Further, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.12}
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) 
  \\[2mm] \notag
&&=\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right)+\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{n}  \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$. For the fixed $\gamma > 1$ from Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.3.2}, we can find some large enough $n_0 {\mathbb N}$, such that for any $N \geq n_0$ it holds $\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} i^{-\gamma} \leq 1$. Then we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.13} \notag
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right)  =\mathbf{P} \left( \sup_{{\bf p}\in I_A} {\bf p}^{\top} \left[\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \right] > x \right) \\[2mm] \notag
&&\leq \mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} > x \right) \leq \mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} > x\,\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} i^{-\gamma} \right) \\[2mm] \notag
&&\leq \mathbf{P} \left( \bigcup_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \left\{X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} > x\,i^{-\gamma} \right\} \right) \leq \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P} \left( X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} > x\,i^{-\gamma} \right) \,,\\
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, where at the second step we used the fact that the sequences of events 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\left\{ \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{k} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right)\; k \in {\mathbb N} \right\}\,,\; \left\{ \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{k} X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} > x \right)\,,\; k \in {\mathbb N} \right\}
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
are increasing. 

We define
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.ex.1}
R^{+}(T) : = \exp\left\{ - \inf_{0\leq s \leq T} B(s) - \inf_{0\leq s \leq T} J(s) \right\}\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
Then, the following inequality holds almost surely for all $t \in \Lambda_T$
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.ex.2}
\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta =0\}} \leq  R^{+}(T)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta =0\}}\,{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_i \leq t \}}\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
For each term of the last sum in \eqref{eq.KPY.4.13}, from \eqref{eq.KPY.ex.2}, for all $t \in \Lambda_T$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.ex.3}
&&\mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta =0\}} > x\,i^{-\gamma} ) \leq  \mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)}\,R^{+}(T)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta =0\}} > x\,i^{-\gamma}\,,\;\tau_i \leq t )  \\[2mm] \notag
&& \leq  \mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)}\,R^{+}(T) > x\,i^{-\gamma} )\,\mathbf{P}(\theta=0)\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \leq T) \,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
Following the argumentation in the proof of \cite[Lem. 4.4]{guo:2022}, and applying the generalized Potter inequalities for the $q^*$ in Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.3.2}, see \cite[Prop. 2.2.1]{bingham:goldie:teugels:1987}, we find that for any large enough $x>0$, and any $i \in {\mathbb N}$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.ex.4}
\mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)}\,R^{+}(T)> x\,i^{-\gamma} ) \leq C\,i^{\gamma\,q^*} \mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)}> x ) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for some constant $C = C\left( \mathbf{E}\left[ \left(R^{+}(T) \right)^{\gamma\,q^*} \right]\right)$, 
where 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathbf{E}\left[ \left(R^{+}(T)\right)^{\gamma\,q^*} \right]=\mathbf{E}\left[  \exp\left\{ - \gamma\,q^*\,\left[\inf_{0\leq s \leq T} B(s) - \inf_{0\leq s \leq T} J(s)\right] \right\} \right] < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
that is implied by Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.3.1}. Hence, by \eqref{eq.KPY.4.13}, \eqref{eq.KPY.ex.3} and \eqref{eq.KPY.ex.4}, for all $t \in \Lambda_T$, $N \geq n_0$, we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.ex.5}
&&\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta =0\}} \in x\,A \right) \lesssim C\,\mathbf{P}( {\bf X} \in x\,A) \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \,i^{\gamma\,q^*}\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \leq T) \\[2mm] \notag
&& = \dfrac C{\gamma\,q^* +1}\,\mathbf{P}( {\bf X} \in x\,A) \mathbf{E}\left[ N^{\gamma\,q^* +1}(T)\,{\bf 1}_{\{N(T) > N+1 \}} \right] \,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 


Hence, from \eqref{eq.KPY.ex.5} we obtain that for any $\varepsilon >0$, there exists some $n^* \in {\mathbb N}$ such that for any $N > n^*\vee n_0$, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.16}
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x\,A \right)  \\[2mm] \notag
&&\lesssim \varepsilon\,\mathbf{P}({\bf X}^{(1)} \in x\,A)  \lesssim \varepsilon\,C''\,\mathbf{P}({\bf X}^{(1)}\,\Theta_1(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in \Lambda_T$, where the $C'' \in (0,\,\infty)$ follows by \eqref{eq.KPY.4.10}.

For the second probability on the right member of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.12},  following similar steps, as these for \eqref{eq.KPY.4.13}, \eqref{eq.KPY.ex.5}  we find 
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.17}
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm] \notag
&& \lesssim \dfrac C{\gamma\,q^* +1}\,\mathbf{P}(\widehat{\bf X}^{(1)}\in x\,A)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N^{\gamma\,q^* +1}(T)\,{\bf 1}_{\{N(T) > N+1\}} \right] \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, where now the constant $C$, is derived for the term $\mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)}\,e^{-\widehat{Y}} > x ) \geq 
\mathbf{P}(X_A^{(i)}\,e^{-Y_i} > x ) $, for any $x > 0$. 

Hence, from \eqref{eq.KPY.4.17}, for any $\varepsilon>0$, there exists some $n' \in{\mathbb N}$, such that for all $N >n_0\vee n'$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.19}
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm] \notag
&& \lesssim \varepsilon\,\mathbf{P}(\widehat{\bf X}^{(1)}\in x\,A) \lesssim \varepsilon\,C''\,\mathbf{P}(\widehat{\bf X}^{(1)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_1(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A)\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, where at the last step on $C'' \in (0,\,\infty)$ we used \eqref{eq.KPY.4.11}.

From \eqref{eq.KPY.4.12}, \eqref{eq.KPY.4.16}, \eqref{eq.KPY.4.19}, and the arbitrary choice of $\varepsilon>0$, we obtain \eqref{eq.KPY.4.8}.

Relation  \eqref{eq.KPY.4.9} can be proved similarly and easier, since we do not need the sum $\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} i^{-\gamma} $, as in \eqref{eq.KPY.4.13}.
~\hfill \qed

We show only the first relation of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.20}, since the second follows immediately from it. Let consider a fixed, large $N \in {\mathbb N}$. Then by Lemma \ref{lem.KPY.4.1} we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.21} \notag
\lim \sup_{N\geq n \in {\mathbb N}} \left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right)}{ \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left({\bf X}^{(i)} \Theta_i(t) {\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}  \in x A \right)+  \sum_{i=1}^{n}

\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
uniformly for $t \in  \Lambda_T$. It remains to show the first relation of \eqref{eq.KPY.4.20} for all $n> N$.

For the lower bound for all $n> N$, we find
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) \geq \mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{N} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm]
&&\sim \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right) + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm]
&&\geq \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} - \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\right) \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm] 
&&+\left(\sum_{i=1}^n - \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \right) \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right)\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
uniformly for $t \in  \Lambda_T$, where at the second step we applied Lemma \ref{lem.KPY.4.1}. From Lemma \ref{lem.KPY.4.2}, recall relations \eqref{eq.KPY.4.16} and \eqref{eq.KPY.4.19}, for any $\varepsilon > 0$, we can find a large enough $N>0$, such that for all $n> N$, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.22} \notag
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) \gtrsim  \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm]
&&-\varepsilon\, \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(1)}\,\Theta_1(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right) + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm] \notag
&&-\varepsilon\, \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(1)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_1(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right)  \\[2mm] \notag
&&\geq (1-\varepsilon) \Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right) +\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{P} \left( \widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \Bigg)\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
uniformly for $t \in  \Lambda_T$.

From the other hand side, for any $\varepsilon >0$, for all $n > N$, we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.23} \notag
&&\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right) \leq
\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n}  X_{A}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) >x \right) \leq \mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}  X_{A}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) >x \right) \\[2mm] 
&&= \mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}  X_{A}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} >x \right) +\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \widehat{X}_A^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} >x \right) \\[2mm]\notag
&&\sim \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P} \left(  X_{A}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} >x \right) + \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P} \left(\widehat{X}_A^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} >x \right)\leq \left(\sum_{i=1}^n + \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\right) \\[2mm]  \notag
&& \mathbf{P} \left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right) + \left(\sum_{i=1}^n + \sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty} \right) \mathbf{P} \left(\widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \\[2mm] \notag
&&\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,\left[\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{P} \left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right)  + \sum_{i=1}^n  \mathbf{P} \left(\widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right)\right] 
\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent

At the last step we applied Lemma \ref{lem.KPY.4.2}. Hence, by \eqref{eq.KPY.4.22},  \eqref{eq.KPY.4.23}, due to the arbitrary choice of $\varepsilon>0$, we find
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.24} \notag
\limsup_{N < n} \left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t) \in x\,A \right)}{\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{P} \left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Theta_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \in x\,A \right)  + \sum_{i=1}^n  \mathbf{P} \left(\widehat{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right)} - 1 \right| =0 \,,\\
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
uniformly for $t \in  \Lambda_T$. From relations \eqref{eq.KPY.4.23} and  \eqref{eq.KPY.4.24} we obtain that the first limit of  \eqref{eq.KPY.4.20} tends to zero. 

We close the proof with the justification of the application of \cite[Th. 2]{yi:chen:su:2011}. If $J_{F_A}^+ <1$, then \cite[Th. 2]{yi:chen:su:2011} in order to be applied we must show that 
for $q^*$ from Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.3.2}, and for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.14}
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_i^{p_1}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \bigvee \Theta_i^{p_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}\right] < \infty \,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
Indeed, it is enough to show only  
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.15}
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*} \,\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_i^{p_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \right] < \infty \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
since the corresponding convergence with index $p_1$ follows similarly.

From the independence between $\{N(t)\,,\; t\geq 0\}$ and $\theta$, and the independence from all the other sources of randomness, recall Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.1.2}, we obtain 
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\mathbf{E}\left[ \Theta_i^{p_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}\right]  =\mathbf{P}(\theta=0)\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*} \,\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left\{-B(\tau_i)-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}Y_j\right\}\,{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_i \leq t\}} \right]^{p_2} \\[2mm] \notag
&& \leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*} \,\int_0^t \mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}Y_j}\right)^{p_2}\right]\,\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-B(s)} \right)^{p_2}\right]\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \in ds)  \\[2mm] \notag
&& \leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*} \,\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-\widehat{Y}}\right)^{p_2}\right]\right)^{i-1}\,\int_0^t e^{s\,\phi(p_2)} \,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \in ds) \\[2mm] \notag
&& \leq C^* \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*} \,\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-\widehat{Y}}\right)^{p_2}\right]\right)^{i-1}\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \leq t) \leq \dfrac{C^*}{\gamma\,q^* +1}\,\mathbf{E}[(N(t))^{\gamma\,q^*+1}] < \infty\,, 
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, where $C^*= e^{T\,\phi_B(p_2)}$, when $\phi_B(p_2)>0$ and $C^*=1$, when $\phi_B(p_2)\leq 0$. The last step follows by Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.3.2}. If $J_{G_A^{(1)}}^+ <1$, the same theorem can be applied on the second sum through 
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.18}
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\mathbf{E}\left[\widetilde{\Theta}_i^{q_1}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \bigvee \widetilde{\Theta}_i^{q_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}}\right] < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$. It is enough to show only
\begin{eqnarray*}
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\mathbf{E}\left[\widetilde{\Theta}_i^{q_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}}\right] < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, since the corresponding convergence with $q_1$, can be proved through similar arguments.

Following the line of proof for \eqref{eq.KPY.4.15}, taking into consideration that since $\theta>0$, we obtain $N[(\tau_i+ \theta)-] \geq i$, and $N_{\Theta}(t) \leq N(t)$ almost surely, for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$, we find
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\mathbf{E}\left[\widetilde{\Theta}_i^{q_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \right] =\mathbf{P}(\theta >0)\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-B(\tau_i + \theta) +Y_i - \sum_{j=1}^{N[(\tau_i+\theta)-]}}\, {\bf 1}_{\{\tau_i+\theta \leq t\}}\right)^{q_2} \right] \\[2mm] 
&&\leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\int_0^t\,\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-B(s)}\right)^{p_2} \right]\,\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{Y_i}\,e^{ - \sum_{j=1}^{i}Y_j}\right)^{p_2} \right]\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i+ \theta \in ds) \\[2mm] 
&&\leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\left\{\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-\widehat{Y}}\right)^{p_2} \right]\right\}^{i-1}\,\int_0^t\,e^{s\,\phi_B(p_2)}\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i+ \theta \in ds) \\[2mm] 
&&\leq C^*\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,q^*}\,\left\{\mathbf{E}\left[\left(e^{-\widehat{Y}}\right)^{p_2} \right]\right\}^{i-1}\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i+ \theta \leq t) \leq \dfrac{C^*}{\gamma\,q^*+1}\,\mathbf{E}\left[\left(N_{\Theta}(t)\right)^{\gamma\,q^* +1} \right] < \infty\,.
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 

If $J_{F_A}^+ \geq 1$, for the application of \cite[Th. 2]{yi:chen:su:2011}, should hold
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.25} 
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left( \mathbf{E}\left[ \Theta_i^{p_1}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \vee \Theta_i^{p_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}}\right] \right)^{1/p_2} < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
for all $t \in  \Lambda_T$. It is enough to show that the corresponding sum converges for each of the two terms. For the $p_2$ we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.4.26} \notag
&&\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left( \mathbf{E}\left[ \Theta_i^{p_2}(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta=0\}} \right] \right)^{1/p_2} = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left(\mathbf{P}((\theta=0)\, \mathbf{E}\left[ \left( e^{-B(\tau_i)-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} Y_j}\,{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_i \leq t\}} \right)^{p_2}\right] \right)^{1/p_2} \\[2mm] \notag
&&\leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left( \int_0^t \mathbf{E}\left[ (e^{-B(s)})^{p_2}\right]\,\mathbf{E}\left[ \left( e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} Y_j} \right)^{p_2}\right] \mathbf{P}(\tau_i \in ds) \right)^{1/p_2}  \\[2mm] \notag 
&&\leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left( \left\{\mathbf{E}\left[ \left( e^{-\widehat{Y}} \right)^{p_2}\right] \right\}^{i-1}\,\int_0^t e^{-s\,\phi_B(p_2)}\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \in ds) \right)^{1/p_2}\leq C^* \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left(\mathbf{P}(\tau_i \leq t) \right)^{1/p_2}  \\[2mm]
&&= C^*\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left(\mathbf{P}(N(t) \geq i) \right)^{1/p_2} \leq C^* \,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left(\mathbf{E}\left[N^{\gamma\,q^*}(t) \right]\,
i^{-\gamma\,q^*} \right)^{1/p_2}  \\[2mm] \notag
&& \leq C^* \,\mathbf{E}\left[N^{\gamma\,q^*+1}(T) \right] \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\,i^{-\gamma\,q^*/p_2} < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
where $C^* \in (0,\,\infty)$ comes from $\phi_B(\cdot)$, at the sixth step we use Markov inequality. At the last step we recall that 
$p_2 > 1$, $q^* \geq p_2$. So $\gamma\,q^*/p_2 \geq \gamma$, and hence the last sum converges, since 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \,i^{-\gamma\,q^*/p_2} \leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \,i^{-\gamma}\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
because the last sum is a Cauchy series. By similar way we can work for $p_1$.

\cite[Lem. 3.9]{tang:tsitsiashvili:2003} follows that the product $X_A^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}}$ has upper Matuszewska index equal to $J_{F_A}^+$. Further, for the $J_2(x,\,t;\,k)$ and for all $t \in \Lambda_T$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.5.16}  
&&J_2(x,\,t;\,k) \leq \mathbf{P}\left(e^{-Y_i} > x^k \right) \leq \mathbf{E}\left[ (e^{-Y_i})^{p_2} \right]\,x^{-k\,p_2} = M_{p_2}^{(i)}\,x^{-k\,p_2} \\[2mm] \notag
&&=o\left[ \mathbf{P}\left( X_A^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} > x\right) \right] =o\left[ \mathbf{P}\left( {\bf X}^{(i)}\,\widetilde{\Theta}_i(t)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\theta>0\}} \in x\,A \right) \right]\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
where at the second step we used Markov inequality and in the pre-last step we used \eqref{eq.KPY.2.10}. From the other hand side, since for any $i \in {\mathbb N}$, 
\begin{eqnarray*}
&& \mathbf{E}\left[ (e^{-Y_i^*})^{p_2} \right]= \int_0^{\infty} z^{p_2} \,H_i^*(dz) \\[2mm] \notag
&&=\int_0^{\infty} z^{p_2} \,h_i(z)\,H_i(dz)  \leq C\,M_{p_2}^{(i)} \leq C < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
where at the third step we used relation \eqref{eq.KPY.5.3}, and at the fourth step relation \eqref{eq.KPY.3.1}. From the previous relation and \cite[Th. 3.3(iv)]{cline:samorodnitsky:1994} for all $t \in \Lambda_T$ we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.5.17}

\end{eqnarray}\noindent
Therefore, from \eqref{eq.KPY.5.16} and \eqref{eq.KPY.5.17} for all $t \in \Lambda_T$ we find
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.5.18}

\end{eqnarray}\noindent

For $J_{12}(x,\,t;\,k)$, for all $t \in \Lambda_T$, by Markov inequality we find

Now we focus in two examples from $MRV$ case, the one under weak and the other under strong dependence structure between ${\bf X}^{(i)}$ and $Z_i = e^{-Y_i}$, that are based on the multivariate versions of Breiman theorem, given in 
\cite{fougeres:mercadier:2012}. Our examples provide clear picture of Corollary \ref{cor.KPY.3.1}, about the effect of the dependence between insurance and financial risks on the asymptotic behavior of relation \eqref{eq.KPY.1.7}.

Here we use an equivalent definition of the $MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)$, with respect to relation \eqref{eq.KPY.2.6}. We say that $F \in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)$, if it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.5.33}  
\lim x\,\mathbf{P}\left( \dfrac{{\bf X}}{U_V(x)}\in {\mathbb B} \right) = \mu({\mathbb B}) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent 
for a Radon measure $\mu$, non-degenerate to zero, for any Borel set ${\mathbb B} \in [0,\,\infty]^d$, with ${\bf 0} \not\in \overline{{\mathbb B}}$, $\mu(\partial {\mathbb B})=0$, where
\begin{eqnarray*}
U_V(x) =\left( \dfrac 1{\overline{V}}\right)^{\leftarrow}(x)\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
which is named normalization function, where $f^{\leftarrow}$ represents a c\'{a}gl\'{a}d inverse of function $f$, that is such that  $U_V \in \mathcal{R}_{1/\alpha}$, see 
\cite[Prop. 2.6]{resnick:2007}.

We also observe that if  $F \in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)$, with $\alpha >0$, then by \cite[Th. 1.1(i)]{basrak:davis:mikosch:2002}, we obtain that the $|{\bf X}|$ has regularly varying distribution with the same index $\alpha>0$, hence there exists some non-degenerate to zero, Radon measure $\mu^*$, such that it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.5.34}  
\lim x\,\mathbf{P}\left( \dfrac{|{\bf X}|}{U_V(x)}\in {\mathbb B} \right) = \mu^*({\mathbb B}) \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
for any Borel set ${\mathbb B} \in [0,\,\infty]$, with $0 \not\in \overline{{\mathbb B}}$, $\mu^*(\partial {\mathbb B})=0$.

H\"{o}lder inequality, for all $i \in {\mathbb N}$ we obtain
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\mathbf{E}\left[\left( \dfrac{|{\bf X}^{(i)}|\,Z_i}{U_V(x)} \right)^{\delta} {\bf 1}_{\{|{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\}}\right] \\[2mm]
&&=\mathbf{E}\left[\left( \dfrac{|{\bf X}^{(i)}|\,Z_i}{U_V(x)} \right)^{\delta}\;\Big|\; |{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left( |{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\right)  \\[2mm]
&&\leq \mathbf{P}\left( |{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\right)\,\mathbf{E}^{1/2}\left[\left( \dfrac{|{\bf X}^{(i)}|}{U_V(x)} \right)^{2\,\delta}\;\Big|\; |{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\right] \\[2mm]
&&\times \mathbf{E}^{1/2}\left[\left( \dfrac{Z_i}{U_V(x)} \right)^{2\,\delta}\;\Big|\; |{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\right] \leq C\, \mathbf{P}\left( |{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\right)\, \left( \dfrac 1{U_V(x)} \right)^{4\,\delta}\,, 
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent 
where in the last step, the constant $C> 0$ is derived by the inequalities $M_{p_1}^{(i)} < \infty$ and $\mathbf{E}\left[|{\bf X}^{(i)}|^{2\,\delta}\right] < \infty$, since the $|{\bf X}^{(i)}|$ are regularly varying with index $\alpha > 2\,\delta$. Hence, from the last relation, in combination with \eqref{eq.KPY.5.34} we find that for all $i \in {\mathbb N}$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.5.43}  
&&\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \limsup x\,\mathbf{E}\left[\left( \dfrac{|{\bf X}^{(i)}|\,Z_i}{U_V(x)} \right)^{\delta} {\bf 1}_{\{|{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\}}\right] \\[2mm] \notag
&&\leq C\, \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \limsup x\,\mathbf{P}\left( |{\bf X}^{(i)}| \leq \varepsilon\,U_V(x)\right)\, \left( \dfrac 1{U_V(x)} \right)^{4\,\delta} \\[2mm] \notag
&&= C\, \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \limsup \left( \dfrac 1{U_V(x)} \right)^{4\,\delta}
\,\mu^*([0,\,\varepsilon]) \leq C\,C^*\,\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \limsup \left( \dfrac 1{U_V(x)} \right)^{4\,\delta}  =0\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
where the constant $C^*>0$ stems from the fact that $\mu^*$ represents a Radon measure. From \eqref{eq.KPY.5.43}  we obtain \eqref{eq.KPY.5.36}. We should also mention that by Proposition \ref{pr.KPY.5.2} is immediately implied that $G_i(x\,A) \asymp G_j(x\,A)$, for any $A \in \mathscr{R}$ and with $i,\,j \in{\mathbb N}\,,\; i \neq j$.  
~\hfill \qed

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{pr.KPY.5.3}
Under Assumption \ref{ass.KPY.5.3} it holds ${\bf X}\,Z \in MRV\left(\dfrac{\alpha\,\beta}{\alpha + \beta},\,Q,\,\nu \right)$, with
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.KPY.5.46}

for any Borel set ${\mathbb B} \in [0,\,\infty]^{d}$, with ${\bf 0} \not\in {\mathbb B}$, $\nu (\partial {\mathbb B})=0$, where ${\mathbb B}^*:=\{({\bf x},\,z) \in [0,\,\infty)^d \times [0,\,\infty)\;:\; {\bf x}\,z \in {\mathbb B} \}$.

\label{cor.KPY.5.3}

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
\label{rem.KPY.5.4}

<!-- example-6: body_md -->
\label{exam.KPY.a}
Suppose that the non-negative random vector \({\bf X}=(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\) has continuous marginal distribution functions \(F_1,\ldots,F_d\) satisfying satisfy the tail equivalence condition
	$\overline{F}_j(x)\sim c_j\overline{F^*}(x),\ j=1,\ldots,d,$	where \(c_1,\ldots,c_d\) are positive constants and	\( F^* \in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\) for some \(\alpha \in (0,\,\infty)\).
	Let \(Z\) have distribution function \(H\) satisfying	\(H \in\mathcal{R}_{-\beta}\) for some \(\beta \in (0,\,\infty)\).
	Suppose further that \(({\bf X},Z)\) possesses the nested Gumbel copula \eqref{eq:nested-gumbel}, with
	\(1<\theta_0\leq\theta_1<\infty\). Then, 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\lim x\,\mathbf{P}\left[
	\left(\dfrac{\bf X}{U_{F^*}(x)},\dfrac{Z}{U_H(x)}\right)\in\mathbb{B}\right] = \nu_G(\mathbb{B}) \,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
for any Borel set $\mathbb{B} \subset [0,\,\infty]^{d+1}$, with ${\bf 0} \not\in \overline{\mathbb{B}}$, $\nu_G (\partial \mathbb{B})=0$, where
the limit measure \(\nu_G\) is determined by
	\begin{equation} \label{eq:gumbel-exponent-measure}
		\nu_G\left(\left([0,{\bf t}]\times[0,z]\right)^c\right)=
		\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^d c_j^{\theta_1}\,t_j^{-\alpha\theta_1}\right)^{\theta_0/\theta_1}+z^{-\beta\theta_0}\right]^{1/\theta_0},
	\end{equation}
	for every \({\bf t}\in(0,\infty)^d\) and \(z>0\), and $U_{F^*},\,U_H$ represent the normalization functions, corresponding to distributions $F^*$ and $H$.
<!-- end example-6 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let ${\bf U}=(U_1,\ldots,U_d,V)$ be a \((d+1)\)-dimensional uniform random vector distributed according to the nested Gumbel copula \(C_G\), defined by relation \eqref{eq:nested-gumbel}. For every ${\bf a}=(a_1,\ldots,a_d,a_z)\in(0,\infty)^{d+1}$, by (\ref{eq:nested-gumbel}) and Taylor expansion we have
\begin{align*}
&\lim x\,\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^d\left\{U_j>1-\dfrac{a_j}{x}\right\}\cup\left\{V>1-\dfrac{a_z}{x}\right\}\right) 		\\[2mm]
&=\lim x\,\left[1-C_G\left(1-\dfrac{a_1}{x},\ldots,1-\dfrac{a_d}{x},1-\dfrac{a_z}{x}\right)\right]=\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^d a_j^{\theta_1}\right)^{\theta_0/\theta_1}+a_z^{\theta_0}\right]^{1/\theta_0}.
\end{align*}
Then we have the upper bound
\begin{align*}
	&x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\left(\dfrac{\bf X}{U_{F^*}(x)},\dfrac{Z}{U_H(x)}\right)\in\left([{\bf 0},{\bf t}]\times[0,z]\right)^c\right]\\[2mm]
	&=x\,\left[	1-C_G\left(	F_1\left(U_{F^*}(x)\,t_1\right),\ldots,F_d\bigl(U_{F^*}(x)\,t_d\bigr),H\bigl(U_H(x)\,z\bigr)\right)\right]\\[2mm]
	&=x\,\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^d\left\{U_j>1-\overline{F}_j\bigl(U_{F^*}(x)\,t_j\bigr)\right\}\cup\left\{V>1-\overline{H}\bigl(U_H(x)\,z\bigr)\right\}\right)\\[2mm]
	&\lesssim x\,\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^d\left\{U_j>1-\dfrac{(1+\varepsilon)c_j\,t_j^{-\alpha}}{x}\right\}\cup\left\{V>1-\dfrac{(1+\varepsilon)z^{-\beta}}{x}\right\}\right)\\[2mm]
	&\rightarrow (1+\varepsilon)\,\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^d c_j^{\theta_1}\,t_j^{-\alpha\,\theta_1}\right)^{\theta_0/\theta_1}+z^{-\beta\theta_0}\right]^{1/\theta_0}.
\end{align*}
Because of the arbitrary choice of $\varepsilon >0$, we obtain the desired upper bound for \eqref{eq:gumbel-exponent-measure}.

Establishing the corresponding lower bound by a similar way, completes the proof.

<!-- example-7: body_md -->
\label{exam.KPY.6.f}
Let us consider that the $({\bf X},\,Z)$ satisfies the nested copula condition \eqref{eq.KPY.6.A}. 
Further we suppose that the marginal distributions of ${\bf X}$, are such that it holds 
$\overline{F}_j(x) \sim c_j\,\overline{F}^*(x)$ for some positive constants $c_j$, for $j=1,\,\ldots,\,d$, and 
$F^* \in \mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}$, with $\alpha \in (0,\,\infty)$. Then \eqref{eq.KPY.5.38} holds uniformly 
for $z \in S(Z)$.
<!-- end example-7 -->
<!-- head-17: body_md -->
~
We next proceed to verify the assumption under the nested Frank model for the given set $B_{\bf t}:=[{\bf 0},\,{\bf t}]^c,\ {\bf t}:=(t_1,\ldots,t_d)\in(0,\infty)^d$.
Direct differentiation of the nested Frank copula gives
\begin{eqnarray*}
\dfrac{\partial C_F({\bf u},v)}{\partial v}=\dfrac{e^{-\theta_0v}\left(1-e^{-\theta_0C_1^F({\bf u})}\right)}{(1-e^{-\theta_0})-\left(1-e^{-\theta_0C_1^F({\bf u})}\right)\left(1-e^{-\theta_0v}\right)}.
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
Then
\begin{align*}
	\mathbf{P}({\bf X} \in xB_{\bf t}\;\big|\;  Z=z)	&=
	1-\dfrac{\partial C_F({\bf u},v)}{\partial v}
	\big|_{{\bf u}={\bf u}_x,\,v=H(z)}\\
	&=
	\dfrac{e^{-\theta_0C_1^F({\bf u}_x)} -e^{-\theta_0} }{(1-e^{-\theta_0})-	\left( 1-e^{-\theta_0 C_1^F({\bf u}_x} )\right) 
		\left( 1-e^{-\theta_0 H(z)}\right) }.
\end{align*}
where ${\bf u}_x=\bigl(F_1(xt_1),\ldots,F_d(xt_d)\bigr),\ v=H(z)$. It follows that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\dfrac{\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in xB_{\bf t}\;\big|\; Z=z)}{\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in xB_{\bf t})}
=\dfrac{e^{-\theta_0C_1^F({\bf u}_x)}-e^{-\theta_0}}{\left( 1-C_1^F({\bf u}_x)\right) \left[(1-e^{-\theta_0})-\left( 1-e^{-\theta_0C_1^F({\bf u}_x)}\right) \left( 1-e^{-\theta_0H(z)}\right) \right]}.
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
	Hence,
\begin{align*}
	h(z)=	\lim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\bigl({\bf X}\in xB_{\bf t}\;\big|\; Z=z\bigr)}{\mathbf{P}\bigl({\bf X}\in xB_{\bf t}\bigr)}
	=\dfrac{\theta_0 e^{\theta_0 H(z)}}{e^{\theta_0}-1}.
\end{align*}
Since $(1-e^{-\theta_0})e^{-\theta_0H(z)}\geq(1-e^{-\theta_0})e^{-\theta_0}>0$ and $\inf_{z\in S(Z)}h(z)
\geq\dfrac{\theta_0}{e^{\theta_0}-1}>0$  the preceding convergence is uniform for \(z\in S(Z)\), namely it holds
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,B_{\bf t}\;\big|\; Z=z) \sim h(z)\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,B_{\bf t})\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
uniformly for $z \in S(Z)$. Applying in the last relation the Dynkin's $\pi - \lambda$ theorem, 
see \cite[Cor. 2.2.1]{resnick:1999}, we obtain that relation \eqref{eq.KPY.5.38} is true for any ${\mathbb B} \in [0,\,\infty]^d$, with ${\bf 0} \not\in \overline{{\mathbb B}}$, with the desired 
uniformity with respect to  \(z\in S(Z)\).
~\hfill \qed
