<!-- theo-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
    Let $Y$ be a closed subspace of $L_1$ whose unit ball is relatively compact with respect to the topology of convergence in measure. Then, $Y$ is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex.
<!-- end theo-1 -->
<!-- coro-1: body_md -->
The space $X_{\mathsf{KW}}$ is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex, fails the Point of Continuity Property, and thus does not admit an equivalent norm that is asymptotically uniformly convex.
<!-- end coro-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and let $\ensuremath{\{y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ and $\ensuremath{\{z_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ in $B_Y$ form an $\varepsilon$-spider rooted at $x\in B_Y$. Since $B_Y$ is relatively compact in measure, we can extract a common subsequence of $\ensuremath{\{y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ and $\ensuremath{\{z_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ (still denoted the same) such that $\ensuremath{\{y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ and $\ensuremath{\{z_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ converge almost everywhere to $y$ and $z$, respectively,
                                                                                                     
for some $y,z\in L_1$. Note that $\frac{y+z}{2}=x$ and it follows from Fatou's lemma that $\max\{\| y\|_1, \| z\|_1\}\leqslant 1$. What remains to be shown is that the vectors $y$ and $z$ are uniformly far from the unit sphere to be able to control the norm of the root of the $\varepsilon$-spider. The key ingredient is the following elementary measure-theoretic fact. 

\begin{clai}
\label{claim}
If a sequence $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ in $L_1$ converges almost everywhere to some $f\in L_1$, then $\lim_{n\to\infty}( \| f_n\|_1-\| f_n-f\|_1)=\| f\|_1$.
\end{clai}

This claim (which can be found in \cite{BrezisLieb1983}) is easily derived from the reverse triangle inequality and the dominated convergence theorem.

\smallskip\noindent

Since $\ensuremath{\{y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ is bounded, we can pass to a further subsequence so that $\lim_{n\to\infty}\| y_n-y\|_1 = \rho$ for some $\rho\in[0,2]$. It follows from the separation of $\ensuremath{\{y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ and the triangle inequality that $\rho\geqslant\varepsilon/2$. Now, observe that 
\[
 z_n-z=(2x-y_n)-(2x-y)=-(y_n-y),
\]
and hence we also have $\| z_n-z\|_1\longrightarrow\rho$. 

\smallskip\noindent

To conclude the proof, we invoke Claim \ref{claim} twice, once for $\ensuremath{\{y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$ and once for $\ensuremath{\{z_n\}_{n=1}^\infty}$, so that  
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}(\| y_n\|_1 - \| y_n-y\|_1) = \| y\|_1 
 \qquad 
\text{ and } 
\qquad
 \lim_{n\to\infty}(\| z_n\|_1 - \| z_n-z\|_1) = \| z\|_1.
\]
It then follows from the estimates collected above that 
\[
 \| y\|_1\leqslant 1-\rho\leqslant 1- \frac{\varepsilon}{2} 
 \qquad\text{ and }
 \qquad
 \qquad \| z\|_1\leqslant 1-\rho\leqslant 1- \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

Recalling that $x=(y+z)/2$, the triangle inequality now gives $\| x\|_1\leqslant 1- \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
