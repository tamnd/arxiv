<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $X$ be an irreducible shift space.
  Then it is synchronized and bi-balanced if and only if it has variable specification.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A shift space $X$ is called \emph{boundedly supermultiplicative} (BSM for short) if there is a constant $c\ge1$ such that we have
  \[ |\mathcal B_m(X)|\,|\mathcal B_n(X)|\le c|\mathcal B_{m+n}(X)| \]
  for all $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\cite{BG15}\label{prop::BSM_entropy_condition}
  A shift space $X$ is BSM if and only if there is a constant $c\ge1$ such that we have
  \[ \frac1c\le\frac{\exp(nh(X))}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|}\le1 \]
  for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\cite{KimThesis24}\label{prop::BSM_zero_entropy_condition}
  A zero-entropy shift space $X$ is BSM if and only if it consists of finitely many periodic points.
  Furthermore, it is irreducible and BSM if and only if it consists of a single periodic orbit.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a BSM shift space with $h(X)=0$.
  Then by Proposition~\ref{prop::BSM_entropy_condition} we have a constant $c\ge1$ such that for all $n$ in $\mathbb N$ we have
  \[ |\mathcal B_n(X)|\le c. \]
  So $X$ consists of periodic points.
  The remaining implications are trivial.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\cite{KimThesis24}\label{prop::S_gap_shift}
  Let $S=\{s_1,s_2,\cdots\}$ be a possibly finite subset of natural numbers.
  Then the following are equivalent for an $S$-gap shift $X=\mathsf X(S)$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $G_S=\{s_n-s_{n-1}\}$ is bounded.
    \item $X$ has variable specification.
    \item $X$ is bi-balanced.
    \item $X$ is one-sided balanced.
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The equivalence between the first two statements is shown in \cite{Jun11}.
  
  When $\sup G_S=\infty$, for each $k>0$ we have $n_k$ with $s_{n_k+1}-s_{n_k}>k$.
  Clearly $10^{s_{n_k}+1}$ and $0^{s_{n_k}+1}1$ have exactly one follower and exactly one predecessor of length $k$, respectively, both of which are just $0^k$.
  However, $S$ has infinitely many elements,
  so it is easy to verify that $X$ has positive entropy (see Proposition 2.1 of \cite{BG15}).
  Then $X$ is neither right nor left-balanced since both
  \[ \frac{|\mathcal F_{k}(10^{s_{n_k}+1},X)|}{|\mathcal B_k(X)|}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal B_k(X)|}\text{ and }\frac{|\mathcal P_{k}(0^{s_{n_k}+1}1,X)|}{|\mathcal B_k(X)|}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal B_k(X)|}\]
  go to 0.
  So, if $X$ is one-sided balanced, then $G_S$ is bounded, then $X$ has variable specification, then $X$ is bi-balanced.

\label{eg::right_not_left_balanced}
  Let
  \[ C=\{a^nb^n,a^lb^mc,c\mid l,m,n\in\mathbb{N},l>m\} \]
  and $X=\mathsf{X}_C$.
  It is a coded system presented by the graph described in Figure~\ref{fig::right_not_left_balanced} and clearly has positive entropy, say $\log\lambda,\lambda>1$.
  We will show that $X$ is right- but not left-balanced.
  Immediately, it cannot satisfy variable specification as such shifts must be bi-balanced.
  
  Note that $\mathcal P_n(ab^{n+1},X)$ is a singleton $\{a^n\}$.
  Then
  \[ \frac{|\mathcal P_n(ab^{n+1},X)|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|}\le\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\to0 \]
  as $n\to\infty$,
  so $X$ is not left-balanced.
  
  To see that $X$ is right-balanced, we will estimate $|\mathcal F_n(u,X)|$ and $|\mathcal B_n(X)|$ for all $n$ in $\mathbb N$ and $u$ in $\mathcal B(X)$.
  Every edge in Figure~\ref{fig::right_not_left_balanced} has exactly two outgoing edges of distinct labels,
  so every word in $X$ has at least $2^n$ followers of length $n$.
  It follows that for every word $u$ of $X$ we have
  \[ |\mathcal F_n(u,X)|\ge2^n. \]
  
  Now, we estimate the cardinality of all words of length $n$.
  First, every word containing $c$ or $ba$ has a single terminal vertex in the graph.
  Hence, it has exactly $2^n$ followers of length $n$.
  Next, since $b$ is followed by $a,b$ and $c$,
  we have a disjoint union
  \[ \mathcal F_n(b,X)=\bigcup_{s\in\{a,b,c\}}s\mathcal F_{n-1}(bs,X) \]
  where $s\mathcal F_{n-1}(bs,X)=\{sw\mid w\in\mathcal F_{n-1}(bs,X)\}$ for $s=a,b$ or $c$.
  Then
  \[\begin{split}
    |\mathcal F_n(b,X)|=&|\mathcal F_{n-1}(ba,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-1}(bb,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-1}(bc,X)|\\
    =&2^{n-1}+|\mathcal F_{n-2}(bba,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-2}(bbb,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-2}(bbc,X)|+2^{n-1}\\
    =&2\cdot2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}+|\mathcal F_{n-3}(b^3a,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-3}(b^4,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-3}(b^3c,X)|+2^{n-2}\\
    &\vdots\\
    =&2(2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}+\cdots+2)+|\mathcal F_1(b^n,X)|\\
    =&2^{n}+2^{n-1}+\cdots+4+|\{a,b,c\}|\\
    =&2^{n+1}-1.
  \end{split}\]
  Also, $a$ is followed by $a$ and $b$ but not by $c$.
  We have
  \[\begin{split}
    |\mathcal F_n(a,X)|=&|\mathcal F_{n-1}(aa,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-1}(ab,X)|\\
    =&|\mathcal F_{n-2}(aaa,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-2}(aab,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-1}(ab,X)|\\
    &\vdots\\
    =&|\mathcal F_{1}(a^n,X)|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|\mathcal F_{n-k}(a^kb,X)|\\
    \le&|\{a,b\}|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|\mathcal F_{n-k}(b,X)|\\
    =&2+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(2^{n-k+1}-1)\\
    =&2^{n+1}-n-1.
  \end{split}\]
Finally,
\[\begin{split}
  |\mathcal B_n(X)|=&|\mathcal F_{n-1}(a,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-1}(b,X)|+|\mathcal F_{n-1}(c,X)|\\
  \le&2^n-n+2^n-1+2^{n-1}\\
  \le&5\cdot2^{n-1}.
\end{split}\]
Therefore, we have
\[ \frac{|\mathcal F_n(u,X)|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|}\ge\frac{2^n}{5\cdot2^{n-1}}=\frac25 \]
showing that $X$ is right-balanced.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\cite{KimThesis24}\label{thm::one-sided_not_bi-balanced}
For any $h>0$ there is a coded system $X$ satisfying the following properties:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $h(X)=h$.
    \item $X$ is one-sided but not bi-balanced.
    Hence it has no variable specification.
    \item $X$ contains a subshift $Y$ with variable specification and $h(Y)=h$.
  \end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\lambda=e^h>1$.
  For $0<h<1$ it was shown in \cite{BG15} that there exists an $S$-gap shift $Y$ with $h(Y)=\log\lambda=h$ and specification.
  For general $h>0$, by taking a product shift space of an $S$-gap shift and $\{0,1,\cdots,n-1\}^{\mathbb Z}$ for some $n\ge1$,
  we can find a shift space $Y$ with $h(Y)=h$ and variable specification
  By renaming the symbols if needed, we may assume that the alphabet of $Y$ does not include the symbol $\bar1$.
  
  We will define a set $C$ of words which gives a coded system $X=\mathsf X_C$.
  Apply Lemma~\ref{lmm::one-sided_not_bi-balanced} to obtain subshifts $Y_j,0\le j<p$ of $Y$ satisfying the listed four properties.
  Let $M_j$ be a specification gap for each $(Y_j)^p,0\le j<p$, and $M=\max_jM_j$.
  Since $h(Y)=\log\lambda$ and $Y$ is BSM, by Proposition~\ref{prop::BSM_entropy_condition} there is $c_Y\ge1$ such that for all $n$ in $\mathbb N$ we have $|\mathcal B_n(Y)|\le c_Y\lambda^n$.
  Fix an integer $K>pM$ with $\lambda^K>c_Y$,
  and an infinite subset $S$ of $\{s\in p\mathbb N\mid s>K\}$ with $h(\mathsf X(S))=\log\eta<\log\lambda$.
  Define unbounded sequences $\{a_s\}_{s\in S},\{b_s\}_{s\in S}$ of positive multiples of $p$ such that
  \[ a_s+b_s+pM=s\in S,\alpha=\sup_{s\in S}\frac{a_s+K}{s}<1-\frac{\log\eta}{\log\lambda}\text{ and }b_s\text{ is increasing.} \]
  Those sequences are easily found if $S$ is sparse and each $a_s$ is much smaller than $s$.
  (Note that the sparser $S$ is, the smaller $\eta$ is, so $a_s$ is easier to satisfy the desired inequality.)
  As an $S$-gap shift, $\mathsf X(S)$ is BSM \cite{BG15}.
  Then there is $c_S\ge1$ such that for all $n$ in $\mathbb N$ we have $|\mathcal B_n(\mathsf X(S))|\le c_S\eta^n$.
  
  Fix $x^{(j)}$ in $Y_j$ for each $0\le j<p$ and let $v_{j,k}=x^{(j)}_{[-k,-1]},k\in\mathbb N$. 
  Regard each $u$ in $\mathcal B_{a_s}(Y)$ as a word of length $a_s/p$ in $(Y_{j_u})^p$ for some $0\le j_u<p$,
  and find $w_{u,b_s}$ in $\mathcal B_{pM}(Y)$ with
  \[ uw_{u,b_s}v_{j_u,b_s}\in\mathcal B_s(Y). \]
  Such $w_{u,b_s}$ exists since $(Y_{j_u})^p$ has specification with gap $M$.
  A bridge of length $M$ from $u$ to $v_{j_u,b_s}$ in $(Y_{j_u})^p$ is a bridge of length $pM$ in $Y_{j_u}$.
  
  Now, let
  \[ C=\{\bar1uw_{u,b_s}v_{j_u,b_s}\mid u\in\mathcal B_{a_s}(Y),s\in S\} \]
  and $X=\mathsf X(C)$.
  Then $\bar1$ is a synchronizing word of $X$.
  
  We will first compute $h(X)$.
  Since $\{a_s\}$ is unbounded, every word of $Y$ is a subword of some $u$ in $\mathcal B_{a_s}(Y)$.
  Thus we have $\mathcal B(Y)\subset\mathcal B(X)$ and $h(X)\ge h(Y)=h$.
  To show the other inequality, define a $1$-block map $\pi:X\to\mathsf X(S)$ by
  \[ \pi(\bar1)=1,\pi(a)=0\text{ for }a\ne\bar1. \]
  Every word in $\pi^{-1}(10^s1)$ is of the form $\bar1w\bar1$ where $\bar1$ does not appear in $w$.
  So $\bar1w$ must belong to $C$ and $w$ is a word of length $s$ determined by some $u$ in $\mathcal B_{a_s}(Y)$.
  It follows that
  \[ |\pi^{-1}(10^s1)|=|\mathcal B_{a_s}(Y)|\le c_Y\lambda^{a_s}\le\lambda^{K+a_s}\le\lambda^{s\alpha}. \]
  Since $\{a_s\}$ is unbounded, we have
  \[\begin{split}
    \pi^{-1}(10^m)=&\bar1\mathcal B_{m}(Y),\\
    |\pi^{-1}(10^m)|=&|\mathcal B_{m}(Y)|\le c_Y\lambda^{m}.
  \end{split}\]
 
  We turn to estimating $|\pi^{-1}(0^l1)|$.
  Let $s_0=\min\{s\in S\mid s\ge l\}$ and $b^*=b_{s_0}$.
  In the case $l\le b^*$, the $0^l$ in $0^l1$ is always lifted by $\pi^{-1}$ to a suffix of $v_{j,b^*}$ for some $0\le j<p$.
  This is because $\{b_s\}$ is unbounded and increasing, so $v_{j_u,b_s}$ is a suffix of $v_{j_u,b_{s'}}$ for every $s'>s$:
  even if that $0^l$ comes from a suffix of $u_{a_s}w_{u,b_s}v_{j_u,b_s}$ for some $s>s_0$, the suffix is also a suffix of $v_{j_u,b^*}$.
  Hence $|\pi^{-1}(0^l1)|$ is no greater than the number, which is just $p$, of $v_{j,b^*}$.
  In the case $l>b^*$, the $0^{b^*}$ right before $1$ is always lifted by $\pi^{-1}$ to $v_{j,b^*}$ for some $0\le j<p$,
  and the preimages of the leftmost $0^{l-b^*}$ in $0^l1$ are no more than $|\mathcal B_{l-b^*}(Y)|$.
  Then we obtain
  \[\begin{split}
    \alpha s_0\ge&a_{s_0}+K\\
    b^*=&s_0-a_{s_0}-pM\\
    \ge&s_0-(\alpha s_0-K)-pM\\
    \ge&(1-\alpha)l+(K-pM)\\
    l-b^*\le&l\alpha-(K-pM)\le l\alpha\\
    |\mathcal B_{l-b^*}(Y)|\le&c_Y\lambda^{n-b^*}\le c_Y\lambda^{l\alpha}\\
    |\pi^{-1}(0^l1)|\le&(\#\text{ of }v_{j,b^*})\cdot|\mathcal B_{l-b^*}(Y)|\le pc_Y\lambda^{l\alpha}
  \end{split}\]
  In either case $l\le b^*$ or $l>b^*$, we get
  \[ |\pi^{-1}(0^l1)|\le pc_Y\lambda^{l\alpha} \]
  
  Since $\bar1$ is synchronizing for $X$, for a word $w=0^l10^{s_1}10^{s_2}\cdots10^{s_k}10^m,l,m\ge0,s_j$ in $S$, of length $n$ in $\mathcal B(\mathsf X(S))$ we have
  \[\begin{split}
    |\pi^{-1}(w)|\le&|\pi^{-1}(0^l1)|\,|\pi^{-1}(10^{s_1}1)|\,|\pi^{-1}(10^{s_2}1)|\cdots|\pi^{-1}(10^{s_k}1)|\,|\pi^{-1}(10^m)|\\
    \le& pc_Y\lambda^{l\alpha}\lambda^{s_1\alpha}\lambda^{s_2\alpha}\cdots\lambda^{s_k\alpha}c_Y\lambda^m\\
    \le&pc_Y^2\lambda^{(n-m)\alpha+m}
  \end{split}\]
  
  Denote by $\mathcal B_n(\mathsf X(S),k)$ the set of all words in $\mathcal B_n(\mathsf X(S))$ in which the last appearance of $1$ is at the $k$-th coordinate.
  For every word $w$ in  $\mathcal B_n(\mathsf X(S),k)$ its suffix of length $(n-k)$ is fixed to be $0^{n-k}$ so
  \[ |\pi^{-1}(w)|\le pc_Y^2\lambda^{k\alpha+(n-k)}. \]
  Then
  \[\begin{split}
    |\mathcal B_n(X)|=&\sum_{k=1}^n\sum_{w\in\mathcal B_n(\mathsf X(S),k)}|\pi^{-1}(w)|+|\{0^n\}|\,|\pi^{-1}(0^n)|\\
    \le&\sum_{k=1}^n|\mathcal B_n(\mathsf X(S),k)|\,pc_Y^2\lambda^{k\alpha+(n-k)}+|\mathcal B_n(Y)|\\
    \le&\sum_{k=1}^nc_S\eta^k\cdot pc_Y^2\lambda^{k\alpha+(n-k)}+c_Y\lambda^n\\
    \le&pc_Y^2c_S\lambda^n\sum_{k=1}^n\eta^{k}\lambda^{k\alpha-k}+c_Y\lambda^n\\
    \le&pc_Y^2c_S\lambda^n\sum_{k=0}^\infty\left(\lambda^{\frac{\log\eta}{\log\lambda}+\alpha-1}\right)^k+c_Y\lambda^n\\
    \le&c\lambda^n
  \end{split}\]
  where $c=pc_Y^2c_SL+c_Y$ and $L=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\left(\lambda^{\frac{\log\eta}{\log\lambda}+\alpha-1}\right)^k$.
  (Note that $L$ converges since $\lambda>1$ and $\alpha<1-\dfrac{\log\eta}{\log\lambda}$.)
  This shows $h(X)\le\log\lambda$ and completes the proof of $h(X)=h$.
  
  Next we prove the right-balancedness of $X$.
  Let $u$ belong to $\mathcal B(X)$.
  In the case that $u$ contains $\bar1$, write $u=v_1\bar1v_2\bar1\cdots\bar1v_k$ where $v_1,\cdots,v_k$ are in $\mathcal B(Y)$.
  Then $\mathcal F_n(u,X)=\mathcal F_n(\bar1v_k,X)$ since $\bar1$ is synchronizing.
  Also $\mathcal F_n(\bar1v_k,X)$ contains $\mathcal F_n(v_k,Y)$ as $\{a_s\}$ is unbounded.
  A shift $Y$ with variable specification is already right-balanced, hence there is $0<c_r\le1$ with
  \[ \frac{|\mathcal F_n(v_k,Y)|}{|\mathcal B_n(Y)|}\ge c_r. \]
  It follows that
  \[\begin{split}
    \frac{|\mathcal F_n(u,X)|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|}\ge\frac{|\mathcal F_n(v_k,Y)|\,/\,|\mathcal B_n(Y)|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|\,/\,|\mathcal B_n(Y)|}
    \ge \frac{c_r}{c\lambda^n/\lambda^n}
    =\frac{c_r}{c}.
  \end{split}\]
  In the other case, $u$ avoids $\bar1$ and belongs to $\mathcal B(Y)$.
  Again $\mathcal F_n(u,X)$ contains $\mathcal F_n(u,Y)$ as $\{a_s\}$ is unbounded.
  Hence, we see
  \[ \frac{|\mathcal F_n(u,Y)|}{|\mathcal B_n(Y)|}\ge c_r \]
  and
  \[\begin{split}
    \frac{|\mathcal F_n(u,X)|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|}\ge\frac{|\mathcal F_n(u,Y)|\,/\,|\mathcal B_n(Y)|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|\,/\,|\mathcal B_n(Y)|}
    \ge\frac{c_r}{c}.
  \end{split}\]
  Therefore $X$ is right-balanced.
  
  We show that $X$ is not left-balanced by contradiction.
  Suppose that $X$ is left-balanced and that there is $0<c_l\le1$ such that for all $u$ in $\mathcal B(X)$ we have
  \[ \frac{|\mathcal P_n(u,X)|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|}\ge c_l. \]
  Denote by $\mathcal B_n(X,k)$ the set of all words in $\mathcal B_n(X)$ in which the last appearance of $\bar1$ is at the $k$-th coordinate.
  The last $\bar1$ at the $k$-th coordinate is synchronizing and followed by any words in $\mathcal B(Y)$, so for $1\le k\le n$ we have
  \[\begin{split}
    |\mathcal B_n(X,k)|=&|\mathcal P_{k-1}(\bar1)|\,|\mathcal B_{n-k}(Y)|\\
    \ge &c_l|\mathcal B_{k-1}(X)|\,|\mathcal B_{n-k}(Y)|\\
    \ge&c_l|\mathcal B_{k-1}(Y)|\,|\mathcal B_{n-k}(Y)|\ge\frac{c_l}{\lambda}\lambda^{n}.
  \end{split}\]
  Considering the words of $\mathcal B(X)$ which avoid $\bar1$, we see
  \[\begin{split}
    |\mathcal B_n(X)|=&\sum_{k=1}^n|\mathcal B_n(X,k)|+|\mathcal B_n(Y)|\\
    \ge&\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{c_l}{\lambda}\lambda^{n}+\lambda^n=\lambda^n\left(\frac{nc_l}{\lambda}+1\right),
  \end{split}\]
  contradicting $|\mathcal B_n(X)|\le c\lambda^n$, for large enough $n$.
  Hence $X$ is not left-balanced, and has no variable specification.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
We call a shift space $X$ \emph{entropy minimal} if every proper subshift $Y$ of $X$
  satisfies $h(Y)<h(X)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{defn::measure-theoretic-entropy}
  Let $\mathcal M(X)$ be the set of $\sigma$-invariant Borel probability measures on a shift space $X$.
  Given $\mu$ in $\mathcal M(X)$, we define \emph{measure-theoretic entropy} $h_\mu(X)$ by
\[ h_\mu(X)=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{w\in\mathcal B_n(X)}\mu([w]_0)\log\mu([w]_0). \]
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{defn::mme}
The well-known variational principle states that
\[ h(X)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal M(X)}h_\mu(X) \]
and that there is $\mu$ on $X$ attaining the supremum \cite{DGS76,LinM95}.
Such $\mu$ is called a \emph{measure of maximal entropy} (MME for short).
When a shift space $X$ admits only one MME, $X$ is called \emph{intrinsically ergodic}.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{defn::gibbs}
  A $\sigma$-invariant probability measure $\mu$ on a shift space $X$ is called an (\emph{invariant}) \emph{Gibbs measure} if there is a \emph{Gibbs constant} $K>0$ satisfying the \emph{Gibbs inequality}
  \[ \frac1K\leq \mu([w]_0)e^{n h(X)}\leq K \]
  for all $w$ in $\mathcal B_n(X)$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\cite{THOM06}\label{lmm::synchronized_fully-supported_mme}
  A synchronized shift has at most one MME with full support.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lmm::erogidc_gibbs_entropy-minimal}
  A shift space equipped with an ergodic Gibbs measure is entropy minimal.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $\mu$ be an ergodic Gibbs measure on a shift space $X$.
  We prove the entropy minimality of $X$ by contradiction.
  Suppose that $X$ is not entropy minimal.
  There is a proper subshift $Y\subsetneq X$ with $h(Y)=h(X)$.
  For all $n\ge1$ let
  \[ E_n=\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal B_{2n+1}(Y)}[w]_{-n}. \]
  Then $E_n$ is decreasing as $n$ grows and $Y=\bigcap_{n\ge1}E_n$.
  
  By Gibbs inequality,
  \[ \mu(E_n)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal B_{2n+1}(Y)}\mu([w]_{-n})\ge|\mathcal B_{2n+1}(Y)|\frac{e^{-h(2n+1)}}K \]
  where $K$ is a Gibbs constant and $h=h(X)$.
  Since $|\mathcal B_{2n+1}(Y)|\ge e^{h(2n+1)}$, we have
  \[ \mu(E_n)\ge\frac{1}K\text{ and }\mu(Y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(E_n)\ge\frac1K>0. \]
  Also, for any $w$ in $\mathcal B(X)\setminus\mathcal B(Y)$, again by Gibbs inequality, we obtain
  \[ \mu(Y)\le1-\mu([w]_0)\le1-\frac{e^{-h|w|}}{K}<1. \]
  Then $0<\mu(Y)<1$, which contradicts the ergodicity of $\mu$.
  Hence there is no such $Y$ and $X$ must be entropy minimal.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop::synchronized_bi-balanced_entropy-minimal}
  A synchronized and bi-balanced shift $X$ is entropy minimal.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Kim showed in \cite{KimMK24} that a shift space is bi-balanced if and only if it has an invariant Gibbs measure $\mu$ and that in such a case the measure is a fully supported MME on it.
  As $X$ is synchronized, $\mu$ is the unique fully supported MME of $X$ by Lemma~\ref{lmm::synchronized_fully-supported_mme}.
  
  We claim that $\mu$ is ergodic.
  It is a little modification of a standard result that the unique MME is ergodic.
  Suppose not and let $A$ be a $\sigma$-invariant $\mu$-measurable subset of $X$ with
  \[ 0<t=\mu(A)<1. \]
  Define $\mu_A(B)=\dfrac{\mu(B\cap A)}{t}$ and $\mu_{A^C}(B)=\dfrac{\mu(B\cap A^C)}{1-t}$.
  Both are invariant and $\mu=t\mu_A+(1-t)\mu_{A^C}$.
  So
  \[ h(X)=h_\mu(X)=th_{\mu_A}(X)+(1-t)h_{\mu_{A^C}}(X). \]
  Since $h_{\mu_A},h_{\mu_{A^C}}\le h(X)$, we have
  \[ h(X)=h_\mu(X)=h_{\mu_A}(X)=h_{\mu_{A^C}}(X). \]
  Then $\mu_s=s\mu_A+(1-s)\mu_{A^C}$ is also a fully supported MME of X for every $s$ in $(0,1)$, contradicting the uniqueness.
  Hence there is no such $A$, and $\mu$ is ergodic.
  Now the previous Lemma~\ref{lmm::erogidc_gibbs_entropy-minimal} ensures the entropy minimality of $X$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor::synchronized_bi-balanced_intrinsically-ergodic}
  A synchronized and bi-balanced shift is intrinsically ergodic.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a synchronized and bi-balanced shift.
  Then $X$ is entropy minimal and has the unique MME $\mu$ of full support.
  Another MME $\nu$ of $X$, if it exists, must have smaller support than $X$, say $Y\subsetneq X$.
  But, the entropy minimality of $X$ together with the variational principle forces
  \[ h_\nu(Y)\le h(Y)<h(X) \]
  denying that $\nu$ is an MME of $X$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lmm::entropy-minimal_bi-balanced_followers_predecessors}
Let $X$ be entropy minimal and bi-balanced.
Fix any $v$ in $\mathcal B(X)$.
Then, there is $N=N(v)$ such that, for every $u$ in $\mathcal B(X)$,
both $\mathcal F_N(u,X)$ and $\mathcal P_N(u,X)$ contain words $s$ and $p$, respectively, in which $v$ occurs as a subword.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
As $X$ is bi-balanced, there is $c>0$ with
\[ \frac{|\mathcal F_N(u,X)|}{|\mathcal B_N(X)|},\frac{|\mathcal P_N(u,X)|}{|\mathcal B_N(X)|}>c. \]
Let
\[ D=\{w\in\mathcal B(X)\mid v\text{ is not a subword of }w\}\text{ and }D_n=D\cap\mathcal A^n. \]
Then $D$ is factorial and
\[ \mathsf X(D)=\{x\in X\mid v\text{ does not appear in }x\}\subsetneq X. \]
Consequently, $h(\mathsf X(D))=h(D)<h(X)$ by entropy minimality of $X$.

Choose $\alpha$ with $h(D)<\alpha<h(X)$.
There is some $M>0$ such that for all $n\ge M$ we have
\[ |D_n|<e^{n\alpha}<e^{nh(X)}\le|\mathcal B_n(X)|. \]
Then
\[ \frac{|D_n|}{|\mathcal B_n(X)|}<e^{n(\alpha-h(X))}\to0\]
so there exists $N>0$ with $|D_N|\,/\,|\mathcal B_N(X)|<c$.
Suppose that for a word $u$ in $\mathcal B(X)$ no word in $\mathcal F_N(u,X)$ contains $v$ as a subword.
Immediately, $\mathcal F_N(u,X)\subset D_N$, which induces
a contradiction
\[ c\le\frac{|\mathcal F_N(u,X)|}{|\mathcal B_N(X)|}\le\frac{|D_N|}{|\mathcal B_N(X)|}<c. \]
 It follows that for any $u$ in $\mathcal B(X)$ there is a word in $\mathcal F_N(u,X)$ containing $v$ as its subword.
 Symmetrically, for any $u$ in $\mathcal B(X)$ there is a word in $\mathcal P_N(u,X)$ containing $v$ as its subword.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm::main_equivalence}
Let $X$ be an irreducible shift space.
Then it is synchronized and bi-balanced if and only if it has variable specification.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
$\Leftarrow$): We refer to \cite{BG15} and \cite{Jun11}.
  
  $\Rightarrow$): By Proposition~\ref{prop::synchronized_bi-balanced_entropy-minimal} $X$ is entropy minimal.
  Choose a synchronizing word $v$ of $X$.
  Apply Lemma~\ref{lmm::entropy-minimal_bi-balanced_followers_predecessors} to $v$, obtaining $N=N(v)$.
  Given arbitrary $u,w\in\mathcal B(X)$ choose a word $\alpha v$ in $\mathcal F(u)$ and a word $v\beta$ in $\mathcal P(w)$ with $|\alpha v|,|v\beta|\le N$.
  Since $v$ is synchronizing, we have $u\alpha v\beta w\in\mathcal B(X)$.
  The bridge $\alpha v\beta$ has length at most $2N$, proving variable specification.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:variable-specification_intrinsic-ergodicity}
  A shift with variable specification is intrinsically ergodic.
