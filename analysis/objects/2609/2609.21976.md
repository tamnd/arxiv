<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{remark:OMWU}
Multiplicative optimism is not specific to the potential used by
\texttt{MORM}{}. With the log-sum-exp potential associated with negative
entropy, the same construction gives
\[
    x_i^{(t)}[a]
      \propto
      \exp\!\left(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}[a]\right)
      \bigl(1+4\eta u_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bigr).
\]
Thus, the underlying response is MWU, with the previous utility entering
as a multiplicative optimistic correction. Standard Optimistic MWU, or Optimistic Hedge, instead uses an
exponential correction
\citep{rakhlin2013optimization,daskalakis2021near}. In our notation,
its update can be written as
\[
    x_i^{(t)}[a]
      \propto
      \exp\!\left(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}[a]\right)
      \exp\!\left(\eta u_i^{(t-1)}[a]\right).
\]
Since $\exp(z)=1+z+\mathcal{O}(z^2)$, the correction used by
\texttt{MORM}{} is a first-order analogue of the standard optimistic
correction. The potential in this paper is chosen differently in order
to obtain the curvature and stability properties needed for our
analysis.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Since $u_i^{(t-1)}[a]\in[-1,1]$  and
$4\eta=1/(8\sqrt n)\leq1/8$, every optimistic correction satisfies
$1+4\eta u_i^{(t-1)}[a]\in[7/8,9/8]$.
Together with the strict positivity of every coordinate of
$\nabla\Psi_i$, this ensures that the normalization in
\eqref{eq:explicit-rule} is well defined on every round and that
$x_i^{(t)}$ has full support. On the first round,
$\bm{U}_i^{(1)}=\bm{0}$ and $u_i^{(0)}=\bm{0}$. Since $a_c(0)=1$, all
actions receive the same weight, so $x_i^{(1)}$ is uniform. The same
conclusions hold under the learning-rate safeguard, since the safeguard
can only decrease the learning rate.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The factor
$a_c(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}[a])$ captures the cumulative performance of
action $a$. It is increasing in $\bm{U}_i^{(t)}[a]$, so actions with
larger cumulative advantage receive larger weight. For actions with
large negative cumulative advantage, this weight approaches zero, but
it remains strictly positive at every finite input. The second factor,
$1+4\eta\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]$, provides the optimistic correction. It
increases the weight of an action that performed better than the
player's mixed strategy in the preceding round and decreases the weight
of an action that performed worse. Thus, the first factor summarizes
past performance over all previous rounds, while the second reacts to
the most recent observation.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Consider a fixed $n$-player finite game with at most $d\geq2$ actions
per player and utilities in $[0,1]$. If all players $i\in[n]$ follow
\texttt{MORM}{} (\Cref{alg:learning}) with $c=2+\log d$ and learning rate
$\eta=1/(32\sqrt n)$, observing their exact expected-utility vectors
after each round, then every player $i\in[n]$ has external regret
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_i^{(T)}\leq \mathcal{O}(\sqrt n\log d).
\]
Moreover, \texttt{MORM}{} is adaptive to adversarial utilities through the
learning-rate safeguard in \Cref{app:safeguard}, which only decreases
the learning rate adaptively and leaves the rest of the update unchanged. The safeguarded rule for any individual
player $i$ guarantees
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_i^{(T)}\leq \mathcal{O}(\sqrt n\log d+\sqrt{T\log d})
\]
against arbitrary, possibly adaptive, utility vectors
$\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_i|}$. Both bounds hold uniformly over all $T\geq1$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:calculus}
Fix a player $i$ and let $c=2+\log d$. The potential $\Psi_i$ is
convex and continuously differentiable on $\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal A_i|}$, with
locally Lipschitz gradient. For every $\bm{U}$,
\[
    \Psi_i(\bm{U})>0,
    \qquad
    \Psi_i(\bm{0})
      =c|\mathcal A_i|^{1/(c-1)}
      <3c,
    \qquad
    \Psi_i(\bm{U})
      \geq c+\max_a\bm{U}[a].
\]
Moreover,
\[
    \partial_a\Psi_i(\bm{U})
      =
      \left(\frac{\Psi_i(\bm{U})}c\right)^{2-c}
      a_c(\bm{U}[a])
      >0,
    \qquad
    \sum_a\partial_a\Psi_i(\bm{U})\leq3.
\]
Where the Hessian exists,
\[
    0
      \preceq
      \nabla^2\Psi_i(\bm{U})
      \preceq
      \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\partial_a\Psi_i(\bm{U})\bigr).
\]
The same directional second-derivative bound holds almost everywhere
along every line segment.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:taylor}
For every $\bm{U},\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal A_i|}$ with
$\left\lVert \bm{u}\right\rVert_\infty\leq1$ and every $0<\eta\leq1/8$,
\[
    \Psi_i(\bm{U}+\eta\bm{u})-\Psi_i(\bm{U})

    \frac{2\eta^2}{3}
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\bm{U})\bm{u}[a]^2.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:one-round}
Fix a player $i$ and a round $t$. Suppose that on this round the player
uses the \texttt{MORM}{} response \eqref{eq:gradient-rule} with some
$0<\eta\leq1/16$. After observing
$\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_i|}$ and updating according to
\eqref{eq:centered} and \eqref{eq:cumulative},
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
      -
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    \leq
    2\eta^2
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
       \bigl(\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bigr)^2
    -
    \eta^2
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
       \bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2.
\]
The bound is local to round $t$ and remains valid even if the learning rate changes across rounds.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\left\lVert \bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty\leq1$ and
$\eta\leq1/16$, every optimistic correction factor is at least $3/4$.
Together with the positivity of
$\partial_a\Psi_i$ from \Cref{lem:calculus}, this ensures that the
response is well defined.

By the centering identity in \eqref{eq:two-identities},
$\left\langle \bm{x}^{(t)}_i,\bm{u}^{(t)}_i\right\rangle=0$. Substituting the normalized response and
clearing its positive normalizing denominator gives
\[
    0
      =
      \sum_a
         \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]
      +
      4\eta
      \sum_a
         \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
         \bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a].
\]
Hence, the first-order term in \Cref{lem:taylor} satisfies
\[
    \eta
    \left\langle \nabla\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}),\bm{u}^{(t)}_i\right\rangle
      =
      -4\eta^2
      \sum_a
         \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
         \bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a].
\]
For every action $a$,
\[
    -4\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]
      =
      2\bigl(\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bigr)^2
      -
      2\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2
      -
      2\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]^2.
\]
Dropping the last, nonpositive contribution and applying
\Cref{lem:taylor} with input $\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}$ and direction
$\bm{u}^{(t)}_i$ gives
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
      -
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    \leq
    2\eta^2
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
       \bigl(\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bigr)^2
    -
    \frac{4\eta^2}{3}
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
       \bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2.
\]
Only the learning rate used on round $t$ enters the argument.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:budget}
For any fixed $0<\eta\leq1/16$, any sequence of utility vectors in
$[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_i|}$ observed after play, and every $T\geq1$,
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)})
    +
    \eta^2
    \sum_{t=1}^T\sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2
    \leq
    3c
    +
    6\eta^2
    \sum_{t=1}^T
       \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
     
  \IfNoValueTF{eq:budget}{ 
    \refstepcounter{equation}\tag{\theequation} 
  }{ 
    \tag{eq:budget} 
  } 
  \label{\]}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Apply \Cref{lem:one-round} on each round and move the negative weighted
square to the left. By the total derivative-mass bound in
\Cref{lem:calculus},
\[
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
       \bigl(\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bigr)^2
    \leq
    3\left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
\]
Therefore,
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
      -
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    +
    \eta^2
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2
    \leq
    6\eta^2
    \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
\]
Because the learning rate is fixed, summing over
$t=1,\ldots,T$ telescopes the potential terms,
\[
    \sum_{t=1}^T
    \left(
       \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
       -
       \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    \right)
    =
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)})-\Psi_i(\bm{0}).
\]
Using $\Psi_i(\bm{0})<3c$ from \Cref{lem:calculus} proves
\eqref{eq:budget}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:movement}
Suppose player $i$ uses \texttt{MORM}{} with a fixed rate
$0<\eta\leq1/16$ and receives any sequence of utility vectors in
$[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_i|}$ after play. For every $t\geq1$,
\[
    \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}_i^{(t+1)}}-\sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_i}\right\rVert_2^2
    \leq
    4\eta^2\sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2
    +16\eta^2
       \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
     
  \IfNoValueTF{eq:movement}{ 
    \refstepcounter{equation}\tag{\theequation} 
  }{ 
    \tag{eq:movement} 
  } 
  \label{\]}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix $t$. Between $\bm{x}^{(t)}_i$ and $\bm{x}_i^{(t+1)}$, both the cumulative
input and the optimistic correction change. We separate these two
effects using the intermediate distribution
\[
    \frac{
       a_c(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}[a])
       (1+4\eta\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a])
    }{
       \sum_b
       a_c(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}[b])
       (1+4\eta\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[b])
    }.
\]
This distribution keeps the old cumulative input
$\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}$ but uses the new correction $\bm{u}^{(t)}_i$.

By \Cref{lem:correction-change}, changing only the correction contributes
at most
\[
    8\eta^2
    \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2
\]
to the squared distance between the square-root vectors. By
\Cref{lem:input-change}, changing the cumulative input from
$\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)}$ to $\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)}$ while keeping the
correction fixed contributes at most
\[
    2\eta^2
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2.
\]
Both estimates are proved in \Cref{app:movement} by differentiating
normalized paths and integrating their square-root speeds.

Applying the triangle inequality to the square-root vectors and then
$(x+y)^2\leq2x^2+2y^2$ gives
\[
    \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}_i^{(t+1)}}-\sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_i}\right\rVert_2^2
    \leq
    4\eta^2\sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2
    +16\eta^2
       \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:movement}. In particular, the same
gradient-weighted square that appears in the potential bound
\eqref{eq:budget} controls strategy movement.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:rvu}
For a fixed rate $0<\eta\leq1/16$, any sequence of utility vectors in
$[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_i|}$ observed after play, and every integer
$T\geq1$,
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)})
    \leq

       +10\eta^2\sum_{t=1}^T

          \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_i}-\sqrt{\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)}}\right\rVert_2^2.
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Suppose first that $T\geq2$. Summing \eqref{eq:movement} over
$t=1,\ldots,T-1$, dividing by four, and extending the two nonnegative
sums on the right through round $T$ gives
\[
    \frac14\sum_{t=2}^T
       \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_i}-\sqrt{\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)}}\right\rVert_2^2
    \leq
    \eta^2\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(t)})\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2
    +4\eta^2\sum_{t=1}^T
       \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
\]
Adding $\Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)})$ and applying
\eqref{eq:budget} to the potential and weighted-square terms yields
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)})
       +\frac14\sum_{t=2}^T
          \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_i}-\sqrt{\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)}}\right\rVert_2^2
    \leq
    3c
       +10\eta^2\sum_{t=1}^T
          \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
\]
For $T=1$, the movement sum is empty, and the same conclusion follows

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:game}
In the model of \Cref{sec:game}, for every player $i$ and every $t\geq2$,
\[
    \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2
    \leq
    5\sum_{j=1}^n
       \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_j}-\sqrt{\bm{x}_j^{(t-1)}}\right\rVert_2^2.
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Fix $i$ and $t\geq2$. We first control the change in action utilities
caused by the opponents. For any $a,b\in\mathcal A_i$, the function
\[
    \mathcal U_i(a,\bm{s}_{-i})-\mathcal U_i(b,\bm{s}_{-i})
\]
takes values in $[-1,1]$. Applying the expectation comparison from
\Cref{lem:expectation-comparison} to the opponents' current and previous
product distributions gives
\[
    \left|
       (\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i[b])
       -
       (\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}[a]-\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}[b])
    \right|
    \leq
    2
    \left\|
       \sqrt{\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\bm{x}^{(t)}_j}
       -
       \sqrt{\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\bm{x}_j^{(t-1)}}
    \right\|_2.
\]
Since the same payoff function is evaluated on both rounds, the
fixed-game assumption applies. Using the product comparison from
\Cref{lem:product-comparison},
\[
    \left|
       (\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i[b])
       -
       (\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}[a]-\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}[b])
    \right|
    \leq
    2
    \left(
       \sum_{j\ne i}
       \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_j}-\sqrt{\bm{x}_j^{(t-1)}}\right\rVert_2^2
    \right)^{1/2}.
\]

We next account for centering. Adding and subtracting
$\left\langle \bm{x}^{(t)}_i,\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rangle$ gives
\[
    \bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]
    =
    \bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}[a]
    -
    \left\langle \bm{x}^{(t)}_i,\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i-\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rangle
    -
    \left\langle \bm{x}^{(t)}_i-\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)},\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rangle.
\]
The first three terms can be written as
\[
    \sum_b\bm{x}^{(t)}_i[b]
    \left[
       (\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i[b])
       -
       (\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}[a]-\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}[b])
    \right],
\]
so their absolute value is bounded by the preceding opponent-movement
estimate.

For the remaining centering term,
$\left\langle \bm{x}^{(t)}_i-\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)},\bm{1}\right\rangle=0$, so
\[
    \left|
       \left\langle \bm{x}^{(t)}_i-\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)},\bm{\nu}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rangle
    \right|
    \leq
    \frac12\left\lVert \bm{x}^{(t)}_i-\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_1
    \leq
    \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}^{(t)}_i}-\sqrt{\bm{x}_i^{(t-1)}}\right\rVert_2.
\]
Combining the two contributions and maximizing over $a$ yields
\[
    \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty
    \leq
    2

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:self-play}
Consider a fixed $n$-player finite game with at most $d\geq2$ actions
per player and utilities in $[0,1]$. Suppose every player uses

The algorithm is deterministic, uses only the player's own past
observations, and does not require knowledge of the horizon.

{lem:calculus}
Fix a player $i$ and let $c=2+\log d$. The potential $\Psi_i$ is
convex and continuously differentiable on $\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal A_i|}$, with
locally Lipschitz gradient. For every $\bm{U}$,
\[
    \Psi_i(\bm{U})>0,
    \qquad
    \Psi_i(\bm{0})
      =c|\mathcal A_i|^{1/(c-1)}
      <3c,
    \qquad
    \Psi_i(\bm{U})
      \geq c+\max_a\bm{U}[a].
\]
Moreover,
\[
    \partial_a\Psi_i(\bm{U})
      =
      \left(\frac{\Psi_i(\bm{U})}c\right)^{2-c}
      a_c(\bm{U}[a])

    \qquad
    \sum_a\partial_a\Psi_i(\bm{U})\leq3.

\[

      \preceq
      \nabla^2\Psi_i(\bm{U})
      \preceq
      \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\partial_a\Psi_i(\bm{U})\bigr).
\]
The same directional second-derivative bound holds almost everywhere
along every line segment.

\emph{Scalar properties.}
Recall that
\[
    f(z)=
    \begin{cases}
       (1-z)^{-1},&z\leq0,\\
       1+z,&z\geq0,
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    f'(z)=
    \begin{cases}
       (1-z)^{-2},&z\leq0,\\
       1,&z\geq0.
    \end{cases}
\]
The two branches agree in value and first derivative at zero. Hence,
$f$ is positive and continuously differentiable, with
$0<f'(z)\leq1$. Away from zero,
\[
    f''(z)=
    \begin{cases}
       2(1-z)^{-3},&z<0,\\
       0,&z>0.
    \end{cases}
\]
Thus, $f$ is convex and $f'$ is globally $2$-Lipschitz. We will use
the identity
\[
    f'(z)=\min\{f(z)^2,1\}.
\]
We will also use the lower bound $f(z)\geq1+z$. It is immediate for
$z\geq0$, while for $z\leq0$,
\[
    f(z)-(1+z)
      =
      \frac{z^2}{1-z}
      \geq0.
\]

\emph{Convexity and the potential certificate.}
Since $c-1>1$, the $\ell_{c-1}$ norm is convex and coordinatewise
nondecreasing on the nonnegative orthant. For
$0\leq\alpha\leq1$, convexity of $f$ gives, coordinatewise,
\[
    f\!\left(
       \frac{\alpha\bm{U}[a]+(1-\alpha)\bm{V}[a]}c
    \right)
    \leq
    \alpha f\!\left(\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right)
    +(1-\alpha)f\!\left(\frac{\bm{V}[a]}c\right).
\]
Monotonicity and convexity of the norm therefore imply
\[
    \Psi(\alpha\bm{U}+(1-\alpha)\bm{V})
    \leq
    \alpha\Psi(\bm{U})+(1-\alpha)\Psi(\bm{V}),
\]
so $\Psi$ is convex.

Since the norm dominates each of its coordinates, for every action
$a$,
\[
    \Psi(\bm{U})
      \geq
      c f\!\left(\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right)
      \geq
      c+\bm{U}[a].
\]
Taking the maximum over $a$ gives the stated lower bound. Moreover,
$f(0)=1$, so
\[
    \Psi(\bm{0})
      =
      c|\mathcal A_i|^{1/(c-1)}.
\]
Since $|\mathcal A_i|\leq d$ and $c-1=1+\log d$,
\[
    |\mathcal A_i|^{1/(c-1)}
    \leq
    d^{1/(1+\log d)}
    =
    \exp\!\left(\frac{\log d}{1+\log d}\right)
    <e<3.
\]
Hence $\Psi(\bm{0})<3c$. Positivity follows immediately from $f>0$.

\emph{Gradient and total derivative mass.}
Direct differentiation of the potential gives
\[
    \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})
      =
      \left(\frac{\Psi(\bm{U})}c\right)^{2-c}
      f\!\left(\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right)^{c-2}
      f'\!\left(\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right).
\]
Using $f'(z)=\min\{f(z)^2,1\}$, the action-dependent factor is
\[
    \begin{cases}
       \left(1-\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right)^{-c},
          &\bm{U}[a]\leq0,\\
       \left(1+\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right)^{c-2},
          &\bm{U}[a]\geq0.
    \end{cases}
\]
This is exactly $a_c(\bm{U}[a])$, and therefore
\[
    \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})
      =
      \left(\frac{\Psi(\bm{U})}c\right)^{2-c}
      a_c(\bm{U}[a])
      >0.
\]

To bound the total derivative mass, use $f'\leq1$ and H\"older's
inequality,
\[
    \sum_a
       f\!\left(\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right)^{c-2}
    \leq
    \left(
       \sum_a
       f\!\left(\frac{\bm{U}[a]}c\right)^{c-1}
    \right)^{(c-2)/(c-1)}
    |\mathcal A_i|^{1/(c-1)}.
\]
Since the power of the sum equals
$(\Psi(\bm{U})/c)^{c-2}$, we obtain
\[
    \sum_a\partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})
    \leq
    |\mathcal A_i|^{1/(c-1)}
    <3.
\]

\emph{Weighted curvature.}
For $z\ne0$,
\[
    \frac{a_c'(z)}{a_c(z)}
      =
      \begin{cases}
         (1-z/c)^{-1},&z<0,\\
         (c-2)/(c+z),&z>0.
      \end{cases}
\]
Both branches belong to $[0,1]$. At an input with no zero coordinates,
differentiating the gradient formula gives
\[
    \partial_b\partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})
      =
      \mathbf{1}_{\{a=b\}}
      \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})
      \frac{a_c'(\bm{U}[a])}{a_c(\bm{U}[a])}
      -
      \frac{c-2}{\Psi(\bm{U})}
      \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})\partial_b\Psi(\bm{U}).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
    \nabla^2\Psi(\bm{U})
      =
      \operatorname{diag}\!\left(
         \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})
         \frac{a_c'(\bm{U}[a])}{a_c(\bm{U}[a])}
      \right)
      -
      \frac{c-2}{\Psi(\bm{U})}
      \nabla\Psi(\bm{U})\nabla\Psi(\bm{U})^\top.
\]
The second term is positive semidefinite before subtraction. Hence, for
every $\bm{v}$,
\[
    \bm{v}^\top\nabla^2\Psi(\bm{U})\bm{v}
    \leq
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})
       \frac{a_c'(\bm{U}[a])}{a_c(\bm{U}[a])}
       \bm{v}[a]^2
    \leq
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})\bm{v}[a]^2.
\]
Since $\Psi$ is convex, its Hessian is positive semidefinite wherever
it exists. This proves
\[
    0
    \preceq
    \nabla^2\Psi(\bm{U})
    \preceq
    \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\partial_a\Psi(\bm{U})\bigr)
\]
at every point where the Hessian exists.

\emph{Regularity at zero coordinates.}
It remains to justify the regularity needed when a coordinate crosses
the junction at zero. The scalar function $f$ is continuously
differentiable with Lipschitz derivative. On every compact set of
inputs, the values $f(\bm{U}[a]/c)$ are uniformly bounded away from
zero. Since the $\ell_{c-1}$ norm is smooth on the strictly positive
orthant, the gradient formula above is locally Lipschitz on all of
$\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal A_i|}$.

Now fix a line $\bm{U}+s\bm{v}$. Every nonconstant coordinate crosses
zero at most once. Away from these finitely many crossing points, the
Hessian exists and the preceding quadratic-form bound applies. A
coordinate that is identically zero has $\bm{v}[a]=0$ and does not
contribute to the directional second derivative. Thus, almost
everywhere along the line,
\[
    0
    \leq
    \frac{d^2}{ds^2}\Psi(\bm{U}+s\bm{v})
    \leq
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi(\bm{U}+s\bm{v})\bm{v}[a]^2.
\]
Finally, local Lipschitz continuity of $\nabla\Psi$ makes the first
derivative along the line absolutely continuous on compact intervals,
so this bound can be integrated across the zero crossings. No Hessian
value at a crossing is required.

Combining this with \eqref{eq:root-tv} gives the factor $2$.

If instead $g$ takes values in $[0,1]$, then
$\sum_a(\bm{x}[a]-\bm{x}'[a])=0$, so subtracting $1/2$ from $g$ does not
change the expectation difference. Therefore,
\[
    \left|
       \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\bm{x}}[g(a)]
       -
       \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\bm{x}'}[g(a)]
    \right|
    =
    \left|
       \sum_a
          (\bm{x}[a]-\bm{x}'[a])
          \left(g(a)-\frac12\right)
    \right|
    \leq
    \frac12\left\lVert \bm{x}-\bm{x}'\right\rVert_1.
\]
Applying \eqref{eq:root-tv} once more gives the stated factor $1$.

\label{lem:product-comparison}
Consider any finite collection of pairs $\bm{x}_j,\bm{x}'_j$ of probability
vectors, where each pair is defined on the same finite set
$\mathcal A_j$. Then
\[
    \left\|
       \sqrt{\bigotimes_j\bm{x}_j}
       -
       \sqrt{\bigotimes_j\bm{x}'_j}
    \right\|_2^2
    \leq
    \sum_j
       \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}_j}-\sqrt{\bm{x}'_j}\right\rVert_2^2.
     
  \IfNoValueTF{eq:product}{ 
    \refstepcounter{equation}\tag{\theequation} 
  }{ 
    \tag{eq:product} 
  } 
  \label{\]} 

All products and sums are over the same collection. The statement
also holds for the empty collection, with both sides equal to zero.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
For each factor $j$, define its square-root overlap by
\[
    h_j
      =
      \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_j}
         \sqrt{\bm{x}_j[a]\bm{x}'_j[a]}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $0\leq h_j\leq1$. Expanding the squared
square-root distance gives
\[
    \left\lVert \sqrt{\bm{x}_j}-\sqrt{\bm{x}'_j}\right\rVert_2^2
      =
      2(1-h_j).
\]

The corresponding overlap of the product distributions factors into
the product of the individual overlaps. Indeed,
\[
    \sum_{\bm{s}\in\prod_j\mathcal A_j}
       \prod_j
          \sqrt{\bm{x}_j[\bm{s}[j]]\bm{x}'_j[\bm{s}[j]]}
    =
    \prod_j h_j.
\]
Therefore,
\[
    \left\|
       \sqrt{\bigotimes_j\bm{x}_j}
       -
       \sqrt{\bigotimes_j\bm{x}'_j}
    \right\|_2^2
    =
    2\left(1-\prod_j h_j\right).
\]

It remains to compare the product overlap with the individual ones.
Since every $h_j$ belongs to $[0,1]$,
\[
    1-\prod_j h_j
    \leq
    \sum_j(1-h_j).
\]
For example, this follows by expanding $1-\prod_j h_j$ successively
and observing that every remaining product of overlaps is at most one.
Multiplying by two gives
\[
    2\left(1-\prod_j h_j\right)
    \leq
    \sum_j2(1-h_j),
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:product}.

For the empty collection, both product distributions are the point
mass on the empty profile, so both sides are zero.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:adversarial}
Fix $n\geq1$, $d\geq2$, and a player $i$ with
$1\leq|\mathcal A_i|\leq d$. With $c=2+\log d$ and initial rate
$\eta_i^{(1)}=1/(32\sqrt n)$, the rule
\eqref{eq:safeguard-rule}--\eqref{eq:safeguard-rate} satisfies
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_i^{(T)}
      \leq
      96\sqrt n\,(2+\log d)
      +21\sqrt{T(2+\log d)}
\]
for every sequence of utility vectors
$\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_i|}$ observed in full after play and
every integer $T\geq1$. The utility vectors may be chosen adaptively,
with no assumption on the other players' behavior. The rule is
deterministic, uncoupled, and independent of the horizon.

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
\emph{Positivity of the learning rates and response weights.}
The excess $\delta_i^{(t)}$ is finite and nonnegative at every finite
time. Hence, \eqref{eq:safeguard-rate} gives
\[
    0
    <
    \eta_i^{(t+1)}
    \leq
    \eta_i^{(t)}
    \leq
    \frac1{32\sqrt n}
    \leq
    \frac1{32}.
\]
Moreover, centering gives $\left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i\right\rVert_\infty\leq1$. Therefore every
optimistic correction factor satisfies
\[
    1+4\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]
    \geq
    1-4\eta_i^{(t)}
    \geq
    \frac78.
\]
Together with the positivity of the gradient coordinates from
\Cref{lem:calculus}, this shows that the response is well defined on
every round.

\emph{Keeping the potential below the threshold.}
We first prove
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    \leq
    4c
    \qquad\text{for every }t\geq1.
     
  \IfNoValueTF{eq:safeguard-cap}{ 
    \refstepcounter{equation}\tag{\theequation} 
  }{ 
    \tag{eq:safeguard-cap} 
  } 
  \label{\]} 

At $t=1$, the input is zero and \Cref{lem:calculus} gives
$\Psi_i(\bm{0})<3c<4c$.

Suppose \eqref{eq:safeguard-cap} holds at the beginning of round $t$.
If $\delta_i^{(t)}=0$, then
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
    \leq
    4c
\]
and the learning rate does not change. Hence
\eqref{eq:safeguard-cap} also holds at $t+1$.

Now suppose $\delta_i^{(t)}>0$. By its definition,
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
      =
      4c+\delta_i^{(t)}.
\]
The rate update gives
\[
    \eta_i^{(t+1)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)}
      =
      \frac{c}{c+\delta_i^{(t)}}
      \eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)}
      +
      \frac{\delta_i^{(t)}}{c+\delta_i^{(t)}}\bm{0}.
\]
Thus the new potential input lies on the segment between the old
scaled input and zero. By convexity of $\Psi_i$ and
$\Psi_i(\bm{0})<3c$,
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t+1)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
    \leq
    \frac{c}{c+\delta_i^{(t)}}(4c+\delta_i^{(t)})
    +
    \frac{\delta_i^{(t)}}{c+\delta_i^{(t)}}3c
    =
    4c.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:safeguard-cap}. The argument uses convexity of
the potential along this segment and does not require the potential to
be monotone as the learning rate decreases.

\emph{Bounding the excess above the threshold.}
We next control how far the potential can move above $4c$ before the
rate adjustment is applied. Although the cumulative fixed-rate bound
\eqref{eq:budget} does not telescope when the learning rate changes,
the one-round estimate in \Cref{lem:one-round} remains valid with the
rate used on that round.

Apply \Cref{lem:one-round} with
$\eta=\eta_i^{(t)}$. Since \eqref{eq:safeguard-rule} is exactly the
corresponding one-round response,
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
    -
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    \leq
    2(\eta_i^{(t)})^2
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
       \bigl(\bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}[a]\bigr)^2
\]
\[
    \hspace{4em}
    -
    (\eta_i^{(t)})^2
    \sum_a
       \partial_a\Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
       \bm{u}^{(t)}_i[a]^2.
\]
The last term is nonpositive. Using
$\sum_a\partial_a\Psi_i\leq3$ from \Cref{lem:calculus} therefore gives
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
    -
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    \leq
    6(\eta_i^{(t)})^2
    \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty^2.
\]
Both centered-utility vectors have infinity norm at most one, so
\[
    \left\lVert \bm{u}^{(t)}_i-\bm{u}_i^{(t-1)}\right\rVert_\infty
    \leq
    2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)})
    -
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(t)}\bm{U}_i^{(t)})
    \leq
    24(\eta_i^{(t)})^2.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:safeguard-cap}, this yields
\[
    0
    \leq
    \delta_i^{(t)}
    \leq
    24(\eta_i^{(t)})^2.
\]

\emph{Controlling the decrease of the learning rate.}
The preceding estimate controls the rate update through the reciprocal
squared learning rate. From \eqref{eq:safeguard-rate},
\[
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(t+1)})^2}
    -
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(t)})^2}
    =
    \frac{2\delta_i^{(t)}}
         {c(\eta_i^{(t)})^2}
    +
    \frac{(\delta_i^{(t)})^2}
         {c^2(\eta_i^{(t)})^2}.
\]
Using $\delta_i^{(t)}\leq24(\eta_i^{(t)})^2$ gives
\[
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(t+1)})^2}
    -
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(t)})^2}
    \leq
    \frac{48}{c}
    +
    \frac{576(\eta_i^{(t)})^2}{c^2}.
\]
Since $\eta_i^{(t)}\leq1/32$ and $c>2$,
\[
    576(\eta_i^{(t)})^2
    \leq
    \frac{576}{1024}
    <
    c,
\]
and hence
\[
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(t+1)})^2}
    -
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(t)})^2}
    \leq
    \frac{49}{c}.
\]
Summing from $t=1$ to $T$ telescopes these reciprocal-rate differences,
which gives
\[
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(T+1)})^2}
    \leq
    \frac1{(\eta_i^{(1)})^2}
    +
    \frac{49T}{c}
    =
    1024n+\frac{49T}{c}.
\]
Thus the safeguard can decrease the learning rate only at the rate
allowed by this finite-horizon bound.

\emph{Converting the potential bound into regret.}
After round $T$, \eqref{eq:safeguard-cap} gives
\[
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(T+1)}\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)})
    \leq
    4c.
\]
Applying the potential certificate from \Cref{lem:calculus},
\[
    c
    +
    \eta_i^{(T+1)}
    \max_a\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)}[a]
    \leq
    \Psi_i(\eta_i^{(T+1)}\bm{U}_i^{(T+1)})
    \leq
    4c.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:two-identities},
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_i^{(T)}
    \leq
    \frac{3c}{\eta_i^{(T+1)}}.
\]
The reciprocal-rate bound now gives
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_i^{(T)}
    \leq
    3c
    \sqrt{
       1024n+\frac{49T}{c}
    }
    \leq
    96\sqrt n\,c
    +
    21\sqrt{cT}.
\]
Substituting $c=2+\log d$ proves the stated regret bound.

All inequalities above hold pathwise for every realized sequence of
utility vectors. No independence or probabilistic assumption on the
sequence is used, so the result also allows the utility vectors to be
chosen adaptively from the preceding history. The update uses only
quantities available by the end of the current round and does not
depend on $T$.

\label{prop:safeguard-selfplay}
Suppose all players use
\eqref{eq:safeguard-rule}--\eqref{eq:safeguard-rate}
in the fixed-game setting of \Cref{thm:self-play}. Then
\[
    \eta_i^{(t)}=\frac1{32\sqrt n}
    \qquad\text{for every player }i\text{ and every }t\geq1.
\]
Their mixed strategies coincide, round by round, with those of the
fixed-rate algorithm. The regret and equilibrium bounds in
\Cref{thm:self-play,cor:cce} therefore remain unchanged.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
We argue by induction on the round. Initially, every player has the
same learning rate, cumulative vector, and preceding centered utility
as in the fixed-rate algorithm. Hence the first-round strategies
coincide. Suppose all players have retained the rate $1/(32\sqrt n)$ through
round $t$. Their played strategies and observations through that round
then coincide with those of fixed-rate self-play, so their updated
cumulative vectors $\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)}$ also coincide.

Apply \Cref{prop:closure} to this finite prefix. For every player $i$,
\[
    \Psi_i\!\left(
       \frac{\bm{U}_i^{(t+1)}}{32\sqrt n}
    \right)
    \leq
    4c.
\]
Therefore $\delta_i^{(t)}=0$ for every player, and
\eqref{eq:safeguard-rate} gives
\[
    \eta_i^{(t+1)}
      =
      \frac1{32\sqrt n}.
\]
This proves the induction step simultaneously for all players. Hence
the safeguard never changes a learning rate, and the entire self-play
trajectory agrees with the fixed-rate trajectory.

{thm:main}[Regret bounds for \texttt{MORM}{}]
Consider a fixed $n$-player finite game with at most $d\geq2$ actions
per player and utilities in $[0,1]$. If all players $i\in[n]$ follow
\texttt{MORM}{} (\Cref{alg:learning}) with $c=2+\log d$ and learning rate
$\eta=1/(32\sqrt n)$, observing their exact expected-utility vectors
after each round, then every player $i\in[n]$ has external regret
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_i^{(T)}\leq \mathcal{O}(\sqrt n\log d).
\]
Moreover, \texttt{MORM}{} is adaptive to adversarial utilities through the
learning-rate safeguard in \Cref{app:safeguard}, which only decreases
the learning rate adaptively and leaves the rest of the update unchanged. The safeguarded rule for any individual
player $i$ guarantees
\[
    \operatorname{Reg}_i^{(T)}\leq \mathcal{O}(\sqrt n\log d+\sqrt{T\log d})
\]
against arbitrary, possibly adaptive, utility vectors
$\bm{\nu}^{(t)}_i\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_i|}$. Both bounds hold uniformly over all $T\geq1$.
