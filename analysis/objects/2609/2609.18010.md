\label{teoremaprincipal0}
Let $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \mu)$ be as in \eqref{perturbation} on system \eqref{langfordsystem}. Assume that conditions \eqref{conditions} hold. Then, for $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small, system \eqref{langfordsystem} admits a periodic solution $\phi(t,\varepsilon)$ satisfying $\phi(t,\varepsilon) \rightarrow 0$ as $\varepsilon \rightarrow 0$. Moreover, this periodic solution is asymptotically stable (resp. unstable) if
$$
2 \gamma _0 (\mu_1 - \alpha_1)+\mu _1 <0 \text{ (resp. } >0 ).

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{teoremaprincipal}
Let $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \mu)$ be as in \eqref{perturbation} on system \eqref{langfordsystem}. Assume that conditions \eqref{conditions} hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)]  For a sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, the system has a periodic solution $\phi(t,\varepsilon)$ satisfying $\phi(t,\varepsilon) \rightarrow 0$ when $\varepsilon \rightarrow 0$.

\item[(b)] Let $\phi(t,\varepsilon) = \phi(t,\mu_1,\varepsilon)$ be the periodic solution from item \textit{(a)}. Then there exists a smooth curve $\mu(\varepsilon) = \widehat{\mu_0} + \widehat{\mu}_1 \varepsilon + \widehat{\mu_2} \varepsilon^2+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^3)$ and intervals $J_\varepsilon$ containing $\mu(\varepsilon)$ such that a unique invariant torus bifurcates from the periodic solution $\phi(t,\mu(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)$ as $\mu_1$ passes through $\mu(\varepsilon)$. The torus exists for $\mu \in J_\varepsilon$, provided $\ell_{1,2} (\mu_1- \mu(\varepsilon)) <0$, and surrounds the periodic solution. Moreover, since $\ell_{1,2}>0$ the torus is unstable whenever it exists.
\end{itemize}

\label{zerodapromed} Suppose that there exists $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \Omega$ such that $\mathbf{g}_1(\mathbf{x}_0)=0$ and $\det D\mathbf{g}_1 (\mathbf{x}_0) \neq 0$. Then, for $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small the system \eqref{eqavrnormalform} admits a unique isolated $T$-periodic solution $\phi(t,\varepsilon)$ satisfying
$\phi(0,\varepsilon) \to \mathbf{x}_0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$. Moreover, the stability of this periodic solution is determined by the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix $D\mathbf{g}_1(\mathbf{x}_0)$: if all eigenvalues have negative (resp. positive) real parts, the periodic orbit is asymptotically stable (resp. unstable).

\label{lema} (\cite{candido2020torus}) Assume that hypotheses {\bf H1} and {\bf H2} hold. Let $\lambda(\mu, \varepsilon)$ and $\overline{\lambda(\mu, \varepsilon)}$ be the conjugate eigenvalues of $D_\mathbf{x} P(\xi(\mu, \varepsilon), \mu, \varepsilon)$.  Then there exists an unique smooth function $\mu: (-\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_2) \to J_0$, with $0<\varepsilon_2<\varepsilon_1$ and $\mu(0)=\mu_0$ such that
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $| \lambda(\mu(\varepsilon), \varepsilon) |= 1,$ 
\item[(ii)] $(\lambda(\mu(\varepsilon), \varepsilon) )^k \neq 1$, for $k=1,2,3,4$,
\item[(iii)] $\left.\dfrac{d}{d\mu} |\lambda(\mu,\varepsilon)| \right|_{\mu=\mu(\varepsilon)} \neq 0.$
\end{itemize}

\label{teoremamurilo} (\cite{candido2020torus})
Assume that hypotheses {\bf H1} and {\bf H2} hold and let $j \in \{1,2\}$ be the smallest index such that and that $\ell_{1,j} \neq 0$. Then, for each $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small there exists a smooth curve $\mu(\varepsilon) \in J_0$ with $\mu(0)=\mu_0$, neighborhoods $U_\varepsilon \subset \mathbb{S}^1 \times \Omega$ of the periodic solution $\phi(t, \mu(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)$ and intervals $J_\varepsilon \subset J_0$ of $\mu(\varepsilon)$ for which the following are true:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] For $\mu \in J_\varepsilon$ such that $\ell_1 (\mu - \mu(\varepsilon)) \geq0$, the periodic solution $\phi(t,\mu(\varepsilon), \varepsilon)$ is unstable (resp. asymptotically stable) provided that $\ell_1 >0$ (resp. $\ell_1<0)$ and the differential equation \eqref{eqavrnormalform2} does not admit any invariant tori in $U_\varepsilon$.
\item[(ii)] For $\mu \in J_\varepsilon$ such that $\ell_1 (\mu - \mu(\varepsilon)) <0$, the differential equation \eqref{eqavrnormalform2} admits a unique invariant torus $T_{\mu,\varepsilon}$ in $U_\varepsilon$ surrounding the periodic solution $\phi(t, \mu, \varepsilon)$. Moreover, $T_{\mu,\varepsilon}$ is unstable (resp. asymptotically stable) and the periodic solution $\phi(t,\mu,\varepsilon)$ is asymptotically stable (resp. unstable).
\item[(iii)] $T_{\mu,\varepsilon}$ is the unique torus of the differential equation \eqref{eqavrnormalform2} bifurcating from the periodic solution $\phi(t, \mu(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)$ in $U_\varepsilon$ as $\mu$ passes through $\mu(\varepsilon)$.
\end{itemize}

Consider system \eqref{langfordsystem} with parameters
\begin{align*}
\alpha = - \varepsilon, \quad
\beta = \dfrac{1}{100} , \\
\gamma = \dfrac{2}{5}- \dfrac{1}{20}\varepsilon, \quad
\mu  =-\dfrac{11}{12} \varepsilon.
\end{align*}

From Theorem \ref{teoremaprincipal0}, with these values system \eqref{langfordsystem} has a periodic solution near the origin; this periodic solution is asymptotically stable since
$$
2 \gamma _0 \left(\mu _1-\alpha _1\right)+\mu _1 = -\dfrac{17}{20} <0.
$$
We remark that no invariant torus bifurcates from the periodic solution. Figure \ref{fig1} illustrates the behaviour of a solution near the origin for these values.

\label{exemplo2} \textit{An invariant unstable torus bifurcating from a periodic solution.}

Consider system \eqref{langfordsystem} with parameters
\begin{align*}
\alpha = \varepsilon + \dfrac{1}{100} \varepsilon^2, \quad
\beta = \dfrac{1}{10}+ \dfrac{443}{100}\varepsilon + \dfrac{1}{25} \varepsilon^2 , \\
\gamma = -1- \varepsilon+   \dfrac{1}{25} \varepsilon^2, \quad
\mu  = \dfrac{399317}{100000} \varepsilon+  \dfrac{1}{25} \varepsilon^2.
\end{align*}

With these values we have
$$
\ell_{1,2} = \dfrac{49 \pi ^2-11}{144} >0 .
$$

Using a small $\varepsilon$ such as $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{1000}$ we have $\mu_1 - \mu(\varepsilon) \approx -7.4 \cdot 10^{-3}$. Theorem \ref{teoremamurilo} guarantees the existence of a periodic orbit of \eqref{langfordsystem} surrounded by an unstable invariant torus. In Figure \ref{fig2} we see the orbit with initial conditions $(0.0005, 0, 0.0005)$. In Figure \ref{fig3} the Poincar\'e map of this orbit is illustrated.
