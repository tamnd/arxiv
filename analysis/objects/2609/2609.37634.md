<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:continuous-polymatroid-base-extension}
Let $\rho$ be a 
submodular function. For each $x\in{\mathbf I}_\rho$, there exists $b\in{\mathbf P}_\rho$ such that $x\leqslant b$. If $\rho$ is integer-valued and $x\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H$, then $b$ may be chosen in ${\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\Lambda={\mathbb R}$. If $\rho$ and $x$ are integral, we may instead take
$\Lambda={\mathbb Z}$. In either case, consider
\[
    C_x^\Lambda
    \coloneqq
    \{y\in{\mathbf I}_\rho\cap\Lambda^H\mid \, x\leqslant y\}.
\]
This set is nonempty and compact when $\Lambda={\mathbb R}$, while it is finite when $\Lambda={\mathbb Z}$. Choose $b\in C_x^\Lambda$ maximizing $b(H)$. It is enough to show that $b(H)=\rho(H)$.

We claim that for each $v\in H$, there must be a subset $I_v\subseteq H$ containing $v$ such that $b(I_v)=\rho(I_v)$. Indeed, if no such subset existed for some $v$, then all the finitely many slacks $\rho(I)-b(I)$, for $I$ containing $v$, would be positive. We could then increase the $v$-th coordinate of $b$ by a sufficiently small positive real number when $\Lambda={\mathbb R}$, or by $1$ when $\Lambda={\mathbb Z}$, and obtain a point of $C_x^\Lambda$ with larger modulus, a contradiction, proving the claim.

Finally, the family of tight sets $I_v$ is closed under unions by submodularity; see \cite[Thm.~44.2, p.~767]{Schrijver_B_2003}. Since $\bigcup_{v\in H}I_v=H$, it follows that $b(H)=\rho(H)$.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:multiplicity-free-hilbert-polynomial}
Let $Y\subseteq{\mathbb P}_H$ be a multiplicity-free variety. Then
\[
    P_Y({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{q\in D_Y}B_q({\mathbf n}).
\]
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The hockey-stick identity gives
\(
    C_q({\mathbf n})=\sum_{a\leqslant q}B_a({\mathbf n})
\)
for every $q\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H$. Therefore,
\begin{align*}
    P_Y({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{q\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H}e_q(Y)
    \sum_{a\leqslant q}B_a({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H}
    \bigg(\sum_{q\geqslant a}e_q(Y)\bigg)B_a({\mathbf n}).
\end{align*}
By \cite[Thm.~6.9]{castillo2025kpolynomialsmultiplicityfreevarieties}, if $e_q(Y)\neq 0$ then $q\leqslant b$ for some $b\in M_Y$.
Thus, if $a\notin D_Y$, then
\[
    \sum_{q\geqslant a}e_q(Y)=0.
\]
On the other hand, if $a\in D_Y$, then there exists $m\in M_Y$ with
$m\geqslant a$ by \autoref{lem:continuous-polymatroid-base-extension}. So, \cite[Prop.~6.12(iii)]{castillo2025kpolynomialsmultiplicityfreevarieties} yields
\[
    \sum_{q\geqslant a}e_q(Y)=1.
\]
Substitution in the preceding expansion proves the result.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:normal-crossings-inclusion-exclusion}
Let $Y\subseteq{\mathbb P}_H$ be a simple normal-crossings scheme.
Then
\[
    P_Y({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{\substack{\varnothing\neq\Delta\subseteq E_Y\\
                     Y_\Delta\neq\varnothing}}
    (-1)^{|\Delta|-1}P_{Y_\Delta}({\mathbf n}).
\]
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
With the usual alternating restriction maps, the augmented
\v{C}ech-type complex
\[
    \begin{tikzcd}
        0 \arrow[r] & {\mathcal O}_Y \arrow[r] & \displaystyle\bigoplus_{|\Delta|=1}{\mathcal O}_{Y_\Delta} \arrow[r] & \displaystyle\bigoplus_{|\Delta|=2}{\mathcal O}_{Y_\Delta}   \arrow[r] & \cdots \arrow[r] & {\mathcal O}_{Y_{E_Y}} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
\]
is exact, where an empty intersection contributes zero; see
\cite[Def.~2.9, pp.~30--31]{Fujino_LMMP}, where it is called the
Mayer--Vietoris simplicial resolution. Although the reference works over
${\mathbb C}$, the same statement holds over an arbitrary algebraically closed field. Tensoring by
${\mathcal O}_{{\mathbb P}_H}({\mathbf n})$ preserves exactness, and the additivity of Euler
characteristics gives the formula.

\label{prop:normal-crossings-euler-cancellation}
Let $Y\subseteq{\mathbb P}_H$ be a simple normal-crossings scheme such that 
every nonempty $Y_\Delta$ is a multiplicity-free variety.
For each $a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H$, let $K_a$ be the simplicial complex on
$E\coloneqq E_Y$ whose nonempty faces are the nonempty $\Delta\subseteq E$ such that $Y_\Delta\neq\varnothing$ and $a\in D_{Y_\Delta}$.
Then
\[
    P_Y({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H}\chi(K_a)B_a({\mathbf n}),
\]
where $\chi(K_a)$ is the Euler characteristic of $K_a$. If, in addition,
every $K_a$ having a nonempty face is connected and acyclic, then
\[
    P_Y({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{a\in\bigcup_{e\in E}D_{Y_e}}
    B_a({\mathbf n}).
\]
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We check first that $K_a$ is a simplicial complex. Let $\Delta\in K_a$ and $\varnothing\neq\Gamma\subseteq\Delta$. Since
$Y_\Delta\subseteq Y_\Gamma$, one has
that $Y_\Gamma\neq\varnothing$ and $\varphi_{Y_\Delta}(I)\leqslant\varphi_{Y_\Gamma}(I)$ for every
$I\subseteq H$. Thus $D_{Y_\Delta}\subseteq D_{Y_\Gamma}$, and hence
$\Gamma\in K_a$.

By \autoref{prop:normal-crossings-inclusion-exclusion} and
\autoref{prop:multiplicity-free-hilbert-polynomial}, reordering the finite
sums gives
\begin{align*}
    P_Y({\mathbf n}) & =
    \sum_{\substack{\varnothing\neq\Delta\subseteq E\\
                     Y_\Delta\neq\varnothing}}
    (-1)^{|\Delta|-1}\sum_{a\in D_{Y_\Delta}}B_a({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H}\Big(
        \sum_{\substack{\Delta\in K_a\\\Delta\neq\varnothing}}
        (-1)^{|\Delta|-1}
    \Big)B_a({\mathbf n})
    =
    \sum_{a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H}\chi(K_a)B_a({\mathbf n}).
\end{align*}

Now assume that every $K_a$ having a nonempty face is connected and acyclic then $\chi(K_a)=1$, see \cite[Thm.~2.44, p.~146]{Hatcher_2002}.
If it has only the empty face, then $\chi(K_a)=0$. Since $K_a$ is simplicial, it has a nonempty face if and only if it contains a singleton, which is equivalent to $a\in\bigcup_{e\in E}D_{Y_e}$. Thus, the last formula follows.

\label{prop:projection-rank-dilworth-truncation}

\(

\label{prop:polygon-linked-projective-space-mustafin}
Let $\mathfrak{V}$ be an exact finitely generated linked net of vector spaces of finite dimension $r>0$ over a ${\mathbb Z}^n$-quiver $Q$. If $\mathfrak{V}$ is generated by a polygon, then 
there is a finite convex lattice configuration $\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{B}^0_r$ such that
\[
\mathbb{LP}(\mathfrak{V})={\mathcal M}(\Gamma)_{\mathbf{k}}.
\]
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $v_1,\dots,v_m$ be vertices of $Q$ forming an oriented $m$-gon $\Delta$ generating $\mathfrak V$. 
For convenience, put $v_{m+1}\coloneqq v_1$. Since $\mathfrak{V}$ is generated by $\Delta$, it follows from \cite[Prop.~10.1, p.~550]{Esteves22072025} that $\mathfrak{V}$ is the direct sum of exact linked nets $\mathfrak{V}_1,\dots,\mathfrak{V}_{r}$. Since $\mathfrak{V}$ is generated by $\Delta$, so are the $\mathfrak{V}_j$. By \cite[Thm.~7.8, p.~544]{Esteves22072025}, for each $j\in[r]$, the net $\mathfrak{V}_j$ is generated by $\{v_{\ell_j}\}$ for a unique $\ell_j\in[m]$; let $s_j\in V_{v_{\ell_j}}$ be a generator. Reorganize so that $\ell_1\leqslant\ell_2\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\ell_r$. For each $\ell\in[m]$, let $r_\ell\coloneqq |\lbrace j~\vert~ \ell_j=\ell\rbrace|$. We have $\sum r_\ell=r$. The $s_j$ induce a basis for $V_{v_i}$ for each $i\in[m]$, and thus a decomposition $V_{v_i}=V_{i,1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{i,m}$, where $V_{i,\ell}$ is the subspace freely generated by the $\varphi^{v_\ell}_{v_i}(s_j)$ for $j$ with $\ell_j=\ell$, for $\ell\in[m]$. Notice that $V_{i,\ell}$ has dimension $r_\ell$. 

For each $i\in[m]$, the map $\varphi^{v_i}_{v_{i+1}}$ can thus be represented by a diagonal matrix $M_i$ of size $r$. If $i<m$, all of its diagonal entries are $1$ but for those in position $r_1+\cdots+r_i+j$ for $j=1,\dots,r_{i+1}$, which are $0$. The matrix $M_m$ has all of its diagonal entries $1$ but for those in position $1,\dots,r_1$, which are $0$. 

Put $R\coloneqq{\mathbf{k}}\llbracket T \rrbracket$. Let $\overline{x}\in{\mathbf{k}}$ denote the residue of $x\in R$. 
Define, for each $i\in[m]$, 
$$
\alpha_i\coloneqq (\alpha_{i,1},\dots,\alpha_{i,r}),\quad \text{ where }
\alpha_{i,j}=\begin{cases}
    1 &\text{if }j\leqslant r_1+\cdots+r_i,\\
    0 &\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}, \quad \text{ and }
\Lambda_i\coloneqq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r} T^{-\alpha_{ij}}R.
$$
Put $\Gamma\coloneqq\lbrace [\Lambda_1],\dots,[\Lambda_m]\rbrace$. Let $\Lambda\coloneqq R^{r}$.

First, we verify that $\Gamma$ is convex. All lattices in $\Gamma$ are in the same apartment. Hence, it follows from \cite[Lem.~4.1, p.~773]{Cartwright2011} that $\Gamma$ is convex if and only if the classes $[\alpha_1],\dots,[\alpha_m]$ in the tropical projective space $\mathbb{TP}^{r-1}$ are the lattice points of their tropical convex hull 
\[
    C\coloneqq \mathrm{tconv}([\alpha_1],\dots,[\alpha_m])\subseteq \mathbb{TP}^{r-1}.
\]
Let $[\beta]\in C$ be a lattice point, $\beta=(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_r)\in{\mathbb Z}^{r}$. Since $[\beta]$ is in $C$, the representative $\beta$ can be written as a tropical linear combination of the $\alpha_i$, that is, there are $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\in{\mathbb R}$ such that
$$
\beta=\lambda_1\odot\alpha_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\lambda_m\odot\alpha_m,
$$
where $\odot$ is the tropical product and $\oplus$ is the tropical sum. 
It follows that for each $i\in[m]$ and $j=r_1+\cdots+r_{i-1}+1,\dots,r_1+\cdots+r_i$,
$$
\beta_j=\tau_i\coloneqq \min\lbrace \lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{i-1}, \lambda_i+1,\dots,\lambda_m+1 \rbrace.
$$
Notice that
$$
\tau_1\geqslant\tau_2\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\tau_m\geqslant\tau_1-1.
$$
Let $\ell\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ be the largest integer such that $\tau_\ell=\tau_1$. Then $\tau_i=\tau_1$ for $i\leqslant \ell$ and $\tau_i=\tau_1-1$ for $i>\ell$. It follows that 
$\beta=(\tau_1-1)\odot\alpha_\ell$. Thus $[\beta]=[\alpha_\ell]$, as required.

It remains to identify the associated Mustafin variety. Let $Q(\Gamma)$ be the quiver associated to $\Gamma$. Any two lattices in $\Gamma$ are adjacent. Indeed, if $i\geqslant\ell$ then $T\Lambda_\ell\subseteq\Lambda_i\subseteq\Lambda_\ell$. For each $i\in[m]$, let $g_i$ be the diagonal matrix whose entries in the diagonal are $1$ but those in position $j$ for $j\in [r_1+\cdots +r_{i}]$, which are $T$. Notice that $g_i\Lambda_i=\Lambda$. For $i,\ell\in[m]$, let $p_{i,\ell}$ be the minimum integer such that $T^{p_{i,\ell}}\Lambda_i\subseteq\Lambda_\ell$, and put $g_{i,\ell}\coloneqq g_{\ell}T^{p_{i,\ell}}g_{i}^{-1}$. We have  
\[
    p_{i,\ell}=\left\lbrace
    \begin{array}{cc}
        0 & i\leqslant \ell, \\
        1 & i>\ell.
    \end{array}\right.
\] 
By \cite[Cor.~4.6, p.~194]{HahnLi2021}, 
\[
{\mathcal M}(\Gamma)_{\mathbf{k}}=\bigg\lbrace ([s_1],\dots,[s_m])\in \prod_{i=1}^m {\mathbb P}^{r-1}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\,\,\Big|\,\, \overline{g_{i,\ell}}(s_i)\wedge s_\ell=0 \text{ for all } i,\ell\in[m]\bigg\rbrace.
\]
Thus, we need only show that $\overline{g_{i,i+1}}=M_i$ for each $i\in[m]$, where $g_{m,m+1}\coloneqq g_{m,1}$. Indeed, for $i\in[m-1]$, we have 
\[
    \overline{g_{i,i+1}}=\overline{g_{i+1}}\,\overline{g_i}^{-1}=M_i,
\]
whereas for $i=m$ we have
\[
    \overline{g_{m,1}}=\overline{g_{1}Tg_m^{-1}}=M_m.
\]
This proves that ${\mathcal M}(\Gamma)_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{LP}(\mathfrak{V})$ and completes the proof.

By \cite[Lem.~5.1]{Chow.class} and \cite[Prop.~3.4(I)(3)]{amini2024tropicalizationlinearseriestilings}, the relative interiors of  ${\mathbf P}_{v_1}$ and ${\mathbf P}_{v_2}$ are disjoint. Since the polytopes ${\mathbf P}_v$ form a tiling by \cite[Thm.~5.2]{Chow.class}, the intersection ${\mathbf F}\coloneqq {\mathbf P}_{v_1}\cap {\mathbf P}_{v_2}$ is a proper common face of  ${\mathbf P}_{v_1}$ and ${\mathbf P}_{v_2}$. On the other hand, by \cite[Lem.~4.4(1)(2)]{Chow.class}, ${\mathbf P}_\Delta$ is a common facet of ${\mathbf P}_{v_1}$ and ${\mathbf P}_{v_2}$. Since ${\mathbf P}_\Delta\subseteq{\mathbf F}$ and  ${\mathbf F}$ is proper, we conclude that ${\mathbf F}={\mathbf P}_\Delta$. 

We consider now the general case. 
Since $\Delta$ is a polygon, so is $\Gamma_j=\lbrace v_1,v_{j+1}\rbrace$ for each $j\in[m-1]$. Also, $R^{\Gamma_j}_{v_1}\cap H=F_j$ and  $R^{\Gamma_j}_{v_{j+1}}\cap H=F_j^c$. Thus, 
by the first statement, 
\[  
    {\mathbf P}_{v_1}\cap \left\lbrace q\in{\mathbb R}_{\geqslant0}^H\,\middle|\,q(F^c_j)=\rho_{v_1}(F_j^c)\right\rbrace = {\mathbf P}_{\Gamma_j}. 
\] 
Hence, using the first statement and the second for $m=2$, we have
\[
{\mathbf P}_\Delta=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m-1}  {\mathbf P}_{\Gamma_j} =\bigcap_{j=1}^{m-1}  {\mathbf P}_{v_1}\cap {\mathbf P}_{v_{j+1}} =\bigcap_{j=1}^{m} {\mathbf P}_{v_j},
\]
as required.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{P_Delta-gives-Delta}
    Put $\Omega_R\coloneqq\{x\in{\mathbb R}_{\geqslant0}^H\mid x(H)=R\}$, where $R\coloneqq r+1$.
    The following statements hold: \begin{enumerate}
        \item If $\Delta,\Delta'\subseteq H$ are polygons, and ${\mathbf P}_\Delta={\mathbf P}_{\Delta'}$, then $\Delta=\Delta'$.
        \item If $\Sigma\subseteq H$ is nonempty subset such that
        \[
            \Big(\bigcap_{v\in\Sigma}{\mathbf P}_v \Big)\cap~ \operatorname{relint}(\Omega_R)\neq\varnothing
        \]
        then $\Sigma$ is a polygon.
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $v_1,\dots,v_m$ forming an oriented polygon $\Delta$. Let $\pi_i\coloneqq R^\Delta_{v_i}\cap H$ and $F_i\coloneqq\pi_1\cup\cdots\cup\pi_i$ for each $i\in[m]$, and put $\pi\coloneqq(\pi_1,\dots,\pi_m)$. Then by \autoref{lem:intersection-polygon}, we have
\[
    {\mathbf P}_\Delta={\mathbf P}_{v_1}\cap\{q\in{\mathbb R}_{\geqslant0}^H\,|\,q(F_j^c)=\rho_{v_1}(F_j^c)\,\forall j\in [m-1]\}.
\]
Moreover, by \cite[Lem.~4.4]{Chow.class}, the polytope ${\mathbf P}_\Delta$ has codimension $m-1$ in $\Omega_R$. Hence the affine span of  ${\mathbf P}_\Delta$ is obtained from the affine span of $\Omega_R$ by imposing the equations $q(F_j^c)=0$ with $j\in[m-1]$. Equivalently, 
\[
    \operatorname{dir}({\mathbf P}_\Delta)=\left\{q\in{\mathbb R}^H\,|\,q(\pi_j)=0\,\forall j\in [m]\right\}.
\]

Let now $\Delta'$ be another polygon such that ${\mathbf P}_\Delta={\mathbf P}_{\Delta'}$, and let us proof that $\Delta'=\Delta$. First, $\operatorname{dir}({\mathbf P}_\Delta)=\operatorname{dir}({\mathbf P}_{\Delta'})$, and thus the unordered partitions induced by $\Delta$ and $\Delta'$ are equal. For each $i\in[m]$, let $u_i$ be the vertex in $\Delta'\cap\pi_i$, and put $S_i\coloneqq W_{v_i}\cap V_{\pi_i}$ and $S'_i\coloneqq W_{u_i}\cap V_{\pi_i}$. Then 
\[
\prod_{i=1}^m{\mathbf P}_{S_i}={\mathbf P}_{\Delta}={\mathbf P}_{\Delta'}=\prod_{i=1}^m{\mathbf P}_{S'_i}
\]
and thus ${\mathbf P}_{S_i}={\mathbf P}_{S'_i}$ for each $i\in[m]$. Furthermore, since ${\mathbf P}_{\Delta}$ has codimension $m-1$ in $\Omega_R$, we must have that $S_i$ and $S'_i$ are simple for each $i\in[m]$, that is, their polytopes have dimension $|\pi_i|-1$; see \cite[Sec.~2.5]{Chow.class}

Now, by \cite[Lem.~5.1]{Chow.class}, for each $i\in[m]$, there exists a nonzero $c_i\in{\mathbf{k}}^H$ such that $c_iW_{v_i}\subseteq W_{u_i}$. Furthermore, by the proof of loc.~cit, we may choose $c_i$ such that $c_i(u_i)\neq 0$, and $c_i(v_i)=0$ if $v_i\neq u_i$. Now, $c_i|_{\pi_i}S_i\subseteq S'_i$. By \cite[Prop.~2.2]{Chow.class}, since ${\mathbf P}_{S}={\mathbf P}_{S'}$, and $c_i(u_i)\neq 0$, we must have that $c_i|_{\pi_i}$ is invertible, whence $c_i(v_i)\neq 0$, and thus $v_i=u_i$. So $\Delta'=\Delta$.

Consider now a subset $\Sigma\subseteq H$ as in the second statement, and put ${\mathbf F}\coloneqq\bigcap_{v\in\Sigma}{\mathbf P}_v$. By \cite[Thm.~5.2]{Chow.class} the ${\mathbf P}_v$ form a polyhedral tiling. Then ${\mathbf F}$ is a face of ${\mathbf P}_v$ for each $v\in \Sigma$. Thus, by \cite[Prop.~2.7]{amini2024tropicalizationlinearseriestilings}, for each $v\in V$, there is a partition $\pi_v=(\pi_{v,1},\dots,\pi_{v,m})$ of $H$  such that 
\[
{\mathbf F}={\mathbf P}_v\cap \lbrace q\in{\mathbb R}_{\geqslant0}^H\,|\,q(F_j^c)=\rho_v(F_j^c) \,\forall j\in [m-1]\rbrace,
\]
where $F_j\coloneqq\pi_{v,1}\cup\cdots\cup\pi_{v,j}$ for each $j\in[m]$. Since ${\mathbf F}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\Omega_R)\neq\varnothing$, by \cite[Prop.~4.5]{Chow.class} there is a $m$-gon $\Delta_v$ such that $v\in\Delta_v$ and ${\mathbf P}_{\Delta_v}={\mathbf F}$. In particular, ${\mathbf P}_{\Delta_v}={\mathbf P}_{\Delta_w}$, and thus, by the first statement, $\Delta_v=\Delta_w$ for each $v,w\in\Sigma$. Let $\Delta$ be the common polygon. By construction, for each $v\in\Sigma$, we have $v\in \Delta_v=\Delta$. Then $\Sigma\subseteq\Delta$. Since $\Delta$ is a polygon, so is $\Sigma$. This finishes the proof of the lemma.

<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:multivariate-hilbert-polynomial}
Let $\mathfrak{V}$ be a nontrivial exact finitely generated linked net of
${\mathbf{k}}$-vector spaces of dimension $r+1$ over a ${\mathbb Z}^n$-quiver $Q$, and let $H$ be
its minimum set of generators. Then
\[
    P_{\mathbb{LP}(\mathfrak V)}\bigl({\mathbf n}\bigr)=\binom{r+\sum_{u\in H}n_u}{r}.
\]
In words, $\mathbb{LP}(\mathfrak V)$ has the multivariate Hilbert polynomial of the small
diagonal ${\mathbb P}^r\hookrightarrow({\mathbb P}^r)^H$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
The hypotheses of
\autoref{prop:normal-crossings-euler-cancellation} are verified for $Y\coloneqq\mathbb{LP}(\mathfrak V)$. Indeed, $\mathbb{LP}(\mathfrak V)$ is simple normal-crossings by
\autoref{thm:linked-projective-space-snc}, and its nonempty strata are the
$Y_\Delta\coloneqq \mathbb{LP}(\mathfrak V)_\Delta$ for polygons $\Delta\subseteq H$, hence multiplicity-free varieties by \autoref{prop:polygon-intersection-hilbert-polynomial}.

For each $a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H$, let $K_a$ be the simplicial complex on
$E\coloneqq E_Y$ whose nonempty faces are the nonempty $\Delta\subseteq E$ such that $Y_\Delta\neq\varnothing$ and $a\in D_{Y_\Delta}$. By  \cite[Prop.~6.3, p.~21]{Esteves2025}, identifying $E_Y$ with $H$, we have that $Y_\Delta\neq\varnothing$ if and only if $\Delta$ is a polygon in $H$. Also, $D_{Y_\Delta}=D_\Delta$ by \autoref{prop:polygon-intersection-hilbert-polynomial}. 

If $a(H)>r$, then
\autoref{prop:general-vector-space-hilbert-polynomial} yields that $a\notin D_\Delta$ for any polygon $\Delta\subseteq H$, whence $K_a$ has only the empty face. 

Suppose $a(H)\leqslant r$. We prove that $K_a$ is contractible, whence connected and acyclic.
Choose
$0<\varepsilon<1/|H|$ and set
\[
    C_{a,\varepsilon}
    \coloneqq a+\varepsilon\mathbf1
    +\Omega_{R-a(H)-\varepsilon|H|},
\]
where 
\[
    \Omega_t=\{x\in{\mathbb R}_{\geqslant0}^H\mid x(H)=t\} \quad \text{for each } t\ge0.
\]
Note that $C_{a,\varepsilon}$ is a nonempty closed convex subset of
$\operatorname{relint}(\Omega_R)$. 

For each $v\in H$, set $U_{v,a}\coloneqq
{\mathbf P}_v\cap C_{a,\varepsilon}$. By
\cite[Thm.~5.2]{Chow.class}, we have
 \[
\Omega_R=\bigcup_{v\in H}{\mathbf P}_v.
\] 
Hence, the sets $U_{v,a}$ cover
$C_{a,\varepsilon}$. For each nonempty subset $\Delta\subseteq H$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cover-intersections}
    \bigcap_{v\in\Delta}U_{v,a}
    =\bigg(\bigcap_{v\in\Delta}{\mathbf P}_v\bigg)\cap C_{a,\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
If the intersection in \eqref{eq:cover-intersections} is nonempty, then it meets $\operatorname{relint}(\Omega_R)$. Then $\Delta$ is a polygon by 
\autoref{P_Delta-gives-Delta}, and 
\[ 
\bigcap_{v\in\Delta}{\mathbf P}_v={\mathbf P}_\Delta,
\] 
by \autoref{lem:intersection-polygon}. Using \autoref{prop:interior-criterion} now, we get that the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:cover-intersections} is nonempty exactly when $\Delta$ is a polygon and 
$a\in D_\Delta$. Thus the nerve of the cover
$(U_{v,a})_{v\in H}$ is precisely $K_a$.

The $U_{v,a}$ are closed convex sets, as are all their nonempty finite
intersections. By Nerve Theorem for finite closed convex covers
\cite[Thm.~3.1, p.~17]{BauerKerberRollRolle_2023},  we have that
$K_a$ is homotopically equivalent to $ C_{a,\varepsilon}$. Since $C_{a,\varepsilon}$ is nonempty and
convex, $K_a$ is contractible. 

Consequently, $K_a$ has a nonempty face exactly when $a(H)\leqslant r$, in which case $K_a$ is acyclic. We may now apply
\autoref{prop:normal-crossings-euler-cancellation}, obtaining \[
 P_X({\mathbf n})=\sum_{\substack{a\in \bigcup_{v\in H} D_{Y_v}}}
 B_a({\mathbf n}).
\]
Then, by \cite[Thm 5.2]{Chow.class}, we have \[
P_X({\mathbf n})=\sum_{\substack{a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H\\a(H)\leqslant r}}
B_a({\mathbf n}).
\] 

Put $N\coloneqq\sum_{u\in H}n_u$. From Multivariate Chu--Vandermonde identity \cite[Thm.~5.2, p.~63]{Vignat_Moll_2015}, it follows that
\[
\sum_{\substack{a\in{\mathbb Z}_{\geqslant0}^H\\a(H)=s}} B_a({\mathbf n})  =\binom{N+s-1}{s}.
\] 
Summing over $0\leqslant s\leqslant r$ and using the hockey-stick identity gives
\[
    P_X({\mathbf n})=\sum_{s=0}^r\binom{N+s-1}{s} =\binom{N+r}{r},
\]
finishing the proof.
