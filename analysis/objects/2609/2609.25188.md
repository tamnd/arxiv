{Particle system---Local rule}{PS-CA---LR}
    \begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
        The local rule transports left-moving particles to the left, and right-moving particles to the right, which means consuming the particle at a cell, to give it to another. <!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let ${\ensuremath{\mathcal{X}}}$ be an infinite countable set of \emph{positions}.
Let $\pi$ be a finite set of \emph{ports} and $\Sigma$ be a finite set of \emph{states}.
Let us denote ${\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}} := \{\, t.x \mid (t,x) \in \mathbb{Z} \times {\ensuremath{\mathcal{X}}} \,\}$ and call its elements \emph{names}. We say that $t.x\in {\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}}$ is at position $x$. For any subsets $U \subseteq {\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}}$ and $X\subseteq \mathcal{X}$, let us define $\lfloor U\rfloor = \{\, x\in {\ensuremath{\mathcal{X}}} \mid \exists t\in \mathbb{Z}, t.x\in U \,\}$. 
Let us also denote $\overline{X} := \mathcal{X} \setminus X$, and                                                                                                    
$t'.u$ for $(t'+t).x \in {\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def : graphs}
A \emph{graph} $G$ is given by a tuple $(\mathrm{I}_G, \mathrm{B}_G, \mathrm{E}_G, \sigma_G)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathrm{I}_G \subseteq {\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}}$ is the set of \emph{internal vertices of $G$},
    \item $\mathrm{B}_G \subseteq {\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}} \setminus \mathrm{I}_G$ is the set of \emph{border vertices of $G$},
    \item $\mathrm{E}_G \subseteq (\mathrm{V}_G\!:\!\pi )^{2} \setminus ( \mathrm{B}_{G}\!:\!\pi )^2$ is the set of \emph{(oriented) edges}, and
    \item $\sigma_G :\mathrm{I}_G\rightarrow \Sigma$ maps each internal vertex to its state.
\end{itemize}
where we denote by $\mathrm{V}_G := \mathrm{I}_G\cup \mathrm{B}_G$ the set of all vertices of the graph, and by $(V\!:\!\pi):=\{v\!:\!p\mid v\in V,\,p\in\pi\}$ the set of ports of some set of vertices $V$. Moreover the tuple has to be such that:
\begin{description}[itemsep = 0.25ex, topsep = 0.5ex]
    \item[acyclicity\label{graph : acyclicity}]                                                                                                                                                                                                               
    the graph has no cycles.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       
                                                                  
    \item[border-attachment\label{graph : border-attachment}] $\forall u \in \mathrm{B}_G,\, \exists (v\!:\!a,v'\!:\!a') \in \mathrm{E}_G,\, u \in \{\,v,v'\,\}$,\\ i.e. border vertices are connected to at least one edge.
    \item[port-saturation\label{graph : port-saturation}] $\forall (u\!:\!a,v\!:\!b),(u'\!:\!a',v'\!:\!b') \in {\mathrm{E}_G},\, u\!:\!a\neq v'\!:\!b' \,\wedge\, (u\!:\!a=u'\!:\!a' \Leftrightarrow v\!:\!b=v'\!:\!b'$)\\
    i.e. ports are used only once---so as to distinguish each neighbour.
                                                                                                                           
    \item[unicity of positions\label{graph : unicity of positions}] $\forall t.x, t'.x' \in {\mathrm{V}_{G}},\, x = x' \Rightarrow t = t'$,\\
    i.e. positions appear only once.                                 
    \item[finite chains\label{graph : finite chains}] the graph only contains paths of finite length.
\end{description}
We denote by $\mathrm{Past}(G)\subseteq \mathrm{I}_G$ the vertices of $\mathrm{I}_G$ with no incoming edges,                                                                                         
and                                                                                                                                                                 
by $\mathcal{G}$ the set of all graphs.

{Border vertices are attached to internal vertices}{border vertices}

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def : induced graph}
    Given a subset $U \subseteq \mathcal{V}$, we define the induced subgraph $G_U$ as the graph whose internal vertices $\mathrm{I}_{G_U}$ are given by $\mathrm{I}_G\cap U$, and whose edges $\mathrm{E}_{G_U}$ are all those edges of $G$ which touch a vertex in $\mathrm{I}_{G_U}$. {Thus, its border vertices $\mathrm{B}_{G_U}$ are the vertices of $\mathrm{V}_G\setminus \mathrm{I}_{G_U}$ which lie at distance one of $\mathrm{I}_{G_U}$ in $G$, and the internal state of a vertex $v\in \mathrm{I}_{G_U}$ is given by $\sigma_{G_U}(v)=\sigma_G(v)$. }
    This defines a partial order relation: given any graph $H$ if there exists $U \subseteq \mathcal{V}$ such that $H=G_U$ we say that $H$ is a subgraph of $G$ and denote this relation by $H\sqsubseteq G$. For a subset $X\subseteq \mathcal{X}$, we write $G_X$ for the induced subgraph $G_{\{\, t.x \mid t\in \mathbb{Z},x\in X \,\}}$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
{Induction on internal vertices is trivial}{induction on internal vertices}
    Note how Rmk.~\ref{rem:border vertices} implies that, for any graph $G$, we have :
    \begin{equation}
        G_{X} = G \equiv \mathrm{I}_G \subseteq X
    \end{equation}
    In particular $G_{\mathrm{I}_G}=G$, so the relation $\sqsubseteq$ is reflexive.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Given $G,H\in {\ensuremath{\mathcal{G}}}$ we introduce the operation $G\sqcup H$ which is only defined if $G$ and $H$ can both be obtained as induced subgraphs of the same graph, in which case $G\sqcup H$ is the smallest graph (for the order $\sqsubseteq$) such that $G\sqsubseteq G\sqcup H$ and $H\sqsubseteq G\sqcup H$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
{Composition as union}{composition of graphs}
    Whenever $G\sqcup H$ is defined, its vertices and edges are obtained by taking the corresponding unions in $G$ and $H$:
    \begin{align*}
        \mathrm{I}_{G\sqcup H} &= \mathrm{I}_G\cup \mathrm{I}_H, &
        \mathrm{V}_{G\sqcup H} &= \mathrm{V}_G\cup \mathrm{V}_H, &
        \mathrm{E}_{G\sqcup H} &= \mathrm{E}_G\cup \mathrm{E}_H.
    \end{align*}
    Its internal-state map is inherited from its components: $\sigma_{G\sqcup H}(u)=\sigma_G(u)$ for $u\in \mathrm{I}_G$, and $\sigma_{G\sqcup H}(u)=\sigma_H(u)$ for $u\in \mathrm{I}_H$. 
    In particular, $\sigma_G$ and $\sigma_H$ necessarily agree on $\mathrm{I}_G\cap \mathrm{I}_H$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
{Characterization of compatible disjoint subgraphs}{compatible subgraphs}
    Consider two graphs $G$ and $H$ that share no internal vertices---i.e. $\mathrm{I}_G\cap \mathrm{I}_H=\emptyset$. $H\sqcup G$ is well defined if and only if :
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item positions are unique---i.e. $\forall t.x\in \mathrm{V}_G, \forall t'\!.x'\in \mathrm{V}_H,\ x= x'\implies t=t'$;
        \item all border edges are coherent, meaning that border edges of $G$ linked to internal vertices of $H$ must also exist in $H$---i.e. for all $e\in {\mathrm{E}_{G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{G}\!:\!\pi)^2}$ in between $u\in \mathrm{I}_G$ and $v\in \mathrm{B}_G\cap \mathrm{I}_H$, we have $e\in {\mathrm{E}_{H}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{H}\!:\!\pi)^2}$ and $u\in \mathrm{B}_H$---and symmetrically border edges of $H$ pointing towards internal vertices of $G$ must also exist in $G$.
        \item the pseudo graph $U$ defined by $\mathrm{V}_U = \mathrm{V}_H\cup \mathrm{V}_G$ and $\mathrm{E}_U = \mathrm{E}_H\cup \mathrm{E}_G$ has no cycle, and only contain finite chains.
        \item all common border vertices $v\in \mathrm{B}_H\cap \mathrm{B}_G$ have their ports not oversaturated---i.e. edges connected to $v$ in $\mathrm{E}_H$ use different ports than edges connected to $v$ in $\mathrm{E}_G$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose $\mathrm{I}_H\cap \mathrm{I}_G=\emptyset$ and $H\sqcup G$ well defined. 1/ Positions cannot appear multiple times, otherwise either $H\sqcup G$ would not be a graph because of \hyperref[graph : unicity of positions]{unicity of positions}, or they would contradict $\mathrm{I}_H\cap \mathrm{I}_G=\emptyset$. 2/ Now consider a border edge $e$ of $G$, in between $u\in \mathrm{I}_G$ and $v\in \mathrm{I}_H\cap \mathrm{B}_G$. Then $e\in \mathrm{E}_{H\sqcup G}$. Since $H= (H\sqcup G)_{U}$ and $v\in \mathrm{I}_H$, we necessarily also have $e\in \mathrm{E}_H$. Since $\mathrm{I}_H\cap \mathrm{I}_G = \emptyset$, this implies $u\in \mathrm{B}_H$, thus $e\in {\mathrm{E}_{H}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{H}\!:\!\pi)^2}$. 3/ and 4/ $H\sqcup G$ being larger than both $H$ and $G$, implies that it contains all their edges and vertices, thus $3/$ is true and \hyperref[graph : port-saturation]{port-saturation} of $H\sqcup G$ implies $4/$.\\
    Suppose the four conditions above. Then the pseudo graph $K$, with $\mathrm{I}_K = \mathrm{I}_H\cup \mathrm{I}_G$, $\sigma_K = \sigma_G$ on $\mathrm{I}_G$ and $\sigma_K = \sigma_H$ on $\mathrm{I}_H$, is a graph such that $K_{\mathrm{I}_H} = H$ and $K_{\mathrm{I}_G}=G$. Indeed \hyperref[graph : acyclicity]{acyclicity} and \hyperref[graph : finite chains]{finite chains} come directly from $3/$, \hyperref[graph : border-attachment]{border-attachment} comes from border-attachement of $G$ and $H$, \hyperref[graph : unicity of positions]{unicity of positions} comes from $1/$, and \hyperref[graph : port-saturation]{port-saturation} comes from $4/$ and $2/$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def : boundary}
    We denote $X^-$ the set $\{x\in \lfloor \mathrm{I}_G\rfloor  \;|\; \lfloor \mathrm{V}_{G_x}\rfloor \subseteq X\}$, and call these the interior positions of $X$ in $G$. 
    Its other vertices, namely $X\setminus X^-$, are referred to as the boundary of $X$ in $G$, see Fig.~\ref{fig : Induced subgraph}.

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:renaminginvariance}
Consider a function $R:{\ensuremath{\mathcal{X}}}\rightarrow {\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}}$ mapping each position $x$ to a name $R(x) = t.y$. It is a renaming whenever its action on positions $\lfloor R(.)\rfloor:x\mapsto \lfloor R(x)\rfloor$ is a bijection on ${\ensuremath{\mathcal{X}}}$.                   
                                                                
                                                        
It is extended to act on any $t.y\in {\ensuremath{\mathcal{V}}}$ as follows: $R(t.y)=t.R(y)$. 
It is extended to act upon graphs by renaming their nodes, written $R(G)$.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
{Renaming graphs}{renaming on graphs}
    Since the induced map on positions $\lfloor R(\cdot)\rfloor$ is bijective, renaming preserves all the graph conditions. Hence $G\in{\ensuremath{\mathcal{G}}}$ implies $R(G)\in{\ensuremath{\mathcal{G}}}$. It also preserves induced-subgraph inclusion:
    \[
        G\sqsubseteq H \implies R(G)\sqsubseteq R(H).
    \]
    Finally, whenever $G\sqcup H$ is defined, so is $R(G)\sqcup R(H)$, and
    \[
        R(G)\sqcup R(H)=R(G\sqcup H).
    \]
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def : closure}
Consider $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{G}$. This $\mathcal{S}$ is said to be closed under
\begin{description}[itemsep = 0.25ex, topsep = 0.5ex]
    \item[renaming\label{S : renaming}] if $G\in \mathcal{S}$ implies $R(G)\in \mathcal{S}$ where $R$ is a \hyperref[def:renaminginvariance]{renaming} (see Def.~\ref{def:renaminginvariance}).
    \item[inclusion\label{S : inclusion}] if $G\in \mathcal{S}$ implies $\forall H\sqsubseteq G,\ H\in \mathcal{S}$.
                                                                                                                                                                    
                                                                                                              
    \item[disjoint-union\label{S : disjoint-union}] if $G, H\in \mathcal{S}$ and $\mathrm{V}_G\cap \mathrm{V}_H=\varnothing$ implies $G\sqcup H\in \mathcal{S}$.
\end{description}

\label{def : locality}
    Given some forward neighbourhood scheme ${\mathcal{N}_{}}$, an \emph{${\mathcal{N}_{}}$-local operator} $A_{(-)}$ is a renaming-invariant operator from $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{S}$ to $\mathcal{S}$ such that $\forall x\in{\ensuremath{\mathcal{X}}}$,

    \end{cases}
    $$
    where the action of $A_x$ on $G_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}$ must be ``context-preserving''---i.e. it preserves the border $\mathrm{B}_{G}= \mathrm{B}_{A_xG}$ and its border edges $e\in {\mathrm{E}_{G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{G}\!:\!\pi)^2} \implies e \in \mathrm{E}_{A_x G}$.
<!-- end definition-10 -->
<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
\label{def : locality}
    Given some forward neighbourhood scheme ${\mathcal{N}_{}}$, an \emph{${\mathcal{N}_{}}$-local operator} $A_{(-)}$ is an operator from $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{S}$ to $\mathcal{S}$ such that $\forall x\in{\ensuremath{\mathcal{X}}}$ and $\forall G \in \mathcal{S}$, 
    $A_{\lfloor R(x)\rfloor}(R(G))=R(A_x G)$ for every renaming $R$, and                                                                                      
    $$A_x G := 
    \begin{cases}
    (A_x G_{\mathcal{N}_{x}}) \sqcup G_{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{x}}&\textrm{if $G\in \Gamma_{{x}}$}\\
    G&\textrm{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    $$
    where the action of $A_x$ on $G_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}$ must preserve the border---i.e. $\mathrm{B}_{G_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}}= \mathrm{B}_{A_xG_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}}$.
<!-- end definition-11 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
{$A_{(-)}$ is determined by its action on $\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}$}{local rule and disks}
    The definition above may seem inductive, as $A$ appears on both sides of the equation. However, by idempotence (Rmk.~\ref{rem:idempotence}) of the neighbourhood scheme, this induction stops after the first step. Thus the action of $A_x$ is fully determined by its effect on the set of disks $\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}$. Hence, we will always define local rules only on this set of graphs.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
{Only names of interior vertices are modified}{boundaries are stable}
    $A_x$ does not change the name of boundary vertices---i.e. for all $t.y\in \mathrm{I}_{G}$ we have $y\in {\mathcal{N}_{x}}\setminus {\mathcal{N}_{x}}^-$ implies $t.y\in \mathrm{I}_{A_x G}$. Indeed by definition such a boundary vertex $t.y$ is the extremity of a border edge $e$. Since by context-preservation $e\in \mathrm{E}_{A_xG}$, we also have $t.y\in \mathrm{I}_{A_x G}$.
<!-- end remark-7 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
{Position preservation}{easy name preservation}
                                                                                                               
                                                                                                                                                                    
                                
  Let $A_x:{\mathcal{S}}\to {\mathcal{S}}$ be a local rule, then $\lfloor \mathrm{I}_{A_x G}\rfloor \subseteq \lfloor \mathrm{I}_{G}\rfloor$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We proceed by contradiction. Consider $G\in  \Gamma_{{x}}$, and $y\in \lfloor \mathrm{I}_{A_x G}\rfloor\setminus\lfloor \mathrm{I}_{G}\rfloor$. 
    Construct a graph $G'\in \mathcal{S}$ such that $ \mathrm{I}_G\cup \{t.y\} \subseteq \mathrm{I}_{G'}$ and $G\sqsubseteq G'$. Such a graph can exist since $y\notin\lfloor \mathrm{V}_{G}\rfloor=\lfloor \mathrm{I}_G\rfloor\cup \lfloor \mathrm{B}_G\rfloor$ (by border preservation we have $y\notin \lfloor \mathrm{B}_G\rfloor$), but we must make sure that $G\in \mathcal{S}$. This can be done by first constructing $R(G)$ a copy of $G$ which contains only fresh names, including $t.y$. This $R(G)\in \mathcal{S}$ since $\mathcal{S}$ is closed under \hyperref[S : renaming]{renaming}. Second, since $\mathcal{S}$ is closed under \mbox{\hyperref[S : disjoint-union]{disjoint union}}, we can take $G'= G\sqcup R(G)$. Note that by \hyperref[neighbourhood : stability]{stability} of ${\mathcal{N}_{}}$ we have $G'\in \Gamma_{{x}}$. Moreover we have $G'_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}\sqsubseteq G$ since the neighbourhood cannot select some unconnected nodes in $R(G)$, so by extensivity, $G'_{\mathcal{N}_{x}}=G_{\mathcal{N}_{x}}$, from which it follows that $A_xG'=(A_x G_{\mathcal{N}_{x}}) \sqcup G'_{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{x}}$. But this union is undefined as $y\in \lfloor \mathrm{I}_{A_x G_{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}\rfloor$ and $t.y\in G'_{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{x}}$ violates the \hyperref[graph : unicity of positions]{unicity of positions} condition.  This proves $\lfloor \mathrm{I}_{A_xG}\rfloor \subseteq \lfloor \mathrm{I}_{G}\rfloor$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
{Boundary preservation}{border edges are preserved}
    For any graph $G$ such that $\exists H\in\mathcal{S},\ \mathrm{V}_G\subseteq \mathrm{I}_H,\ H_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}=G$, $A_x$ preserves boundary vertices and border edges---i.e.
    \begin{align*}
        \forall u\in \mathrm{I}_G,\lfloor u\rfloor\in{\mathcal{N}_{x}}\setminus {\mathcal{N}_{x}}^- \implies u\in \mathrm{I}_{A_xG} && {\mathrm{E}_{G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{G}\!:\!\pi)^2} = {\mathrm{E}_{A_x G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{A_x G}\!:\!\pi)^2}
    \end{align*}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix $H\in\mathcal{S}$ such that $\mathrm{V}_G\subseteq \mathrm{I}_H$ and $H_{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}=G$. Then $A_xH=(A_xG)\sqcup H_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{x}}}$ by locality of $A_x$.\\
    \textbf{$\forall u\in \mathrm{I}_G,\lfloor u\rfloor\in{\mathcal{N}_{x}}\setminus {\mathcal{N}_{x}}^- \implies u\in \mathrm{I}_{A_xG}$.} Consider $t.y\in \mathrm{I}_G$ such that $y\in{\mathcal{N}_{x}}\setminus {\mathcal{N}_{x}}^-$. There exists a border edge $e$ linking $t.y\in \mathrm{I}_G$ to some border vertex $t'.z\in \mathrm{B}_G\subseteq \mathrm{I}_H$. Since $t'.z\in \mathrm{I}_{H_{\overline{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}}}$, we have $e\in \mathrm{E}_{H_{\overline{{\mathcal{N}_{x}}}}}$. By locality of $A_x$ on $H$, we have $e\in \mathrm{E}_{A_xH}$, hence $t.y\in \mathrm{I}_{A_xG}$.\\
    \textbf{${\mathrm{E}_{G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{G}\!:\!\pi)^2} \subseteq {\mathrm{E}_{A_x G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{A_x G}\!:\!\pi)^2}$.} Consider $e\in {\mathrm{E}_{G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{G}\!:\!\pi)^2}$. <!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
{Simulation of synchronous cellular automata}{Simulation of synchronous cellular automata}
    Let $F$ be a radius-one-half cellular automaton with initial configuration $\sigma^0$, and let $G$ be its encoding defined above. For every $t\in\mathbb{N}$, $x\in\mathbb{Z}$, and $H\in\mathcal{M}(G)$:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item if $u=\lfloor t/2\rfloor.f_{2x-t}$ belongs to $H$ and has no incoming edge through its left port, then $\sigma_H^l(u)=\sigma_x^t$;
        \item if $v=\lfloor t/2\rfloor.f_{2(x-1)-t}$ belongs to $H$ and has no incoming edge through its right port, then $\sigma_H^r(v)=\sigma_x^t$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Moreover, for every $t$ and $x$, there exists a graph $H\in\mathcal{M}(G)$ in which the hypotheses of both items hold.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We proceed by induction on $t$. For $t=0$, both statements follow directly from the encoding of $\sigma^0$: the state $\sigma_x^0$ is stored in the left component of $0.f_{2x}$ and in the right component of $0.f_{2(x-1)}$.

    Suppose that both statements hold at time $t$. The update producing $\sigma_x^{t+1}$ is applied to the vertex
    \[
        w=\lfloor t/2\rfloor.f_{2(x-1)-t},
    \]
    whose left and right components contain, by the induction hypothesis, $\sigma_{x-1}^t$ and $\sigma_x^t$, respectively. By definition of the simulating rule, this update computes
    \[
        f(\sigma_{x-1}^t,\sigma_x^t)=\sigma_x^{t+1}
    \]
    and stores the result in both neighbouring vertices: in the left component of $\lfloor (t+1)/2\rfloor.f_{2x-(t+1)}$ and in the right component of $\lfloor (t+1)/2\rfloor.f_{2(x-1)-(t+1)}$. These components have no incoming edge on the corresponding side precisely once this information has been received. The claim follows for $t+1$.

    Finally, the dependency cone required to compute any fixed $\sigma_x^t$ is finite, thus there exists a finite valid sequence whose application produces both completed copies described above, proving the reachability statement.

<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
Consider a neighbourhood scheme ${\mathcal{N}_{}}$. An operator $A_{(-)}$ is said to be $\omega$-local iff, for all $G$ for which $\omega\in \Omega_{G}$ we have
    $$A_\omega G = A_\omega (G_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}) \sqcup G_{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}}.$$
    An operator is said to be local iff this holds for any $\omega\in \Omega_{G}$ such that $|\omega|=1$, in which case we call it a local rule. It is said to be $n$-local if this holds for any $\omega$ of length at most $n$, and $*$-local if it holds for any $\omega\in \Omega_{G}$. \\
    In a similar manner, given a local rule $A_{(-)}$, if the formula $G_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}} \sqsubseteq H\sqsubseteq G \implies \omega\in \Omega_{H}\land G_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}} = H_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}}$ holds for any $\omega$ of length at most $n$ such that $\omega$ is valid on $G$, we call it $n$-extensive. If it holds for any $\omega$ we call it $*$-extensive.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
{$n$-locality implies $(n+1)$-extensivity}{*-extensivity}                            
     Let $n\geq 0$. Consider a neighbourhood scheme ${\mathcal{N}_{}}$, an $n$-local rule $A_{(-)}$, a graph $G$ and $\omega \in \Omega_{G}$ of length at most $n+1$. Then $G_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}} \sqsubseteq H\sqsubseteq G \text{ implies $\omega\in \Omega_{H}$ and } G_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}} = H_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}}$.
     \todo[inline,color=cyan]{Checked.}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We prove $(n+1)$-extensivity by finite induction. The basis case is $1$-extensivity, which comes immediately from extensivity of ${\mathcal{N}_{}}$. Our induction hypothesis is $k$-extensivity, with $1<k<n+1$.
    
    Consider $y\omega\in \Omega_{G}$ with $|\omega|=k$. 
    Suppose that $G_{{\mathcal{N}_{y\omega}}} \sqsubseteq H\sqsubseteq G$. Notice the following decomposition :
    $$\mathcal{N}_{y\omega}(G) = \bigcup_{\alpha x\beta = y \omega}\mathcal{N}_{x}(A_\beta G) = \mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)\cup \bigcup_{\substack{\alpha x\beta = y \omega\\ |\alpha |>0}}\mathcal{N}_{x}(A_\beta G) = \mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)\cup \mathcal{N}_{\omega}(G)$$
    As long as $y\omega$ is valid on $H$, we can derive the same way $\mathcal{N}_{y\omega}(H) =\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega H)\cup \mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)$.
    
    Thus to prove $y\omega\in\Omega_{H}$ and $\mathcal{N}_{y\omega}(G) = \mathcal{N}_{y\omega}(H)$, we only have to prove, on the one hand $\omega\in\Omega_{H}$ and $\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(G) = \mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)$, and on the other hand $A_\omega H\in \Gamma_{{y}}$ and $\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G) = \mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega H)$.

    Since by monotony (Lem.~\ref{lem:monotony}) we have $G_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}}\sqsubseteq G_{{\mathcal{N}_{y\omega}}} \sqsubseteq H\sqsubseteq G$
    the $k$-extensivity induction hypothesis readily gives $\omega\in\Omega_{H}$ and $\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(G) = \mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                      

    Then we prove $\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G) = \mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega H)$. Notice that by \hyperref[neighbourhood : stability]{stability}\                                                             
    and \hyperref[neighbourhood : extensivity]{extensivity} of ${\mathcal{N}_{}}$ it is enough to prove $$(A_\omega G)_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}\sqsubseteq A_\omega H \sqsubseteq A_\omega G$$
    We start with the second inclusion :
    \begin{align*}
        A_\omega G &= A_\omega G_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(G)} \sqcup G_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}(G)}} \tag{$n$-locality}\\
        &= A_\omega H_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(G)} \sqcup G_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}(G)}} \tag{$G_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(G)} \sqsubseteq H$}\\
        &= A_\omega H_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)} \sqcup G_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}(H)}} \tag{$\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(G) = \mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)$}\\
        &\sqsupseteq A_\omega H_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)} \sqcup H_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}(H)}} \tag{$H\sqsubseteq G$}\\
        &= A_\omega H
    \end{align*}

    Finally we prove the last inclusion. First we notice that by monotony (Lem.~\ref{lem:monotony}) we have $G_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}\sqsubseteq G_{\mathcal{N}_{y\omega}(G)}\sqsubseteq H$. This notably implies that $(G_X)_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}\sqsubseteq (H_X)_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}$ for any set of positions $X$. Then we derive :
    \begin{align*}
        (A_\omega G)_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}&= (A_\omega H_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)} \sqcup G_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}(H)}})_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}\\
        &= (A_\omega H_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)})_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)} \sqcup (G_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}(H)}})_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}\\
        &\sqsubseteq (A_\omega H_{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}(H)})_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)} \sqcup (H_{{{\overline{\mathcal{N}}}_{\omega}(H)}})_{\mathcal{N}_{y}(A_\omega G)}\\
        &\sqsubseteq A_\omega H
    \end{align*}

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
{$*$-Position and Boundary preservation}{* easy name preservation}
  Let $A_{(-)}$ be a local rule, let $H\in\mathcal{S}$ and $\omega\in\Omega_{H}$, and set $G=H_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}}$. If $\mathrm{V}_G\subseteq \mathrm{I}_H$, then
  \begin{align*}
      \lfloor \mathrm{I}_{A_\omega G}\rfloor&\subseteq\lfloor \mathrm{I}_G\rfloor,\\
      \forall u\in \mathrm{I}_G,\ \lfloor u\rfloor\in{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}\setminus{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}^-&\implies u\in \mathrm{I}_{A_\omega G},\\
      {\mathrm{E}_{G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{G}\!:\!\pi)^2}&={\mathrm{E}_{A_\omega G}\setminus (\mathrm{I}_{A_\omega G}\!:\!\pi)^2}.
  \end{align*}
  Consequently, all new names are at positions of ${\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}^-$, i.e. $\lfloor \mathrm{I}_{A_\omega G}\setminus \mathrm{I}_G\rfloor\subseteq{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}^-$, where the interior ${\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}^-$ is computed in $H$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->

Renaming invariance is preserved by composition. Moreover, each step replaces a disk while preserving its border and leaving its context unchanged; hence, by induction on $|\omega|$, $A_\omega$ preserves the border of the disk $G_{{\mathcal{N}_{\omega}}}$. Together with $*$-locality (Th.~\ref{th: star ext and star loc}), these are precisely the properties used in the proofs of position preservation (Lem.~\ref{lem:easy name preservation}) and boundary preservation (Lem.~\ref{lem:border edges are preserved}). Applying the same arguments with $x$ replaced by $\omega$ gives the three stated properties. The final inclusion then follows as in Rem.~\ref{rem:boundaries are stable}.
