<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:maximal}
Following \cite[Definition 5.1.(iii)]{AI15}, a curve $X$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ attaining the bound~\eqref{thm:AP} is called a \emph{maximal curve} over $\mathbb{F}_q$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:AIcharacterization}
If $X$ is maximal, then so is $\widetilde{X}$. Moreover, every singular point $Q$ of $X$ satisfies the following properties: (a) $Q$ is rational; (b) $\delta_Q=1$; (c) $Q$ is unibranch and its only branch $P$ in $\widetilde X$ has degree 2.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:nonsplit}
A point $Q$ of a curve $X$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ is called a \emph{non-split singularity} if it satisfies (a), (b), and (c) above.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:nonsplitplane}
For plane curves, the typical example of a non-split singularity is an ordinary double point $Q$ whose tangent cone $ax^2+bxy+cy^2$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{F}_q[x,y]$, and hence splits into two distinct conjugate linear factors in $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}[x,y]$. Indeed, over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ the point $Q$ is an ordinary node, so $\delta_Q=1$ and there are two geometric branches; since these branches are exchanged by Frobenius (as their tangent directions are) they are glued into a single closed point of degree two on the normalization, so that $X$ is unibranch at $Q$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. The terminology is borrowed from the classical dichotomy between split and non-split ordinary double points, according to whether the two branches are individually defined over the ground field or not; cf.\ \cite{Bal}. Since a non-split singularity has $a_Q=0$, by~\eqref{lem:pointcount} it increases the number of rational points by one with respect to the normalization, hence if all singularities of $X$ are non-split one has
\[
\#X(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{X}(\mathbb{F}_q)+\#\operatorname{Sing} X .
\]
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:cubic}
Let $q$ be odd and consider the plane cubic over $\mathbb{F}_q$
\[
C_q\colon\ (x^2+y^2)z+xy^2=0\ \subset\ \mathbb{P}^2 .
\]
The cubic $C_q$ is irreducible, since $x^2+y^2$ and $xy^2$ are coprime, and has arithmetic genus $\pi=1$, by the genus-degree formula for plane curves. The Jacobian criterion gives $\operatorname{Sing} C_q=\{Q_0\}$, where $Q_0=(0:0:1)$ is an ordinary node, whence $\delta_{Q_0}=1$ and $g=0$. The tangent cone at $Q_0$ is defined by the polynomial $x^2+y^2$, which is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_q$ exactly when $-1$ is a non-square in $\mathbb{F}_q$, that is, exactly when $q\equiv 3\pmod 4$. So for $q\equiv 3\pmod 4$ the node is non-split by Remark~\ref{rem:nonsplitplane}, and therefore $C_q$ is maximal:
\[
\#C_q(\mathbb{F}_q)=(q+1)+1=q+1+g\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+\pi-g .
\]
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:localchar}
Let $Q$ be a non-split singularity of a curve $X$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ and let $P\in\widetilde{X}$ be the point lying over $Q$. Then, inside $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X},P}$ the following holds
\[
\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{X},P}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Note first that the rationality of $Q$, i.e., $\mathbb{F}_q(Q)=\mathbb{F}_q$, is equivalent to the equality
\[
\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{X,Q},
\]
from which it readily follows that $\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{X},P}$.
Likewise, it is clear that in the chain of $\mathbb{F}_q$-vector spaces 
\[ \mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{X},P}\subseteq \mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X},P} \]
the latter inclusion is proper, because the point $P$ has degree $2$ and therefore is not rational.
Thus the proof will be complete once we check that the $\mathbb{F}_q$-vector space $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X},P}/\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$ has dimension $1$.
But this holds true because the singularity degree $\delta_Q=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_Q\big/\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$ is equal to $1$ and $X$ is unibranch at $Q$, so that $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_Q=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X},P}$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:semigroup}
Let $Q\in X$ be a unibranch singular point with branch $P\in\widetilde{X}$, and suppose $\deg Q=\deg P=1$. Then $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_Q=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X},P}$ is a discrete valuation ring with valuation $v:=v_P$ and residue field $\mathbb{F}_q$. The \emph{value semigroup} of $Q$ is
\[
S(Q):=v\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\setminus\{0\}\bigr)\subseteq\mathbb{N} .
\]
It contains $0$, it is closed under addition, and its complement in $\mathbb{N}$ is finite, because $\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$ contains the conductor $(\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}:\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_Q)$, which is a nonzero ideal of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_Q$. The point $Q$ is called an \emph{ordinary cusp} when $S(Q)=\langle 2,3\rangle$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:gaps}
With the above notation, let $D_Q\subseteq\mathbb{F}_q(X)$ be a fractional ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$ with $D_Q\supseteq\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$, and set $S(D_Q):=v\bigl(D_Q\setminus\{0\}\bigr)$. Then
\[
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}D_Q\big/\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}=\#\bigl(S(D_Q)\setminus S(Q)\bigr).
\]
In particular $\delta_Q=\#\bigl(\mathbb{N}\setminus S(Q)\bigr)$ is the number of gaps of $S(Q)$ and, for $f\in\mathbb{F}_q(X)^{\times}$ with $n:=\max\bigl(0,-v(f)\bigr)$ one has
\[
n-\delta_Q\;\leq\;\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}+f\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\bigr)\big/\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\;\leq\;n+\delta_Q .
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Write $\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$, and for each $m\in\mathbb{Z}$ let $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m}:=\{h\in\mathbb{F}_q(X)\,:\,v(h)\geq m\}$. Each quotient $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m}/\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m+1}$ is one-dimensional over the residue field $\mathbb{F}_q$. Choose $m_0$ with $D_Q\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m_0}$ and $c\geq 0$ with $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq c}\subseteq\mathcal{O}$, and consider the filtration
\[
D_Q=F_{m_0}\supseteq F_{m_0+1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq F_c=\mathcal{O},
\qquad
F_m:=\mathcal{O}+\bigl(D_Q\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m}\bigr).
\]
Fix $m$. If $m\in S(D_Q)\setminus S(Q)$, pick $d\in D_Q$ with $v(d)=m$; its class generates $F_m/F_{m+1}$: indeed any $d'\in D_Q$ with $v(d')=m$ is congruent to a scalar multiple of $d$ modulo $D_Q\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m+1}$, since the residue field is $\mathbb{F}_q$, and the class of $d$ is nonzero, for an identity $d=o+d'$ with $o\in\mathcal{O}$ and $d'\in D_Q\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m+1}$ would force $v(o)=m\in S(Q)$. If instead $m\in S(Q)$, then every $d\in D_Q$ with $v(d)=m$ is congruent modulo $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq m+1}$ to an element of $\mathcal{O}$ with valuation $m$, so $F_m=F_{m+1}$; and the same conclusion holds trivially when $m\notin S(D_Q)$. Therefore $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}F_m/F_{m+1}$ equals $1$ if $m\in S(D_Q)\setminus S(Q)$ and $0$ otherwise, and the first assertion follows by summing over $m$. Taking $D_Q=\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_Q$, whose value set is $\mathbb{N}$, gives $\delta_Q=\#(\mathbb{N}\setminus S(Q))$. Finally, for $D_Q=\mathcal{O}+f\mathcal{O}$: on the one hand $D_Q\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\geq -n}$, so $S(D_Q)\setminus S(Q)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-n}\setminus S(Q)$, a set of cardinality $n+\delta_Q$; on the other hand $S(D_Q)\supseteq v(f)+S(Q)$, and already the negative part $\bigl(v(f)+S(Q)\bigr)\cap\mathbb{Z}_{<0}$, which has cardinality $\#\bigl(S(Q)\cap[0,n)\bigr)\geq n-\delta_Q$, is disjoint from $S(Q)$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:lambda}
There is a unique polynomial $\lambda(x)=\ell_1x+\ell_0$ of degree at most one with
\[
\lambda(\alpha)=\beta
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lambda(\alpha^q)=\beta^q ,
\]
whose coefficients $\ell_1,\ell_0$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_q$. Moreover, $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$ if and only if $\lambda$ is the constant polynomial $\beta$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since $\alpha\neq\alpha^q$, Lagrange interpolation over $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ gives existence and uniqueness, with
\[
\ell_1=\frac{\beta^q-\beta}{\alpha^q-\alpha},
\qquad
\ell_0=\beta-\ell_1\alpha .
\]
As $\alpha^{q^2}=\alpha$ and $\beta^{q^2}=\beta$, raising to the $q$-th power permutes the interpolation conditions, so $\ell_1^q=\ell_1$ and $\ell_0^q=\ell_0$, that is, $\ell_1,\ell_0\in\mathbb{F}_q$. Finally, $\ell_1=0$ if and only if $\beta^q=\beta$, in which case $\lambda=\ell_0=\beta$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:h}
The polynomial $\varphi$ divides $f-\lambda^2$ in $\mathbb{F}_q[x]$. The quotient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h}
h(x):=\frac{f(x)-\lambda(x)^2}{\varphi(x)}\in\mathbb{F}_q[x]
\end{equation}
has degree $d-2=2g-1$ and the same leading coefficient as $f$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We have $(f-\lambda^2)(\alpha)=f(\alpha)-\beta^2=0$, and $f-\lambda^2\in\mathbb{F}_q[x]$; since $\varphi$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, it divides $f-\lambda^2$. As $\deg\lambda^2\leq 2<d$, the leading term of $f-\lambda^2$ is that of $f$, and the degree statement follows.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:construction}
With the notation above, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The rational map $\rho\circ\iota$ extends to a morphism $\Phi\colon\widetilde{C}\to\mathbb{P}^3$, defined over $\mathbb{F}_q$ and given on the chart $(x,y)$ of $\widetilde{C}$ by
\[
\Phi(x,y)=\bigl(\varphi(x):x\varphi(x):y-\lambda(x):1\bigr),
\]
with
\[
\Phi(P_\infty)=
\begin{cases}
(0:1:0:0) & \text{if } g\leq 2,\\[2pt]
(0:0:1:0) & \text{if } g\geq 3.
\end{cases}
\]
\item[(b)] $C:=\Phi(\widetilde{C})$ is a complete, geometrically irreducible curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$, and $\Phi\colon\widetilde{C}\to C$ is its normalization.
\item[(c)] If $\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q$, then $\operatorname{Sing} C=\{Q\}\cup\Sigma_\infty$, where $Q=(0:0:0:1)$ is a non-split node whose branch is $P$ and $\Sigma_\infty$ is the set described in (e). Consequently the arithmetic genus of $C$ is $g+1+\delta_\infty$, where $\delta_\infty:=\sum_{Q'\in\Sigma_\infty}\delta_{Q'}$, and
\[
\#C(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+1 .
\]
\item[(d)] If $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, then $\operatorname{Sing} C=\{Q,Q^-\}\cup\Sigma_\infty$, where $Q=(0:0:0:1)$ and $Q^-=(0:0:-2\beta:1)$ are non-split nodes whose branches are $P$ and $P^-$, respectively, and $\Sigma_\infty$ is as in (e). Consequently the arithmetic genus of $C$ is $g+2+\delta_\infty$ and
\[
\#C(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+2 .
\]
\item[(e)] $\Sigma_\infty=\emptyset$ if $2\leq g\leq 3$. If $g=1$ or $g\geq 4$, then $\Sigma_\infty=\{Q_\infty\}$, where $Q_\infty:=\Phi(P_\infty)$ is a rational unibranch singular point of $C$ whose branch is $P_\infty$: for $g=1$ its value semigroup is $S(Q_\infty)=\langle 2,3\rangle$ --- an ordinary cusp, with $\delta_{Q_\infty}=1$ --- while for $g\geq 4$ its value semigroup contains $2g-5$, $2g-3$ and $2g+1$, and
\[
2g-6\;\leq\;\delta_{Q_\infty}\;\leq\;\#\bigl(\mathbb{N}\setminus\langle 2g-5,\,2g-3,\,2g+1\rangle\bigr).
\]
\item[(f)] If $2\leq g\leq 3$ and $\widetilde{C}$ is maximal over $\mathbb{F}_q$, then $C$ attains the Aubry--Perret bound \eqref{eq:AP} over $\mathbb{F}_q$. For $g=1$ and for $g\geq 4$ the bound is never attained: when $\widetilde{C}$ is maximal over $\mathbb{F}_q$,
\[
\#C(\mathbb{F}_q)=q+1+g\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+(\pi-g)-\delta_{Q_\infty} .
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We write $\pi$ for the arithmetic genus of $C$ and proceed in eight steps.

\emph{Step 1: the affine expression and two relations.}
Composing $\iota$ with the linear forms defining $\rho$ and dividing by $X_0$, we obtain, on the affine chart of $\widetilde{C}$,
\[
u=x^2-\sigma x+\eta=\varphi(x),
\qquad
w=x^3-\sigma x^2+\eta x=x\varphi(x),
\qquad
t=y-\lambda(x) ,
\]
which proves the displayed formula in (a); since the last coordinate equals $1$, the restriction of $\Phi$ to the affine chart of $\widetilde{C}$ is a morphism with image in $\{S\neq 0\}$. The three coordinate functions satisfy two relations. First, from $w=xu$ and $u=\varphi(x)$,
\[
w^2-\sigma uw+\eta u^2=u^2\bigl(x^2-\sigma x+\eta\bigr)=u^2\varphi(x)=u^3 .
\]
Second, substituting $x=w/u$ in the functional equation \eqref{eq:functional} and multiplying by $u^{d-3}$ (respectively by $u$ when $d=3$) and using $\lambda(w/u)u=\ell_1w+\ell_0u$ together with Lemma~\ref{lem:h}, we obtain the two relations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:I}
w^2-\sigma uw+\eta u^2=u^3
\tag{I}
\end{equation}
and, writing $H(W,U):=U^{\,d-2}h(W/U)\in\mathbb{F}_q[W,U]$ for the homogenization of $h$ in degree $d-2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:II}
u^{\,d-3}t^2+2\ell_0u^{\,d-3}t+2\ell_1wu^{\,d-4}t=H(w,u)
\tag{II}
\end{equation}
for $d\geq 5$, which for $g=1$, i.e.\ $d=3$ and $h(x)=h_1x+h_0$, reads $ut^2+2\ell_0ut+2\ell_1wt=h_1uw+h_0u^2$. These identities hold on $\widetilde{C}$, and hence on $C$.

\emph{Step 2: extension to $P_\infty$.}
Multiplying the homogeneous coordinate vector of $\Phi$ by $\xi^{\,g+1}$, where $\xi=1/x$, and using $x^i\xi^{\,g+1}=\xi^{\,g+1-i}$ together with $\gamma=y\,\xi^{\,g+1}$, we get
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tupleinfty}
\begin{gathered}
(U:W:T:S)=\bigl(\xi^{\,g-1}A(\xi):\xi^{\,g-2}A(\xi):\gamma-\ell_1\xi^{\,g}-\ell_0\xi^{\,g+1}:\xi^{\,g+1}\bigr),\\
A(\xi):=1-\sigma\xi+\eta\xi^2 ,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
so that $A(\xi)=\xi^2\varphi(x)$ and $A$ is a unit at $P_\infty$, with value $A(0)=1$. By \eqref{eq:valinfty} we have $v_{P_\infty}(\xi)=2$ and $v_{P_\infty}(\gamma)=1$.

\emph{Case $g=1$.} Multiplying \eqref{eq:tupleinfty} once more by $\xi$ we obtain
\[
(U:W:T:S)=\bigl(\xi A(\xi):A(\xi):\xi\gamma-\ell_1\xi^{2}-\ell_0\xi^{3}:\xi^{3}\bigr),
\]
in which all four coordinates lie in $\mathcal{O}_{P_\infty}$ and the second one is a unit. Hence $\Phi$ extends to a morphism at $P_\infty$, with $\Phi(P_\infty)=(0:1:0:0)$.

\emph{Case $g=2$.} Here \eqref{eq:tupleinfty} reads
\[
(U:W:T:S)=\bigl(\xi A(\xi):A(\xi):\gamma-\ell_1\xi^{2}-\ell_0\xi^{3}:\xi^{3}\bigr),
\]
all coordinates are already in $\mathcal{O}_{P_\infty}$, the second one is a unit, and again $\Phi(P_\infty)=(0:1:0:0)$.

\emph{Case $g\geq 3$.} Since $v_{P_\infty}(\gamma)=1$, the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P_\infty}$ is $\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P_\infty}=\gamma\,\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P_\infty}$, and $v_{P_\infty}(\xi)=2$ gives
\[
\xi=\gamma^2B
\qquad\text{with $B\in\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P_\infty}$ a unit.}
\]
Substituting in \eqref{eq:tupleinfty} we obtain
\begin{align*}
U&=\xi^{\,g-1}A(\xi)=\gamma^{2(g-1)}B^{\,g-1}A(\xi), &
T&=\gamma-\ell_1\gamma^{2g}B^{\,g}-\ell_0\gamma^{2(g+1)}B^{\,g+1},\\
W&=\xi^{\,g-2}A(\xi)=\gamma^{2(g-2)}B^{\,g-2}A(\xi), &
S&=\xi^{\,g+1}=\gamma^{2(g+1)}B^{\,g+1},
\end{align*}
whence
\[
\begin{gathered}
v_{P_\infty}(U)=2(g-1)>2,\qquad
v_{P_\infty}(W)=2(g-2)\geq 2,\\
v_{P_\infty}(T)=1,\qquad
v_{P_\infty}(S)=2(g+1)>2 .
\end{gathered}
\]
Therefore
\[
(U:W:T:S)=\bigl(\gamma^{-1}U:\gamma^{-1}W:\gamma^{-1}T:\gamma^{-1}S\bigr),
\]
where all four coordinates on the right-hand side lie in $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P_\infty}$ and the third one is a unit. Hence $\Phi$ extends to a morphism at $P_\infty$, with $\Phi(P_\infty)=(0:0:1:0)$.

Since the affine chart of $\widetilde{C}$ together with $P_\infty$ covers $\widetilde{C}$, statement (a) follows; note that $\Phi$ is defined over $\mathbb{F}_q$ because so are $\iota$ and $M$.

\emph{Step 3: the image curve.}
As $\widetilde{C}$ is complete and geometrically irreducible and $\Phi$ is a non-constant morphism defined over $\mathbb{F}_q$ (it is non-constant because $u/s=\varphi(x)$ is a non-constant function) its image $C=\Phi(\widetilde{C})$ is a complete, geometrically irreducible curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$.

\emph{Step 4: an isomorphism onto an open subset.}
Let $U_{\widetilde{C}}\subset\widetilde{C}$ be the open complement of the closed points $P$, $P^-$ and $P_\infty$; over $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ it is the complement of $\{P_1,P_2,P_1^-,P_2^-,P_\infty\}$. Let
\[
U_C:=C\cap\{S\neq 0\}\cap\{U\neq 0\} .
\]
The zeros of $u=\varphi(x)$ on the affine chart of $\widetilde{C}$ are exactly the points lying over $P$ and $P^-$, while $P_\infty$ is the only point of $\widetilde{C}$ mapped into $\{S=0\}$ by Step~2; hence $\Phi^{-1}(U_C)=U_{\widetilde{C}}$ and the restriction
\[
\psi_1\colon U_{\widetilde{C}}\longrightarrow U_C,
\qquad
(x,y)\longmapsto\bigl(\varphi(x),\,x\varphi(x),\,y-\lambda(x)\bigr),
\]
of $\Phi$ is a surjective morphism. In the opposite direction, define
\[
\psi_2\colon U_C\longrightarrow U_{\widetilde{C}},
\qquad
(u,w,t)\longmapsto\Bigl(\frac{w}{u},\;t+\lambda\Bigl(\frac{w}{u}\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
This is well defined: on $U_C$ we have $u\neq 0$, and dividing the relation $w^2-\sigma uw+\eta u^2=u^3$ of Step~1 by $u^2$ yields
\[
\varphi\Bigl(\frac{w}{u}\Bigr)=u ;
\]
setting $x:=w/u$ and dividing the second relation of Step~1 by $u^{d-3}$ (respectively by $u$ when $d=3$) we recover the functional equation $t^2+2\lambda(x)t=u\,h(x)=\varphi(x)h(x)$, whence, by \eqref{eq:functional},
\[
\bigl(t+\lambda(x)\bigr)^2=\varphi(x)h(x)+\lambda(x)^2=f(x) ,
\]
so that $\bigl(x,\,t+\lambda(x)\bigr)$ is an affine point of $\widetilde{C}$ with $\varphi(x)=u\neq 0$, i.e., a point of $U_{\widetilde{C}}$. Finally,
\[
\psi_2\circ\psi_1=\mathrm{id}_{U_{\widetilde{C}}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\psi_1\circ\psi_2=\mathrm{id}_{U_C},
\]
the first identity because $w/u=x$ and $t+\lambda(x)=y$, and the second because $\varphi(w/u)=u$ and $(w/u)\varphi(w/u)=(w/u)u=w$. Hence $\psi_1$ is an isomorphism, and in particular $\Phi$ is birational onto $C$.

\emph{Step 5: the differential of $\Phi$ on the affine part and, for $2\leq g\leq 3$, at $P_\infty$.}
We check that $d\Phi$ is nonzero at every affine point of $\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ and, when $2\leq g\leq 3$, also at $P_\infty$; the behaviour at $P_\infty$ for the remaining genera is analysed in Step~8. Since $q$ is odd, $x$ is a separating variable of $K$.

At an affine point with $y\neq 0$, the function $x$ is a local parameter and
\[
dU=\varphi'(x)\,dx=(2x-\sigma)\,dx,
\qquad
dW=\bigl(\varphi(x)+x\varphi'(x)\bigr)\,dx=(3x^2-2\sigma x+\eta)\,dx .
\]
If $dU$ vanishes at the point, then $x=\sigma/2$ there, and
\[
dW=\Bigl(\tfrac{3\sigma^2}{4}-\sigma^2+\eta\Bigr)dx=-\tfrac{\sigma^2-4\eta}{4}\,dx\neq 0 ,
\]
because $\sigma^2-4\eta\neq 0$ and $q$ is odd.

At an affine point with $y=0$ we have $f(x)=0$ and $f'(x)\neq 0$, as $f$ is squarefree; differentiating $y^2=f(x)$ gives $dx=\bigl(2y/f'(x)\bigr)dy$, which vanishes at the point, so $y$ is a local parameter and
\[
dT=dy-\ell_1\,dx=dy\neq 0 .
\]

At $P_\infty$ we use the expressions of Step~2, written in terms of the local parameter $\gamma$ by means of $\xi=\gamma^2B$. For $g=2$ we work in the chart $\{W\neq 0\}$: by Step~2,
\[
\frac{T}{W}=\frac{\gamma-\ell_1\gamma^{4}B^{2}-\ell_0\gamma^{6}B^{3}}{A(\xi)} ,
\]
so that
\[
\frac{d(T/W)}{d\gamma}\Big|_{P_\infty}=\frac{1}{A(0)}=1\neq 0 .
\]
For $g=3$ we work in the chart $\{T\neq 0\}$: writing $T=\gamma u_0$, where $u_0:=1-\ell_1\gamma^{2g-1}B^{g}-\ell_0\gamma^{2g+1}B^{g+1}$ is a unit at $P_\infty$ with value $1$, the expressions of Step~2 give
\[
\frac{W}{T}=\gamma^{\,2g-5}\,\frac{B^{\,g-2}A(\xi)}{u_0}=\gamma\,\frac{B\,A(\xi)}{u_0} ,
\qquad
\frac{d(W/T)}{d\gamma}\Big|_{P_\infty}=B(0)\neq 0 .
\]
For $g=1$ and for $g\geq 4$, on the other hand, all three affine coordinates of the relevant chart have valuation at least $2$ at $P_\infty$ --- respectively $2$, $3$ and $6$ in the chart $\{W\neq 0\}$ when $g=1$, and $2g-3$, $2g-5$ and $2g+1$ in the chart $\{T\neq 0\}$ when $g\geq 4$ --- so that $d\Phi$ vanishes at $P_\infty$; see Step~8.

\emph{Step 6: the fibres of $\Phi$ and the smooth locus of $C$.}
Since $f(\alpha)\neq 0$, the four points $P_1,P_2,P_1^-,P_2^-$ are not Weierstrass points of the model \eqref{eq:model}. At these points $u=\varphi(x)$ and $w=x\varphi(x)$ vanish, while
\[
\begin{gathered}
t(P_1)=\beta-\lambda(\alpha)=0,\qquad
t(P_2)=\beta^q-\lambda(\alpha^q)=0,\\
t(P_1^-)=-2\beta,\qquad
t(P_2^-)=-2\beta^q .
\end{gathered}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Phi(P_1)=\Phi(P_2)=(0:0:0:1)=:Q\in C(\mathbb{F}_q),\\
\Phi(P_1^-)=(0:0:-2\beta:1),
\qquad
\Phi(P_2^-)=(0:0:-2\beta^q:1) .
\end{gathered}
\]
If $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, the last two coincide with the rational point $Q^-:=(0:0:-2\beta:1)$, distinct from $Q$ because $\beta\neq 0$; if $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^2}\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$, they are distinct and conjugate, and form a closed point $\Phi(P^-)\in C$ of degree two. Moreover, $\Phi(P_\infty)$ is the unique point of $C$ lying on the hyperplane $\{S=0\}$. Combining this with Step~4, the only identifications made by $\Phi$ on geometric points are
\[
P_1,P_2\longmapsto Q,
\qquad\text{and, when }\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q,\qquad
P_1^-,P_2^-\longmapsto Q^- .
\]
In particular $\nu^{-1}(Q)=\{P\}$ and, when $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, $\nu^{-1}(Q^-)=\{P^-\}$, so $C$ is unibranch at these points.

Let $V\subseteq\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ be the complement of the identified points, namely
\[
\begin{gathered}
V=(\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q)\setminus\{P_1,P_2\}
\quad\text{if }\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q,\\
V=(\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q)\setminus\{P_1,P_2,P_1^-,P_2^-\}
\quad\text{if }\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q ,
\end{gathered}
\]
further removing the point $P_\infty$ from $V$ when $g=1$ or $g\geq 4$.
Being a non-constant morphism between complete curves, $\Phi$ is finite; by the above, its restriction to $V$ is one-to-one, and by Step~5 its differential is injective at every point of $V$. By the Lemma of Shafarevich \cite[Chapter~II, \S 5.4]{Sha13}, a finite morphism is an isomorphic embedding if and only if it is one-to-one and its differential is an isomorphic embedding of the tangent space at every point; hence $\Phi$ restricts to an isomorphism from $V$ onto its image, which is the complement in $C\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ of $\{Q\}$ (resp.\ of $\{Q,Q^-\}$ when $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$), with the point $Q_\infty:=\Phi(P_\infty)$ removed as well when $g=1$ or $g\geq 4$. Since $V$ is smooth, this image is smooth, and therefore $\operatorname{Sing} C\subseteq\{Q\}\cup\Sigma_\infty$ if $\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q$, and $\operatorname{Sing} C\subseteq\{Q,Q^-\}\cup\Sigma_\infty$ if $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, where $\Sigma_\infty\subseteq\{Q_\infty\}$ and $\Sigma_\infty=\emptyset$ when $2\leq g\leq 3$. Being finite and birational (Step~4), with $\widetilde{C}$ smooth, $\Phi\colon\widetilde{C}\to C$ is the normalization of $C$, which proves (b).

Moreover, $Q$ is an ordinary node. Indeed, its two geometric branches are the images of the smooth points $P_1$ and $P_2$, and since $d\Phi$ is injective at $P_1$ and $P_2$ (Step~5), each branch is smooth with a well-defined tangent direction. On the chart $(u,w,t)$ we have, at $P_1$, $du=\varphi'(\alpha)\,dx$ and, using $\varphi(\alpha)=0$, $dw=\bigl(\varphi(\alpha)+\alpha\varphi'(\alpha)\bigr)dx=\alpha\varphi'(\alpha)\,dx$, with $\varphi'(\alpha)=\alpha-\alpha^q\neq 0$; hence the tangent direction of the branch through $\Phi(P_1)$ satisfies $dw/du=\alpha$, and likewise $dw/du=\alpha^q$ at $P_2$. Since $\alpha\neq\alpha^q$, the two tangent directions are distinct --- and conjugate, so that the tangent cone of $Q$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_q$ --- and $Q$ is an ordinary node. When $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, the same computation at $P_1^-$ and $P_2^-$ shows that $Q^-$ is an ordinary node as well.

\emph{Step 7: the local rings at $Q$ and $Q^-$, the genus and the number of rational points.}
We first show that $Q$ is a non-split singularity. Every element of $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}$ can be written as $G(u,w,t)/G'(u,w,t)$ with $G,G'\in\mathbb{F}_q[u,w,t]$ and $G'(Q)\neq 0$; since $u,w,t$ vanish at $P$, its residue at $P$ equals $G(0,0,0)/G'(0,0,0)\in\mathbb{F}_q$, whence
\[
\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P} .
\]
As the residue field of $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}$ is $\mathbb{F}_q$ while that of $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}$ is $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$, the inclusion $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}$ is strict, so $Q$ is singular and $\delta_Q\geq 1$. (Note also that the place of $\mathbb{F}_q(x)$ defined by $\varphi$ is unramified in $K$, because $\varphi\nmid f$; hence $v_P(u)=v_P(\varphi(x))=1$, and likewise $v_{P^-}(u)=1$.) For the reverse estimate we argue directly, comparing $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}$ with $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}$ degree by degree. Write $\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}$ and $\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}$. Since $\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}$ and $1\in\mathcal{O}$, we have $\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{F}_q\oplus M$ as $\mathbb{F}_q$-vector spaces, where $M:=\mathcal{O}\cap\mathfrak{m}$, and the chain $\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deltasplit}
\delta_Q=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}\big/\bigl(\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}\bigr)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\mathfrak{m}/M=1+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\mathfrak{m}/M ,
\end{equation}
because $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}/(\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^2}/\mathbb{F}_q$ has dimension one. It therefore suffices to prove that $\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathcal{O}$.

We claim that $\mathfrak{m}^{\,n}\subseteq\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{m}^{\,n+1}$ for every $n\geq 1$. Indeed, $v_P(u)=1$, so $u$ is a uniformizer at $P$ and, for each $n\geq 1$, the map $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}\to\mathfrak{m}^{\,n}/\mathfrak{m}^{\,n+1}$, $c\mapsto c\,u^{\,n}$, is an $\mathbb{F}_q$-linear isomorphism, the residue field of $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}$ being $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$. Under it, the class of $u^{\,n}$ corresponds to $1$ and, since $x\equiv\alpha\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}$, the class of $x\,u^{\,n}$ corresponds to $\alpha$. As $\alpha\notin\mathbb{F}_q$ and $[\mathbb{F}_{q^2}:\mathbb{F}_q]=2$, the set $\{1,\alpha\}$ is an $\mathbb{F}_q$-basis of $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$, so the classes of $u^{\,n}$ and $x\,u^{\,n}$ span $\mathfrak{m}^{\,n}/\mathfrak{m}^{\,n+1}$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Both elements lie in $\mathcal{O}$: the first because $u\in\mathcal{O}$, the second because $x\,u^{\,n}=w\,u^{\,n-1}$ with $w\in\mathcal{O}$ and $n\geq 1$. This proves the claim; note that it is precisely here that the hypothesis $\alpha\notin\mathbb{F}_q$ is used, making $w$, and not only $u$, contribute a new direction in each degree.

Since $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}$ is a finitely generated $\mathcal{O}$-module with the same field of fractions, the conductor $(\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P})$ is a nonzero ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}$, hence equal to $\mathfrak{m}^{\,c}$ for some $c\geq 0$; in particular $\mathfrak{m}^{\,c}\subseteq\mathcal{O}$. Descending induction now gives $\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathcal{O}$: if $\mathfrak{m}^{\,n+1}\subseteq\mathcal{O}$ with $1\leq n\leq c-1$, then $\mathfrak{m}^{\,n}\subseteq\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{m}^{\,n+1}\subseteq\mathcal{O}$ by the claim. By \eqref{eq:deltasplit} we conclude that $\delta_Q=1$ and, moreover, that $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}$. Note that the whole codimension sits in degree zero, in the jump of residue fields from $\mathbb{F}_q$ to $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$: no degree $n\geq 1$ contributes, which is why the value semigroup of Definition~\ref{def:semigroup}, defined only for rational branches, is not the relevant invariant at a non-split point. Geometrically, over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ the point $Q$ is the ordinary node exhibited in Step~6. By Step~6, $C$ is unibranch at $Q$ with $\nu^{-1}(Q)=\{P\}$ and $\deg P=2$: the point $Q$ is a non-split singularity in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:nonsplit}, in accordance with Lemma~\ref{lem:localchar}. When $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, the same argument, applied with $P^-$ in place of $P$ --- recall that $v_{P^-}(u)=1$ and that the residue of $x$ at $P^-$ is again $\alpha$ --- shows that $Q^-$ is a non-split singularity with branch $P^-$ and $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q^-}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P^-}$.

\emph{Step 8: the point $Q_\infty$ and the conclusion.}
Set $Q_\infty:=\Phi(P_\infty)$. By Step~6, $P_\infty$ is the unique point of $\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ mapping to $Q_\infty$, so $C$ is unibranch at $Q_\infty$ with branch $P_\infty$, and $\deg Q_\infty=\deg P_\infty=1$. For $2\leq g\leq 3$ the point $Q_\infty$ is a smooth point of $C$ by Step~6, so $\Sigma_\infty=\emptyset$, as claimed in (e).

Assume now $g=1$ or $g\geq 4$; we compute the value semigroup of Definition~\ref{def:semigroup}. For $g=1$ the curve $C$ lies, near $Q_\infty=(0:1:0:0)$, in the chart $\{W\neq 0\}$, whose coordinate functions are, by Step~2,
\[
\frac{U}{W}=\xi,
\qquad
\frac{T}{W}=\frac{\xi\bigl(\gamma-\ell_1\xi-\ell_0\xi^{2}\bigr)}{A(\xi)},
\qquad
\frac{S}{W}=\frac{\xi^{3}}{A(\xi)} ,
\]
of valuations $2$, $3$ and $6$ at $P_\infty$. The local ring $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q_\infty}$ is the localization of the $\mathbb{F}_q$-algebra generated by these three functions at the maximal ideal of $Q_\infty$: its elements are the quotients $G/G'$, with $G,G'$ polynomials in the three coordinate functions and $G'$ not vanishing at $Q_\infty$. Since $G-G(Q_\infty)$ lies in the ideal generated by the coordinate functions, each of valuation at least $2$, while $G'$ is a unit at $P_\infty$, every element of $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q_\infty}$ is congruent to a constant modulo functions of valuation at least $2$; in particular every element of positive valuation has valuation at least $2$, so that $1\notin S(Q_\infty)$, while $2,3\in S(Q_\infty)$. Therefore $S(Q_\infty)=\langle 2,3\rangle$: the point $Q_\infty$ is an ordinary cusp, $\delta_{Q_\infty}=\#\bigl(\mathbb{N}\setminus\langle2,3\rangle\bigr)=1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:gaps}, and in fact $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q_\infty}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P_\infty}^{2}$.

For $g\geq 4$ we work in the chart $\{T\neq 0\}$: by Step~2 its coordinate functions $U/T$, $W/T$ and $S/T$ have valuations $2g-3$, $2g-5$ and $2g+1$ at $P_\infty$, so $S(Q_\infty)$ contains the numerical semigroup $\langle 2g-5,2g-3,2g+1\rangle$, while, arguing exactly as in the case $g=1$, every element of $\mathcal{O}_{C,Q_\infty}$ is congruent to a constant modulo functions of valuation at least $2g-5$, so that every element of positive valuation has valuation at least $2g-5$ and $1,\dots,2g-6\notin S(Q_\infty)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:gaps} then gives
\[
2g-6\;\leq\;\delta_{Q_\infty}\;\leq\;\#\bigl(\mathbb{N}\setminus\langle 2g-5,\,2g-3,\,2g+1\rangle\bigr),
\]
and in particular $Q_\infty$ is singular. This proves (e).

The arithmetic genus of $C$ is now computed by the genus formula $\pi=g+\sum_{Q'\in\operatorname{Sing} C}\delta_{Q'}$ (see \cite{AP96}), which gives the values stated in (c) and (d). Since $\nu^{-1}(Q)=\{P\}$ with $\deg P=2$, there is no rational branch over $Q$, i.e., $a_Q=0$, and likewise $a_{Q^-}=0$; over $Q_\infty$, when singular, the unique branch $P_\infty$ is rational, so $a_{Q_\infty}=1$ and $Q_\infty$ contributes no additional rational point. Equation~\eqref{lem:pointcount} then yields
\[
\#C(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+k ,
\]
where $k$ is the number of nodes, as stated in (c) and (d).

Finally, for (f): if $2\leq g\leq 3$, then $\pi-g=k$, and if $\widetilde{C}$ is maximal over $\mathbb{F}_q$, that is, $\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)=q+1+g\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor$, then
\[
\#C(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+(\pi-g)=q+1+g\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+(\pi-g) ,
\]
which is the Aubry--Perret bound \eqref{eq:AP} over $\mathbb{F}_q$. For $g=1$ and for $g\geq 4$ we have instead $\pi-g=k+\delta_{Q_\infty}$, whence
\[
\#C(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+k\;\leq\;q+1+g\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+(\pi-g)-\delta_{Q_\infty} ,
\]
with equality precisely when $\widetilde{C}$ is maximal over $\mathbb{F}_q$: the Aubry--Perret bound is missed by exactly $\delta_{Q_\infty}\geq 1$. This proves (f) and completes the proof.

\emph{Extension at $P_\infty$.} The pole orders of $(u,z,t)$ at $P_\infty$ are $4$, $5$ and $3$. Dividing the tuple $(u:z:t:1)$ by $z$ we obtain the coordinates $u/z$, $t/z$ and $1/z$ of the chart $\{W\neq 0\}$, of valuations $1$, $2$ and $5$ at $P_\infty$. Hence $\Phi_{\mathrm e}$ extends to $P_\infty$ with $\Phi_{\mathrm e}(P_\infty)=(0:1:0:0)$ and, since $u/z$ has valuation~$1$, the differential $d\Phi_{\mathrm e}$ does not vanish at $P_\infty$.

\emph{Identifications.} Let $R,R'$ be distinct points of $\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$, away from $P_\infty$, with $\Phi_{\mathrm e}(R)=\Phi_{\mathrm e}(R')$, say $R=(x_0,y_0)$ and $R'=(x_0',y_0')$. From $u(R)=u(R')$ we get $x_0'\in\{x_0,\sigma-x_0\}$; if $x_0'=x_0$, then $t(R)=t(R')$ forces $y_0'=y_0$, a contradiction; so $x_0'=\sigma-x_0\neq x_0$. Then $t(R')=t(R)$ gives $y_0'=y_0+\ell_1(\sigma-2x_0)$, and $z(R')=z(R)$ reduces, by $z=xt+\ell_1u$ and $t(R')=t(R)$, to $t(R)\,(x_0'-x_0)=0$, that is, $t(R)=0$. Hence $y_0=\lambda(x_0)$ and $y_0'=\lambda(x_0')$, and the functional equation gives $\varphi(x_0)h(x_0)=0=\varphi(x_0')h(x_0')$. If $\varphi(x_0)=0$, then $\{x_0,x_0'\}=\{\alpha,\alpha^q\}$ and $\{R,R'\}=\{P_1,P_2\}$. Otherwise $h(x_0)=h(\sigma-x_0)=0$ with $x_0\neq\sigma-x_0$, which is impossible since $h$ has degree one. Therefore $\Phi_{\mathrm e}$ identifies exactly $P_1$ with $P_2$ and is injective elsewhere; in particular $\Phi_{\mathrm e}(P_1)=\Phi_{\mathrm e}(P_2)=(0:0:0:1)=Q$, while $\Phi_{\mathrm e}(P_1^-)=(0:-2\alpha\beta:-2\beta:1)$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm e}(P_2^-)=(0:-2\alpha^q\beta^q:-2\beta^q:1)$ remain distinct --- also when $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, since $\alpha\neq\alpha^q$.

\emph{The differential at the affine points.} We have $du=\varphi'(x)\,dx$, $dt=dy-\ell_1\,dx$ and $dz=t\,dx+x\,dt+\ell_1\,du$. At a point with $y=0$ the function $y$ is a local parameter, $dx$ vanishes there and $dt=dy\neq 0$. At a point with $y\neq 0$ the function $x$ is a local parameter; if $du$ and $dt$ vanish simultaneously, then $x=\sigma/2$ and $dz=t\,dx$ there, and if also $dz=0$, then $t=0$, i.e., $y=\lambda(\sigma/2)$; from $t=0$ and $\varphi(\sigma/2)=-(\sigma^2-4\eta)/4\neq 0$ the functional equation gives $h(\sigma/2)=0$, whence
\[
0=\bigl(f-\lambda^2\bigr)'(\sigma/2)=(\varphi h)'(\sigma/2)=\varphi(\sigma/2)\,h_1\neq 0 ,
\]
a contradiction (the first equality is $dt=0$ rewritten as $f'(\sigma/2)=2y\ell_1$). Hence $d\Phi_{\mathrm e}$ vanishes nowhere on $\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$.

\emph{Conclusion.} As in Steps~6--7 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}, the Lemma of Shafarevich \cite[Chapter~II, \S5.4]{Sha13} applied to $V:=(\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q)\setminus\{P_1,P_2\}$ shows that $C_{\mathrm e}$ is smooth away from $Q$ and that $\Phi_{\mathrm e}$ is the normalization of $C_{\mathrm e}$, proving (a). At $Q$ the two branches are smooth with tangent directions determined by $(dz-\ell_1du)/dt=x$, evaluated at $P_1$ and $P_2$; here $dt(P_1)\neq 0$ because $dt(P_1)=0$ would give $(\varphi h)'(\alpha)=\varphi'(\alpha)h(\alpha)=0$, while $\varphi'(\alpha)=\alpha-\alpha^q\neq 0$ and $h(\alpha)\neq 0$, the root of $h$ being rational. The two directions are therefore distinct and conjugate, and $Q$ is an ordinary node. The argument of Step~7 now applies with one adaptation, since $w$ is not a coordinate of $C_{\mathrm e}$: as there, $\mathcal{O}_{C_{\mathrm e},Q}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}$, $v_P(u)=1$, and it suffices to show that the classes of elements of $\mathcal{O}_{C_{\mathrm e},Q}$ span $\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}^{\,n}/\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}^{\,n+1}$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ for every $n\geq 1$. Since $v_P(t)=1$ as well, the function $t/u$ is a unit at $P$; let $c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^2}^{\times}$ be its residue. Then
\[
t\,u^{\,n-1}\equiv c\,u^{\,n}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(z-\ell_1u)\,u^{\,n-1}=x\,t\,u^{\,n-1}\equiv\alpha c\,u^{\,n}
\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}^{\,n+1}},
\]
both elements lying in $\mathcal{O}_{C_{\mathrm e},Q}$, and $\{c,\alpha c\}$ is an $\mathbb{F}_q$-basis of $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ because $\alpha\notin\mathbb{F}_q$. The conductor argument of Step~7 then gives $\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{C_{\mathrm e},Q}$, whence $\mathcal{O}_{C_{\mathrm e},Q}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P}$ and $\delta_Q=1$: the point $Q$ is a non-split singularity, the arithmetic genus of $C_{\mathrm e}$ equals $g+1=2$, and $\#C_{\mathrm e}(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+1$; assertion (c) follows as in Step~8.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:cone}
Let $g\geq 4$ and keep the standing hypotheses on $P$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\Psi\colon\widetilde{C}\to C'$ is the normalization of $C'$, and it is an isomorphism away from the vertex $Q'$ of the cone;
\item[(b)] $\operatorname{Sing} C'=\{Q'\}$, and $Q'$ is a non-split node of $C'$ whose branch is the hyperelliptic conjugate $P^-$ of $P$, the closed point of degree two lying under $P_1^-=(\alpha,-\beta)$ and $P_2^-=(\alpha^q,-\beta^q)$; consequently the arithmetic genus of $C'$ is $g+1$ and $\#C'(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+1$;
\item[(c)] if $\widetilde{C}$ is maximal over $\mathbb{F}_q$, then $C'$ attains the Aubry--Perret bound \eqref{eq:AP} over $\mathbb{F}_q$ --- for every $g\geq 4$;
\item[(d)] $C'$ is a trigonal Gorenstein curve with Maroni invariant zero, whose $g^1_3$ is cut out by the rulings of the cone and has $Q'$ as its unique base point.
\end{enumerate}

We first locate the points of $\widetilde{C}$ lying over the vertex. The valuations of $Y=t/\varphi$ are computed from those of $t=y-\lambda(x)$ and $\varphi(x)=(x-\alpha)(x-\alpha^q)$: at $P_1$ and $P_2$ we have $v(t)\geq 1=v(\varphi)$, so $Y$ is regular there (the zero of $t$ is simple exactly when $h(\alpha)\neq 0$, but this is not needed); at $P_1^-$ we have $t(P_1^-)=-2\beta\neq 0$ while $v_{P_1^-}(\varphi)=1$, so $v_{P_1^-}(Y)=-1$, and likewise at $P_2^-$; at $P_\infty$, by \eqref{eq:valinfty}, $v_{P_\infty}(Y)=-(2g+1)+4=-(2g-3)$; and $Y$ is regular at every other point. In the coordinates of $\Psi$, a point $R\in\widetilde{C}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ maps to the vertex if and only if the last coordinate dominates all the others, that is, $v_R(Y)<\min\bigl(0,(g-1)v_R(x)\bigr)$. At $P_1^-$ and $P_2^-$ this reads $-1<0$: both map to $Q'$. At $P_\infty$ we have $v_{P_\infty}(x^{g-1})=-2(g-1)<-(2g-3)=v_{P_\infty}(Y)$, so the coordinate $x^{g-1}$ dominates and
\[
\Psi(P_\infty)=(0:\cdots:0:1:0) ,
\]
a point of the ruling of $S$ over the point at infinity of the rational normal curve, distinct from $Q'$; and no other point maps to $Q'$. Hence $\Psi^{-1}(Q')=\{P^-\}$, the two geometric branches of $C'$ at $Q'$ being $P_1^-$ and $P_2^-$ --- in accordance with \cite{RS02}, where the branches at the vertex correspond to the roots $\alpha,\alpha^q$ of $c_2=\varphi$.

By \cite[Theorem~3]{RS02}, the vertex is a node: under the correspondence of loc.\ cit., the blow-up of $C'\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ at $Q'$ is the hyperelliptic curve $z^2+c_1(x)z+c_0(x)c_2(x)=0$, which is precisely the functional equation \eqref{eq:functional}, hence $\widetilde{C}$, marked at the two points $P_1^-$ and $P_2^-$; these are smooth and not conjugate under the hyperelliptic involution, since the involution maps $P_1^-$ to $P_1\neq P_2^-$. Now the genus does the rest: $C'\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$ is a canonical Gorenstein curve of arithmetic genus $\pi=g+1$ by \cite[Theorem~1]{RS02}, its normalization $\widetilde{C}$ has genus $g$, and the node at the vertex already accounts for $\delta_{Q'}=1=\pi-g$; by the genus formula, $C'$ is smooth away from $Q'$, and $\Psi$, being finite and birational onto $C'$ with $\widetilde{C}$ smooth, is the normalization, an isomorphism away from $Q'$. This proves (a) and the first part of (b). Over $\mathbb{F}_q$, the point $Q'$ is rational and unibranch with branch $P^-$ of degree two and $\delta_{Q'}=1$: a non-split singularity in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:nonsplit}, so that $\mathcal{O}_{C',Q'}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P^-}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:localchar}. The genus formula gives arithmetic genus $g+1$, and~\eqref{lem:pointcount}, with $a_{Q'}=0$, gives $\#C'(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)+1$, completing (b); assertion (c) follows exactly as in Step~8 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}, since here $\pi-g=1$. Finally, (d) is \cite[Theorem~2]{RS02}, applicable because $\pi=g+1\geq 5$.

\label{lem:normalized}

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:degformula2}
\label{prop:degformula}
Let $X$ be a curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$ whose singularities are either non-split or rational unibranch with rational branch, as in Definition~\ref{def:semigroup}. For $Q\in\operatorname{Sing} X$ let $P\in\widetilde{X}$ be the branch of $X$ at $Q$. Then, for every $f\in\mathbb{F}_q(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$ one has
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:degformula}
\deg_X\mathcal{O}\langle 1,f\rangle
=\deg_{\widetilde{X}}\bigl(\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)\bigr)
+\sum_{Q\in\operatorname{Sing} X}\varepsilon_Q(f),
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$ is the pole divisor of $f$ on $\widetilde{X}$, 
\begin{equation*}
\varepsilon_Q(f)=
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } f\in\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X},P}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{X,Q},\\[2pt]
0 & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
if $Q$ is non-split, 
while
\[
\varepsilon_Q(f):=\deg_Q\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}+f\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\bigr)-\max\bigl(0,-v_P(f)\bigr),
\qquad
\bigl|\varepsilon_Q(f)\bigr|\leq\delta_Q,
\] 
if $Q$ is rational unibranch with rational branch. 
In particular $\deg_{\widetilde{X}}(\operatorname{div}_\infty(f))\leq\deg_X\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle$, in accordance with \cite[Theorem~2.2]{Car99}.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We compute $\deg_Q\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}+f\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\bigr)$ at every point $Q\in X$.

Suppose first that $Q$ is a smooth point, so that $\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$ is a discrete valuation ring with valuation $v_Q$ and residue field of degree $\deg Q$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. If $v_Q(f)\geq0$, then $\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}+f\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}=\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$ and the local degree is $0$. If $v_Q(f)=-n<0$, then $\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}+f\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}=f\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}=t^{-n}\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}$ for a local parameter $t$, and
\[
\deg_Q\bigl(f\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\bigr)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\bigl(t^{-n}\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\big/\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}\bigr)=n\deg Q .
\]
Summing these local degrees over the smooth points of $X$, we obtain the contribution of all poles of $f$ lying over the smooth locus to $\deg_{\widetilde{X}}(\operatorname{div}_\infty(f))$, each pole counted with its multiplicity and degree.

Now let $Q$ be a non-split singularity with branch $P$. Write $\mathfrak{m}_P:=\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{X},P}$, $\mathcal{O}_P:=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X},P}$, and  $\mathcal{O}_Q:=\mathcal{O}_{X,Q}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_P$, so that $\mathcal{O}_Q\subset\mathcal{O}_P$. Set $D_Q:=\mathcal{O}_Q+f\mathcal{O}_Q$. Recall that $\deg P=2$, so the residue field of $\mathcal{O}_P$ is $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$. Three cases occur.

If $f\in\mathcal{O}_Q$, then $D_Q=\mathcal{O}_Q$ and $\deg_QD_Q=0$; moreover $v_P(f)\geq0$, so $P$ does not occur in $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$. This matches \eqref{eq:degformula} with $\varepsilon_Q(f)=0$.

If $f\in\mathcal{O}_P\setminus\mathcal{O}_Q$, then $f +\mathfrak{m}_P \in\mathbb{F}_{q}(P)\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$. Since $f\mathfrak{m}_P\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_P\subseteq\mathcal{O}_Q$, we get $D_Q=\mathcal{O}_Q+\mathbb{F}_q f=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathbb{F}_qf+\mathfrak{m}_P=\mathcal{O}_P$, because $\{1+\mathfrak{m}_P,f+\mathfrak{m}_P\}$ is an $\mathbb{F}_q$-basis of $\mathbb{F}_{q}(P)=\mathcal{O}_P/\mathfrak{m}_P$. Hence
\[
\deg_QD_Q=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_P/\mathcal{O}_Q\bigr)=\delta_Q=1 ,
\]
while again $P$ does not occur in $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$: this is the case $\varepsilon_Q(f)=1$.

If $v_P(f)=-n<0$, then $f^{-1}\in\mathfrak{m}_P\subseteq\mathcal{O}_Q$, so $\mathcal{O}_Q=f\cdot f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Q\subseteq f\mathcal{O}_Q$ and $D_Q=f\mathcal{O}_Q$. We claim that $\mathcal{O}_Q\subseteq\mathcal{O}_P\subseteq f\mathcal{O}_Q\subseteq f\mathcal{O}_P$: indeed $f\mathcal{O}_Q=\mathbb{F}_qf+f\mathfrak{m}_P=\mathbb{F}_qf+\mathfrak{m}_P^{\,1-n}\supseteq\mathfrak{m}_P^{\,1-n}\supseteq\mathcal{O}_P$, since $1-n\leq0$. As $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}(\mathcal{O}_P/\mathcal{O}_Q)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}(f\mathcal{O}_P/f\mathcal{O}_Q)=1$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}(f\mathcal{O}_P/\mathcal{O}_P)=n\deg P=2n$, we conclude
\[
\deg_QD_Q=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\bigl(f\mathcal{O}_Q/\mathcal{O}_Q\bigr)
=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_q}\bigl(f\mathcal{O}_Q/\mathcal{O}_P\bigr)+1
=(2n-1)+1
=n\deg P ,
\]
which is exactly the contribution of $P$, with multiplicity $n$, to $\deg_{\widetilde{X}}(\operatorname{div}_\infty(f))$: again $\varepsilon_Q(f)=0$.

At a rational unibranch singular point $Q$, the pole of $f$ at the branch $P$ contributes $\max(0,-v_P(f))\cdot\deg P=\max(0,-v_P(f))$ to $\deg_{\widetilde{X}}(\operatorname{div}_\infty(f))$, so the discrepancy at $Q$ is precisely the stated $\varepsilon_Q(f)$, and the bound $|\varepsilon_Q(f)|\leq\delta_Q$ is given by Lemma~\ref{lem:gaps}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:linkage}
Let $Q'\in\{Q,Q^-\}$ be a non-split node of $C$, with branch $P'\in\widetilde{C}$, and let $f\in K\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$ satisfying $\varepsilon_{Q'}(f)=0$. Then
\[

\]

<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
By definition of $\varepsilon_{Q'}$, either $v_{P'}(f)<0$ or $f\in\mathcal{O}_{C,Q'}$. In the first case $P'\leq\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$, and we may take $E:=\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)-P'\geq0$; here the equivalence is an equality. In the second case, $c:=f\bmod\mathfrak{m}_{P'}$ lies in $\mathbb{F}_q$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity}, and $f-c$ vanishes at $P'$; since
\[
\deg\operatorname{div}_0(f-c)=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f-c)=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f),
\]
we may write $\operatorname{div}_0(f-c)=P'+E$ with $E\geq0$ of degree $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)-2$, and then
\[
\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=\operatorname{div}_\infty(f-c)\sim\operatorname{div}_0(f-c)=P'+E .
\qedhere
\]

\label{lem:cusp}
Let $g=1$ and let $Q_\infty$ be the cusp of the curve $C$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}, so that $S(Q_\infty)=\langle2,3\rangle$, $\delta_{Q_\infty}=1$ and $\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}_{C,Q_\infty}=\mathbb{F}_q+\mathfrak{m}_{\widetilde{C},P_\infty}^{2}$. \begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] if $f$ is regular at $P_\infty$, then $\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(f)=0$ if and only if $v_{P_\infty}\bigl(f-f(P_\infty)\bigr)\neq 1$;
\item[(ii)] $\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(x)=0$ and $\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(y)=0$.
\end{enumerate}

Write $v:=v_{P_\infty}$, $S:=\langle2,3\rangle$, $D:=\mathcal{O}+f\mathcal{O}$ and $n:=\max(0,-v(f))$, so that $\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(f)=\deg_{Q_\infty}D-n=\#\bigl(S(D)\setminus S\bigr)-n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:gaps}, and $|\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(f)|\leq\delta_{Q_\infty}=1$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:gonality}
The gonalities of the curves constructed in Section~\ref{sec:construction} are as given in Table~\ref{tab:gonality}. More precisely:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the curve $C_{\mathrm e}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptic} has $\operatorname{gon}(C_{\mathrm e})=2$, for either position of $\beta$;
\item[(b)] the curve $C$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:construction} with $\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q$ and $2\leq g\leq3$ has $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$; the same holds for the cone curves $C'$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}, for every $g\geq4$ and either position of $\beta$;
\item[(c)] the curve $C$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:construction} with $g=1$ has $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$, for either position of $\beta$: the cusp at infinity raises the gonality from $2$ to $3$ when $\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q$;
\item[(d)] the curve $C$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:construction} with $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$ and $g=3$ has $\operatorname{gon}(C)=4$.
\end{enumerate}
For $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$ and $g=2$ one has $\operatorname{gon}(C)\in\{3,4\}$, and both values occur; Theorem~\ref{thm:char} below characterizes when each of them does. For the curves in Theorem~\ref{thm:construction} with $g\geq4$ see Remark~\ref{rem:gong4}.

Throughout we use \eqref{eq:gonmin} and the degree formula \eqref{eq:degformula}. First observe that $\operatorname{gon}(C)\geq2$ in all cases: if $\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=1$ for some $f\notin\mathbb{F}_q$, then $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=1$, whence $[K:\mathbb{F}_q(f)]=1$ \cite[Theorem~1.4.11]{Sti09} and $K=\mathbb{F}_q(f)$ would be rational, contradicting $g\geq1$. The same argument shows that $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)\geq2$ for every $f\in K\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$.

(a) Here $\operatorname{Sing} C_{\mathrm e}=\{Q\}$, with branch $P$, and $\widetilde{C}$ is elliptic. Let $\kappa$ be a canonical divisor in $\widetilde{C}$. By the Riemann--Roch theorem on $\widetilde{C}$,
\[
\ell(P)=\deg P+1-g+\ell(\kappa-P)=2+0=2 ,
\]
since $\deg(\kappa-P)=-2<0$. Choose $f\in L(P)\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$: then $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$ is a nonzero divisor bounded by the prime divisor $P$, so $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=P$, of degree $2$; in particular $v_P(f)<0$, so $\varepsilon_Q(f)=0$ and
\[
\deg_{C_{\mathrm e}}\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=2 .
\]
Hence $\operatorname{gon}(C_{\mathrm e})\leq2$, and therefore $\operatorname{gon}(C_{\mathrm e})=2$.

(b) Here $\operatorname{Sing} C=\{Q\}$: this holds for the curve in Theorem~\ref{thm:construction} with $\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q$ and $2\leq g\leq3$, and for the cone curves in Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}, whose single node lies under $P^-$; the argument below is written for $C$ and the branch $P$, and applies verbatim to $C'$, with $P^-$ in place of $P$, since the residue of $x$ at $P^-$ is again $\alpha\notin\mathbb{F}_q$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:standardfunctions}(i), $x\in\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},P}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}$, so $\varepsilon_Q(x)=1$ and
\[
\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,x\rangle=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(x)+1=2+1=3 ,
\]
whence $\operatorname{gon}(C)\leq3$. Suppose $\operatorname{gon}(C)=2$, realized by $f$: then $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=2$ and $\varepsilon_Q(f)=0$, so $[K:\mathbb{F}_q(f)]=2$ and, by the uniqueness of the rational subfield of index two of $K$ for $g\geq2$ (Subsection~\ref{subsec:degtwo}), $\mathbb{F}_q(f)=\mathbb{F}_q(x)$: thus
\[
f=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d},\qquad ad-bc\neq0 .
\]
The functions $ax+b$ and $cx+d$ are then $\mathbb{F}_q$-linearly independent, so they span the same $\mathbb{F}_q$-subspace of $K$ as $1$ and $x$, and consequently generate the same $\mathcal{O}_{Q'}$-module at every point $Q'\in C$:
\[
(ax+b)\mathcal{O}_{Q'}+(cx+d)\mathcal{O}_{Q'}=\mathcal{O}_{Q'}+x\,\mathcal{O}_{Q'} .
\]
Dividing by $cx+d$ gives $\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=\operatorname{div}(cx+d)\cdot\mathcal{O}\langle1,x\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}\langle1,x\rangle$, and equivalent divisors have equal degrees:
\[
2=\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,x\rangle=3 ,
\]
a contradiction. Hence $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$.

(c) Here $g=1$, so $\operatorname{Sing} C$ contains the cusp $Q_\infty$ besides the nodes, and every local term in Proposition~\ref{prop:degformula2} is nonnegative: at the nodes by \eqref{eq:degformula}, at the cusp by Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp}. We use throughout that $\deg 3P_\infty=3>2g-2=0$, so that Riemann--Roch gives $\ell(3P_\infty)=3$ and $L(3P_\infty)=\mathbb{F}_q\oplus\mathbb{F}_qx\oplus\mathbb{F}_qy$, the three functions having pole orders $0$, $2$ and $3$ at $P_\infty$; in particular $\operatorname{div}_\infty(y)=3P_\infty$ and $\operatorname{div}_\infty(x)=2P_\infty$.

Assume first $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$, so that $\operatorname{Sing} C=\{Q,Q^-,Q_\infty\}$ and $\pi=4$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:standardfunctions}(ii), $y\in\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C,Q^-}$, so $\varepsilon_Q(y)=\varepsilon_{Q^-}(y)=0$, while $\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(y)=0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp}(ii); hence
\[
\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,y\rangle=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(y)=3 ,
\]
whence $\operatorname{gon}(C)\leq3$.

Suppose $\operatorname{gon}(C)=2$, realized by $f$: by the nonnegativity of the local terms, $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=2$ and $\varepsilon_Q(f)=\varepsilon_{Q^-}(f)=0$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:linkage} at each node, with $E$ of degree $0$, we obtain
\[
P\sim\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)\sim P^- .
\]
We claim this is impossible. Pass to the constant field extension $\widetilde{C}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ and apply the conorm $\operatorname{Con}=\operatorname{Con}_{K\mathbb{F}_{q^2}|K}$ of \cite[Lemma~5.1.9]{Sti09}: since $v_{P'}(h)=e(P'|\,\cdot\,)\,v(h)$ for $h\in K$, conorms of principal divisors are principal, so $P\sim P^-$ would imply
\[
P_1+P_2=\operatorname{Con}(P)\sim\operatorname{Con}(P^-)=P_1^-+P_2^- 
\]
on the elliptic curve $\widetilde{C}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$. Recall from Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda} that here $\lambda=\beta$ is constant, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:h}
\[
f(x)-\beta^2=\varphi(x)h(x),\qquad h(x)=h_1(x-x_3),\quad h_1\in\mathbb{F}_q^{\times},\ x_3\in\mathbb{F}_q ,
\]
$h$ having degree $2g-1=1$. Since $f-\beta^2=\varphi h$ is separable ($\varphi$ is irreducible, and its roots $\alpha,\alpha^q\notin\mathbb{F}_q$ differ from the rational root $x_3$ of $h$) and $\beta\neq0$, the functions $y-\beta$ and $y+\beta$ have simple zeros and
\[
\operatorname{div}(y-\beta)=P_1+P_2+P_3-3P_\infty,
\qquad
\operatorname{div}(y+\beta)=P_1^-+P_2^-+P_3^--3P_\infty ,
\]
where $P_3:=(x_3,\beta)$ and $P_3^-:=(x_3,-\beta)$; moreover $\operatorname{div}(x-x_3)=P_3+P_3^--2P_\infty$. Hence
\[
P_1+P_2\sim3P_\infty-P_3\sim P_\infty+P_3^-,
\qquad
P_1^-+P_2^-\sim P_\infty+P_3 ,
\]
and $P_1+P_2\sim P_1^-+P_2^-$ would give $P_3^-\sim P_3$. On an elliptic curve, each divisor class of degree one contains a unique rational point \cite[Proposition~6.1.6]{Sti09}, so $P_3^-=P_3$, that is, $\beta=-\beta$: impossible, since $q$ is odd and $\beta\neq0$. Hence $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$ when $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$.

Assume now $\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q$, so that $\operatorname{Sing} C=\{Q,Q_\infty\}$ and $\pi=3$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:standardfunctions}(i) and Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp}(ii),
\[
\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,x\rangle=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(x)+\varepsilon_Q(x)+\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(x)=2+1+0=3 ,
\]
whence $\operatorname{gon}(C)\leq3$. Suppose $\operatorname{gon}(C)=2$, realized by $f$: by nonnegativity again, $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=2$, $\varepsilon_Q(f)=0$ and $\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(f)=0$. We claim that this forces $P\sim2P_\infty$ on $\widetilde{C}$. Indeed, by Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp}, $\varepsilon_{Q_\infty}(f)=0$ excludes $v_{P_\infty}(f)=-1$; so either $v_{P_\infty}(f)=-2$, in which case $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=2P_\infty$, or $f$ is regular at $P_\infty$ and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp}(i), $v_{P_\infty}\bigl(f-f(P_\infty)\bigr)\geq2$, so that the zero divisor of $f-f(P_\infty)$, of degree $2$, equals $2P_\infty$ and again $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=\operatorname{div}_\infty\bigl(f-f(P_\infty)\bigr)\sim2P_\infty$. On the other hand, at the node: if $v_P(f)<0$, then $P\leq\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$, so $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=P$ by degrees; if $f$ is regular at $P$, then Lemma~\ref{lem:linkage} with $E$ of degree $0$ gives $P\sim\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$. In all cases $P\sim\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)\sim2P_\infty$, proving the claim. Passing to $\widetilde{C}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ by the conorm, as in the previous case, $P\sim2P_\infty$ yields $P_1+P_2\sim2P_\infty$. But $\operatorname{div}(x-\alpha)=P_1+P_1^--2P_\infty$ on $\widetilde{C}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$, so $2P_\infty-P_1\sim P_1^-$, and the uniqueness of the rational point in a divisor class of degree one on an elliptic curve \cite[Proposition~6.1.6]{Sti09} forces $P_2=P_1^-=(\alpha,-\beta)$, that is, $\alpha^q=\alpha$: impossible, since $\alpha\notin\mathbb{F}_q$. Hence $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$ also when $\beta\notin\mathbb{F}_q$.

(d) Assume $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$ and $2\leq g\leq3$, so that $\operatorname{Sing} C=\{Q,Q^-\}$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:standardfunctions}(iii), $u=\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C,Q^-}$, so $\varepsilon_Q(u)=\varepsilon_{Q^-}(u)=0$ and
\[
\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,u\rangle=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(u)=4 :
\]
$\operatorname{gon}(C)\leq4$. Next, $\operatorname{gon}(C)>2$: if $\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=2$, then $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=2$, and exactly as in case (b) the uniqueness of the rational subfield of index two gives $\mathbb{F}_q(f)=\mathbb{F}_q(x)$ and $\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}\langle1,x\rangle$, whose degree is now
\[
\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(x)+\varepsilon_Q(x)+\varepsilon_{Q^-}(x)=2+1+1=4\neq2 ,
\]
a contradiction.

Now let $g=3$ and suppose $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$, which is realized by some $f$:
\[
3=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)+\varepsilon_Q(f)+\varepsilon_{Q^-}(f),
\qquad
\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)\geq2 .
\]
The value $\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=2$ is impossible: it would force, as above, $\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}\langle1,x\rangle$ of degree $4\neq3$. Hence $[K:\mathbb{F}_q(f)]=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=3$. Apply Castelnuovo's inequality \cite[Theorem~3.11.3]{Sti09} to the subfields $F_1=\mathbb{F}_q(x)$ and $F_2=\mathbb{F}_q(f)$ of $K$: their compositum is $K$, since $[K:F_1]=2$ is prime and $f\notin F_1$ --- indeed $[K:\mathbb{F}_q(w)]=2\,[\mathbb{F}_q(x):\mathbb{F}_q(w)]$ is even for every $w\in\mathbb{F}_q(x)\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$, while $[K:\mathbb{F}_q(f)]=3$. With $n_1=2$, $g_1=0$, $n_2=3$, $g_2=0$, Castelnuovo's inequality gives
\[
g\leq n_1g_2+n_2g_1+(n_1-1)(n_2-1)=2 ,
\]
contradicting $g=3$. Hence $\operatorname{gon}(C)=4$ for $g=3$.

Finally, for $g=2$ the two bounds just proved give $\operatorname{gon}(C)\in\{3,4\}$; both values occur, by Examples~\ref{ex:F3trigonal} and~\ref{ex:F3tetragonal} below.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:char}
Assume $\beta\in\mathbb{F}_q$ and $g=2$. Then $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$ if and only if there exists a divisor $D\geq0$ of degree $3$ on $\widetilde{C}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] $D\sim P+\widetilde R$ and $D\sim P^-+\widehat R$ for some rational points $\widetilde R,\widehat R\in\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)$;
\item[(2)] $D\not\sim\kappa+R$ for every rational point $R\in\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)$.
\end{enumerate}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:gonality}, $\operatorname{gon}(C)\in\{3,4\}$, and $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$ holds precisely when $\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=3$ for some $f\in K\setminus\mathbb{F}_q$.

($\Leftarrow$) Let $D$ be as in the statement. We first show that $|D|$ is base-point-free. If $R$ has $\deg R\geq2$, then $0\leq\deg(D-R)\leq1\leq2g-2$, and Clifford's theorem \cite[Theorem~1.6.13]{Sti09} gives
\[
\ell(D-R)\leq1+\tfrac{1}{2}\deg(D-R)\leq\tfrac{3}{2},
\]
so $\ell(D-R)\leq1<2=\ell(D)$ and $R$ is not a base point. If $R\in\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)$ is a rational base point, then $\ell(D-R)=\ell(D)=2$ with $\deg(D-R)=2$, and Riemann--Roch gives $\ell(\kappa-D+R)=1$ with $\deg(\kappa-D+R)=0$; a divisor of degree zero with a nonzero section is principal, so $D\sim\kappa+R$, contradicting (2). Hence $|D|$ is base-point-free and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:bpf}, $D=\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$ for some $f$, with $L(D)=\mathbb{F}_q\oplus\mathbb{F}_qf$.

From $D\sim P+\widetilde R$ we get $P+\widetilde R=D+\operatorname{div}(z)$ for some $z\in L(D)\setminus\{0\}$; write $z=a+bf$ with $a,b\in\mathbb{F}_q$. If $b=0$, then $\operatorname{div}(z)=0$ and $\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=D=P+\widetilde R\geq P$, so $v_P(f)<0$ and $\varepsilon_Q(f)=0$. If $b\neq0$, then $\operatorname{div}_\infty(z)=\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=D$, whence $\operatorname{div}_0(z)=P+\widetilde R$ and $z=a+bf$ vanishes at $P$: thus $f\equiv-a/b\in\mathbb{F}_q$ modulo $\mathfrak{m}_P$ and $f\in\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity}, so again $\varepsilon_Q(f)=0$. The condition $D\sim P^-+\widehat R$ gives $\varepsilon_{Q^-}(f)=0$ in the same way. Therefore
\[
\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=3
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{gon}(C)=3 .
\]

($\Rightarrow$) Assume $\operatorname{gon}(C)=3$, realized by $f$. As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gonality}(d),
\[
\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=3,
\qquad
\varepsilon_Q(f)=\varepsilon_{Q^-}(f)=0 .
\]
Set $D:=\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)\geq0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:linkage} applied at $Q$ and at $Q^-$, $D\sim P+\widetilde R$ and $D\sim P^-+\widehat R$ with $\widetilde R,\widehat R$ effective of degree $1$, that is, rational points: condition (1) holds. For (2): if $D\sim\kappa+R$ for some $R\in\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_q)$, then $\ell(D-R)=\ell(\kappa)=2=\ell(D)$, so $R$ would be a base point of $|D|$; but $|D|$ is base-point-free by Lemma~\ref{lem:bpf}, since $D=\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)$.

<!-- example-6: body_md -->
\label{ex:F3trigonal}
Let $\widetilde{C}\colon y^2=F(x)$ be defined over $\mathbb{F}_3$ with $F(x)=x^5-x+1$. Here $F'(x)=2x^4+2=2(x^4+1)$, and so a common root of $F$ and $F'$ would satisfy $x^4=2$ and $0=F(x)=x\cdot x^4-x+1=x+1$, i.e.\ $x=2$, but $2^4=1\neq2$. Thus $F$ is squarefree and $\widetilde{C}$ is smooth of genus $2$. Moreover $\widetilde{C}$ is geometrically irreducible, since $\deg F=5$ is odd. As $F(0)=F(1)=F(2)=1$ is a square, the rational points of $\widetilde{C}$ are the six affine points $(i,\pm1)$, $i\in\{0,1,2\}$, together with $P_\infty$.

Take a root $\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_9$ of the irreducible polynomial $\varphi(x)=x^2+1\in\mathbb{F}_3[x]$. Then $\alpha^4=1$ and
\[
F(\alpha)=\alpha^5-\alpha+1=\alpha-\alpha+1=1 ,
\]
so $f(\alpha)\neq0$ and $\beta=1\in\mathbb{F}_3$: Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}(d) produces a curve $C$ with two non-split nodes and $\pi=4$, and so $\operatorname{gon}(C)\in\{3,4\}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:gonality}. Consider
\[
f:=\frac{y+1}{x^2-1}\in K .
\]
At infinity one has $v_{P_\infty}(f)=-5+4=-1$. At the affine rational points we have
\[
(y-1)(y+1)=y^2-1=F(x)-1=x^5-x=x(x-1)(x+1)(x^2+1),
\]
and since none of the six points is a Weierstrass point, at each point $(i,\pm1)$ exactly one of $y\mp1$ has a simple zero: $v(y-1)=1$ at $(i,1)$ and $v(y+1)=1$ at $(i,-1)$, while $v(x^2-1)=1$ at the four points with $i\in\{1,2\}$. Hence $f$ is regular at $(1,-1)$ and $(2,-1)$, it has simple poles at $(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$, and it is regular at every other affine point of $\widetilde{C}$ (at the points above $x^2+1$, namely $P$ and $P^-$, the denominator satisfies $\alpha^2-1=-2\neq0$). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=P_\infty+(1,1)+(2,1),
\qquad
\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty(f)=3 .
\]
At the nodes, the values of $f$ along the branches are
\[
f(P_1)=\frac{1+1}{\alpha^2-1}=\frac{2}{-2}=-1\in\mathbb{F}_3 ,
\qquad
f(P_1^-)=\frac{-1+1}{\alpha^2-1}=0\in\mathbb{F}_3 ,
\]
so $f\in\mathcal{O}_{C,Q}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C,Q^-}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity} and $\varepsilon_Q(f)=\varepsilon_{Q^-}(f)=0$. Now the degree formula \eqref{eq:degformula} yields $\deg_C\mathcal{O}\langle1,f\rangle=3$, whence
$
\operatorname{gon}(C)=3,
$
i.e., the curve $C$ is trigonal.

\label{ex:F3tetragonal}
Let $\widetilde{C}\colon y^2=F(x)$ be given over $\mathbb{F}_3$ with $F(x)=x^5-x^3-1$. Here $F'(x)=5x^4-3x^2=2x^4$ and $F(0)=-1\neq0$, so $\gcd(F,F')=1$. Thus $\widetilde{C}$ is smooth of genus $2$ and geometrically irreducible, with a single rational point at infinity. There are no affine rational points, since $F(0)=F(1)=F(2)=2$ is not a square in $\mathbb{F}_3$. Thus
\[
\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_3)=\{P_\infty\}.
\]
Take a root $\alpha$ of the irreducible polynomial $x^2-x-1\in\mathbb{F}_3[x]$, so that $\alpha^2=\alpha+1$, $\alpha^3=2\alpha+1$, $\alpha^4=2$, $\alpha^5=2\alpha$, and
\[
F(\alpha)=2\alpha-(2\alpha+1)-1=-2=1.
\]
Hence $f(\alpha)\neq0$ and $\beta=1\in\mathbb{F}_3$. Now Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}(d) applies, and so $C$ has two non-split nodes, $\pi=4$, and $\operatorname{gon}(C)\in\{3,4\}$.

We show that no divisor $D$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:char} exists, so that $\operatorname{gon}(C)=4$. Since $\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_3)=\{P_\infty\}$, condition (1) in the theorem implies $\widetilde R=\widehat R=P_\infty$, hence
\[
D\sim P+P_\infty\sim P^-+P_\infty
\qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad
P\sim P^- .
\]
We claim the latter is impossible. Note that $P\neq P^-$, since $\beta\neq0$. If $P\sim P^-$, pick $z$ with $\operatorname{div}(z)=P^--P$: then $z\in L(P)\setminus\mathbb{F}_3$ and $\ell(P)\geq2$. Riemann--Roch gives $\ell(P)=2+1-2+\ell(\kappa-P)$, so $\ell(\kappa-P)\geq1$ with $\deg(\kappa-P)=0$, forcing $\kappa-P\sim0$, i.e.
\[
P\sim\kappa .
\]
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
On the other hand, $\deg 2P_\infty=2=2g-2$ and $\ell(2P_\infty)=2=g$, as $L(2P_\infty)=\mathbb{F}_3\oplus\mathbb{F}_3x$, which by Riemann--Roch means that the divisor $2P_\infty$ is canonical. Thus $P\sim2P_\infty$, and there is a function $z=a+bx\in L(2P_\infty)$, $b\neq0$, with $\operatorname{div}(z)=P-2P_\infty$. Writing $z=b(x-c)$ with $c\in\mathbb{F}_3$, we get $\operatorname{div}_0(x-c)=P$, and so $x\equiv c\in\mathbb{F}_3$ modulo $\mathfrak{m}_P$, contradicting $x\bmod\mathfrak{m}_P=\alpha\notin\mathbb{F}_3$. This shows that $\operatorname{gon}(C)=4$, i.e., the curve $C$ is tetragonal.

\label{rem:tetragonalcriterion}

Thus every such pair $(\widetilde{C},P)$ produces a tetragonal curve $C$. This provides many further examples.
