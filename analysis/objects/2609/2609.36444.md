<!-- introtheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{intro:suspension}
The functor $\Omega_T$ on $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$ has a left adjoint
\[
 \Sigma_T=\underline G\,\Sigma_B\,\underline F,
 \qquad \Sigma_B=\operatorname{Tr}_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}\Omega_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}\operatorname{Tr}_B.
\]
If $a_X:X\to T^X$ is a left $\operatorname{add} T$-approximation, then $\Sigma_TX$ is represented by $\operatorname{Coker}a_X$. For $M\in\mathcal C$, $d\ge0$ and $k\ge1$, one has $k$-$\operatorname{dell}_T(M)\le d$ if and only if the iterated unit
\[
 \eta_{d+k,\Omega_T^dM}:\Omega_T^dM\longrightarrow
 \Omega_T^{d+k}\Sigma_T^{d+k}\Omega_T^dM
\]
is split monic in $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$.
<!-- end introtheorem-1 -->
<!-- introtheorem-2: body_md -->
\label{intro:gaps}
For every pair of integers $k,d\ge1$, there exist a finite-dimensional algebra $A$, a classical tilting $A$-module $T$ with $\operatorname{pd}_AT=1$, and $M\in\operatorname{Fac} T$ such that, for $B=\operatorname{End}_A(T)$ and $F=\operatorname{Hom}_A(T,-)$,
\[
 k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_BF(M)=0<d=k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_T(M)
 =(k+1)\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_BF(M).
\]
Every simple module on either side of either algebra has infinite projective dimension, whereas
\[
 \operatorname{findim} A=\operatorname{findim} A^{\mathrm{op}}=d+k-1,
 \qquad \operatorname{findim} B=\operatorname{findim} B^{\mathrm{op}}=d+k.
\]
<!-- end introtheorem-2 -->
<!-- introtheorem-3: body_md -->
\label{intro:cyclic}
Let $N(c)$ be a cyclic Nakayama algebra with at least two simple modules, let $R$ be a finite-dimensional local $K$-algebra with residue field $K$, and let $\boldsymbol\sigma$ be a tuple of coefficient automorphisms. Put $\Lambda=\Lambda_R(c;\boldsymbol\sigma)$. For $\Gamma\in\{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\}$, one has
\[
 \operatorname{findim}\Gamma=\operatorname{Findim}\Gamma=\operatorname{ddell}\Gamma=\operatorname{dell}\Gamma
 =\operatorname{findim} N(c)<\infty.
\]
<!-- end introtheorem-3 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:projectives}
The category $\mathcal C$ has enough projectives, with $\operatorname{proj}\mathcal C=\operatorname{add} T$. For every $X\in\mathcal C$, a right $\operatorname{add} T$-approximation gives a conflation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-cover}
 0\longrightarrow\Omega_T X\longrightarrow T_X
 \xrightarrow{p_X}X\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
It remains exact after applying $F$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Choose a right $\operatorname{add} T$-approximation $p_X:T_X\to X$. It is surjective because an epimorphism from a finite sum of copies of $T$ to $X$ factors through it. Let $L=\ker p_X$. The approximation property and $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\overline A}(T,T_X)=0$ give $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\overline A}(T,L)=0$. Thus $L\in\mathcal C$ by \eqref{eq:tilting}, and the sequence is $F$-exact. Objects of $\operatorname{add} T$ are projective in $\mathcal C$. Conversely, if $X$ is projective in $\mathcal C$, this sequence splits, so $X\in\operatorname{add} T$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:transport}
The functor $F$ induces an exact equivalence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Y}
 F:\mathcal C\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal Y,
 \qquad \mathcal Y=\{U\in\operatorname{mod} B\mid\operatorname{Tor}_1^B(U,T)=0\}=\operatorname{Sub}\mathbb D T,
\end{equation}
with quasi-inverse the restriction of $G$. On stable categories one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable-transport}
 \underline G\dashv\underline F,
 \qquad \underline F\Omega_T\cong\Omega_B\underline F,
\end{equation}
and $\underline F$ is fully faithful.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The equivalence \eqref{eq:Y} is the Brenner--Butler tilting theorem applied over $\overline A$ \cite{BrennerButler}. It is exact on the indicated subcategories. Notice that $\mathcal Y$ contains the projective $B$-modules and is closed under submodules and extensions.

For every $U\in\operatorname{mod} B$, a finite free presentation shows that $G(U)$ is generated by $T$, so $G(U)\in\mathcal C$. Also, $F(\operatorname{add} T)=\operatorname{proj} B$ and $G(\operatorname{proj} B)\subseteq\operatorname{add} T$. Hence both functors descend to the stable categories. Under the tensor--Hom adjunction
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_A(GU,X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_B(U,FX),
\]
a factorization through $T'\in\operatorname{add} T$ corresponds to one through the projective $F(T')$. Conversely, a factorization through a projective $P$ gives one through $G(P)\in\operatorname{add} T$. This proves the stable adjunction.

Full faithfulness of $F$ identifies factorizations between objects of its image through projectives with factorizations through $\operatorname{add} T$. Thus $\underline F$ is fully faithful. Finally, applying $F$ to \eqref{eq:relative-cover} gives a projective presentation of $F(X)$. The comparison maps for projective presentations yield the natural stable isomorphism in \eqref{eq:stable-transport}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:adjunction}
The endofunctor $\Sigma_T=\underline G\Sigma_B\underline F$ of $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$ is left adjoint to $\Omega_T$. If $a_X:X\to T^X$ is a left $\operatorname{add} T$-approximation, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cokernel-model}
 \Sigma_T X\cong\operatorname{Coker}a_X\quad\text{in }\underline{\mathcal C}_T.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\cref{lem:transport} and \eqref{eq:ordinary-adjunction} give natural isomorphisms
\begin{align*}
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_T(\underline G\Sigma_B\underline F X,Y)
 &\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_B(\Sigma_B\underline F X,\underline F Y)\\
 &\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_B(\underline F X,\Omega_B\underline F Y)\\
 &\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_T(X,\Omega_T Y).
\end{align*}
This proves the adjunction.

For the concrete description, recall that the ordinary suspension of a module $U$ is represented by the cokernel of a left projective approximation of $U$; this follows by dualizing a projective cover of $\operatorname{Hom}_B(U,B)$ in the construction of \eqref{eq:ordinary-adjunction}. Since $F$ is fully faithful on $\mathcal C$ and identifies $\operatorname{add} T$ with $\operatorname{proj} B$, the map $F(a_X)$ is a left projective approximation of $F(X)$. Tensor is right exact, and the counits $GF(X)\cong X$ and $GF(T^X)\cong T^X$ identify $G(F(a_X))$ with $a_X$. Therefore $G(\operatorname{Coker}F(a_X))\cong\operatorname{Coker}a_X$, which proves \eqref{eq:cokernel-model}. Its cokernel belongs to $\operatorname{Fac} T$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Stable quotients by approximation subcategories occur in the one-sided triangulated framework of Beligiannis--Marmaridis \cite{BeligiannisMarmaridis}. \cref{thm:adjunction} supplies the endomorphism-algebra and cokernel models needed here. It does not assert that $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$ is triangulated or that $\Omega_T$ is an equivalence.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:quotient}
Let $e$ be an idempotent with $A/AeA\ne0$ and take $T=A/AeA$, or its basic representative. This is a support $\tau$-tilting module. Then $\mathcal C=\operatorname{mod}(A/AeA)$ and the relative constructions recover ordinary syzygy and suspension over $A/AeA$. In particular, taking $T=A$ recovers the classical setting.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:delooping}
For $M\in\mathcal C$ and $k\ge1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-delooping}
 k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_T(M)=\inf\left\{d\ge0\ \middle|\
 \begin{array}{l}
 \Omega_T^dM\text{ is a retract of }\Omega_T^{d+k}N\text{ in }\underline{\mathcal C}_T\\
 \text{for some }N\in\mathcal C
 \end{array}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The infimum of the empty set is $\infty$. We write $\operatorname{dell}_T(M)$ when $k=1$. Ordinary $k$-$\operatorname{dell}_B$ uses the same definition in $\underline{\operatorname{mod}}B$, with witnesses in $\operatorname{mod} B$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:unit}
Let $M\in\mathcal C$, $d\ge0$, $k\ge1$ and $X=\Omega_T^dM$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is a retract of $\Omega_T^{d+k}N$ for some $N\in\mathcal C$;
\item $X$ is a retract of $\Omega_T^{d+k}\Sigma_T^{d+k}X$;
\item $\eta_{d+k,X}:X\to\Omega_T^{d+k}\Sigma_T^{d+k}X$ is split monic.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The implications (3)$\Rightarrow$(2)$\Rightarrow$(1) follow from the definitions. To prove (1)$\Rightarrow$(3), choose $u:X\to\Omega_T^{d+k}N$ and $v:\Omega_T^{d+k}N\to X$ with $vu=1_X$. Let $\widetilde u:\Sigma_T^{d+k}X\to N$ be the morphism adjoint to $u$. Then
\[
 u=\Omega_T^{d+k}(\widetilde u)\eta_{d+k,X}.
\]
Consequently $v\Omega_T^{d+k}(\widetilde u)$ is a left inverse of the unit.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:comparison}
For $M\in\mathcal C$ and $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison}
 k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_BF(M)\le k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_T(M)
 \le(k+1)\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_BF(M).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Applying $\underline F$ to a relative delooping retraction gives the first inequality by \eqref{eq:stable-transport}.

For the second, suppose that $(k+1)$-$\operatorname{dell}_BF(M)=d<\infty$. Choose a stable retraction of $\Omega_B^dF(M)$ from $\Omega_B^{d+k+1}U$ with $U\in\operatorname{mod} B$. Since $\mathcal Y$ contains projectives and is closed under submodules, $\Omega_BU\in\mathcal Y$. Choose $N\in\mathcal C$ with $F(N)\cong\Omega_BU$. The two terms of this retraction are stably isomorphic to
\[
 F(\Omega_T^dM)\quad\text{and}\quad F(\Omega_T^{d+k}N),
\]
respectively. Full faithfulness of $\underline F$ lifts the retraction to $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$. Hence $k$-$\operatorname{dell}_T(M)\le d$. The assertion is immediate if the right-hand side is infinite.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:comparison-equality}
Let $M\in\mathcal C$, $k\ge1$ and $d\ge0$. Suppose that
$k$-$\operatorname{dell}_BF(M)\le d$, and choose a representative
\[
 W=\Sigma_B^{d+k}\Omega_B^dF(M)
\]
of the ordinary canonical witness. If $\operatorname{Tor}_1^B(W,T)=0$, then
$k$-$\operatorname{dell}_T(M)\le d$. In particular, if
$d=k$-$\operatorname{dell}_BF(M)<\infty$, this condition gives
$k$-$\operatorname{dell}_T(M)=d$.

If $\Sigma_B(\underline\mathcal Y)\subseteq\underline\mathcal Y$, where
$\underline\mathcal Y$ denotes the full stable image of $\mathcal Y$, then
\[
 k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_T(M)=k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_BF(M)
 \qquad(M\in\mathcal C,\ k\ge1),
\]
including infinite values.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The ordinary version of \cref{thm:unit} makes
$\Omega_B^dF(M)$ a stable retract of $\Omega_B^{d+k}W$.
The Tor condition says precisely that $W\in\mathcal Y$.
Writing $W\cong F(N)$ and using full faithfulness of
$\underline F$ lifts this retraction to $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$.
\cref{prop:comparison} gives equality when $d$ is the
ordinary level. The Tor condition is independent of the chosen
representative, since positive Tor vanishes on projective summands.

For the last assertion, $\Omega_B^dF(M)$ belongs to $\mathcal Y$ because
$\mathcal Y$ is closed under syzygies. Closure under $\Sigma_B$ in the
stable category puts the displayed witness in $\underline\mathcal Y$,
so its first Tor-group vanishes. Apply the first assertion when
the ordinary level is finite; when it is infinite, use the first

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:evaluation}
There is an exact sequence of right $\overline A$-modules
\begin{equation}\label{eq:evaluation}
\begin{split}
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^1_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(\operatorname{Tr}_T X,{}_BT)
\longrightarrow X\xrightarrow{\delta_X}(X^*)^\vee
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^2_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(\operatorname{Tr}_T X,{}_BT)\longrightarrow0.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
For $q\ge1$ one also has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:double-shift}
 \operatorname{Ext}^{q+2}_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(\operatorname{Tr}_T X,{}_BT)
 \cong\operatorname{Ext}^q_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(X^*,{}_BT).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Dualizing \eqref{eq:proper-presentation} gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow X^*\longrightarrow T_0^*
 \longrightarrow T_1^*\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tr}_T X\longrightarrow0,
\]
where $T_i^*$ is projective over $B^{\mathrm{op}}$. Split this sequence at the image of $T_0^*\to T_1^*$ and apply $(-)^\vee$ to the two short exact sequences. The evaluation maps identify $(T_i^*)^\vee$ with $T_i$, and the composite $T_1\to T_0$ is $d$. Comparing cokernels gives \eqref{eq:evaluation}, with the middle map equal to evaluation by naturality. This is the evaluation sequence of \cite[Theorem~3.9]{Xi}. Dimension shifting along the displayed sequence gives \eqref{eq:double-shift}.

\label{def:torsionfree}
An object $X\in\mathcal C$ is \emph{$T$-torsionless} if it embeds into an object of $\operatorname{add} T$. For $k\ge1$, it is \emph{$k$-$T$-torsionfree} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:torsionfree}
 \operatorname{Ext}^i_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(\operatorname{Tr}_T X,{}_BT)=0\qquad(1\le i\le k).
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:stable-invariance}
The condition of being $k$-$T$-torsionfree is invariant under isomorphism in $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:torsionless}
For $X\in\mathcal C$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is $T$-torsionless;
\item $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(\operatorname{Tr}_T X,{}_BT)=0$;
\item $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_T(X,\mathbb D\overline A)=0$.
\end{enumerate}
If these conditions hold, then $X\cong\Omega_TY$ in $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$ for some $Y\in\mathcal C$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By \eqref{eq:evaluation}, (2) is equivalent to injectivity of $\delta_X$. A finite free cover of $X^*$ dualizes to an embedding of $(X^*)^\vee$ into a finite sum of copies of $T$. Conversely, an embedding $X\to T'$ with $T'\in\operatorname{add} T$ and naturality of evaluation imply that $\delta_X$ is injective. Hence (1) and (2) are equivalent.

The module $\mathbb D\overline A$ is injective over $\overline A$ and belongs to $\mathcal C$ by \eqref{eq:tilting}. If $X$ embeds into $T'\in\operatorname{add} T$, every map $X\to\mathbb D\overline A$ extends to $T'$, proving (3). Conversely, embed $X$ into an injective module $E\in\operatorname{add}(\mathbb D\overline A)$. Under (3), this embedding factors through an object of $\operatorname{add} T$. Its first factor is injective, proving (1).

Finally, the cokernel $Y$ of an embedding $X\to T'$ lies in $\operatorname{Fac} T$. Thus $0\to X\to T'\to Y\to0$ is a conflation with projective middle term, and $X$ represents $\Omega_TY$ in $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:Hom}
For $M\in\mathcal C$ and $n\ge0$, the condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hom-condition}
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_T(\Omega_T^nM,\Omega_T^n\mathbb D\overline A)=0
\end{equation}
implies $\operatorname{dell}_T(M)\le n$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
By adjunction, \eqref{eq:Hom-condition} is equivalent to
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_T(\Sigma_T^n\Omega_T^nM,\mathbb D\overline A)=0.
\]
By \cref{prop:torsionless}, a representative $X$ of $\Sigma_T^n\Omega_T^nM$ satisfies $X\cong\Omega_TY$ for some $Y\in\mathcal C$. The triangle identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle-identity}

 =1_{\Omega_T^nM}
\end{equation}

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:approximation}
For $X\in\mathcal C$ and $k\ge1$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is $k$-$T$-torsionfree;
\item there is an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coresolution}
 0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow T^0\longrightarrow T^1
 \longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow T^{k-1},
 \qquad T^i\in\operatorname{add} T,
\end{equation}
such that the following dual sequence is exact:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-coresolution}
 (T^{k-1})^*\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow(T^0)^*
 \longrightarrow X^*\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
No exactness condition is imposed beyond the last displayed term of \eqref{eq:coresolution}. All its successive cokernels, including the last one, lie in $\mathcal C$. In particular, (1) implies $X\cong\Omega_T^kY$ in $\underline{\mathcal C}_T$ for some $Y\in\mathcal C$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
For $k=1$, condition (1) means that evaluation is injective. Choose a projective epimorphism $Q_0\to X^*$ over $B^{\mathrm{op}}$. Its dual, composed with evaluation, gives an injection $X\to Q_0^\vee$. The identity
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\overline A}(Q_0^\vee,T)\cong Q_0
\]
identifies its dual map with $Q_0\to X^*$. Hence it is a left $\operatorname{add} T$-approximation and gives (2). The converse follows from \cref{prop:torsionless}.

Suppose $k\ge2$. By \cref{lem:evaluation}, condition (1) is equivalent to $\delta_X$ being an isomorphism together with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-vanishing}
 \operatorname{Ext}^q_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(X^*,{}_BT)=0\qquad(1\le q\le k-2),
\end{equation}
where this range is empty for $k=2$. Dualize a projective resolution $Q_\bullet\to X^*$ by $(-)^\vee$. Evaluation and \eqref{eq:dual-vanishing} give the exact segment
\[
 0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow Q_0^\vee
 \longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow Q_{k-1}^\vee.
\]
Each $Q_i^\vee$ belongs to $\operatorname{add} T$. Applying $(-)^*$ identifies the resulting segment with the original projective resolution, since $Q_i\cong(Q_i^\vee)^*$ naturally. This proves (2).

Conversely, \eqref{eq:dual-coresolution} is a projective resolution segment of $X^*$. Dualizing its first two terms and comparing with \eqref{eq:coresolution} shows that $\delta_X$ is an isomorphism. For $1\le q\le k-2$, the cohomology computing $\operatorname{Ext}^q_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}(X^*,{}_BT)$ is zero by exactness of \eqref{eq:coresolution}. Equations~\eqref{eq:evaluation} and \eqref{eq:double-shift} now give (1).

Let $C_0=X$ and let $C_{i+1}$ be the cokernel of the induced injection $C_i\to T^i$ for $0\le i<k$. Each $C_{i+1}$ is a quotient of $T^i$, so belongs to $\operatorname{Fac} T$. The conflations $0\to C_i\to T^i\to C_{i+1}\to0$ give $C_i\cong\Omega_TC_{i+1}$ stably. Take $Y=C_k$.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:findim-bound}
With empty index sets omitted, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
 \delta_T=\max\left\{
 \sup_{i\in\mathcal I}\operatorname{dell}_T(E_i),\quad

 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Then

 \operatorname{findim} B^{\mathrm{op}}\le\operatorname{Findim} B^{\mathrm{op}}\le\operatorname{ddell} B

\]

delooping levels of the simple $B$-modules.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:unbounded-gap}
For an integer $d\ge1$, let $Q_d$ be the quiver
\[
 0\xrightarrow{\alpha_0}1\xrightarrow{\alpha_1}2
 \longrightarrow\cdots\xrightarrow{\alpha_d}d+1,
\]
and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-algebra}
 A_d=KQ_d/(\alpha_i\alpha_{i+1}\mid1\le i\le d-1).
\end{equation}
Paths are multiplied in their order of traversal; the relation set
is empty for $d=1$. Write $P_i=e_iA_d$ and $S_i=\operatorname{top}P_i$,
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-tilting}
 T_d=S_0\oplus P_0\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^{d+1}P_i,
 \qquad X_d=P_0/\operatorname{rad}^2P_0.
\end{equation}
Then $T_d$ is a classical tilting module with $\operatorname{pd}_{A_d}T_d=1$.
For $B_d=\operatorname{End}_{A_d}(T_d)$ and $F_d=\operatorname{Hom}_{A_d}(T_d,-)$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-endomorphism}
 B_d\cong KQ_d/J_{Q_d}^2,\qquad
 \operatorname{gl.dim} A_d=d,\qquad \operatorname{gl.dim} B_d=d+1,
\end{equation}
where $J_{Q_d}$ is the arrow ideal. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-all-levels}
 \begin{gathered}
 k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_{T_d}(X_d)=d\qquad(k\ge1),\\
 k\text{-}\operatorname{dell}_{B_d}F_d(X_d)=
 \begin{cases}0,&k=1,\\ d,&k\ge2,\end{cases}
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
and the bound in \cref{prop:findim-bound} is sharp:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-delta}
 \delta_{T_d}=d+1=\operatorname{findim} B_d^{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Put $r=d+1$. The lengths of $P_0,\ldots,P_r$ are $3,2,\ldots,2,1$,
respectively. The projective cover of $S_0$ gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow P_1\longrightarrow P_0
 \longrightarrow S_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
Restriction induces a surjection
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{A_d}(P_0,P_0)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A_d}(P_1,P_0).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{A_d}(P_1,S_0)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A_d}(P_1,P_i)=0\qquad(i\ge2).
\]
Thus $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{A_d}(S_0,T_d)=0$ and $\operatorname{pd}_{A_d}S_0=1$.
The other summands of $T_d$ are projective, and
\[
 0\longrightarrow A_d\longrightarrow
 P_0^{\oplus2}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^{r}P_i
 \longrightarrow S_0\longrightarrow0
\]
is a tilting coresolution. This proves the tilting assertion.
For $1\le i<r$, one has $\Omega_{A_d}S_i=S_{i+1}$, and
$S_r=P_r$ is projective. Hence $\operatorname{gl.dim} A_d=r-1=d$.

Order the summands as $U_0=S_0$, $U_1=P_0$, and $U_i=P_i$ for
$2\le i\le r$. Apart from scalar endomorphisms, the only nonzero
maps between these summands are $U_{i+1}\to U_i$ for $0\le i<r$,
each spanning a one-dimensional Hom-space. Every composite of two
successive maps is zero. The endomorphism convention of \cref{sec:preliminaries}
therefore gives $B_d=KQ_d/J_{Q_d}^2$.
Write $Q_i=e_iB_d$ and $L_i=\operatorname{top}Q_i$.
Then $\Omega_{B_d}L_i=L_{i+1}$ for $i<r$, and $L_r=Q_r$.
Consequently $\operatorname{gl.dim} B_d=r$.

The indecomposable $A_d$-modules are the uniserial quotients of the
$P_i$. Among them, precisely $S_1$ and $P_1$ fail to belong to
$\operatorname{Fac} T_d$: no map from $T_d$ has image meeting their top.
All remaining modules are quotients of summands of $T_d$.
Thus, modulo $\operatorname{add} T_d$, the nonzero indecomposables are
\[
 X_d,\qquad S_i\quad(2\le i<r).
\]
The conflations with middle terms $P_0$ and $P_i$ ($2\le i<r$) give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-relative-chain}
 \Omega_{T_d}X_d=S_2,\qquad
 \Omega_{T_d}S_i=S_{i+1}\quad(2\le i<r),\qquad S_r\in\operatorname{add} T_d.
\end{equation}
Here the displayed epimorphisms are minimal right
$\operatorname{add} T_d$-approximations. The chain is interpreted as terminating
at the relative projective $S_2$ when $d=1$.

For $0\le t<d$, the module $\Omega_{T_d}^tX_d$ is the
nonprojective object in position $t$ of this chain. For any
$N\in\operatorname{Fac} T_d$ and $k\ge1$, each nonprojective summand of
$\Omega_{T_d}^{t+k}N$ occurs strictly later in the chain.
Krull--Schmidt and the description of stable retracts after
\cref{lem:projectives} exclude a retraction onto
$\Omega_{T_d}^tX_d$. At $t=d$ that object is relative projective.
This proves the first equality in \eqref{eq:gap-all-levels}.

Computing Hom from the summands of $T_d$ gives
\[
 F_d(X_d)=L_1,\qquad F_d(S_i)=L_i\quad(2\le i\le r).
\]
Since $L_1=\Omega_{B_d}L_0$, its ordinary delooping level is zero.
For $k\ge2$ and $0\le t<d$, its $t$th syzygy is $L_{t+1}$.
Every indecomposable $B_d$-module is simple or projective, and a
nonprojective $(t+k)$th syzygy of a simple $L_j$ is
$L_{j+t+k}$. Its index is strictly greater than $t+1$.
Thus $L_{t+1}$ cannot be a stable retract of such a syzygy.
At $t=d$, its syzygy $L_r$ is projective, proving the second
equality in \eqref{eq:gap-all-levels}.

The preceding calculations also identify the torsionfree class as
\[
 \mathcal Y_d=\operatorname{add}(Q_0,\ldots,Q_{r-1},L_1,\ldots,L_r).
\]
In the notation of \eqref{eq:test-objects},
$\mathcal I=\{1,\ldots,r\}$, $\mathcal J=\{0\}$,
$E_1=X_d$, $E_i=S_i$ for $i\ge2$, and $N_0=X_d$.
The maximum of the unshifted terms in \eqref{eq:delta} is $d$,
whereas the shifted term is $2$-$\operatorname{dell}_{T_d}(X_d)+1=d+1$.
Since $\operatorname{findim} B_d^{\mathrm{op}}=\operatorname{gl.dim} B_d^{\mathrm{op}}=d+1$,
this proves \eqref{eq:gap-delta}.

\label{thm:tensor-induction}
Let $H$ and $R$ be nonzero finite-dimensional basic $K$-algebras,

 \cong R\otimes B.
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-suspension}
 \Omega_{\widetilde U}\mathsf I\cong\mathsf I\Omega_U,
 \qquad

\end{equation}

 \begin{gathered}

 \qquad \operatorname{pd}_{\widetilde U}(\mathsf I M)=\operatorname{pd}_U M,\\
 M\text{ is }k\text{-}U\text{-torsionfree}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad

 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-transpose}

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-ordinary-level}
 \begin{gathered}

 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:tensor-finite-global}
Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional basic $K$-algebra of finite
global dimension $g$, and let $R$ be as in
\cref{thm:tensor-induction}. Assume in addition that $R$
is local with $R/\operatorname{rad} R=K$. For $\Lambda=R\otimes H$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-dimensions}
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda=\operatorname{Findim}\Lambda=\operatorname{ddell}\Lambda=\operatorname{dell}\Lambda&=g,\\
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\operatorname{Findim}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
 =\operatorname{ddell}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\operatorname{dell}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}&=g.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{rad} R\ne0$, every simple left or right $\Lambda$-module
has infinite projective dimension.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Every simple right $\Lambda$-module has the form
$(R/\operatorname{rad} R)\otimes S$ with $S$ simple over $H$.

\cref{lem:finite-pd-embedding} yields $\operatorname{dell}\Lambda\le g$.
On the other hand, inducing an $H$-module of projective
dimension $g$ gives $\operatorname{findim}\Lambda\ge g$.
Apply the same arguments to $R^{\mathrm{op}}$ and $H^{\mathrm{op}}$, whose
global dimension is also $g$. The chain
\eqref{eq:dimension-chain} on both sides proves all equalities.

Loewy length at most $\ell-1$, whereas every nonzero finitely
generated projective $R$-module is free and has Loewy length
$\ell$. Such a resolution cannot terminate. The proof on the
left is identical.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:cyclic-syzygies}
For $1\le\ell<c_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-syzygy}
 \Omega_\Lambda M(i,\ell)\cong M(i+\ell,c_i-\ell),
 \qquad M(i,c_i)=P_i.
\end{equation}
Consequently, including infinite values,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-pd-lifting}
 \operatorname{pd}_\Lambda M(i,\ell)=\operatorname{pd}_N V(i,\ell).
\end{equation}

<!-- end lemma-6 -->

The kernel of $P_i\to M(i,\ell)$ is $p_{i,\ell}\Lambda$. Left
multiplication by $p_{i,\ell}$ defines a surjection
$P_{i+\ell}\to p_{i,\ell}\Lambda$. By the normal form
\eqref{eq:cyclic-basis}, its kernel is
$P_{i+\ell}\mathfrak a^{c_i-\ell}$. Here
$c_i-\ell\le c_{i+\ell}$ follows from
\eqref{eq:cyclic-admissibility}. Invertibility of the coefficient
automorphisms ensures that no extra kernel occurs. Since these kernels

recursion for $V(i,\ell)$. A module $M(i,\ell)$ is projective exactly
when $\ell=c_i$: its projective cover is $P_i$, and for $\ell<c_i$ this
cover has a nonzero kernel. Thus the two minimal resolutions terminate
at exactly the same step, proving \eqref{eq:cyclic-pd-lifting}.

Choose $0\ne z\in R$ with $z\operatorname{rad} R=0$. The line

$\operatorname{rad} R_j$, because $\sigma_{i,\ell-1}(\operatorname{rad} R)=\operatorname{rad} R$.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{thm:cyclic-uniform}
For every $R,c,\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ as above, put
$\Lambda=\Lambda_R(c;\boldsymbol{\sigma})$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-uniform}
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda=\operatorname{findim}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
 =\operatorname{dell}\Lambda=\operatorname{dell}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\operatorname{findim} N(c)<\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus these four invariants depend only on the underlying Nakayama
algebra. There is no assumption that $N(c)$ has finite global dimension.
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
For each $i$, let $E_i$ be the injective envelope of $U_i$ in
$\operatorname{mod} N$, and set
\[
 e_i'=\min\{\operatorname{pd}_N U_i,\operatorname{pd}_N E_i\}.
\]
Ringel's formula for Nakayama algebras \cite{RingelNakayama} states that
every $e_i'$ is finite and that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ringel-input}
 f:=\operatorname{findim} N=\operatorname{findim} N^{\mathrm{op}}=\max_i e_i'.
\end{equation}
Choose $H_i\in\{U_i,E_i\}$ with $\operatorname{pd}_NH_i=e_i'$. It is
indecomposable with socle $U_i$, so $H_i=V(t_i,\ell_i)$ for suitable
$t_i,\ell_i$ with $t_i+\ell_i-1=i$ in $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.
By \cref{lem:cyclic-syzygies}, the module
$\widetilde H_i=M(t_i,\ell_i)$ satisfies
\[
 S_i\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\widetilde H_i,
 \qquad \operatorname{pd}_\Lambda\widetilde H_i=e_i'.
 \]
\cref{lem:finite-pd-embedding} and an index attaining the maximum
in \eqref{eq:ringel-input} therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-first-side}
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\le\operatorname{dell}\Lambda\le f
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda\ge f.
\end{equation}

We verify the opposite-algebra step explicitly. Reverse the cycle.
At a vertex $j$, the surviving paths ending at $j$ have consecutive
lengths $0,\ldots,d_j-1$, where
\[
 d_j=\#\{\ell\ge0\mid \ell<c_{j-\ell}\}.
\]
Indeed, every suffix of a surviving path survives. In $\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$
these become the paths starting at $j$, and the coefficient algebra
is $R^{\mathrm{op}}$. For the reversed arrow $b_i=a_i^{\mathrm{op}}:i+1\to i$,
\eqref{eq:cyclic-crossing} becomes
\[
 b_i r_i^{\mathrm{op}}
   =\sigma_i^{-1}(r)_{i+1}^{\mathrm{op}}b_i.
\]
After relabeling the vertices, this is the same construction with
lengths $d_j$ and inverse automorphisms. Its underlying Nakayama
algebra is $N^{\mathrm{op}}$. The argument proving
\eqref{eq:cyclic-first-side} thus applies to $\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$.
By \eqref{eq:ringel-input}, it gives
\[
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda\le\operatorname{dell}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\le f
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\ge f.
\]
Combining the two pairs of inequalities proves \eqref{eq:cyclic-uniform}.

where $Q$ is the oriented cycle without the coefficient loops. The uniform theorem and \cref{cor:cyclic-big-derived} show that the ordinary and derived delooping levels and both finitistic dimensions are independent of all coefficient automorphisms. \cref{rem:cyclic-xixu} explains the existing finitistic-dimension result; the additional calculations identify the delooping levels and exhibit modules of maximal finite projective dimension. The complementary applications with nonprojective tilting modules are \cref{thm:unbounded-gap,thm:tensor-gap}.
