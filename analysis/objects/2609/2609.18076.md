<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:period}
Let $\mathbf F$ be a continuously differentiable field satisfying
\begin{equation*}
\nabla\times\mathbf F=\mathbf 0\qquad\text{in }\Omega.
\end{equation*}
Let $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_g$ be the independent handle loops and $g=b_{1}(\Omega)$. If
\begin{equation*}
\oint_{\gamma_i}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l=0,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,g,
\end{equation*}
then the circulation of $\mathbf F$ around every closed curve in $\Omega$ is zero, and there exists a globally single-valued scalar potential $q$ such that $\mathbf F=\nabla q$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $L$ be an arbitrary oriented closed curve in $\Omega$. In general, $L$ need not be the boundary of a surface contained in $\Omega$, so Stokes' theorem cannot be applied directly to $L$.

By the choice of the independent circulation loops
$\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_g$, there exist integers
$m_1,\ldots,m_g$ such that the cycle $
L-\sum_{i=1}^{g}m_i\gamma_i
$
bounds an oriented surface chain $S$ contained in $\overline{\Omega}$, with its interior lying in $\Omega$. Hence
$$
\partial S
=
L-\sum_{i=1}^{g}m_i\gamma_i.
$$
Stokes' theorem can therefore be applied to this boundary,
$$
\begin{aligned}
0
&=
\int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf n\,dS\\
&=
\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l=
\oint_L\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l
-
\sum_{i=1}^{g}m_i
\oint_{\gamma_i}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l.
\end{aligned}
$$

By the zero-circulation assumption on the independent loops,
$\oint_{\gamma_i}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l=0,~i=1,\ldots,g,$
and consequently $\oint_L\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l=0$. Since $L$ is arbitrary, the circulation of $\mathbf F$ vanishes around every closed curve in $\Omega$. Fix $\mathbf x_0\in\Omega$ and define
$$
q(\mathbf x)
=
\int_{\mathbf x_0}^{\mathbf x}
\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l.
$$

If two paths connect $\mathbf x_0$ to $\mathbf x$, their difference forms a closed curve. Since the circulation around every closed curve is zero, the above integral is independent of the chosen path. Therefore $q$ is globally single-valued, and differentiation gives
$$
\mathbf F=\nabla q
\qquad\text{in }\Omega.
$$
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The statement ``curl-free implies a global scalar potential'' is generally false in a multiply connected domain. A curl-free field always admits a local potential, but a globally single-valued potential exists only when all independent circulations around the handles vanish. Lemma~\ref{lem:period} makes this condition explicit. For example, in a solid torus, a curl-free field may still have a nonzero circulation around the handle and therefore cannot be represented by a globally single-valued scalar potential. The circulation conditions remove this topological obstruction.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:N}
For a bounded, orientable and connected three-dimensional domain of finite topology, $\dim\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega)=b_1(\Omega)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Equation \eqref{eq:periodmatrix} immediately shows that the $g=b_1(\Omega)$ fields $\mathbf N_1,\ldots,\mathbf N_g$ are linearly independent.

It remains to show that there are no other linearly independent Neumann harmonic fields. Let $\mathbf F\in\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega)$ and measure its $g$ basic circulations, $\alpha_i=\oint_{\gamma_i}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf l$. Define $\mathbf G=\mathbf F-\sum_{j=1}^{g}\alpha_j\mathbf N_j$. Then $\mathbf G\in\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega)$. Using \eqref{eq:periodmatrix}, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gzero}
\oint_{\gamma_i}\mathbf G\cdot d\mathbf l=0,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,g.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:Gzero} and $\nabla\times\mathbf G={\bf0}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:period} shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ggrad}
\mathbf G=\nabla q
\end{equation}
for a globally single-valued scalar potential $q$. From $\nabla\cdot\mathbf G=0$ and $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf G=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qNeu}
\nabla^2q=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,
\qquad
\frac{\partial q}{\partial n}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.
\end{equation}
Green's first identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:greenN}
\int_\Omega |\nabla q|^2\,d\Omega
=
\int_{\partial\Omega}q\frac{\partial q}{\partial n}\,dS
-
\int_\Omega q\nabla^2q\,d\Omega
=0.
\end{equation}
Hence $\nabla q=0$, so $\mathbf G=0$. Consequently every field in $\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega)$ is a linear combination of $\mathbf N_1,\ldots,\mathbf N_g$, hence $\dim\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega)=b_1(\Omega)$ is valid.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:D}
For a bounded, orientable and connected three-dimensional domain of finite topology, $\dim\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega)=b_2(\Omega)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf F\in\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega)$. By \eqref{eq:Dpot} and \eqref{eq:Ck}, $\mathbf F=\nabla\phi$, where $\phi$ is harmonic in $\Omega$ and $\phi=C_k$ on $\Gamma_k$. Define
$$
q=\phi-C_0-\sum_{k=1}^{M}(C_k-C_0)\varphi_k.
$$
Then $\nabla^2q=0$ in $\Omega$ and $q=0$ on $\partial\Omega$. By the maximum principle for harmonic functions \cite{Evans2010}, $q=0$ in $\Omega$. Therefore,
$$
\mathbf F=\sum_{k=1}^{M}(C_k-C_0)\mathbf D_k.
$$
Hence $\mathbf D_1,\ldots,\mathbf D_M$ span $\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega)$. Since they are also linearly independent, $\dim\mathcal{H}_{D}(\Omega)=M=b_2(\Omega)$ holds.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
