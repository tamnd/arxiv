<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $P$ be an abelian $p$-subgroup of a finite group $G$. If $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$ is a saturated fusion system then $S(G, P)$ is Brauer indecomposable.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{Th1.2}
Suppose that $P$ is an abelian $p$-subgroup of a finite group $G$ and $M$ is the endopermutation Scott $kG$-module with respect to the fusion stable endopermutation source pair $(P, V)$. If $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$ is a saturated fusion system, then $M$ is slash indecomposable.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a finite group and $P$ a $p$-subgroup of $G$. Suppose that $M= S(G, P)$ and that $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$ is saturated. TFAE:

(1) $M$ is Brauer indecomposable.

(2) $\mathrm{Res}_{Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}S(\mathrm{N}_G(Q), \mathrm{N}_P(Q))$ is indecomposable for each fully normalized subgroup $Q\leq P$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{Th3.3}
Let $G$ be a finite group and $P$ a $p$-subgroup of $G$. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the set of nontrivial fully normalized subgroups of $P$ in the fusion system $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$.  Assume $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$ is saturated. Suppose every fully normalized subgroup of $P$ is a normal subgroup of $P$. Let $(P, V)$ be a fusion-stable endopermutation source pair of $G$ and choose a Frobenius-friendly category $\mathcal{C}_G(P, V)$, a $P$-slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}: \mathcal{C}_G(P, V)\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(P))$. Let $M$ be an endopermutation Scott module with respect to $(P, V)$ and $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}$. For any $Q\in\mathcal{S}$, there exists $t(Q)\in \mathrm{N}_G(P)$ satisfying:

\begin{itemize}
\item $(P, {^{t(Q)}V})$ is a fusion-stable endopermutation source pair of $\mathrm{N}_G(Q)$;
\item Fix a $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}(P, {^{t(Q)}V})$, then there exists a $P$-slash functor \begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), P}: \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}(P, {^{t(Q)}V})\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q, P))
\end{equation*} 
such that the endopermutation Scott module $M_Q$ with respect to $(P, {^{t(Q)}V})$ and $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), P}$ is isomorphic to a direct summand of $\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}^GM$. 
\end{itemize}

For any $Q\in\mathcal{S}$, we fix a quadruple $(t(Q), \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}(P, {^{t(Q)}V}), \mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), P}, M_Q)$. If for any $Q\in\mathcal{S}$, $M_Q$ is slash indecomposable, then $M$ is slash indecomposable.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $P$ be a $p$-group and let $V$ be an indecomposable $kP$-module. 

(1) If $P$ is a vertex of $V$, we say $V$ is \emph{capped} and $(P, V)$ is called a \emph{source pair}. 

(2) A source pair $(P, V)$ is called a \emph{fusion-stable endopermutation source pair} of $G$ if for any $p$-subgroup $Q\leq G$ and any two morphisms $\phi_1, \phi_2: Q\rightarrow P$ in $\mathrm{Hom}_G(Q, P)$, the direct sum $\mathrm{Res}_{\phi_1}V\oplus \mathrm{Res}_{\phi_2}V$  is an endopermutation $kQ$-module.

(3) We say two fusion-stable endopermutation source pairs $(P_1, V_1)$ and $(P_2, V_2)$ are \emph{compatible} if for any $p$-subgroup $Q\leq G$ and any two morphisms $\phi_1: Q\rightarrow P_1, \phi_2: Q\rightarrow P_2$, the direct sum $\mathrm{Res}_{\phi_1}V_1\oplus \mathrm{Res}_{\phi_2}V_2$ is an endopermutation $kQ$-module.

(4) We say a $kG$-module $M$ is \emph{Frobenius-friendly} if it is a direct sum of indecomposable $kG$-modules with compatible fusion-stable endopermutation source pairs. We say that two Frobenius-friendly $kG$-modules $L, M$ are \emph{compatible} if the direct sum $L\oplus M$ is also a Frobenius-friendly $kG$-module.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a full subcategory of the category $\mathrm{mod}(kG)$ consisting of finite-dimensional modules. $\mathcal{C}$ is called \emph{Frobenius-friendly} if: (1) For any object $V$ in $\mathcal{C}$, $V$ is a Frobenius-friendly module; (2) For any two objects $V_1, V_2$ in $\mathcal{C}$, they are compatible Frobenius-friendly modules.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a full subcategory of the category $\mathrm{mod}(kG)$ consisting of finite-dimensional modules. $\mathcal{C}$ is called \emph{Frobenius-friendly} if: (1) For any object $V$ in $\mathcal{C}$, $V$ is an endo-$p$-permutation module; (2) For any two objects $V_1, V_2$ in $\mathcal{C}$, $V_1\oplus V_2$ is also an endo-$p$-permutation module.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a finite group, and $\mathcal{C}$ be a full subcategory of $\mathrm{mod}(kG)$. Let $P$ be a $p$-subgroup of $G$, and $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ such that $P\mathrm{C}_G(P)\leq H\leq \mathrm{N}_G(P)$. What we called a \emph{$P$-slash} functor $\mathrm{Sl}: \mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(kH)$ is defined by the following data:

\begin{itemize}
\item for each object $V$ of $\mathcal{C}$, a $kH$-module $\mathrm{Sl}(V)$ s.t. $P\leq \mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{Sl}(V))$.

\item for each pair $V, W$ of objects in the category $\mathcal{C}$, a $k$-linear map
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Sl}^{V, W}: \mathrm{Hom}_{kP}(V, W)     \rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_k(\mathrm{Sl}(V), \mathrm{Sl}(W));
\end{equation*}
such that

(1) $\mathrm{Sl}^{V, V}(id)= id$;

(2) $\mathrm{Sl}^{U, W}(v\circ u)= \mathrm{Sl}^{V, W}(v)\circ \mathrm{Sl}^{U, V}(u)$;

(3) for any two objects $V, W$ of $\mathcal{C}$, the map $\mathrm{Sl}^{V, W}$ factors through an isomorphism of $k(\mathrm{C}_G(P)\times \mathrm{C}_G(P))\Delta H$-modules
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Br}_{\Delta P}(\mathrm{Hom}_k(V, W))\cong \mathrm{Hom}_k(\mathrm{Sl}(V), \mathrm{Sl}(W)).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
By (3), $\mathrm{Sl}$ is a $k$-linear functor from $\mathcal{C}$ to $\mathrm{mod}(kH)$.

If $\mathcal{C}$ is the category consisting of $p$-permutation $kG$-modules, then the Brauer functor $\mathrm{Br}_P$ is a $P$-slash functor.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{Lem2.5}
Let $G$ be a finite group, and $\mathcal{C}$ be a Frobenius-friendly category of $kG$-modules. Let $P$ be a $p$-subgroup of the group $G$, and $H$ a subgroup such that $P\mathrm{C}_G(P)\leq H\leq \mathrm{N}_G(P)$.

(1) There exists a $P$-slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_P: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(kH)$.

(2) If $\mathrm{Sl}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(kH)$ is another $P$-slash functor, then there exists a linear character $\chi: H/ P\mathrm{C}_G(P)\rightarrow k^\times$ and an isomorphism of slash functors $\mathrm{Sl}\cong \chi \mathrm{Sl}_P$.

(3) If $(Q, V)$ is a source pair of an indecomposable direct summand of $\mathrm{Sl}_P(M)$, where $M$ is an object in $\mathcal{C}$, then there is a source pair $(Q', V')$ of an indecomposable direct summand of $M$ such that $P\leq Q\leq Q'$.

(4) Assume $H= \mathrm{N}_G(P)$ and $M$ is an object in $\mathcal{C}$. If $\mathrm{Sl}_P(M)$ admits an indecomposable direct summand with vertex $P$, then the $kG$-module $M$ admits an indecomposable direct summand with vertex $P$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{Lem2.6}
Let $G$ be a finite group, and $\mathcal{C}$ be a Frobenius-friendly category of $kG$-modules. Let $P\lhd Q$ be $p$-subgroups. Let $\mathrm{Sl}_P: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(P))$ be a $P$-slash functor. There exists a Frobenius-friendly category $\mathcal{C}'$ of $k\mathrm{N}_G(P)$-modules that contains the essential image of $\mathrm{Sl}_P$. Let $\mathrm{Sl}_Q: \mathcal{C}' \rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(P, Q))$ be a $Q$-slash functor. Then the composition 
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Sl}_Q\circ \mathrm{Sl}_P: \mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(P, Q))
\end{equation}
is a $Q$-slash functor, where $\mathrm{N}_G(P, Q)= \mathrm{N}_G(P)\cap \mathrm{N}_G(Q)$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a finite group, and let $(P, M)$ be a fusion-stable endopermutation source pair of the group $G$. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a Frobenius-friendly category of $kG$-modules such that any finite sum of indecomposable $kG$-modules with source pair $(P, M)$ is an object of $\mathcal{C}$. Let $\mathrm{Sl}_P: \mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(P))$ be a $P$-slash functor. Then the mapping $X\mapsto \mathrm{Sl}_P(X)$ induces a one-to-one correspondence between the isomorphism classes of indecomposable $kG$-modules with source pair $(P, M)$ and the isomorphism classes of projective indecomposable $k\mathrm{N}_G(P)/ P$-modules.

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{Def2.8}[Feng--Li]
Let $(P, V)$ be a fusion-stable endopermutation source pair, and let $\mathcal{C}, \mathrm{Sl}_P$ be as in the above lemma. 

(1)There is a unique (up to isomorphism) indecomposable module $M$ with source pair $(P, V)$ in $\mathcal{C}$ such that the $k[\mathrm{N}_G(P)/ P]$-module $\mathrm{Sl}_P(M)$ has trivial top, i.e. $\mathrm{Sl}_P(M)/ \mathrm{rad}(\mathrm{Sl}_P(M))\cong {_{\mathrm{N}_G(P)/P}k}$, which is a 1-dimensional module with trivial $\mathrm{N}_G(P)/ P$-action. Then $M$ is called an \emph{endopermutation Scott module} with respect to $(P, V)$ and $\mathrm{Sl}_P$.

(2) A Frobenius-friendly $kG$-module $V$ is said to be \emph{slash indecomposable} if for any $p$-subgroup $Q\leq G$ and $Q$-slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_Q: \mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q))$, where $\mathcal{C}$ is a Frobenius-friendly category containing $V$, we have $\mathrm{Sl}_Q(V)$ is indecomposable or zero as a $k[Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)/ Q]$-module.

\label{Lem2.9}
Suppose that $R, H$ are subgroups of a finite group $G$ such that $R\leq H$. Assume $R$ is a $p$-subgroup. <!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem1}
Let $G$ be a finite group and let $P$ be a $p$-subgroup. Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ such that $P\leq H$. Let $(P, V)$ be a fusion-stable endopermutation source pair of $G$ and we choose a $\mathcal{C}_G(P, V)$. Let $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}: \mathcal{C}_G(P, V)\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(P))$ be a $P$-slash functor. Let $M$ be an endopermutation Scott module with respect to $(P, V)$ and $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}$. Then there exists a fusion-stable endopermutation source pair $(P, {^tV})$ of $H$, where $t\in \mathrm{N}_G(P)$, such that there is an endopermutation Scott module $\widetilde{M}$ with source pair $(P, {^tV})$ satisfying $\widetilde{M}\mid \mathrm{Res}_H^G(M)$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{C}_2$ be a full subcategory of $\mathrm{mod}(kH)$ consisting of $\mathrm{Res}_H^G(M)$ and $\mathrm{Decom}(\mathrm{Res}_H^G(M))$. $\mathcal{C}_2$ is a Frobenius-friendly category and there exists a Slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}: \mathcal{C}_2\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_H(P))$ such that $\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}(\mathrm{Res}_H^G(M))= \mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_H(P)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(P)}\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}(M)$ by Lemma \ref{Lem2.9}. As a $k[\mathrm{N}_G(P)/ P]$-module, $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}(M)$ is an indecomposable projective module satisfying
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{top}(\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}(M))\cong \mathrm{soc}(\mathrm{Sl}_{G, P}(M))\cong {_{\mathrm{N}_G(P)/ P}k}.
\end{equation*}
So there exists an indecomposable direct summand $X\mid \mathrm{Res}_H^G(M)$ such that 
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Hom}_{k\mathrm{N}_H(P)}(k, \mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}(X))\neq 0.
\end{equation*}
Thus $P\leq_H \mathrm{vx}(X)\leq_G P$. So there exists $t\in \mathrm{N}_G(P)$ such that $X$ has a source pair $(P, {^tV})$. We choose a $\mathcal{C}_H(P, {^tV})$. Let $\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}': \mathcal{C}_H(P, {^tV})\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_H(P))$ be a $P$-slash functor. By Lemma \ref{Lem2.5} (2), after twisting by a linear character we may assume $\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}'(X)= \mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}(X)$. It is known that $\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}'(X)$ is an indecomposable projective $k[\mathrm{N}_H(P)/ P]$-module. Since $\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}'(X)$ contains a trivial submodule $_{\mathrm{N}_H(P)/ P}k$, we have $\mathrm{top}(\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}'(X))= _{\mathrm{N}_H(P)/ P}k$. Thus $X$ is an endopermutation Scott module with respect to the source pair $(P, {^tV})$ and slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_{H, P}'$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem2}
Let $G$ be a finite group and let $P$ be a $p$-subgroup. Suppose $Q$ is a $p$-subgroup of $P$. Let $x\in G$. Let $(P, S)$ be a fusion-stable endopermutation source pair of $G$ and we choose a $\mathcal{C}_G(P, S)$. Let $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}: \mathcal{C}_G(P, S)\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q))$ be a $Q$-slash functor. Set $(^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q})(Y)= {^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(Y)}$ for any object $Y$ of $\mathcal{C}_G(P, S)$. Then $^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}: \mathcal{C}_G(P, S)\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(^xQ))$ is a $^xQ$-slash functor.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $V, W$ be any two objects of $\mathcal{C}_G(P, S)$. By the definition of $Q$-slash functor, there exists a commutative diagram of $k(C_G(^xQ)\times C_G(^xQ))\Delta\mathrm{N}_G(^xQ)$-modules:

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
{\mathrm{Hom}_{k {^xQ}}({^xV}, {^xW})} \arrow[rd] \arrow[rr] &                                                             & {\mathrm{Hom}_k({^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(V)}, {^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(W)})} \\
                                                    & {\mathrm{Br}_{\Delta(^xQ)}(\mathrm{Hom}_k({^xV}, {^xW}))} \arrow[ru, "\cong"] &                                             .
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

Since $V\cong {^xV}$ as $kG$-modules, we get the following commutative diagram of $k\mathrm{N}_{G\times G}(\Delta(^xQ))$-modules:

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
{\mathrm{Hom}_{k {^xQ}}(V, W)} \arrow[r, "\cong"] \arrow[d]  & {\mathrm{Hom}_{k{^xQ}}({^xV}, {^xW})} \arrow[d]  \\
{\mathrm{Br}_{\Delta(^xQ)}(\mathrm{Hom}_k(V, W))} \arrow[r, "\cong"] & {\mathrm{Br}_{\Delta(^xQ)}(\mathrm{Hom}_k({^xV}, {^xW}))}.
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

We define $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, {^xQ}}(V)= {^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(V)}$. Then we get the following commutative diagram of $k(C_G(^xQ)\times C_G(^xQ))\Delta\mathrm{N}_G(^xQ)$-modules:

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
{\mathrm{Hom}_{k {^xQ}}(V, W)} \arrow[rd] \arrow[rr] &                                                             & {\mathrm{Hom}_k(\mathrm{Sl}_{G, {^xQ}}(V), \mathrm{Sl}_{G, {^xQ}}(W))} \\
                                                    & {\mathrm{Br}_{\Delta(^xQ)}(\mathrm{Hom}_k(V, W))} \arrow[ru, "\cong"] &.                                      
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

So $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, {^xQ}}$ is a $^xQ$-slash functor.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
First, the existence of $t(Q)$ follows from Lemma \ref{lem1}. Now we assume for any $Q\in \mathcal{S}$, $M_Q$ is slash indecomposable.

Claim: For any subgroup $Q\leq P$ and any $Q$-slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}: \mathcal{C}_G(P, V)\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q))$, $\mathrm{Res}_{Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)$ is indecomposable.

Proof of claim: First we assume $Q= 1$. Then by Lemma \ref{Lem2.5} (2), $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}$ is the identity functor. Therefore, $\mathrm{Res}_{Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)= M$, which is indecomposable. Now we assume $Q> 1$ and we use induction on $|P|/ |Q|$. 

Case $P= Q$: By \cite[Lemma 4.8]{MR3789018}.

Case $P> Q$: First we assume $Q\in\mathcal{S}$. There exists a $k\mathrm{N}_G(Q)$-module $N_Q$ such that $\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}^G(M)\cong M_Q\oplus N_Q$. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a full subcategory of $\mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q))$ consisting of $\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}^G(M), M_Q, N_Q$ and $\mathrm{Decom}(M_Q), \mathrm{Decom}(N_Q)$. Then $\mathcal{C}$ is a Frobenius-friendly category. There exists a $Q$-slash functor $\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{N_G(Q), Q}: \mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q))$ such that $\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}^G(M))= \mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)$. So we get
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Res}_{Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}(\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M))\cong \mathrm{Res}_{Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(M_Q))\oplus \mathrm{Res}_{Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q)).
\end{equation*}
Since $M_Q$ is slash indecomposable, we only need to prove $\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q)= 0$. 

If $\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q)\neq 0$, it has an indecomposable summand $L$. Let $R$ be a vertex of $L$. By Lemma \ref{Lem2.5}, there exists an indecomposable summand $\widetilde{L}\mid N_Q$ with vertex $B$ such that $B\geq R\geq Q$. If $R= Q$, then $P= Q$ by Lemma \ref{Lem2.5} (4), which contradicts our assumption $P> Q$. Since $\widetilde{L}\mid \mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}^G\mathrm{Ind}_P^G(V)$, by Mackey's decomposition formula, we get $\widetilde{L}\mid \mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{N}_{^tP}(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_{^tP}(Q)}^{^tP}(^tV)$ for some $t\in G$. So $Q\leq B\leq_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)} \mathrm{N}_{^tP}(Q)$. By \cite[Lemma 3.2]{MR3565442}, we have $\mathrm{N}_{^tP}(Q)\leq_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)} P$. Thus we may assume $Q\leq R\leq B\leq P$.

By Lemma \ref{Lem2.6}, there exists a Frobenius-friendly category $\mathcal{D}$ of $k\mathrm{N}_G(Q)$-modules that contains the essential image of $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}$. Let $\mathrm{Sl}_{N_G(Q), R}': \mathcal{D}\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q, R))$ be an $R$-slash functor. By Lemma \ref{Lem2.6}, we know $\mathrm{Sl}_{N_G(Q), R}'\circ \mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}: \mathcal{C}_G(P, V)\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q, R))$ is an $R$-slash functor. Since $\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q, R)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(R)}\circ \mathrm{Sl}_{G, R}$ is also an $R$-slash functor, we may twist $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}'$ by a linear character $\chi: \mathrm{N}_G(Q, R)/ R\mathrm{C}_G(R)\rightarrow k^\times$ such that 
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}'\circ \mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)\cong \mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q, R)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(R)}(\mathrm{Sl}_{G, R}(M)).
\end{equation*}
By the induction hypothesis, $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}'\circ \mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)$ is indecomposable. Now we let $\mathcal{D}'$ be a full subcategory of $\mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q))$ consisting of $\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M), \widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(M_Q), \widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q)$ and $\mathrm{Decom}(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(M_Q)), \mathrm{Decom}(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q))$. We require $L\in \mathrm{Decom}(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q))$. There exists an $R$-slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}'': \mathcal{D}'\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q, R))$ such that 
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}'\circ \mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)= \mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}''\circ \mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M).
\end{equation*}

Since $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}''(L)\neq 0$, we get $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}''(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q))\neq 0$. For any $P$-slash functor $\mathrm{Sl}_P: \mathcal{D}'\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(Q, P))$, $\mathrm{Sl}_P\circ\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(M_Q)\neq 0$ since $P$ is a vertex of $M_Q$. Thus there exists an indecomposable summand $Y\mid \widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(M_Q)$ such that $\mathrm{Sl}_P(Y)\neq 0$. Let $T$ be a vertex of $Y$. Then by Lemma \ref{Lem2.5}, we have 
\begin{equation*}
P\leq_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)} T\leq_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)} P,
\end{equation*}
where the first inclusion comes from $\mathrm{Sl}_P(Y)\neq 0$. So we may assume $T= P$. In order to prove $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}''(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(M_Q))\neq 0$, we only need to prove $R\leq_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)} P$, but we already have $R\leq P$.

Combining  $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}''(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(N_Q))\neq 0$ and $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}''(\widetilde{\mathrm{Sl}}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), Q}(M_Q))\neq 0$, we know $\mathrm{Sl}_{\mathrm{N}_G(Q), R}''\circ \mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)$ is a direct sum of two nonzero modules, which contradicts its indecomposability.

Then we assume $Q$ is just a subgroup of $P$. There exists $x\in G$ such that $^xQ\leq P$ is fully normalized. By Lemma \ref{lem2}, $^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}: \mathcal{C}_G(P, V)\rightarrow \mathrm{mod}(k\mathrm{N}_G(^xQ))$ is a $^xQ$-slash functor. So $\mathrm{Res}_{^xQ\mathrm{C}_G(^xQ)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(^xQ)}{^x\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}}(M)$ is indecomposable. Thus $\mathrm{Res}_{Q\mathrm{C}_G(Q)}^{\mathrm{N}_G(Q)}\mathrm{Sl}_{G, Q}(M)$ is indecomposable.

By the definition of slash indecomposability we also need to consider the case $Q\nleq P$, but in such case, we only need to use Lemma \ref{lem2} and the Remark 3 after Definition \ref{Def2.8} directly.

Let $q$ be an odd prime power. Assume $q\equiv 7~\text{or}~9(\text{mod}~16)$. Let $G= \mathrm{PSL}(2, q)~\text{or}~\mathrm{SL}(2, q)$, and let $P\in \mathrm{Syl}_2(G)$, then 

(1) $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$ is saturated; 

(2) If $Q\leq P$ is a fully normalized subgroup, then $Q\lhd P$;

(3) $P$ is not Dedekind.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By \cite[p.8, Theorem 2.3]{MR2848834}, $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$ is saturated.

First, we assume $G= \mathrm{PSL}(2, q)$. Since $q\equiv 7~\text{or}~9(\text{mod}~16)$, we get $v_2(q^2-1)= 4$. Note that $|G|= q(q^2-1)/ 2$. So $|P|= 8$. By \cite[p.223, Theorem 8.27]{MR4928289}, we get $P\cong D_8$. Let $Q$ be a subgroup of $P$ such that $Q\ntriangleleft P$. By direct calculation, we know $|Q|= 2$ and $|\mathrm{N}_P(Q)|= 4$. By \cite[p.297, paragraph 4]{MR3418059}, we know all involutions in $G$ are conjugate with each other. So there exists $g\in G$ such that $gQg^{-1}= Z(P)$. But $|\mathrm{N}_P(Q)|= 4< 8= |\mathrm{N}_P(Z(P))|= |P|$. So $Q$ is not fully normalized.

Now we assume $G= \mathrm{SL}(2, q)$. $v_2(|G|)= v_2(q(q^2- 1))= 4$. So $|P|= 16$. Let $\pi: G\rightarrow \mathrm{PSL}(2, q)$ be the canonical epimorphism. Let $x$ be an involution in $G$, then $x^2= I$. Thus $x$ is diagonalizable and the set of eigenvalues of $x$ can only be $\{1\}$ or $\{-1\}$ since $\mathrm{det}(x)= 1$. So $x= -I$ and $\mathrm{ker}(\pi)= \langle -I\rangle$. Therefore, $\pi(P)\cong D_8$. $P$ is a group of order 16 and has a unique involution. The quotient of $P$ by this involution is $D_8$. By direct calculation, we know 
\begin{equation*}
P= \langle a, b\mid a^8= 1, b^2= a^4, bab^{-1}= a^{-1}\rangle\cong Q_{16}. 
\end{equation*}

Let $Q$ be a subgroup of $P$ such that $Q\ntriangleleft P$. By direct calculation, $|Q|= 4$ and $|\mathrm{N}_P(Q)|= 8$. Since $Q$ contains the unique involution $-I$, we get $|\pi(Q)|= 2$ and thus there exists $g\in G$ such that $\overline{g}\pi(Q)\overline{g}^{-1}= Z(\pi(P))= \pi(\langle a^2\rangle)$. So $gQg^{-1}= \langle a^2\rangle$. Now we have $|\mathrm{N}_P(Q)|= 8< 16= |\mathrm{N}_P(\langle a^2\rangle)|$. So $Q$ is not fully normalized.

$D_8, Q_{16}$ are non-abelian and non-Dedekind follows from direct calculation.

In order to prove there are infinitely many such groups, it suffices to prove there are infinitely many odd primes $q$ satisfying $q\equiv 7~\text{or}~9(\text{mod}~16)$, which follows from the well-known Dirichlet's theorem.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lemnor}
Let $G$ be a finite group and let $P\in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ (we do not require $p$ is odd). TFAE: (1) For any fully normalized subgroup $Q$ in $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$, we have $Q\lhd P$; (2) For any $Q\leq P$, we have $p\nmid [G: \mathrm{N}_G(Q)]$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
$(1)\Rightarrow (2):$ Let $Q\leq P$ and let $g\in G$ be such that $R:= {^gQ}$ is a fully normalized subgroup of $P$. By our assumption, $R\lhd P$ and thus $Q= {^{g^{-1}}R}\lhd {^{g^{-1}}P}$. So $p\nmid [G: \mathrm{N}_G(Q)]$.

$(2)\Rightarrow (1):$ Suppose $Q\leq P$ is fully normalized. There exists an $S\in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ such that $Q\leq S\leq \mathrm{N}_G(Q)$. There exists $g\in G$ such that $^gS= P$. So 
\begin{equation*}
|\mathrm{N}_P({gQg^{-1}})|= |g\mathrm{N}_{g^{-1}Pg}(Q)g^{-1}|= |\mathrm{N}_S(Q)|= |S|= |P|.
\end{equation*}
Since $|\mathrm{N}_P({gQg^{-1}})|\leq |\mathrm{N}_P(Q)|$, we get $Q\lhd P$.

Let $G= {^2F_4(2)'}$, the Tits group. Let $P\in \mathrm{Syl}_3(G)$.  Then

(1) $\mathcal{F}_P(G)$ is saturated; 

(2) If $Q\leq P$ is a fully normalized subgroup, then $Q\lhd P$;

(3) $P$ is not Dedekind.
<!-- end example-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By ATLAS \cite{MR827219}, $|G|= 2^{11}\cdot3^3\cdot5^2\cdot13$ and $\mathrm{PSL}(3, 3)$ is a subgroup containing a Sylow $3$-subgroup of order $27$. Let $P$ be the subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}(3, 3)$ consisting of upper unitriangular matrices. $P$ is not abelian. Let $Q\leq P$. We only need to show $3\nmid[G: \mathrm{N}_G(Q)]$. If $|Q|\in \{1, 27\}$, then we are done. If $|Q|= 9$, then $[P: Q]= 3$, which implies $Q\lhd P$. So $P\leq \mathrm{N}_G(Q)$ and $3\nmid [G: \mathrm{N}_G(Q)]$. 

Assume $|Q|= 3$. Let $x\in Q\backslash\{1\}$. Again, by ATLAS, there exists a unique conjugacy class containing elements of order $3$ and the size of the centralizer is $108= 2^2\cdot 3^3$. So $3\nmid [G: \mathrm{N}_G(Q)]$.
