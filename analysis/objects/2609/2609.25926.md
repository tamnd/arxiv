<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{theo:diffeomorph_ideal}
Let $n_{\textnormal{bin}} = n_{\textnormal{spec}}$ and the sensitivity functions satisfy $\int_{I_i}s_b(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ if and only if $ b = i$, then $\Psi : \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \to \mathbb R_{>0}^{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}$ is a $\mathcal{C}^1$-diffeomorphism.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
To prove this we can use the Hadamard global inverse function theorem~\cite{Ruzhansky2014inversion}. For this we consider $\ln ( \Psi)$ and have to prove that it is a $C^1$-map with non vanishing Jacobian and proper. As a combination and summation of differential functions $\ln ( \Psi)$ is a $C^1$-map. Then for a point $v \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ we get, because of the $ s_b(E) \geq0$ and following from the assumption that  $\int_{I_i}s_b(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ if and only if $ b = i$, that $\Psi_b(v) = \int\limits_{I_b} s_b(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big)  \mathrm{d}E $ and for the Jacobian at $v$
\begin{align*}
	J(\ln \Psi)_{b,i}  = \frac{\partial}{\partial v_i} [ \ln \Psi_b(v)]&= -\left(\Psi_b(v)\right)^{-1} \int\limits_{I_i} s_b(E) E^{-c} \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big)  \mathrm{d}E\\
	& \leq -  E_{i+1}^{-c} \frac{ \int\limits_{I_i} s_b(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big)  \mathrm{d}E }{\int\limits_{I_b} s_b(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big)  \mathrm{d}E}
\end{align*}
for $b = 1, \dots, n_{\textnormal{bin}}$ and $i = 1, \dots, n_{\textnormal{spec}}$. This gives us that $J(\ln \Psi)_{b,i} \neq 0 $ if and only if $ b = i$  and  $J(\ln \Psi)_{b,b} < 0 $. Since $J(\ln \Psi)$ is a diagonal matrix we have $|\det J(\ln \Psi)| \geq \prod_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} E_{i+1}^{-c}$. This holds for all $v \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$. To show that $\ln ( \Psi)$ is proper we look at $|v| \to \infty$. In this case we must also have a $i = 1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}$ with $|v_i| \to \infty$. For $\ln (\Psi_i)$ we then get
\begin{align*}
	\lim\limits_{ |v_i| \to \infty} \left| \ln (\Psi_i(v))  \right| &=	\lim\limits_{ |v_i| \to \infty} \left| \ln \left( \int\limits_{I_i} s_i(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E \right)\right| \\
	&\geq \lim\limits_{ |v_i| \to \infty} \left| \ln \left( \exp\Big( -  E_{i+1}^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big)  \int\limits_{I_i} s_i(E) \mathrm{d}E \right) \right| \\
	&\geq \lim\limits_{ |v_i| \to \infty} \left| E_{i+1}^{-c} v_i \right|- \left| \ln \left( \int\limits_{I_i} s_i(E) \mathrm{d}E \right) \right| \\
	&= \infty
\end{align*}
and therefore $|\ln (\Psi) | \to \infty$ whenever $|v| \to \infty$. Theorem 2.2 from~\cite{Ruzhansky2014inversion} then gives us that $\ln(\Psi): \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \to \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}$ is a $\mathcal{C}^1$-diffeomorphism and therefore also that  $\Psi : \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \to \mathbb R_{>0}^{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}$ is a $\mathcal{C}^1$-diffeomorphism.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{theo:uniqueness_ideal}
Let $n_{\textnormal{bin}} = n_{\textnormal{spec}}$. If the sensitivity functions satisfy that $\int_{I_i}s_b(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ if and only if $ b \leq i$, then $\Psi $ is injective.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Assuming that we have $v,\tilde{v} \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ with $\Psi(v) = \Psi( \tilde{v})$ we denote by $j$ the largest index from $\{ 1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ such that $v_j \neq \tilde{v}_j$. Then we have $v_l = \tilde{v}_l$ for all $j < l \leq n_{\textnormal{spec}}$ and for this $j$ it holds that
\begin{align*}
	&\quad \Psi_j(v) -\Psi_j(\tilde{v}) \\
	&= \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \int\limits_{I_i} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E - \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \int\limits_{I_i} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot \tilde{v}_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E \\
	&=  \sum_{i=j}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \int\limits_{I_i} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E - \sum_{i=j}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \int\limits_{I_i} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot \tilde{v}_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E \\
	&= \int\limits_{I_j} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_j \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E -  \int\limits_{I_j} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot \tilde{v}_j \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E \\
	                                                                                                                                                  
	                                                                                                                                                               
	&=  \int\limits_{I_j} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_j \Big )\left( 1 - \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot (\tilde{v}_j -v_j) \Big )\right) \, \mathrm{d}E.
\end{align*}
Since $v_j \neq \tilde{v}_j$ we have $\exp( -  E^{-c} \cdot (\tilde{v}_j -v_j)) \neq 1$, $\Psi_j(v) -\Psi_j(\tilde{v}) \neq 0$ and consequently a contradiction to the assumption. This gives us that for all $y \in  \operatorname{Im}(\Psi)$ there exists  only one $v \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ so that $\Psi (v) = y$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:surjectivity}
Let $n_{\textnormal{bin}} = n_{\textnormal{spec}}$ and the sensitivity functions satisfy that $\int_{I_i}s_b(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ if and only if $ b \leq i$, then for every $\tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} \in \mathbb R_{>0}$ there exist $\tilde{y_1} ,\dots, \tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}-1} \in \mathbb R_{>0}$ so that $(\tilde{y_1} ,\dots, \tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}) \notin \operatorname{Im}(\Psi)$ and for all $y \in \operatorname{Im}(\Psi)$ with $y_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} = \tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}$ holds that $y_1 >\tilde{y_1}   ,\dots, y_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}-1}>\tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}-1} $.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For $j = 1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}-1$ let $\tilde{y}_j$ be given as
\begin{align*}
	\tilde{y}_j  &=  \int\limits_{I_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}} s_{j}(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot \tilde{v}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E > 0,
\end{align*}
where $\tilde{v}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} \in \mathbb R$ is the unique solution to
\begin{align*}
	\tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} &=  \int\limits_{I_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}} s_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot \tilde{v}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E.
\end{align*} 
From this follows also that for all  $y \in \operatorname{Im}(\Psi)$ with $y_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} = \tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}$ and their corresponding solutions $v \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ it must hold that $v_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} = \tilde{v}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}$. For $j = 1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}-1$ we then have
\begin{align*}
	y_j &= \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \int\limits_{I_i} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E \\
	&> \int\limits_{I_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E \\
	&=  \int\limits_{I_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}} s_j(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot \tilde{v}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}} \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E \\
	&= \tilde{y}_j.
\end{align*}
This implies directly that $(\tilde{y_1} ,\dots, \tilde{y}_{n_{\textnormal{bin}}}) \notin \operatorname{Im}(\Psi)$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{theo:uniqueness_overdetermined}
Let $n_{\textnormal{bin}}>n_{\textnormal{spec}}$ and assume that for all $i \in \{1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ we can find a sensitivity function $s_{b_i}$ so that $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ for all $j\geq i$ and $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E = 0$ for all $j<i$. Then for each $y \in  \operatorname{Im}(\Psi)$ there exists a unique $v \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ so that $\Psi (v) = y$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Assuming that we have $v, \tilde{v} \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ with $\Psi(v) = \Psi(\tilde{v}) = y$, then for the function $\tilde{\Psi} : \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \to \mathbb R_{>0}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ with
\begin{align*}
	\tilde{\Psi}_j(v) &= \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}} \int\limits_{I_i} s_{b_j}(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot v_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E
\end{align*}
for $j \in \{1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ holds $\tilde{\Psi}(v) = \tilde{\Psi}(\tilde{v}) $. The same argumentation from theorem \ref{theo:uniqueness_ideal} gives us then that $v = \tilde{v}$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{theo:uniqueness_underdetermined}
Let $n_{\textnormal{bin}} < n_{\textnormal{spec}}$ and $k \in \{1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ be so that for all $i \in \{k,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ we can find a sensitivity function $s_{b_i}$ with $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ for all $j\geq i$ and $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E = 0$ for all $j<i$, then for $v,\tilde{v} \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ follows from $\Psi(v) = \Psi(\tilde{v})$ that $v_i =  \tilde{v}_i $ for all $i \in \{k,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We define the function $\tilde{\Psi} : \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}-k+1} \to \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}-k+1}_{>0}$ with 
\begin{align*}
	\tilde{\Psi}_j(v) &= \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}-k+1} \int\limits_{I_{i+k-1}} s_{b_{j+k-1}}(E) \exp\Big( -  E^{-c} \cdot w_i \Big) \, \mathrm{d}E
\end{align*}
for $j \in \{1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}-k+1\}$ and $w \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}-k+1}$. For  $v, \tilde{v} \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}$ with $\Psi(v) = \Psi(\tilde{v}) = y$ we also have $\tilde{\Psi} ( v_k,\dots,v_{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}) = \tilde{\Psi} ( \tilde{v}_k,\dots,\tilde{v}_{n_{\textnormal{spec}}}) $ and therefore $v_i = \tilde{v}_i$ for all $i \in \{ k,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}} \}$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{theo:bin_sinogram_decomposition_ideal}
Let  $\ker(A) = \{0\}$ and for all $i \in \{1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ let there exist a sensitivity function $s_{b_i}$ so that $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ for all $j\geq i$ and $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E = 0$ for all $j<i$. Then $\Phi$ is injective.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Theorem \ref{theo:uniqueness_overdetermined} gives us that $\Psi$ is injective. Since $\ker(A) = \{0\}$ we also know that $A$ is injective. Therefore $\Psi\circ A$ is also injective. Because of \eqref{eq:Phi_Psi_A} this then holds also for $\Phi$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{theo:bin_sinogram_decomposition_non_ideal}
In case there exists a $k \in \{1,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ so that $\ker(A^{(k)}) = \{0\}$ and for all $i \in \{k,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ we can find a sensitivity function $s_{b_i}$ with $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E \neq 0$ for all $j\geq i$ and $\int_{I_j}s_{b_i}(E)  \mathrm{d}E = 0$ for all $j<i$, it holds for all  $y \in \operatorname{Im}(\Phi)$ that there only exists one $z \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{mat}}}$ so that $\Phi(z) = y$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For a given $y \in \operatorname{Im}(\Phi)$ let $z, \tilde{z} \in \mathbb R^{n_{\textnormal{mat}}}$ satisfy $y = \Phi(z) = \Phi(\tilde{z})$. For $v = Az$ and $\tilde{v} = A \tilde{z}$ then holds $y = \Psi(v) = \Psi(\tilde{v})$. Thanks to theorem \ref{theo:uniqueness_underdetermined} we know that $v_i = \tilde{v}_i$ for all $i \in \{ k,\dots,n_{\textnormal{spec}}\}$ and therefore that $A^{(k)} z = A^{(k)} \tilde{z}$. Since $A^{(k)}$ is injective we must have $z =  \tilde{z}$.
