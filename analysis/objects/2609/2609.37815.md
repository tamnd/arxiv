<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:ess}
  An \emph{event structure with symmetry} consists of an event
  structure $E$ equipped with a family $\widetilde{E} = \{
  \widetilde{E} [x, y]\}_{x, y \in {\mathscr C}(E)}$, where each $\widetilde{E}[x, y]$ is a
  set of bijections $x 
  \mathrel{
    \mathrlap{\smash{\xrightarrow[\smash{\raisebox{0mm}{\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\scriptsize\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8} $\widetilde{}$}}]{\
          \ \ }}}
    \hphantom{\ \xrightarrow{\ }\ }}
 y$, satisfying the following
  axioms:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Congruence}: The family $\widetilde{E}$ contains all
    identity bijections, and it is closed under composition of
    bijections, inverses, and restriction of a bijection to a
    sub-configuration.
    \item \emph{Bisimulation}: for every $\theta \in \widetilde{E}[x, y]$, if
      $x \subseteq x' \in {\mathscr C}(E)$, then there exists $y' \in
      {\mathscr C}(E)$ and $\theta' \in \widetilde{E} [x', y']$ such that
      $\theta'_{\mid x} = \theta$.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:spans} Event structures with symmetry arise from the combination of two abstract ideas: internal equivalence relations as
  spans in a category (e.g. \cite{johnstone2002sketches}), and bisimulation as open maps
  \cite{JNW}. Indeed, to give an event structure with symmetry
  $(E, \widetilde{E})$ is to give a jointly monic span
  $E \xleftarrow{l} R \xrightarrow{r} E$ satisfying the axioms for an
  internal equivalence relation in the category of event structures
  and in which the maps $l$ and $r$ are open. (\emph{Open} means for
  $l$ that it is rigid and if $x \in {\mathscr C}(R)$ and $y \in {\mathscr C}(E)$ with
  $l x \subseteq y$ then there is $z \in {\mathscr C}(R)$ such that
  $x \subseteq z$ and $l z = y$.) The notions of maps and homotopy we
  give below, as well as games with symmetry, could all be developed
  in this style.
<!-- end remark-1 -->

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
An \emph{event structure with
    symmetry and polarity} is an event structure with symmetry
  $\mathcal{E} = (E, \widetilde{E})$ equipped with a labelling function
  ${\mathrm{pol}} : E \to \{ \boxminus, \boxplus\}$, such that every bijection in $\widetilde{E}$
  preserves the polarity of events.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
If $\mathcal{E}$ is an event structure with polarity and symmetry,
the \emph{Scott category} of $\mathcal{E}$ is the subcategory $\mathscr C(\mathcal{E})$ of $\mathbf{pSet}$ with
as objects the elements of ${\mathscr C}(E)$, and morphisms generated by:
\begin{itemize}
\item symmetry bijections $x 
  \mathrel{
    \mathrlap{\smash{\xrightarrow[\smash{\raisebox{0mm}{\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\@setfontsize\scriptsize\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8}\@viiipt{8} $\widetilde{E}$}}]{\
          \ }}}
    \hphantom{\ \xrightarrow{}\ }}
 y$;
\item \emph{positive} inclusions maps $x \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus$}}} y$, where $x \subseteq y$ and $y \setminus x$ only contains positive events;
    and
    \item \emph{negative} \emph{reverse} inclusions maps $y \mathrel{\xleftarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}\joinrel\rhook} x$, represented by partial
      functions $y \rightharpoonup x$ acting as identity
      on $x$ and undefined otherwise.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
One can see an ordinary event structure with symmetry (without
  polarity) as one with polarity having only positive events. This gives an embedding ${\mathbf {EvSym}} \to {\mathbf {EvSym}}_\mathrm{p}$. This
  way, the category of configurations of Definition~\ref{def:cat-config}
  becomes a special case of the Scott category.

<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
A functor $p : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ has \emph{essentially unique lifts}
  when the following condition holds: for every $B \in \mathcal{C}$ and
  $h : X \to pB$ in $\mathcal{D}$, if $f : A \to B$ and $f' : A' \to B$
  satisfy $pf = pf' = h$ then there is a unique isomorphism
  $\varphi : A \to A'$ such that $p\varphi = {\relax id}_X$ and
  $f' = f \circ \varphi$.

  Similarly, we say that $p : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ has \emph{essentially
    unique pseudo-lifts} when the following condition holds: for every
  $B \in \mathcal{C}$ and $h : X \to pB$ in $\mathcal{D}$, if
  $(f : A \to B, \theta : pA \xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-.5mm}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}} X)$ and
  $(f' : A' \to B, \theta' : pA' \to X)$ satisfy $pf = \theta \circ h$
  and $pf' = \theta' \circ h$ then there is a unique isomorphism
  $\varphi : A \to A'$ such that $\theta' = \theta \circ p\varphi$ and
  $f' = f \circ \varphi$.

<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
The tricategory $\mathbf{Setoid}\text{-}\mathbf{Prof}$ is given by the following components.
\begin{itemize}
\item Objects are small categories ($\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}, \dots$).
\item For objects $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$, $\mathbf{Setoid}\text{-}\mathbf{Prof}[\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}]$ is the 2-category
    $[\mathcal{D}^{\relax op} \times \mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Setoid}]$ of pseudo-functors, pseudo
    natural transformations, and equivalences. We write $P : \mathcal{C}
    \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!$\mid$\hfil\cr}} \mathcal{D}$ if $P \in \mathbf{Setoid}\text{-}\mathbf{Prof}[\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}]$.
\item The identity on $\mathcal{C}$ is the hom-functor
   $\mathcal{C}(-, =) : \mathcal{C}^{\relax op} \times \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$ post-composed with
    the embedding $\mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Setoid}$ that regards a set as a discrete
    setoid.
\item The composition of $P : \mathcal{C} \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!$\mid$\hfil\cr}} \mathcal{D}$ and $Q : \mathcal{D} \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!$\mid$\hfil\cr}}
  \mathcal{E}$ is the profunctor $Q \odot P : \mathcal{E}^{\relax op} \times \mathcal{D} \to
  \mathbf{Setoid}$ whose action on objects is given by
  \[
(Q\odot P)(E, C) = \intop^{D \in \mathcal{D}} P(D, C) \times Q(E, D)
\relax\ifmmode \egroup $\hfil \egroup
  \end{trivlist}                                                     
  \else \@badmath \fi
where the integral sign denotes a pseudo-coend in $\mathbf{Setoid}$, constructed as
the set $\sum_{D \in \mathcal{D}} P(D, C) \times Q(E, D)$ with the smallest
equivalence relation $\sim$ such that
\begin{align*}
& (D, p, q) \sim (D, p', q)  && \text{when } p \sim p' \text{ in }
  P(D, C), \\
& (D, p, q) \sim (D, p, q')  & &\text{when } q \sim q' \text{ in }
                               Q(E, D), \\
 & (D, P(\alpha, C)(p'), q) \sim (D', p', P(E, \alpha)(q))  &&
                                                             \text{for
                                                             all } \alpha
                                                             \in
                                                             \mathcal{D}(D',
                                                             D), \\
&&&\quad  p' \in P(D', C), q \in Q(E, D)
                                                             .
\end{align*}
We omit the action of the functor $Q\circ P$ on morphisms, as well as
the horizontal composition of 2-cells and 3-cells.
\end{itemize}

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
We emphasize that $\mathbf{Setoid}\text{-}\mathbf{Prof}$ is not a fully weak tricategory. The hom-2-categories are all
  strict and so the ``vertical'' composition of 2-cells and 3-cells is
  strictly associative and unital. However, the tricategory is not
  fully strict because the composition of profunctors is only
  associative and unital up to equivalence, where an \emph{equivalence}
  is a pair of 2-cells which are inverses of each other up to a 3-cell.

The tricategory ${\mathbf{Strat}}$ is given by the following components.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Tricategories are generally hard to construct because of the many coherence axioms, but in ${\mathbf{Strat}}$ all axioms involving equations of 3-cells hold automatically because the structure is thin. So it is enough to check that the axioms of a bicategory hold up to the equivalence relation represented by the 3-cells. This is much simpler and follows from our description of composition using pullback and hiding. (We emphasize that these axioms \emph{do not} hold up to equality, and that a three-dimensional structure is really needed to describe the compositional framework.)

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Throughout this section we consider pre-strategies on a game $A$, rather than $A^\perp \mathbin{\raisebox{0.15em}{\resizebox{\mywid}{\mylen}{$\Vert$}}} B$. We regard them as morphisms $\varnothing \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!$\mid$\hfil\cr}} A$ in ${\mathbf{Strat}}$ and study the post-composition with copycat on $A$. \label{def:lifting-properties}
A pre-strategy $\sigma : S\to A$ is said to be:

\end{itemize}

\label{lem:minusinnocence}
For a pseudo-receptive pre-strategy $\sigma : S \to A$, the following \emph{$\boxminus$-innocence} condition holds: if $s, s \in S$ with $s \rightarrowtriangle s'$ and both $s, s'$ are negative, then $\sigma s \rightarrowtriangle \sigma s'$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We sketch a proof of the lemma. Necessarily $x \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^s x_1 \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{s'} x'$ for some $x, x_1, x' \in \mathscr C(S)$. Suppose for a contradiction that $s \rightarrowtriangle s'$ and $\sigma s$ and $\sigma s'$ are concurrent, so both $\sigma x \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{\sigma s} \sigma x_1 \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{\sigma s'} \sigma x'$ and also $\sigma x \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{\sigma s'} y \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{\sigma s} \sigma x'$ for some $y \in \mathscr C(A)$. By applying pseudo-receptivity to the inclusions $\sigma x \hookrightarrow y \hookrightarrow \sigma x'$ we obtain $x \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{t'} x_2 \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{t} x''$ and a symmetry $\theta : \sigma x'' \cong \theta x'$ where $\theta$ fixes $\sigma x$ and acts by $\sigma t' \mapsto \sigma s'$ and $\sigma t \mapsto \sigma s$. We now have two pseudo-lifts of the inclusion $\sigma x \hookrightarrow \sigma x'$, namely $x \hookrightarrow x'$ and $x \hookrightarrow x''$, so there must be a symmetry $\varphi : x \cong x'$ which maps to $\theta$. Necessarily $\varphi$ acts by $s \mapsto t$ and $s' \mapsto t'$. Since $s \rightarrowtriangle s'$ we must have $t \rightarrowtriangle t'$ which is a contradiction as $t'$ comes before $t$ in the chain $x \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{t'} x_2 \mathbin{{{\mathrel-\joinrel\subset}}}^{t} x''$.

<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
A \emph{thin game} is an event structure with symmetry $A = (A, \widetilde{A})$ equipped with two sub-families of $\widetilde{A}$, denoted $\widetilde{A}_+$ and $\widetilde{A}_-$, both satisfying the conditions of event structures with symmetry (Def.~\ref{def:ess}) and subject to the following axioms:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $\theta \in \widetilde{A}_+[x, y] \cap \widetilde{A}_-[x, y]$, then $x = y$ and $\theta = {\relax id}_{x}$.
\item If $\theta \in \widetilde{A}_+[x, y]$, if 
$x \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus$}}} x'$ and $\theta' \in \widetilde{A}[x', y']$ with $\theta'|_{x} = \theta$ then $\theta' \in \widetilde{A}_+[x, y]$.
\item If $\theta \in \widetilde{A}_-[x, y]$, if 
$x \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus$}}} x'$ and $\theta' \in \widetilde{A}[x', y']$ with $\theta'|_{x} = \theta$ then $\theta' \in \widetilde{A}_-[x, y]$.
\end{itemize}
A \emph{thin strategy} on a thin game $(A, \widetilde{A}_+, \widetilde{A}_-)$
is a pre-strategy $\sigma : S \to A$ (in the sense of
\S\ref{subsec:pre-strategies}, {\it i.e.}\ $S = (S, \widetilde{S})$ is an ordinary
event structure with symmetry) which is innocent and strongly
receptive, and additionally satisfies the following condition: for all $\theta \in \widetilde{S}$, if $\sigma \theta \in \widetilde{A}_+$ then $\theta = {\relax id}_x$ for some $x\in {\mathscr C}(S)$.
<!-- end definition-15 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
There is a bicategory $\mathbf{Thin}$ of thin games, thin strategies between them as morphisms, and (weak) isomorphisms of strategies as 2-cells.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
There is a pseudo-functor of tricategories $J : \mathbf{Thin} \to {\mathbf{Strat}}$, where the bicategory $\mathbf{Thin}$ is regarded as having only identity 3-cells.
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We give only a sketch of the construction. For a thin game $\mathcal{A} = (A, \widetilde{A}_+, \widetilde{A}_-)$ we define the plain game $J\mathcal{A} = A$. A thin strategy is in particular a strategy in the sense of ${\mathbf{Strat}}$ and indeed this extends to an embedding $\mathbf{Thin}[\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}] \to {\mathbf{Strat}}[J\mathcal{A}, J\mathcal{B}]$. 

We must then relate identities and compositions. We have already stated that ${\relax C\!\!C}_A$ and ${\relax C\!\!C}^t_A$ are equivalent strategies.  To show that $\odot$ and $\odot^t$ are also equivalent, one direction uses the universal property of the pseudo-pullback and the other that the pullback of thin strategies is in fact a bipullback \cite{cg2}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:embedding}
Let $\mathcal{A} = (A, \widetilde{A}_+, \widetilde{A}_-)$ and $\mathcal{A}' = (A, \widetilde{A}_+', \widetilde{A}_-')$ be thin games with the same underlying event structure with symmetry and polarity $A$. Then there is an internal equivalence $\mathcal{A} \simeq \mathcal{A}'$ in the bicategory $\mathbf{Thin}$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
An equivalence in $\mathbf{Thin}$ is given by a pair of strategies that are each other's pseudo-inverse. The key observation is that the thin copycat strategy ${\relax C\!\!C}^t_A \to A^\perp \mathbin{\raisebox{0.15em}{\resizebox{\mywid}{\mylen}{$\Vert$}}} A$ can simultaneously serve as a strategy $\mathcal{A} \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!$\mid$\hfil\cr}} \mathcal{A}'$ and a strategy $\mathcal{A}' \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!$\mid$\hfil\cr}} \mathcal{A}$, in addition to its usual role as identity strategy on $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{A}'$. That this forms an equivalence follows quickly from the fact that copycat is an identity strategy. The only minor difficulty is in verifying the thinness axiom for copycat in each case. This follows from the observation that any bijection in the intersection of $\widetilde{A}_+$ and $\widetilde{A}_-'$ (similarly $\widetilde{A}_-$ and $\widetilde{A}_+'$) must be an identity bijection. This can be shown by induction on the size of the bijection. We omit the details.
<!-- end proof-13 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
The pseudo-functor $J$ is identity on morphisms and higher-cells, and Proposition~\ref{prop:embedding} makes it `essentially injective on objects' in a bicategorical sense. It would seem that $J$ can thus be regarded as a higher-categorical embedding, in a sense that is consistent with existing 1-categorical notions of property-like structure and embeddings \cite{kelly1997property,Carboni1994modulated}. We content ourselves with this informal observation, as more work is required for a formal statement: it is still unclear whether $J$ reflects equivalences in an appropriate sense and higher-categorical generalizations can be subtle.

<!-- definition-16: body_md -->
A \emph{stable family} is a nonempty family ${\mathcal F}$ of finite sets which is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Complete:} if $Z \subseteq_{\relax fin} {\mathcal F}$ is compatible, meaning that there
    exists $x \in {\mathcal F}$ with $\bigcup Z \subseteq x$, then $\bigcup Z \in {\mathcal F}$;
  \item \emph{Stable:} if $Z \subseteq_{\relax fin} {\mathcal F}$ is compatible and nonempty, then
    $\bigcap Z \in {\mathcal F}$;
  \item \emph{Coincidence-free:} for all $x \in {\mathcal F}$ and all $e, e' \in x$ with
    $e \not\nobreak\mkern -2mu = e'$, there exists $x_0 \in {\mathcal F}$ with $x_0 \subseteq x$  satisfying $e \in x_0 \iff e' \notin x_0$. 
\end{itemize}

\label{def:collapse}{\relax Let $\sigma:S\to A$ be a  $\boxminus$-innocent pre-strategy with essentially unique $\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}}$-lifts.  (In particular these assumptions hold if $\sigma$ is pseudo-receptive.)
W.l.o.g.~assume that $S^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}$ and $A^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ are disjoint. 

Taking
$$
{\cal S} \coloneqq {\{  x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup (\sigma x)^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}\  | \  x\in {\mathscr C}(S) \} }\,.
$$
defines
a stable family; this only depends on the $\boxminus$-innocence of $\sigma$ ---see Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} below.  (Noting that $(\sigma x)^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} = \sigma (x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}})$ we shall simply write $\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ from now on.)

It is easy to see   that for any $x\in {\mathscr C}(S)$ there is a bijection
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:collapse-bij}
x\cong x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}
\end{equation}
taking $s \in x$ to $s$ if $s$ is positive
and to $\sigma(s)$ if $s$ is negative.  Equip $\cal S$ with a symmetry $\widetilde{\cal S}$, the family comprising all composite bijections
$$
x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} \cong x \cong^\theta_S y \cong y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} 
$$
for $x\cong^\theta_S y$ in the isomorphism family of $S$, using the bijections \eqref{eq:collapse-bij}. Using that $\sigma$  is both $\boxminus$-innocent and  
pseudo-receptive, we can show $\widetilde{\cal S}$ is an isomorphism  
family---Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} below.
 
Define 
$\mathit{collapse}(S)$ to be 
the event structure $\Pr({\cal S})$ with the polarity of $p\in \Pr({\cal S})$ the same as the polarity of ${\it top} (p)$.  Its symmetry 
$\widetilde{\mathit{collapse}(S)}=\Pr(\widetilde{\cal S})$ where $\widetilde{\cal S}$ is the
stable family $\widetilde{\cal S}$ which comprises all composite bijections
$$
x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} \cong  x \cong^\theta  y \cong y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}\,,
$$
where $x \cong_S^\theta y$.  
The family $\widetilde{\cal S}$  is shown to be a stable family below in Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}.  
  
The prime configurations of $\cal S$ are precisely the configurations $[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ for $s\in S$   ---see Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} below.  (By definition, the prime configurations are the events of $\mathit{collapse}(S)$.) 
Define the function 
$\mathit{collapse}(\sigma): \mathit{collapse}(S) \to A$ to take a prime configuration $[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$, where $s\in S$, to $\sigma(s)$. 
}

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:wneginnoc}
Let $\sigma:S\to A$ be a $\boxminus$-innocent  pre-strategy. Suppose that $x_0\subseteq x$ in ${\mathscr C}(S)$ and that $
\sigma x_0 \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} y \subseteq \sigma x$ in ${\mathscr C}(A)$.  Then there is a (necessarily unique) $z\in {\mathscr C}(S)$ such that $x_0\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z \subseteq x$ and $\sigma z =y$.

Assume  $x_0\subseteq x$ in ${\mathscr C}(S)$ and
$\sigma x_0 \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} y \subseteq \sigma x$
in ${\mathscr C}(A)$.   
Define the set 
$
z\coloneqq {\{  s\in x\  | \  \sigma(s) \in y \} }.
$

Clearly $x_0\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z \subseteq x$ and $\sigma z = y$.  
It remains to show that $z$ is a configuration of $S$. 
As $z\subseteq x$ the set $z$ is clearly consistent.  
So for $z$ to be a configuration of $S$ it suffices to show it down-closed.  And for this it suffices to
show for all $s \in z\setminus x_0$ if $s'\rightarrowtriangle_S s$ then $s' \in z$ ---this property already holds of all events in $x_0$ as it is a configuration.   
Suppose $s \in z\setminus x_0$.  Then $s$ is negative, so as $\sigma$ is  $\boxminus$-innocent, $\sigma(s')\rightarrowtriangle_A \sigma(s)$. As $\sigma(s)\in y$, and $y$ is down-closed,  $\sigma(s')\in y$ making $s'\in z$ ---and $z$ down-closed so a configuration.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:collapse}
Above, in Definition~\ref{def:collapse},  on the assumption that the pre-strategy $\sigma:S\to A$ is $\boxminus$-innocent, 
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)]
the family $\cal S$ is stable. 
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, on the further assumption that $\sigma$ is pseudo-receptive,
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(B)]
 the family $\widetilde{\cal S}$ is an isomorphism family, alternatively described by
 $
\widetilde{\cal S} = {\{  \theta^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \widetilde{\sigma} \theta^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}\  | \  \theta \in \widetilde{S} \} }\,;
$
 \item[(C)]
  the prime configurations of $\cal S$ are precisely those of the form $[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma [s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$, for $s\in S$; and 
 the functions $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)$ and $g$ are maps 
 of event structures with polarity for which $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma) g = \sigma\,,$
 such that 
 \item[(D)]
 if $z\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z_1, z_2$ and 
$\mathit{collapse}(\sigma) z_1 =  \mathit{collapse}(\sigma) z_1$ then $z_1=z_2$, for all $z, z_1, z_2\in {\mathscr C}(\mathit{collapse}(S))$; and 
 \item[(E)]
 $g x_1 = g x_2 \implies x_1\cong_S x_2$, for all $x_1, x_2\in {\mathscr C}(S)$. 
 \end{enumerate}

(A). 
We show $\cal S$ satisfies the three axioms of stable families, namely\@ifnextchar[{\@xfootnote}{\refstepcounter
   {mpfootnote}\xdef\alph{mpfootnote}{\alph{mpfootnote}}\@footnotemark\@mpfootnotetext}{It suffices to show binary versions of completeness and stability. We use $\uparrow$ to denote binary compatibility: $u \uparrow v$ means that $u$ and $v$ admit an upper bound in the family.}: (i) if $u \uparrow v$  in ${\cal S}$ then $u\cup v\in {\cal S}$; (ii) if $u \uparrow v$ in ${\cal S}$ then $u\cap v\in {\cal S}$; and (iii) if $e_1, e_2$ are distinct events in $u \in {\cal S}$ then there is $v \in {\cal S}$ with $v\subseteq u$ and $(e_1\in v \iff e_2 \notin v).$
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Suppose that $x_1, x_2, x \in{\mathscr C}(S)$ satisfy $x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  \subseteq x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ and $x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  \subseteq x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ in ${\cal S}$. 
Then, $[x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \subseteq x$ in ${\mathscr C}(S)$ and $
\sigma [x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} \sigma x_1 \cup \sigma x_2 \subseteq  \sigma x
$ in ${\mathscr C}(A)$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:wneginnoc}, there is $z\in{\mathscr C}(S)$ with $\sigma z = \sigma x_1 \cup \sigma x_2$ and $[x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z \subseteq x.$ Then, $z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}\in  {\cal S}$ with 
$
z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} = (x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}) \cup (\sigma x_1 \cup \sigma x_2)^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$. 
\item[(ii)]
Again, suppose $x_1, x_2, x \in{\mathscr C}(S)$ satisfy $x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  \subseteq x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ and $x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  \subseteq x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ in ${\cal S}$. We first claim there is $y\in{\mathscr C}(A)$, necessarily unique, such that $\sigma ([x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \cap [x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]) \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} y  \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus$}}} \sigma x_1  \cap \sigma x_2.
$
To see this suppose $a\in  \sigma x_1  \cap \sigma x_2$ has negative polarity.  Then if 
$a' < a$ with $a'$ positive there is $s\in [x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \cap [x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]$ for which $\sigma(s) =a'$.  Thus, $a'\in \sigma ([x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \cap [x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}])$.
Hence we achieve the claim by 
 taking
$y\coloneqq \sigma ([x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \cap [x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]) \cup {\{  a\in \sigma x_1  \cap \sigma x_2\  | \   a\hbox{ is negative}  \} }.$ 

Now $\sigma ([x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \cap [x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]) \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} y  \subseteq  \sigma x.$ By  Proposition~\ref{prop:wneginnoc}, there is $z\in{\mathscr C}(S)$ with $\sigma z = y$ and
$
[x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \cap [x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] \mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z \subseteq x.
$
We have
$
z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} = (x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cap x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}})\cup ( \sigma x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  \cap \sigma x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}),
$ as required. 

\item[(iii)]
Suppose $e_1, e_2 \in x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$  are distinct in the configuration of ${\cal S}$ obtained from $x\in {\mathscr C}(S)$. 
As remarked in Definition~\ref{def:collapse}, there is a bijection $x\cong x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$. So 
in all cases there is a subconfiguration $v$ of $x$ such that $v^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup \sigma v^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ separates them. For instance, assuming $e_1=s_1\in x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}$ and $e_2=\sigma(s_2)\in \sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ we can take $v$ to be a subconfiguration of $x$  containing one but not the other of $s_1$ and $s_2$. 
\end{enumerate}
We have shown $\cal S$ is a stable family and so determines an event structure $\mathit{collapse}({\cal S}) \coloneqq \Pr({\cal S})$. 

Now, further assuming that $\sigma$  
is pseudo-receptive, we will show properties (B)--(E). It will be helpful to note that, for $x_1, x_2\in {\mathscr C}(S)$, if $x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} = x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ then $x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}  =  x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}$ and $\sigma x_1 =\sigma x_2$ and we claim that moreover there exists a symmetry $x_1 \cong_S^\theta x_2$ such that ${\relax id}_{[x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]} \subseteq \theta$ and $\widetilde{\sigma}\theta ={\relax id}_{\sigma x_1}.$ Indeed, as $x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} = x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}$, their down-closures, the configurations $[x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]$ and $[x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]$ are equal and hence $\sigma [x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}] = \sigma[x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}]\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} \sigma x_1 = \sigma x_2.$
 As $\sigma$  has essentially unique $\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}}$-lifts, we obtain $x_1 \cong_S^\theta x_2$ as above.  

(B). 
By definition, $\widetilde{\cal S}$ comprises all bijections $\theta': x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} \cong x \cong_S^\theta y \cong y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$
as $\theta$ ranges over the isomorphism family of $S$. 
It can be checked that $\theta' = \theta^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \widetilde{\sigma} \theta^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$, so that $\widetilde{\cal S} = {\{  \theta^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \widetilde{\sigma} \theta^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}\  | \  \exists x, y.\ x\cong_S^\theta y \} }.$

We
prove that
  $\widetilde{\cal S}$ inherits the properties required of an isomorphism family from the isomorphism family of $S$.
 It is easy to see reflexivity and symmetry of $\widetilde{\cal S}$.  To check transitivity, suppose given $\theta': x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} \cong x\cong_S^\theta y \cong y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ and $\phi': z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} \cong y\cong_S^\phi w \cong w^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma w^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$
such that $y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  = z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}.
$
 By the paragraph below (A) above, there is $y \cong_S^\psi z$ which restricts to the identity on $y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} = z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}$ and induces the identity between $\sigma y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ and $\sigma z^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$.  The composite bijection
 $x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} \cong x\cong_S^{\phi\psi\theta} w \cong w^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma w^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ provides the composition $\phi'\theta'$.

 necessary and sufficient condition for one bijection to extend another in $\widetilde{\cal S}$.

  (C). 
We show that the functions $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)$ and $g$ are maps of event structures with polarity.

First, a characterization of the primes of $\cal S$, which constitute the events of $\mathit{collapse}(S)$. 
Let $x\in{\mathscr C}(S)$. 
From $(1)$ above, independently of the choice of $x$, the sub-configurations of 
$x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} $ are exactly $y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$,  
where $y$ is a sub-configuration of $x$. It follows that prime configurations of $\cal S$ always take the form 
$[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma [s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$, for some $s\in S$. 

The function $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma): \mathit{collapse}(S) \to A$ takes a prime $[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma [s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ to $\sigma(s)$.  A typical configuration of $\mathit{collapse}(S)$ is a set
$$
{\{  p\in \Pr({\cal S})\  | \  p \subseteq x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  \} }\,,
$$
for some $x\in{\mathscr C}(S)$.  Hence  $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)$ takes it to $\sigma x$ in a locally injective way.  

It is easy to see that $g: S \to \mathit{collapse}(S)$ is a map.  It takes a configuration $x\in{\mathscr C}(S)$ to the set of prime sub-configurations of $x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} $; it is locally injective because of the bijection $x\cong x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} $.
In addition,  
$$\mathit{collapse}(\sigma) g (s) = 
\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)([s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma [s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}) =\sigma(s)\,,$$
 for all $s\in S$.  Hence, $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma) \circ g = \sigma$.  
 
 
  (D).  
 Assume  $z, z_1, z_2\in {\mathscr C}(\mathit{collapse}(S))$ with $z\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z_1$ and $z\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z_2$.  Suppose  
$\mathit{collapse}(\sigma) z_1 =  \mathit{collapse}(\sigma) z_2$.  Then $\bigcup z_1 = x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ and $\bigcup z_2 = x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$, 
for some $x_1, x_2\in {\mathscr C}(S)$. From $z\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-0.35ex}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus$}}} z_1, z_2$, we see that $x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} = x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}$ and, from $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma) z_1 =  \mathit{collapse}(\sigma) z_2$, that $\sigma x_1 = \sigma x_2$.  Therefore  
 $z_1=z_2$. 
 
  (E).   
  Finally we show that
$g x_1 = g x_2 \implies x_1\cong_S x_2$, for all $x_1, x_2\in {\mathscr C}(S)$. Supposing $g x_1 = g x_2$ we also have 
$x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x_1^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}  = \bigcup g x_1 = \bigcup g x_2 =  x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup\sigma x_2^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$ which implies $x_1\cong_S x_2$, by (1) above.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:collapseequiv}
If $\sigma$ is any pseudo-receptive (and therefore $\boxminus$-innocent) pre-strategy,   
$g:\sigma \simeq_{\mathrm{s}} \mathit{collapse}(\sigma)$.

Recall the 
map $g:S\to \mathit{collapse}(S)$ which 
takes $s\in S$ to the prime configuration $[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}$. 

We produce a converse map
$f:\mathit{collapse}(S) \to S$
via Zorn's Lemma.
By Zorn's Lemma there is a $\subseteq$-maximal map $f: S_0 \to S$ such that  $g f ={\relax id}_{S_0}$ and 
$$
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1.8em, column sep=1.8em]
	{S_0} & S \\
	& A
	\rightarrow["f", from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\rightarrow["{\sigma_0}"', from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\rightarrow["\sigma", from=1-2, to=2-2]
\end{tikzcd}
$$
commutes, with a rigid embedding $S_0 \hookrightarrow \mathit{collapse}(S)$ s.t. $\sigma_0$ is  
the restriction of $\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)$ to $S_0$, {\it i.e.}~$\sigma_0$ is the composition
$$
\sigma_0: 
S_0 \hookrightarrow \mathit{collapse}(S) {{\stackrel{\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)}{\longrightarrow}}} A\,.
$$
(Clearly the empty event structure embeds rigidly in $\mathit{collapse}(S)$ and  
satisfies the above  
constraints;  Zorn's Lemma ensures this can be extended maximally.)

We show that $\sigma_0 =\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)$.  Suppose otherwise, that $S_0 \not\nobreak\mkern -2mu = \mathit{collapse}(S)$.  Then, there is $p\in \Pr({\cal S})$ such that $p\notin S_0$ yet $[p) 
\in{\mathscr C}(S_0)$.  By assumption, $g f [p) = [p)$.  

In  Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}, we saw that a 
prime configuration $p$ of $\cal S$, {\it i.e.}~an event of $\mathit{collapse}(S)$, has the form 
$$p=[s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}} \cup \sigma [s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}}\,,$$
 for some $s\in S$.
 We have $g[s) = [p)$ 
 so 
$g[s) = gf [p)$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}, it follows that  
 $ [s) \cong_S f[p)$. 
 Because $[s) {\stackrel{s}{\mathrel-\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\subset}} [s]$ we also have $f[p) {\stackrel{s_0}{\mathrel-\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\subset}} [s_0]$ with $[s]\cong_S [s_0]$ extending $ [s) \cong_S f[p)$.
 
 Extend $S_0$ to $S_0'=S_0\cup {\{ p \} }$, a larger substructure of $\mathit{collapse}(S)$. The map $f$ extends to a map $f':S_0'  \to S$ which  acts so $f'(p) =s_0$, and the function $\sigma_0$ to $\sigma_0': S_0' \to A$ so $\sigma_0'(p) = \sigma(s_0)$.  
Clearly  we still have $\sigma f' = \sigma_0'$.
With the new value $p$, we maintain $g f' ={\relax id}_{S_0'}$, as we have
 $$
 g f'(p) = g(s) = [s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxplus}}\cup \sigma [s]^{{\scriptscriptstyle\boxminus}} = p\,.
 $$
 This contradicts the maximality of $\sigma_0$, unless $S_0 =\mathit{collapse}(S)$ as we require. 
 
 To conclude we show $f$ and $g$ form   an equivalence $$f:\mathit{collapse}(\sigma) \simeq_{\mathrm{s}} \sigma:g\,.$$ Certainly $g f ={\relax id}_{\mathit{collapse}(S)}$. 
Let $x\in {\mathscr C}(S)$. We have
$$
g(fg\, x) = gfg\, x = {\relax id}_{\mathit{collapse}(S)} g\, x =  g\, x\,,
$$
whence 
$$
fg\, x \cong_S x\,,
$$
using Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
(universal characterization)\label{cor:absview}
Let $\sigma:S\to A$ and $\sigma':S'\to A$ be pre-strategies where $\sigma$ is  $\boxminus$-innocent and pseudo-receptive and $\sigma'$ is collapsed.  
 Then for a map of pre-strategies $f:\sigma \to \sigma'$ there is a unique  map of pre-strategies $h: \mathit{collapse}(\sigma) \to \sigma'$ such that $f=h g$:
$$
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1.8em, column sep=2.2em]
	S & {\mathit{collapse}(S)} & {S'} \\
	& A &
	\rightarrow["f", dotted, bend left=32, from=1-1, to=1-3]
	\rightarrow["g", from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\rightarrow["\sigma"', from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\rightarrow["{\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)}"{description}, from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\rightarrow["h", dashed, from=1-2, to=1-3]
	\rightarrow["{\sigma'}", dotted, bend left=18, from=1-3, to=2-2]
\end{tikzcd}
$$

Amongst pre-strategies in $A$ which are $\boxminus$-innocent and pseudo-receptive,
 $\mathit{collapse}$ is left adjoint to the inclusion of the subcategory of collapsed pre-strategies in the category of all pre-strategies.  
 
 If $\sigma:S \to A$ is  $\boxminus$-innocent and pseudo-receptive then  $\widetilde{\mathit{collapse}(S)} \cong {\mathit{collapse}(\widetilde{S})}$ and $\widetilde{\mathit{collapse}(\sigma)} \cong {\mathit{collapse}(\widetilde{\sigma})}$.
