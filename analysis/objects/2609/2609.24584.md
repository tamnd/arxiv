<!-- introthm-1: body_md -->
\label{intro thm: equivalence Higgs bundles}
The twisted pullback and pushforward along the moduli map $\mathcal{X} \to X$ define equivalences of algebraic stacks
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_{n, Dol}^d(X) \cong \mathcal{M}_{n \cdot w(-d), Dol}^0(\mathcal{X}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end introthm-1 -->
<!-- introthm-2: body_md -->
\label{intro thm: twisted character variety}
The connected component of the Betti moduli stack of $\mathcal{X}$ corresponding to the numerical invariant $n \cdot w(-d)$ is isomorphic to the twisted Betti moduli space of $X$, i.e. we have
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_{n \cdot w(-d), B}^0(\mathcal{X}) = \left[ \bigg\{ (A_i, B_i)_{i = 1}^g \in GL_n^{2g} \;\middle|\; \prod_{i=1}^{g}[A_i, B_i] = \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2 \pi \mathrm{i}}{n} d} Id \bigg\} / GL_n \right].
\end{equation}
<!-- end introthm-2 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm shende}
Under the isomorphism $ H^*(\mathcal{M}_{n, DR}^0(X)) \cong H^*(\mathcal{M}_{n, B}^0(X))$, the Künneth components $e_{i,j}$ of the $i$-th Chern class of the universal bundle $c_i(\mathcal{E}_{\text{univ}})$ are mapped to cohomology classes of weight $2i$. The same holds true in the twisted case, under the isomorphism $ H^*(\mathcal{M}_{n, DR}^d(X)) \cong H^*(\mathcal{M}_{n, B}^d(X))$.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- introthm-3: body_md -->
The cohomology classes on $\mathcal{M}_{n, B}^0(\mathcal{X})$ obtained by pulling back the $i$-th tautological classes on $\mathcal{M}_{n, DR}^0(\mathcal{X})$ have pure weight $2i$.
<!-- end introthm-3 -->
<!-- defi-1: body_md -->
\cite[Definition 1.2.7]{Taams}
To keep track of the multiplicities of all of the weights appearing, we denote the multiplicity vector of $\mathcal{E}$ at $p$ by
\[
\underline{m}(\mathcal{E}) \coloneqq m_0 \, w(0) + \dots + m_{n-1} \, w(n-1).
\]
<!-- end defi-1 -->
<!-- rmk-1: body_md -->
\cite[\S 1.2]{Taams} \label{tensor shift}
Since pullback commutes with tensor product and $w(i) \otimes w(j) = w(i+j)$, we see that tensoring with the tautological line bundle acts as a shift operator on the multiplicity vector, mapping $w(i)$ to $w(i+1)$.
<!-- end rmk-1 -->
<!-- defi-2: body_md -->
(See \cite[Definition 1.2.29]{Taams}).
Let $K(\mathcal{X})$ denote the Grothendieck group of the stacky curve $\mathcal{X}$. Then the degree is defined to be the unique group homomorphism
\begin{equation}
\deg\colon K(\mathcal{X}) \to \mathbb{Q}
\end{equation}
such that the pullback $\pi^*\colon K(X) \to K(\mathcal{X})$ is degree preserving.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{properties of pullback and pushforward}
The pullback and pushforward functors along $\pi$ have the following properties.
\begin{proplist}
    \item The pushforward $\pi_* \colon \operatorname{QCoh}(\mathcal{X}) \to \operatorname{QCoh}(X)$ is exact and restricts to a functor $\pi_* \colon \mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un(\mathcal{X}) \to \mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un(X)$. \cite[Theorem 2.3.4]{abramovich-vistoli}
    \item The pullback $\pi^* \colon \operatorname{QCoh}(X) \to \operatorname{QCoh}(\mathcal{X})$ is exact. \cite[Proposition 1.2.3]{Taams}.
	\item If $\mathcal{E}$ is a vector bundle on $\mathcal{X}$ with multiplicity $\underline{m}(\mathcal{E}) = m_0 \, w(0) + \dots + m_{n-1} \, w(n-1)$, then
	\begin{equation}
	\deg \mathcal{E} = \deg(\pi_* \mathcal{E}) + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} i \,  m_i ,
	\end{equation}
	see \cite[Proposition 1.2.30]{Taams}. \zlabel{formula degree-multiplicities}
	\item If $F$ is a coherent sheaf on $X$, then $\underline{m}(\pi^* F) = n \cdot w(0)$. Namely, the pullback $\pi^* F$ is endowed with the trivial $\mu_{n}$ action at $p$. \cite[Example 1.2.9]{Taams} \zlabel{multiplicity of pullback}
\end{proplist}
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\cite[Corollary 3.1.9, Theorem 3.1.12]{Taams} \label{connected components of the moduli stack}
The stack $\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un(\mathcal{X})$ is a smooth algebraic stack. Its connected components are the substacks
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_{\underline{m}}^{d}(\mathcal{X}) \coloneqq \left< \mathcal{E} \in \mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un(\mathcal{X}) \;\middle|\; ( \underline{m}(\mathcal{E}), \deg \mathcal{E}) = (\underline{m}, d)\right>,
\end{equation}
parametrizing vector bundles of fixed degree $d \in \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}$ and multiplicity vector $\underline{m} = m_0 \, w(0) + \dots + m_{n-1} \, w(n-1)$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{pullback and pushforward are equivalences}
The pullback and pushforward functors along the moduli map $\pi\colon \mathcal{X} \to X$ define inverse equivalences of algebraic stacks
\begin{equation}\label{eq: eqv Bun}
\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_{n}^d(X)} & {\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_{n \cdot w(0)}^d(\mathcal{X}),}
	\arrow[""{name=0, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "{\pi^*}", curve={height=-18pt}, from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow[""{name=1, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "{\pi_*}", curve={height=-18pt}, from=1-2, to=1-1]
	\arrow["\cong"{description}, draw=none, from=0, to=1]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
which preserve (semi)stability.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The pullback $\pi^*$ is degree-preserving by definition and by \zcref{multiplicity of pullback} the multiplicity of $\pi^*E$ is $n \cdot w(0)$ for all $E \in \mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_n(\mathcal{X})$. Dually, if the multiplicity of $\mathcal{E} \in \mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un^d(\mathcal{X})$ is $n \cdot w(0)$, then \zcref{properties of pullback and pushforward} shows that $\pi_*\mathcal{E}$ is a vector bundle of degree $d$. This shows that the functors in \eqref{eq: eqv Bun} are well defined. The two compositions are naturally isomorphic to the identity by \cite[Corollary 1.2.6, Theorem 1.2.10]{Taams}.\\
Finally, since rank and degree are preserved, so is (semi)stability.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm: equivalence for Bun}
The \textit{twisted} pullback and pushforward functors, defined by
\begin{equation} \label{equivalence bun}
\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_n^d(X)} &&& {\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_{n \cdot w(-d)}^0(\mathcal{X})}
	\arrow["{\pi^*(-) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p)}", shift left=2, from=1-1, to=1-4]
	\arrow["{\pi_*(\mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p) \otimes -)}", shift left=2, from=1-4, to=1-1]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
define inverse equivalences of algebraic stacks. These equivalences preserve (semi)stability, hence they restrict to equivalences
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_n^{d, \, ss}(X)} &&& {\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_{n \cdot w(-d)}^{0, \, ss}(\mathcal{X}).}
	\arrow["{\pi^*(-) \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p)}", shift left=2, from=1-1, to=1-4]
	\arrow["{\pi_*(\mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p) \otimes -)}", shift left=2, from=1-4, to=1-1]
\end{tikzcd}\]
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Using \zcref{pullback and pushforward are equivalences}, we only need to check that tensoring with $\mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p)= \mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{n}p)^{\otimes-d}$ gives an isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_{n \cdot w(0)}^d(\mathcal{X}) \xrightarrow{\cong} \mathcal{B}\kern -.5pt un_{n \cdot w(-d)}^0(\mathcal{X}),
\end{equation}
namely that $- \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p)$ acts on the numerical invariants as prescribed. For the multiplicity vector, recall that tensoring with the tautological line bundle $\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{n}p)$ acts as a shift, as observed in \zcref{tensor shift}. For the degree, use the formula for the degree of a product of vector bundles to get
\[
\deg\left( \mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p) \right) = \deg \mathcal{E} + n \left( -\frac{d}{n} \right) = 0.
\]
Finally, notice that
\[
\mu(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p)) = \frac{\deg \mathcal{E} - \operatorname{rk} \mathcal{E} \frac{d}{n}}{\operatorname{rk} \mathcal{E}} = \mu(\mathcal{E}) - \frac{d}{n},
\]
showing that tensoring with $\mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{1}{n}p)$ acts as a shift on the slope. Therefore (semi)stability is also preserved.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{omega mc(X)}
The canonical morphism $\pi^* \Omega_X \to \Omega_{\mathcal{X}}$ extends along the inclusion
\begin{equation}
n \operatorname{id}_{\pi^* \Omega_X} \otimes s^{n-1} \colon \pi^* \Omega_X \hookrightarrow \pi^* \Omega_X \otimes \mathcal{O}(\tfrac{n-1}{n}p)
\end{equation}
to an isomorphism $\pi^* \Omega_X \otimes \mathcal{O}(\tfrac{n-1}{n}p) \cong \Omega_{\mathcal{X}}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Away from the point $p$ the statement is trivial. Let $U \subseteq X$ be a neighborhood of $p$ admitting an étale morphism $U \to \mathbb{A}^1$ and let $\mathcal{U} \coloneqq \pi^{-1}(U)$ be the corresponding open in $\mathcal{X}$. We have a cartesian square
\begin{equation}
\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathcal{U}} && {[\mathbb{A}^1 / \mu_n]} \\
	U & {\mathbb{A}^1} & {\mathbb{A}^1 / \mu_n,}
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=1-3]
	\arrow["\pi"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow["\lrcorner"{anchor=center, pos=0.125}, draw=none, from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["\pi'", from=1-3, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=2-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["\cong"{description}, draw=none, from=2-2, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
where the horizontal arrows are étale and hence induce isomorphisms on the cotangent sheaves via pullback. Therefore, it suffices to prove the statement after replacing $\pi$ by $\pi'$.
Composing with the atlas $\mathbb{A}^1 \to [\mathbb{A}^1 / \mu_n]$, we get the following diagram
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathbb{A}^1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[x]} & \\
	{[\mathbb{A}^1 / \mu_n]} \\
	{\mathbb{A}^1 / \mu_n} & {\operatorname{Spec} k[x^n] = \operatorname{Spec} k[t].}
	\arrow["{q'}", from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow["{x \mapsto t = x^n}"{pos=0.6}, "q = \pi' \circ q'"' sloped, curve={height=-24pt}, from=1-1, to=3-2]
	\arrow["\pi'", from=2-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["{=}"{description}, draw=none, from=3-1, to=3-2]
\end{tikzcd}\]
Notice that we have the relation
\[
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} t = \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x^n = n \, x^{n-1} \, \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x,
\]
therefore the canonical morphism $q^* \Omega_X \to \Omega_{\mathbb{A}^1}$ corresponds to
\begin{align}
	k[x] \otimes_{k[t]} k[t]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} t \; &\to \; k[x]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x \\
	f(x) \otimes \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} t \quad &\mapsto \; f(x) \, n \, x^{n-1} \, \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x.
\end{align}
Since $q'$ is étale, it induces isomorphisms on the cotangent sheaves. Moreover, the section $x$ corresponds via $q'$ to the tautological section $s$ of $\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{n}p)$. In conclusion, we get that the canonical morphism $\pi^* \Omega_X \to \Omega_{\mathcal{X}} \cong \pi^*\Omega_X \otimes \mathcal{O}(\tfrac{n-1}{n}p)$ has the indicated form.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma Dol}
The functors $(\pi^*, \pi^{\#})$ and $(\pi_*, \pi_{\#})$ define inverse equivalences of categories
\begin{equation} \label{eq: eqv higgs bundles}
\begin{tikzcd}
	{\operatorname{Higgs}_n^d(X)} & {\operatorname{Higgs}^d_{n \cdot w(0)}(\mathcal{X}),}
	\arrow[""{name=0, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "{{(\pi^*, \, \pi^{\#})}}", curve={height=-18pt}, from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow[""{name=1, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "{{(\pi_*, \, \pi_{\#})}}", curve={height=-18pt}, from=1-2, to=1-1]
	\arrow["\cong"{description}, shift right=1.5, draw=none, from=0, to=1]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
which preserve (semi)stability.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
At the level of vector bundles this is \zcref{pullback and pushforward are equivalences}. We need to show that the two compositions act trivially also on the Higgs fields. Indeed we get
\begin{gather}
\varphi \xmapsto{\pi^{\#}} n \, \pi^* \varphi \otimes s^{n-1} \xmapsto{\pi_{\#}} \frac{1}{n} \, \pi_*(n \, \pi^* \varphi \otimes s^{n-1}) = \varphi\\
\eta \xmapsto{\pi_{\#}} \frac{1}{n} \, \pi_* \eta \xmapsto{\pi^{\#}} n \, \pi^*(\frac{1}{n} \, \pi_* \eta) \otimes s^{n-1} = n \, \pi^* \psi \otimes s^{n-1} = \eta,
\end{gather}
which proves the first statement. As in \zcref{pullback and pushforward are equivalences}, (semi)stability is preserved trivially.

\begin{tikzcd}

As discussed in \zcref{thm: equivalence for Bun}, twisting with $\mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{d}{n}p)$ shifts the weight at $p$ by $-d$. It also shifts the degree by $-d$ and thus the slope by $-\tfrac{d}{n}$, preserving semistability.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{prop: fundamental group of stacky curve}
The fundamental group of the stacky curve $\mathcal{X}$ is
\begin{equation} \label{eq: fundamental group of stacky curve}
\pi_1(\mathcal{X}) = \left\langle a_i, \; b_i, \; c \;\middle|\; \prod_{i = 1}^g [a_i, \, b_i] = c, \; c^n = 1 \right\rangle .

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{prop: monodromy representation}
Let $(\mathcal{E}, \nabla)$ be a vector bundle with a flat connection on $[\mathbb{D} / \mu_n]$. Then the representation of $\pi_1([\mathbb{D} / \mu_n], x) \cong \mu_n$ obtained by the monodromy action on $(\mathcal{E}, \nabla)$ coincides with the representation obtained as $\mathcal{E}\vert_{B \mu_n}$.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The statement amounts to observing that both representations come from the equivariant structure induced by the atlas
\begin{equation}
p \colon \mathbb{D} \to [\mathbb{D} / \mu_n].
\end{equation}
Since $\mathbb{D}$ is simply connected, this is the universal bundle and the monodromy action $(\mathcal{E}, \nabla)$ is the $\mu_n$-action on the pullback $(p^*\mathcal{E}, p^*\nabla)$. This is the trivial bundle with the trivial connection, so we get
\begin{equation}
\rho\colon  \mu_n \to \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})^n, \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}) = GL_n(\mathbb{C}) \subseteq GL_n(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})).
\end{equation}
On the other side, by restricting to $B\mu_n$ and pulling back to the universal bundle, we get a vector bundle on the point $\{0\}$ with a $\mu_n$-equivariant structure, i.e. a representation
\begin{equation}
\sigma\colon \mu_n \to GL_n(\mathbb{C}).
\end{equation}
To show that $\rho = \sigma$, notice that the inclusion $\{0\} \subseteq \mathbb{D}$ pulls back to the morphism of evaluation at $0$
\begin{equation}
\text{ev}_0 \colon GL_n(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})) \to GL_n (\mathbb{C}),
\end{equation}
so that $\sigma = \text{ev}_0 \circ \rho = \rho$.

Let $\mathbf{SSet}_G$ be the category of simplicial sets with a $G$-action. Thinking of $G$ as a constant simplicial group, we can use \cite[\S V.2]{SimplicialHomotopyTheory} to study the closed model structure on $\mathbf{SSet}_G$ with weak equivalences and fibrations given by morphisms which are weak equivalences and fibrations in $\mathbf{SSet}$.

The cofibrant objects with respect to this model structure are usually called projectively cofibrant.
With this notation, \cite[\S V, Cor 2.10]{SimplicialHomotopyTheory} says that $S$ is projectively cofibrant if and only if the action of $G$ on $S$ is free. Finally, we conclude the lemma using \cite[\href{https://kerodon.net/tag/03CJ}{Tag 03CJ}]{kerodon}, which states that if a diagram in a functor category $F \in [I, \mathbf{SSet}]$ is projectively cofibrant, then the natural map

\operatorname{Hom}_{SSch}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{SSch}(\Delta_\mathcal{X}, BGL_n) \times \Delta_\mathcal{X}, BGL_n) \\

\end{multline}

\end{equation}
preserves the mixed Hodge structures. Recall that
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}

Since $c_i$ has pure weight $2i$, we get that

has pure weight $2i$. Since the simplicial scheme $\Delta_\mathcal{X}$ is formed by several copies of $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}$ in each degree, its mixed Hodge structure only has weight zero. Therefore the $x_{i,j}$'s have weight zero and $\varepsilon_{i,j}$ has pure weight $2i$ for all $j$.

\label{van kampen}
Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a topological stack and suppose that it is connected, locally path connected and semilocally $1$-connected. Let $\mathcal{U}_1 \xhookrightarrow{i_1} \mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{U}_2 \xhookrightarrow{i_2} \mathcal{X}$ be open substacks such that $\mathcal{X} = \mathcal{U}_1 \cup \mathcal{U}_2$. Let $\mathcal{V} \coloneqq \mathcal{U}_1 \times_{\mathcal{X}} \mathcal{U}_2$. Suppose that $\mathcal{U}_1, \mathcal{U}_2$ and $\mathcal{V}$ are connected. Then, for $x \in \mathcal{V}$, there is an isomorphism
\[
\pi_1(\mathcal{X}, x) \cong \pi_1(\mathcal{U}_1, x) *_{\pi_1(\mathcal{V}, x)} \pi_1(\mathcal{U}_2, x).
\]
Equivalently, the following diagram is cocartesian
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{{\pi_1(\mathcal{V}, x)}} & {\pi_1(\mathcal{U}_1, x)} \\
	{\pi_1(\mathcal{U}_2, x)} & {\pi_1(\mathcal{X}, x).}
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow[from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\arrow[from=2-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["\lrcorner"{anchor=center, pos=0.125, rotate=180}, draw=none, from=2-2, to=1-1]
\end{tikzcd}\]

In order to simplify the notation, let $G\coloneqq \pi_1(\mathcal{X}, x)$, $H \coloneqq \pi_1(\mathcal{V}, x)$ and $G_l \coloneqq \pi_1(\mathcal{U}_l, x)$ for $l=1,2$. The inclusions
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathcal{V}} & {\mathcal{U}_l} & {\mathcal{X}}
	\arrow["{j_l}", hook, from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["j", curve={height=-18pt}, hook, from=1-1, to=1-3]
	\arrow["{i_l}", hook, from=1-2, to=1-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
give rise to a commutative diagram
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	H & {G_1} \\
	{G_2} & {G_1 *_H G_2} \\
	&& G,
	\arrow["{j_{1*}}", from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{j_{2*}}"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow["\iota_1", from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{i_{1*}}", curve={height=-12pt}, from=1-2, to=3-3]
	\arrow["\iota_2", from=2-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{i_{2*}}"', curve={height=12pt}, from=2-1, to=3-3]
	\arrow["v", dashed, from=2-2, to=3-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
where the dashed arrow can be filled out by the universal property of $G_1 *_H G_2$. We want to prove that $v$ is an isomorphism.

Consider the following $2$-commutative diagram
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathbf{Cov}_{\mathcal{X}}} &&& {G\mh\mathbf{Set}} \\
	\\
	{\mathbf{Cov}_{\mathcal{U}_1} \times_{\mathbf{Cov}_{\mathcal{V}}} \mathbf{Cov}_{\mathcal{U}_2}} &&& {G_1\mh\mathbf{Set} \times_{H\mh\mathbf{Set}} G_2\mh\mathbf{Set},}
	\arrow["{{\theta = F_x^{\mathcal{X}}}}", from=1-1, to=1-4]
	\arrow["\alpha", from=1-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["\beta"', from=1-4, to=3-4]
	\arrow["{{\varphi =F_x^{\mathcal{U}_1} \, \times_{F_x^{\mathcal{V}}} \, F_x^{\mathcal{U}_2}}}", from=3-1, to=3-4]
\end{tikzcd}\]
where $\alpha$ is induced by the restriction of covering spaces and $\beta$ is induced by $i_{1*}$ and $i_{2*}$.
Notice that $\alpha$ is an equivalence of categories, obtained by the gluing of covering spaces, $\varphi$ and $\theta$ are equivalences, as a consequence of \cite[Theorem 18.19]{Noohi-foundations}. Therefore, $\beta$ is an equivalence as well.

Notice moreover that the homomorphisms $(\iota_1, \iota_2)$ induce an equivalence of categories
\[
\gamma \colon (G_1 *_H G_2)\mh\mathbf{Set} \, \to \, G_1\mh\mathbf{Set} \, \times_{H\mh\mathbf{Set}} \,G_2\mh\mathbf{Set},
\]
making the following diagram $2$-commute
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{G\mh\mathbf{Set}} \\
	& {(G_1 *_H G_2)\mh\mathbf{Set}} \\
	{G_1\mh\mathbf{Set} \times_{H-set} G_2\mh\mathbf{Set}.}
	\arrow["{v^*}", from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["\beta"', from=1-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["\gamma", from=2-2, to=3-1]
\end{tikzcd}\]
Since both $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are equivalences, this shows that $v^*$ is also an equivalence and therefore, see \cite[Proposition 4.7.1]{Douady2020}, that $v$ is an isomorphism of groups.
