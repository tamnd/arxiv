<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{essential_vector}
Let $\pi$ be an irreducible admissible representation of $\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(L)$ which is generic. Then, there exists a non-negative integer $c$ such that $V(c) \neq 0$. Moreover, if $c(\pi) \geq 0$ is the minimal integer with this property, then: 
$$
\dim V(c(\pi)) = 1
$$
and $c(\pi)$ coincides with the analytic conductor of $\pi$, i.e the power of $q^{-s}$ in the $\varepsilon$-factor of $\pi$ with respect to an unramified additive character $\psi$ of $L$.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{jacquet-shalika_formula}
Let $S = S_\infty \cup S_\Pi$. Let $\phi = \otimes_w \phi_w \in \Pi$ and $\phi' = \otimes_w \phi'_w \in \Pi^\vee$ decomposable vectors such that $\phi_w$ (resp. $\phi_w'$) is the local factor at $w$ of $\phi_{\Pi}^\circ$ (resp. $\phi_{\Pi^\vee}^\circ$) whenever $w \notin S$. Then, we have the following equality:
\begin{equation}
\langle \phi, \phi' \rangle =  n \cdot {N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{n})^n \cdot D_E^{-n(n+3)/4}}\cdot c_n(E_\infty) \cdot L^{S}(\Pi, \ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1) \cdot \prod_{w \in S} \langle W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w} \rangle_w
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak{n}$ is the mirahoric level of $\Pi$, and $c_n(E_\infty) \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is some computable constant depending on our choice of Haar measures at archimedean places.  In particular, when $n=3$ and $E$ is totally real of dimension $d$, one has $c_n(E_\infty) = (4\pi)^{-d}$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\Phi \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_E^n)$ be a Schwartz–Bruhat function which is a tensor product $\Phi = \bigotimes_w \Phi_w$ of local Schwartz–Bruhat functions $\Phi_w \in \mathcal{S}(E_w^n)$ with:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Phi_w$ is the characteristic function $\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{O}_w^n}$ of $\mathcal{O}_w^n$ when $w\notin S_\Pi$ (i.e when $\Pi$ is unramified at $w$);
\item $\Phi_w$ is the characteristic function $\Phi_{w,c}$ of $(\wp_w^c)^{n-1} \times (1 + \wp^c_w)$ in $E_w^n$ when $w \in S_\Pi$, where $c = v_{\wp_w}(\mathfrak{n})$ is the mirahoric conductor of $\Pi_w$;
\item $\Phi_w$ is such that $\widehat{\Phi_w}(0) = 1$ when $w \in  S_\infty$. \\
\end{itemize}

We recall that the Fourier transform of $\Phi_w \in \mathcal{S}(E_w^n)$ is defined by:
$$
\widehat{\Phi_w}(x)= \int_{E_w^n} \Phi_w(y) \psi_w(\langle x,y \rangle) dy
$$
Consequently, when $w$ is a finite place:
$$
\widehat{\Phi_w}(0) = \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}_w^n) = N(\mathfrak{d}_w)^{-n/2} & \mbox{if } w \notin S_\Pi \\
         N(\wp_w)^{-nc} \cdot \mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}_w)^n =  N(\wp_w)^{-nc} N(\mathfrak{d}_w)^{-n/2} & \mbox{if } w \in S_\Pi
    \end{array}
\right. 
$$
and thus $\widehat{\Phi}(0) = D_E^{-n/2} \cdot N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{n})^{-n}$. Jacquet and Shalika \cite[Section 4]{J-S81-I} have shown, using the Rankin-Selberg method, that (see \cite[Proposition 3.1]{Zh14} for details):

\begin{equation}
\label{Rankin-Selberg_residue}
\langle \phi, \phi' \rangle =  \frac{n}{\mathrm{vol}(E^\times \backslash \mathbb{A}_E^1)\cdot  \widehat{\Phi}(0)} \cdot \mathrm{Res}_{s=1} \left( \prod_{w \notin S}\Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w}) \right) \cdot  \prod_{w \in S } \Psi(1,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w})
\end{equation}
where the local zeta integrals are defined by:
$$
\Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w}) = \int_{N_n(E_w) \backslash \ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(E_w)} \Phi_w((0,0,\dots,1)g) W_{\phi,w}(g) W_{\phi',w}(g) |\det(g)|_w^s dg
$$
for each place $w$ of $E$. It thus remains to compute the local factors $\Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w})$ in (\ref{Rankin-Selberg_residue}). \\


\textit{Spherical places not dividing $\mathfrak{d}_E$.} Let $w$ be a finite place of $E$ where $\Pi$ is unramified. If $w$ does not divide the different $\mathfrak{d}_E$ of $E$, then the local component $\psi_w$ of our fixed additive character $\psi_E$ is unramified, i.e. $\psi_w(\mathcal{O}_w) = 1$ and $\psi_w(\varpi_w^{-1}) \neq 1$. In this case, $\ensuremath{\mathrm{vol}}(N_n(\mathcal{O}_w)) = 1$. Then $W_{\phi,w} = W_{\Pi_w}^\circ$ and $W_{\phi',w} = W_{\Pi_w^\vee}^\circ$ are the spherical vectors of $\Pi_w$ and $\Pi_w^\vee$ with respect to $\psi_w$ and $\psi_w^{-1}$, and we know from \cite[Lemma 2.3]{J-S81-I} that:
$$
\Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w}) = L(\Pi_w \times \Pi_w^\vee,s)
$$

\textit{Spherical places dividing $\mathfrak{d}_E$.}  When $w$ divides $\mathfrak{d}$, $\psi_w$ has conductor $\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{-1}$. By assumption one has:
$$
W_{\phi,w}(g) = W_{\Pi_w}^\circ(\mathrm{diag}(d_w^{n-1},\dots, d_w,1)g)
$$
where $W_{\Pi_w}^\circ \in \mathcal{W}(\Pi_w,\psi_w^\circ)$ is the spherical vector with respect to the unramified additive character $\psi^\circ_w$ and $d_w \in E_w$ is the representative of $\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{-1}$ chosen in \S\ref{global_mirahoric_theory} so that $\psi_w(x) = \psi^\circ_w(d_wx) $. Then making change of variable and using \cite[Lemma 2.3]{J-S81-I}, we get:
\begin{equation}
\label{change_of_variable}
\begin{aligned}
\Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w}) &= |d_w^{-1}|_w^{n(n-1)s/2} \Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\Pi_w}^\circ,W_{\Pi_w}^\circ) \\
&= |d_w^{-1}|_w^{n(n-1)(s-1/2)/2} L(\Pi_w \times \Pi_w^\vee,s)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the factor $|d_w^{-1}|_w^{-n(n-1)/4}$  appearing in the second equality is the volume of $N_n(\mathcal{O}_w)$ for our choice of measure. Gathering the above unramified local computations, we get:
$$
\Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w}) =| \mathfrak{d}_E^S |^{n(n-1)(s-1/2)/2} L^S(\Pi\times \Pi^\vee,s) \prod_{w\in S} \Psi(s,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w})
$$

Substituting the above local computations in (\ref{Rankin-Selberg_residue}), we get:
$$
\langle \phi, \phi' \rangle =  \frac{n \cdot | \mathfrak{d}_E^S |^{n(n-1)/4} \cdot \mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_E(s)}{\mathrm{vol}(E^\times \backslash \mathbb{A}_E^1)\cdot \widehat{\Phi}(0)} \cdot L^{S}(\Pi, \ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1) \cdot  \prod_{w \in S} \Psi(1,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w})
$$
With our choice of Haar measure on $\mathbb{A}_E$, one has that $\mathrm{vol}(E^\times \backslash \mathbb{A}_E^1) = \mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_E(s)$ (see for exemple \cite[Theorem 4.11.3]{Leahy}) so the front constant simplifies. It remains to simplify the local factors at ramification and archimedean places. \\


\textit{Ramification and archimedean places.} If $w \in S$, there exists a constant $c_n(E_w) \in \mathbb{R}$, depending on our choice of Haar measures, such that (see \cite[(3.9)]{Zh14}):
$$
\Psi(1,\Phi_w,W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w})= c_n(E_w)  \cdot \widehat{\Phi}_w(0) \cdot \langle W_{\phi,w},W_{\phi',w} \rangle_w
$$
for all $W_{\phi,w} \in \mathcal{W}(\Pi_w,\psi_w)$, $W_{\phi',w} \in \mathcal{W}(\Pi_w^\vee,\psi_w^{-1})$ and $\Phi_w \in \mathcal{S}(E_w^n)$. This constant is equal to $1$ for the choice of Haar measures made in \cite[\S 2.1]{Zh14}, which is different from ours. The constant $c_n(\mathbb{R}) \in \mathbb{R}$ is computed in \cite[\S 14.11, Corollary]{AGBI}. When $n=3$, one has that $c_n(\mathbb{R}) = (4\pi)^{-1}$ (see \cite[Remark 5.2]{Che22}). If $w$ is non-archimedean, let $c>0$ be the mirahoric conductor of $\Pi_w$. Then one can see by comparing \cite[Theorem 3.2]{Jo22} and \cite[Theorem 3.7]{Jo22} that:
$$
c_n(E_w) = \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \widehat{\Phi_{w,c}}(0)^{-1} & \mbox{if } w  \mbox{ does not divide } \mathfrak{d}_E \\
        \widehat{\Phi_{w,c}}(0)^{-1} |\mathfrak{d}_w|_w^{n(n-1)/4} & \mbox{if } w  \mbox{ divides } \mathfrak{d}_E
    \end{array}
\right. 
$$
where $\Phi_{w,c}$ is the characteristic function of $(\wp_w^c)^{n-1} \times (1 + \wp^c_w)$ in $E_w^n$. Gathering all the above formulas, we get the announced formula, with $c_n(E_\infty) := \prod_{w\mid \infty} c_n(E_w)$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{split_ramified_computations}
Let $L$ be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic zero and let $\pi$ be an irreductible admissible representation of $\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(L)$ which is generic. Let $\psi$ be a non-trivial unramified additive character of $L$. Let $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle : \mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi) \times \mathcal{W}(\pi^\vee,\psi^{-1}) \to \mathbb{C}$ be the pairing defined by:
$$
\langle W,W' \rangle = \int_{N_{n-1}(L)\backslash \mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(L)}W\left(\begin{array}{ll}
g & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) {W'}\left(\begin{array}{ll}
 g & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right)dg
$$
Suppose that $\pi$ is ramified. Let $W_\pi^\circ$ (resp. $W_{\pi^\vee}^\circ$) be the essential vector of $\pi$ (resp. $\pi^\vee$) with respect to $\psi$ (resp. $\psi^{-1}$). Then one has:
$$
\langle W_\pi^\circ,W_{\pi^\vee}^\circ \rangle = \langle (W_{\pi^{\vee}}^\circ)^\vee,(W_{\pi}^\circ)^\vee\rangle = L^{imp}(\pi\times\pi^\vee,1)
$$
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{O}_L$ be the valuation ring of $L$, $\varpi$ be an uniformizer of $L$ and let $q = \#(\mathcal{O}_L/(\varpi))$. We recall that the Haar measures on $\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(L)$ and $N_{n-1}(L)$ are normalized so that $\ensuremath{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_L)) = \ensuremath{\mathrm{vol}}(N_{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_L))=1$. We first prove $(i)$. Using \cite[Proposition 4.7]{AM17}, one checks that:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle (W_{\pi^{\vee}}^\circ)^\vee,(W_{\pi}^\circ)^\vee\rangle &= \varepsilon(\pi^{\vee},\psi^{-1},1/2)^{n-1}\varepsilon(\pi,\psi,1/2)^{n-1} \langle \pi(\varpi_{n-1}^c)(W_\pi^\circ) ,\pi^\vee(\varpi_{n-1}^c)(W_{\pi^\vee}^\circ)\rangle\\
& =\langle W_\pi^\circ,W_{\pi^\vee}^\circ \rangle
\end{aligned}
$$
where the second equality follows from the $\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(L)$-equivariance of $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle$ and the fact that $\varepsilon(\pi^{\vee},\psi^{-1},1/2) = \varepsilon(\pi,\psi,1/2)^{-1}$. Now, using the Iwasawa decomposition one has that:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle W^\circ_{\pi},W^\circ_{\pi^\vee}  \rangle  &=  \int_{T_{n-1}(L)} \int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_L)} W^\circ_{\pi}\left(\begin{array}{ll}
tk & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) W^\circ_{\pi^\vee} \left(\begin{array}{ll}
tk & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) \delta_{B_{n-1}(L)}^{-1}(t)dkdt \\
&= \int_{T_{n-1}(L)} W^\circ_{\pi}\left(\begin{array}{ll}
t & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) W^\circ_{\pi^\vee} \left(\begin{array}{ll}
t & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) \delta_{B_{n-1}(L)}^{-1}(t)dt \\
&=  \sum_{t \in T_{n-1}(L)/T_{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_L)} W^\circ_{\pi}\left(\begin{array}{ll}
t & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) W^\circ_{\pi^\vee} \left(\begin{array}{ll}
t & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) \delta_{B_{n-1}(L)}^{-1}(t)\\
&= \sum_{\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}} W^\circ_{\pi}\left(\begin{array}{ll}
\varpi^\lambda & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) W^\circ_{\pi^\vee} \left(\begin{array}{ll}
\varpi^\lambda & \\
& 1
\end{array}\right) \delta_{B_{n-1}(L)}^{-1}(\varpi^\lambda) \\
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\delta_{B_m(L)}(\varpi^\mu) = q^{-\sum_{i=1}^m (m+1-2i) \mu_i}$ (for $m \geq 1$ and $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^m$) is the modulus character of $B_m(L)$. The second equality holds because $W^\circ_{\pi}$ and $W^\circ_{\pi^\vee}$ are fixed by the mirabolic subgroup and because the volume of $\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_L)$ with respect to the Haar measure on $\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(L)$ is equal to 1. Write the standard $L$-functions of $\pi$ and $\pi^\vee$ as:
$$
L(\pi,s) = \prod_{i=1}^{r} (1 - \alpha_i q^{-s})^{-1} \quad \mbox{ and } \quad L(\pi^\vee,s) = \prod_{j=1}^{r} (1 - \beta_j q^{-s})^{-1}
$$
where $r<n$ and $\alpha_i, \beta_i \in \mathbb{C}^\times$, as in (\ref{standard_L_factor}). Let $\alpha := (\alpha_i)_{i=1}^{r}$ and $\beta := (\beta_i)_{i=1}^{r}$. Then, using the explicit values of the essential vectors on $T_{n-1}(L)$, we obtain:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle W^\circ_{\pi},W^\circ_{\pi^\vee}  \rangle &= \sum_{\lambda} \delta_{B_n(L)}\left(\begin{smallmatrix} \varpi^\lambda & 0\\ 0 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\right)  \delta_{B_{n-1}(L)}^{-1}(\varpi^\lambda) s_\lambda(\alpha) s_\lambda(\beta)\\
&= \sum_{\lambda}  q^{-\ensuremath{\mathrm{Tr}}\lambda}s_\lambda(\alpha) s_\lambda(\beta) =  \sum_{\lambda} s_\lambda(q^{-1/2}\alpha) s_\lambda(q^{-1/2}\beta) 
\end{aligned}
$$
where sums are indexed by $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$ such that $\lambda_1 \geq \dots \geq \lambda_{n-1} \geq 0$ and $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Tr}}\lambda:= \lambda_1+ \dots + \lambda_{n-1}$ is equal to the degree of $s_\lambda$. Then, by \cite[Formula (4.3) p.63]{Macdonald}, we have:
$$
\langle W^\circ_{\pi},W^\circ_{\pi^\vee}  \rangle = \prod_{i,j} ( 1 - \alpha_i \beta_jq^{-1})^{-1} = L^{imp}(\pi \times \pi^\vee,1)
$$

\label{hecke_base_change}
Let $\rho$ be a cuspidal  automorphic representation of $\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(\mathbb{A}_F)$ of level $k_f$ and let $\Sigma = \mathrm{BC}(\rho)$. Let $\theta_\mathbb{C} : \mathcal{H}_E(K_f,\mathbb{C}) \to \mathcal{H}_F(k_f,\mathbb{C})$ denote the scalar extension of $\theta$ to $\mathbb{C}$. Then for all $T \in \mathcal{H}_E(K_f,\mathbb{C})$, we have:
$$
\lambda_\Sigma(T) = \lambda_\rho(\theta_\mathbb{C}(T))
$$
where $\lambda_\Sigma : \mathcal{H}_E(K_f,\mathbb{C}) \to \mathbb{C}$ and $\lambda_\rho : \mathcal{H}_F(k_f,\mathbb{C}) \to \mathbb{C}$ are the Hecke eigensystems associated with $\Sigma$ and $\rho$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $S_\Sigma$ be the set of finite places where $\Sigma$ is ramified, $S_p(E)$ the set of places of $E$ dividing $p$ and $S_E = S_\Sigma \cup S_p(E)$. Since $\mathcal{H}_{E}(K_f;\mathbb{C})$ is generated over $\mathbb{C}$ by the $T_{w,i}$, for $w \notin S_E$ and $0\leq i \leq n$, we only need to prove the formula for these Hecke operators. Let $w \notin S_E$ and let $v$ be the place of $F$ below $w$. Let   $0\leq i \leq n$. Let $\alpha = \ensuremath{\mathrm{diag}}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$ be a diagonal representative of the Satake parameter of $\rho_v$. We recall that:
$$
\lambda_\rho(T_{v,i}) = q_v^{i(n-i)/2} \sigma_i(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)
$$
where $q_v = \# \mathcal{O}_v / (\varpi_v)$ and $\sigma_i(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is the $i$-th symmetric polynomial (see \cite[Proposition 7.2 p.53]{LALF}). The Satake parameters of $\Sigma_w$ is given by the conjugacy class of:
$$
\beta:= \ensuremath{\mathrm{diag}}(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n) = \ensuremath{\mathrm{diag}}(\alpha_1^{f_v},\dots,\alpha_n^{f_v})
$$
When $v$ is split or ramified, $f_v =1$ and $q_w = q_v$. Thus:
$$
\lambda_\rho(T_{w,i}) = q_w^{i(n-i)/2} \sigma_i(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n) = q_v^{i(n-i)/2} \sigma_i(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n) = \lambda_\rho(T_{v,i}) = \lambda_\rho(\theta(T_{w,i}))
$$
When $v$ is inert, $f_v =2$ and $q_w = q_v^2$. Thus:
$$
\lambda_\rho(T_{w,i}) = q_w^{i(n-i)/2} \sigma_i(\alpha_1^2,\dots,\alpha_n^2)
$$
In this case, the lemma then follows from the definition of $\theta$ at inert places and from the following formula:
$$
(-1)^i \sigma_i(X_1^2,\dots,X_n^2) =  \sum_{k=\max(0,2i-n)}^{\min(2i,n)} (-1)^k \sigma_k(X_1,\dots,X_n)\sigma_{2i-k}(X_1,\dots,X_n)
$$
which can be proven using the identity $\prod_{i=1}^n(X^2-X_i^2) = \prod_{i=1}^n(X-X_i)(X+X_i)$.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
This proof was communicated to us by J. Tilouine, although any potential mistakes or inaccuracies in the following proof are the fault of the author. Let $S_\mathbb{Q} = S_\pi \cup \{p\}$, where $S_\pi$ the set of finite places where $\pi$ is ramified, and let $q \notin S_\mathbb{Q}$ be a prime split in $E$. It follows from the defining formula of $\theta$ that (the image in $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}$ of) $T_{q,i}$ is in $\theta(\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_E)$, which is a closed complete local sub-$\mathcal{O}$-algebra of $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}$. One knows that there exists a Galois representation $\rho: \ensuremath{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q})$, with coefficients in $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}$, such that if $q \notin S_\mathbb{Q}$ is a finite place of $\mathbb{Q}$, then the characteristic polynomial of $\rho(\ensuremath{\mathrm{Frob}}_{q})$ is:
$$
\sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i q^{i(i-1)/2} T_{q,i} X^{n-i}
$$
This representation can be obtained from the Scholze's Galois representation \cite[Corollary 5.4.4]{Scholze15}. Alternatively, one can construct this representation following the section 2.2 of \cite{Carayol} by putting together the Galois representations $\rho_\pi$ attached to each $\pi \in \mathrm{Coh}(G_\mathbb{Q},\mu,k_f)$ by \cite{HLTT16}. By a theorem of Carayol, it follows from the existence of $\rho$ and the hypothesis that it is residually absolutely irreducible, that $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}$ is generated by the traces, i.e. by the $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Tr}}(\rho(g))$, for $g \in \ensuremath{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$. Since $S_\mathbb{Q}$ does not contain any prime ramified in $E$, the subfield $H$ of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ fixed by ${\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ker }} \rho}$ is linearly disjoint from $E$. Thus, by Chebotarev's density theorem, the set of $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Frob}}_q$, for $q\notin S_\mathbb{Q}$ split in $E$, is dense in $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Gal}}(H/{\mathbb{Q}})$, hence its image is dense in the image of $\rho$. Since $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}$ is generated by the traces, and since $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Tr}}(\rho(\ensuremath{\mathrm{Frob}}_q)) = -T_{q,1} \in \theta(\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_E)$ when $q \notin S_\mathbb{Q}$ is split in $E$, this proves that $\theta(\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_E) = \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}$ and thus that $\theta$ is surjective.
