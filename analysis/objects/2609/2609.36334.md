<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We use lower-case Latin and Greek letters for scalar and vector-valued objects, and upper-case letters for matrices and operators. Matrix approximations of operators will use Sans Serif fonts (e.g., $\mathsf{P}$ for $P$). $\mathbb{N}=\{1, 2, \cdots\}$, $\mathbb{R}_+=[0, \infty)$, $\mathbb{R}^d$, and regions or intervals thereon use blackboard fonts. The component functions $x\mapsto x_k$ are denoted as $e_k$, while the standard basis vectors of $\mathbb{R}^d$ are denoted as $\mathsf{e}_k$ ($k=1,\cdots,d$) in Sans Serif for the sake of distinction.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
The \emph{Koopman semigroup} of system \eqref{eq:sys}, $\{K^t\}_{t\in \mathbb{R}_+}$, defined on a space $\mathcal{G}$ of state-dependent functions\footnote{Such a state-dependent function $g\in \mathcal{G}$ is often called an ``observable'' in the literature. Here let us avoid this terminology as it may hamper the discussion of state observation problems.}, is given by $K^t g = g\circ S^t$ for all $g\in \mathcal{G}$. (For well-definedness, $\mathcal{G}$ must be closed under the action of $K^t$ for all $t\in \mathbb{R}_+$.) 
    If $\mathcal{G}$ is a Hilbert space (with inner product $\left\langle \cdot, \, \cdot \right\rangle_{\mathcal{G}})$, then the adjoint Koopman semigroup $\{T^t\}$ ($T^t = K^{t*})$, is called the \emph{Perron--Frobenius semigroup}. In other words, $\left\langle T^tg_1, \, g_2 \right\rangle_{\mathcal{G}} = \left\langle g_1, \, K^tg_2 \right\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}$ for all $g_1,\,g_2\in \mathcal{G}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->

    If $f\in C_\mathrm{b}^s(\mathbb{X})^d$ for some $s\in\mathbb{N}$ (i.e., all components of $f$ and their derivatives up to $s$-th order are bounded in $\mathbb{X}$), then $\{K^t\}$ is a strongly continuous semigroup on $\mathcal{G} = H^{s'}(\mathbb{X})$ for all $0\leq s'\leq s$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We only need to prove for $s'=s$. For any $g\in H^s(\mathbb{X})$, the generalized derivative $\partial^\alpha (K^tg)(x) = \partial^\alpha (g\circ S^t)(x)$ with respect to any multi-index $\alpha$ of length $r\leq s$, by the chain rule, contains terms in the form of a product of $\partial^\beta g(S^t(x))$ and partial derivatives of $S^t$, where $\beta\leq \alpha$. In particular, the term with $\beta=\alpha$ is followed by $\partial_{\alpha_1} S^t(x)\cdots \partial_{\alpha_d} S^t(x)$, while every other term has a factor of higher-order derivatives of $S^t$. 
    This is known as the multivariate version of the Fa{\`{a}} di Bruno's formula. Given the conditions, $S^t \in C^s(\mathbb{X})$ and at small $t$, $S^t(x)=x+O(t)$. 
    Therefore, $\partial^\alpha (K^tg)\rightarrow \partial^\alpha g$ pointwise, and hence in $L^2$ due to Lebesgue convergence theorem, as $t\downarrow 0$. That is, $\lim_{t\downarrow 0} K^tg = g$ in $H^s(\mathbb{X})$ for all $g\in H^s(\mathbb{X})$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->

        \sigma(T^t) \subset \left\{ \lambda\in \mathbb{C}: |\lambda|\leq \max\{|\mathrm{e}^{\mu t}|: \mu\in \sigma(F)\}  \right\}. 
    \end{equation*}
    To this end, take any $g\in \mathring{H}^s(\mathbb{X})$, hence $\tilde{g} = g\circ\psi^{-1} \in \mathring{H}^s(\psi(\mathbb{X}))$, and examine $K^{nt} g(x) = \tilde g(\mathrm{e}^{ntF}y)$ as a function of $y\in \psi(\mathbb{X})$ at large $n\in \mathbb{N}$.

    As $n$ increases, the evaluation of $\partial_y^\alpha$ on $\tilde{g}_k(\mathrm{e}^{ntF}y)$, when taking the squared integral, results in the multiplication of operator norm of $\mathrm{e}^{ntF}$. Denote by $\sigma_1(F)$ the eigenvalue of $F$ with largest real part. Then we have the dependence on $n$ bounded by $\|K^{nt}g\|_{\mathring{H}^s}\lesssim \mathrm{e}^{nt\cdot \operatorname{Re}\sigma_1(F)}$ and hence the spectral radius of $K^t$ bounded by $t\cdot \operatorname{Re}\sigma_1(F)$.     \begin{equation*}

The Luenberger observer gain $\mathsf{P}^{-1}\mathsf{Y}$ resembles that would be expected from a finite-dimensional linear system.
