\label{lem:comparison}
Assume $T'_\bullet$ and $T_\bullet$ are bounded below complexes of flat modules, both with homology concentrated in degree $N$. Let $M':=H_N(T'_\bullet)$ and $M:=H_N(T_\bullet)$. There are natural isomorphisms
\[
(T'_\bullet)_{G'}\simeq\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb{Q}[G']}M'\bigr)[-N],\qquad (T_\bullet)_G\simeq\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}M\bigr)[-N]
\]
in $D(\mathbb{Q})$. If $\tau\colon T'_\bullet\to T_\bullet$ is $\alpha$-equivariant and induces $f\colon M'\to M$ on $H_N$, then the map on coinvariant complexes induced by $\tau$ is the derived-coinvariant map associated to $(\alpha,f)$. Consequently,
\[
H_{N+k}\bigl((T'_\bullet)_{G'}\bigr)\cong H_k(G';M'),\qquad H_{N+k}\bigl((T_\bullet)_G\bigr)\cong H_k(G;M),
\]
and $(\alpha,f)_*$ is the map induced by $\tau$ on homology.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Canonical truncation gives functorial isomorphisms $T'_\bullet\simeq M'[-N]$ in $D(\mathbb{Q}[G'])$ and $T_\bullet\simeq M[-N]$ in $D(\mathbb{Q}[G])$. Ordinary tensor product with a bounded-below complex of flat modules computes the derived tensor product, by \cite[Lemma~10.6.2 and Theorem~10.6.3]{weibel1994introduction}. Hence
\[
(T_\bullet)_G=\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}T_\bullet\simeq\mathbb{Q}\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}T_\bullet\simeq\mathbb{Q}\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}M[-N]=\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}M\bigr)[-N],
\]
and similarly over $G'$. To verify the assertion about $\tau$, use the standard functorial bar resolutions. The homomorphism $\alpha$ and the chain map $\tau$ give a map between the corresponding bar double complexes. These complexes compute the two derived tensor products, and their augmentations to $(T'_\bullet)_{G'}$ and $(T_\bullet)_G$ are quasi-isomorphisms because the terms of $T'_\bullet$ and $T_\bullet$ are flat. Since $H_N(\tau)=f$, canonical truncation identifies the map of bar complexes with the derived-coinvariant map associated to $(\alpha,f)$. The asserted naturality and the formulas on homology follow.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:chain-lifting}
Assume $T'_\bullet$ is a bounded-below complex of projective $\mathbb{Q}[G']$-modules and $T_\bullet$ is a bounded below complex of $\mathbb{Q}[G]$-modules, both with homology concentrated in degree $N$. Then
\[
H_N\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{K(\mathbb{Q}[G'])}(T'_\bullet,\alpha^*T_\bullet)\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}[G']}\bigl(H_N(T'_\bullet),\alpha^*H_N(T_\bullet)\bigr).
\]
Thus every $\alpha$-equivariant map $H_N(T'_\bullet) \to H_N(T_\bullet)$ has an $\alpha$-equivariant chain-level lift, and the lift is unique up to $\alpha$-equivariant chain homotopy.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Morphisms from a bounded-below complex of projective modules in the homotopy and derived categories agree, by  \cite[Corollary~10.4.7]{weibel1994introduction}. Canonical truncation gives $T'_\bullet\cong H_N(T'_\bullet)[-N]$ and $\alpha^*T_\bullet\cong\alpha^*H_N(T_\bullet)[-N]$ in $D(\mathbb{Q}[G'])$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Hom}_{K(\mathbb{Q}[G'])}(T'_\bullet,\alpha^*T_\bullet)&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathbb{Q}[G'])}(T'_\bullet,\alpha^*T_\bullet),\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathbb{Q}[G'])}\bigl(H_N(T'_\bullet)[-N],\alpha^*H_N(T_\bullet)[-N]\bigr),   
                                                                             
\end{align*}
which is $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}[G']}\bigl(H_N(T'_\bullet),\alpha^*H_N(T_\bullet)\bigr)$, and the result follows.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:product}
For ordered bases $v_1, \dots, v_m$ of $\mathbb{Q}^m$ and $w_1, \dots, w_n$ of $\mathbb{Q}^n$,
\[ \mu\bigl(\llbracket v_1, \dots, v_m \rrbracket \otimes \llbracket w_1, \dots, w_n \rrbracket\bigr) \;=\; \llbracket \phi(v_1), \dots, \phi(v_m),\, \psi(w_1), \dots, \psi(w_n) \rrbracket. \]
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Set $u_i = \phi(v_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq m$ and $u_{m+j} = \psi(w_j)$ for $1 \leq j \leq n$, a basis of $\mathbb{Q}^{m+n}$. Write $y_\sigma$, $\sigma \in \Sigma_m$, for the simplices of $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_m$ underlying $\llbracket v_1, \dots, v_m \rrbracket$ as in \eqref{eq:apartment}, and $z_\tau$, $\tau \in \Sigma_n$, for those of $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_n$ underlying $\llbracket w_1, \dots, w_n \rrbracket$. By the Eilenberg--Zilber formula of Section~\ref{sec:chains},
\[ (\theta_{m,n})_*\,\mathrm{EZ}\bigl(y_\sigma \otimes z_\tau\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{(a,b)} \mathrm{sgn}(a,b)\;\theta_{m,n}\bigl(s_b\, y_\sigma,\; s_a\, z_\tau\bigr), \]
the sum over $(m,n)$-shuffles. In the $(a,b)$-term, the flag $s_b\, y_\sigma$ grows by $\phi(v_{\sigma(j)})$ at step $a_j$ and is otherwise constant, while $s_a\, z_\tau$ grows by $\psi(w_{\tau(j)})$ at step $b_j$; their levelwise sum is the flag of initial spans of $u_{\rho(1)}, \dots, u_{\rho(m+n)}$, where $\rho = (\sigma\oplus\tau)\circ\rho_{a,b}$ in the notation of Section~\ref{sec:chains}. This flag is proper and nondegenerate, its spans strictly increasing because the $u_i$ form a basis, so it is the simplex $x_\rho$ of $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_{m+n}$. By the bijection of Section~\ref{sec:chains}, summing $\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)\mathrm{sgn}(a,b) = \mathrm{sgn}(\rho)$ over all triples gives
\[ (\theta_{m,n})_*\,\mathrm{EZ}\bigl(\llbracket v_1,\dots,v_m \rrbracket \otimes \llbracket w_1,\dots,w_n \rrbracket\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{\rho \in \Sigma_{m+n}} \mathrm{sgn}(\rho)\, x_\rho \;=\; \llbracket u_1, \dots, u_{m+n} \rrbracket. \qedhere \]

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:splitting}
The map $r_U$ is a map of pointed simplicial sets, and $r_U(x) \neq \ast$ only if $V_p = U = V_{p+1}$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Suppose $r_U(x) \neq \ast$. Then the front flag $(V_0 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq V_p)$ is not collapsed in $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}(U)$, so $V_p = U$, and the back flag $(V_{p+1}/U \subseteq \cdots \subseteq V_{2p+1}/U)$ is not collapsed in $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}(\mathbb{Q}^n/U)$, so $V_{p+1} = U$. This proves the second claim and shows that if $r_U$ is simplicial then it is pointed. 

We now show $r_U$ is simplicial. Every degeneracy $s_i$ leaves the middle pair $(V_p,V_{p+1})$ unchanged and therefore commutes with $r_U$ factorwise. There are no faces to check in degree $p=0$, so assume $p>0$. Every face $d_i$ with $1 \leq i \leq p-1$ also leaves the middle pair untouched. The faces $d_0$ and $d_p$ delete one of $V_p,V_{p+1}$; hence, the condition in \eqref{eq:rU} says that $r_U(d_0x)$ and $r_U(d_px)$ are not necessarily sent to $\ast$ when $V_p \subseteq U \subseteq V_{p+2}$ and when $V_{p-1} \subseteq U \subseteq V_{p+1}$, respectively. Instead, on simplices meeting these conditions, both $r_U \circ d_i$ and $d_i \circ r_U$ are given by the same factorwise formula. It remains to check that $d_0 x$ and $d_p x$ map to the basepoint whenever $x$ does. If $r_U(d_0 x) \neq \ast$, then $V_1 = 0$ and the second claim applied to $d_0 x$ says that $V_p = U = V_{p+2}$. It follows that $V_0 = 0$ and $V_{p+1} = U$, so $r_U(x) \neq \ast$.  The case of $d_p x$ is similar, and the lemma follows.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:coproduct}
For an ordered basis $v_1, \dots, v_n$ of $\mathbb{Q}^n$,
\[ \delta\llbracket v_1, \dots, v_n \rrbracket \;=\; \sum_{S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}} \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma_S)\; \llbracket v_{i_1}, \dots, v_{i_p} \rrbracket \otimes \llbracket \bar v_{j_1}, \dots, \bar v_{j_{n-p}} \rrbracket. \]
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $A \subseteq \Sigma\mathrm{ST}_n$ be the simplicial subset generated by the simplices $x_\rho$ of \eqref{eq:apartment}, $\rho \in \Sigma_n$. Its non-basepoint simplices are the flags of spans of nested subsets of $\{v_1, \dots, v_n\}$. We compute $\delta\llbracket v_1, \dots, v_n \rrbracket$ by exhibiting a cycle representing $(\varphi_0)_*^{-1}\llbracket v_1, \dots, v_n \rrbracket$ in $\widetilde{C}_n(\mathrm{es}(A))$ and then pushing it forward under $\mathrm{AW} \circ r$.

We first parametrize the top-dimensional nondegenerate simplices of $\mathrm{es}(A)$. A nondegenerate $q$-simplex is a flag $(0 = V_0 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq V_{2q+1} = \mathbb{Q}^n)$ in which, among the paired inclusions $V_{i-1} \subseteq V_i$ and $V_{q+i} \subseteq V_{q+i+1}$, at least one of each pair is strict. The top-dimensional nondegenerate simplices, for $q = n$, thus have exactly one strict inclusion per pair and middle equality $V_n = V_{n+1}$, so they are indexed as
\[ y_{\rho, \varepsilon}, \qquad \rho \in \Sigma_n, \quad \varepsilon \in \{0,1\}^n, \]
where $\varepsilon_i$ records which inclusion of the $i$th pair is strict (so that $\varepsilon_i = 0$ means that $V_{i-1} \subset V_i$ is strict), and $\rho$ is the order in which the basis vectors enter, reading upward.

We now describe a cycle representing the apartment class $\llbracket v_1, \ldots, v_n \rrbracket$ in  $\widetilde{C}_n\bigl(\mathrm{es}(A)\bigr)$. For $\varepsilon \in \{0,1\}^n$, let $\operatorname{inv}(\varepsilon) = \#\{i < j : \varepsilon_i = 1, \varepsilon_j = 0\}$, and set
\[ \widetilde{z} \;=\; \sum_{\rho, \varepsilon} \mathrm{sgn}(\rho)\,(-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(\varepsilon)}\; y_{\rho, \varepsilon} \;\in\; \widetilde{C}_n\bigl(\mathrm{es}(A)\bigr). \]
We check $\partial \widetilde{z} = (\sum_i (-1)^i d_i)\widetilde{z} = 0$, where $d_i$ deletes $V_i$ and $V_{n+1+i}$. The interior faces $d_i$, $1 \leq i \leq n-1$, cancel in pairs via a sign-reversing transposition in $\rho$ when $\varepsilon_i = \varepsilon_{i+1}$, and via the swap changing $\operatorname{inv}(\varepsilon)$ by one when $\{\varepsilon_i, \varepsilon_{i+1}\} = \{0,1\}$. The boundary faces $d_0$ and $d_n$, nonzero only when $\varepsilon_1 = 1$ and $\varepsilon_n = 0$ respectively, instead pair across the chain: the strict inclusion deleted at one end reappears as the middle one, so $d_0 y_{\rho,(1,\eta)} = d_n y_{\rho,(\eta,0)}$ for $\eta \in \{0,1\}^{n-1}$. These faces enter $\partial\widetilde{z}$ with signs $(-1)^0$ and $(-1)^n$, so the two contributions cancel precisely when $\operatorname{inv}(1\eta)-\operatorname{inv}(\eta0)-n$ is odd; and $\operatorname{inv}(1\eta) - \operatorname{inv}(\eta)$ and $\operatorname{inv}(\eta 0) - \operatorname{inv}(\eta)$ are the numbers of zeros and ones of $\eta$, summing to $n-1$, whence $\operatorname{inv}(1\eta)-\operatorname{inv}(\eta0)\equiv n-1$ and the exponent is odd. Hence $\partial \widetilde{z} = 0$. 
Moreover $\varphi_0$ sends $y_{\rho,\varepsilon}$ to its front half $(V_0 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq V_n)$, nondegenerate only for $\varepsilon = 0^n$, where it is $x_\rho$; as $\operatorname{inv}(0^n) = 0$,
\[ \varphi_0(\widetilde{z}) \;=\; \sum_\rho \mathrm{sgn}(\rho)\, x_\rho \;=\; \llbracket v_1, \dots, v_n \rrbracket, \]
so $[\widetilde{z}\,] = (\varphi_0)_*^{-1}\llbracket v_1, \dots, v_n \rrbracket$.

Finally, we compute the chain-level push-forward of $\widetilde{z}$ under $\mathrm{AW} \circ r$. Each $y_{\rho,\varepsilon}$ has $V_n = V_{n+1}$, so $r(y_{\rho,\varepsilon})$ lies in the summand $U = V_n$, and the $p$th Alexander--Whitney term of Section~\ref{sec:chains} is
\[ \bigl(V_0 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq V_p\bigr) \otimes \bigl(V_{n+1+p}/U \subseteq \cdots \subseteq V_{2n+1}/U\bigr). \]
The first factor survives in $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}(U)$ if and only if its inclusions are strict with $V_p = U$, and the second survives in $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}(\mathbb{Q}^n/U)$ if and only if its inclusions are strict with $V_{n+1+p} = U$. Together these say $\varepsilon = 0^p 1^{n-p}$, leaving a single term. Fix $S=\{i_1<\cdots<i_p\}$, with complement $\{j_1<\cdots<j_{n-p}\}$, and write $\rho=\sigma_S \circ (\sigma'\oplus\tau')$, where $\sigma'\in\Sigma_p$ and $\tau'\in\Sigma_{n-p}$. The surviving term is $x_{\sigma'}\otimes x_{\tau'}$ with coefficient $\mathrm{sgn}(\rho)$, since $\operatorname{inv}(0^p1^{n-p})=0$, and
\[
\mathrm{sgn}(\rho)=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma_S)\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma')\mathrm{sgn}(\tau').
\]
Summing over $\sigma'$ and $\tau'$ gives the tensor product of the two apartment cycles with coefficient $\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma_S)$; summing over $S$ gives the stated formula.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:kunneth-sign}
Let $T'_\bullet$ and $T''_\bullet$ be bounded-below complexes of flat modules over $\mathbb{Q}[G_1]$ and $\mathbb{Q}[G_2]$, with homology concentrated in degrees $a$ and $b$, equal there to $M'$ and $M''$. Then $T'_\bullet \otimes T''_\bullet$ is a bounded-below complex of flat $\mathbb{Q}[G_1\times G_2]$-modules with homology $M'\otimes M''$ concentrated in degree $a+b$, and $(T'_\bullet\otimes T''_\bullet)_{G_1\times G_2}=(T'_\bullet)_{G_1}\otimes(T''_\bullet)_{G_2}$. On the summand
\[
H_{a+i}\bigl((T'_\bullet)_{G_1}\bigr)\otimes H_{b+j}\bigl((T''_\bullet)_{G_2}\bigr)\;\cong\;H_i(G_1;M')\otimes H_j(G_2;M''),
\]
the K\"unneth isomorphism into $H_{a+b+i+j}\bigl((T'_\bullet\otimes T''_\bullet)_{G_1\times G_2}\bigr)\cong H_{i+j}(G_1\times G_2;M'\otimes M'')$ equals $(-1)^{aj}$ times the group-homology cross product.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Coinvariants over $G_1\times G_2$ split as $(T'_\bullet\otimes T''_\bullet)_{G_1\times G_2}=(T'_\bullet)_{G_1}\otimes(T''_\bullet)_{G_2}$, and over $\mathbb{Q}$ the displayed map is the algebraic K\"unneth isomorphism of this tensor product. Choose free resolutions $F_1\to\mathbb{Q}$ and $F_2\to\mathbb{Q}$ over $\mathbb{Q}[G_1]$ and $\mathbb{Q}[G_2]$; then $F_1\otimes F_2\to\mathbb{Q}$ is a free resolution over $\mathbb{Q}[G_1\times G_2]$. As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison}, the augmentations $(F_i\otimes T)_{G_i}\to T_{G_i}$ are quasi-isomorphisms by flatness and realize the identifications of Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} on the bicomplexes $(F_i\otimes T)_{G_i}$, a class of degree $a_i+\ell$ being carried in $F_i$-degree $\ell$ and $T$-degree $a_i$. On these bicomplexes the cross product is induced by the interchange
\[
(F_1\otimes T'_\bullet)\otimes(F_2\otimes T''_\bullet)\xrightarrow{\ \chi\ }(F_1\otimes F_2)\otimes(T'_\bullet\otimes T''_\bullet)
\]
that moves the $T'_\bullet$-factor past the $F_2$-factor, with the Koszul sign. On the summand of $F_1$-degree $i$, $T'_\bullet$-degree $a$, $F_2$-degree $j$, and $T''_\bullet$-degree $b$, this transposes a degree-$a$ factor past a degree-$j$ factor, contributing $(-1)^{aj}$. The group-homology cross product is the same interchange with coefficients in degree zero ($a=b=0$), where the sign is trivial; the two therefore differ by $(-1)^{aj}$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:lift}
Let $X_\bullet$ be a bounded-below complex of projective $\mathbb{Q}[\Gamma_n]$-modules with homology $\mathrm{St}_n$ concentrated in degree $N$, and let $T_\bullet$ be a bounded-below complex of flat $\mathbb{Q}[L_d]$-modules with homology $W_d$ concentrated in degree $N$, regarded as a complex of $P_d$-modules via $\pi_d$. If $\tau\colon X_\bullet \to \mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n} T_\bullet$ is $\Gamma_n$-equivariant and induces $\delta_d$ on $H_N$, then, under the canonical identification
\[
\bigl(\mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n} T_\bullet\bigr)_{\Gamma_n} \cong (T_\bullet)_{L_d},
\]
the induced map $\tau_*$ on coinvariant homology is
\[
\rho_d \circ (\mathrm{id},\delta_d)_* \colon H_k(\Gamma_n;\mathrm{St}_n) \longrightarrow H_k(L_d;W_d).
\]
In particular, $\kappa_{\mathrm{tot}}^{-1}\circ\tau_*=\Delta_d$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $R_\bullet$ be a bounded-below complex of projective $\mathbb{Q}[P_d]$-modules with homology $W_d$ concentrated in degree $N$ --- for instance, the $N$-fold shift of a projective $\mathbb{Q}[P_d]$-resolution of $W_d$. Since $\mathbb{Q}[\Gamma_n]$ is free as a right $\mathbb{Q}[P_d]$-module, induction from $P_d$ to $\Gamma_n$ is exact and preserves projectives, so $\mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n}R_\bullet$ and $\mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n}T_\bullet$ have homology $\mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n}W_d$ concentrated in degree $N$, the former by projective modules. By Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-lifting}, applied in degree $N$, there are equivariant lifts
\[
\Lambda\colon X_\bullet \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n}R_\bullet,
\qquad
\widetilde\pi\colon R_\bullet \longrightarrow T_\bullet
\]
of $\delta_d$ and $\mathrm{id}_{W_d}$, respectively, with $\widetilde\pi$ $\pi_d$-equivariant.

Both $\tau$ and $\mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n}(\widetilde\pi)\circ\Lambda$ are $\Gamma_n$-equivariant lifts of $\delta_d$ to $\mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n}T_\bullet$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-lifting} they are equivariantly chain homotopic, and therefore induce the same map on coinvariant homology. On coinvariants, the latter composite is
\[
(X_\bullet)_{\Gamma_n}
\xrightarrow{\;\Lambda\;}
\bigl(\mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\Gamma_n}R_\bullet\bigr)_{\Gamma_n}
\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
(R_\bullet)_{P_d}
\xrightarrow{\;\widetilde\pi\;}
(T_\bullet)_{L_d}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison}, in degree $N$, identifies the first and last maps on homology with $(\mathrm{id},\delta_d)_*$ and $(\pi_d,\mathrm{id})_*$, respectively. The middle isomorphism induces $\Sha_d^{-1}$, since $\Sha_d$ is represented by $r\mapsto 1\otimes r$. Hence $\tau$ induces
\[
(\pi_d,\mathrm{id})_*\circ\Sha_d^{-1}\circ(\mathrm{id},\delta_d)_*
=\rho_d\circ(\mathrm{id},\delta_d)_*,
\]
as claimed.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:duoidal}
The pair $(\mu, \delta)$ exists already on the Steinberg modules, before passing to group homology. The modules $\mathrm{St}$ form a representation of the groupoid $\coprod_n \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$, and the category of such representations carries two monoidal structures: Day convolution \cite{miller2020stability}, for which an algebra is a family of $\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z}) \times \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$-equivariant maps $\mathrm{St}_m \otimes \mathrm{St}_n \to \mathrm{St}_{m+n}$, and parabolic induction \cite{nagpal2019vi}, for which a coalgebra is a family of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$-equivariant maps
\[ \mathrm{St}_n \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{d=0}^{n} \mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})} \bigl( \mathrm{St}_d \otimes \mathrm{St}_{n-d} \bigr), \]
with $P_d$ acting through its Levi quotient. The product $\mu$ is such a family, and so, under the identification above, is the coproduct $\delta$. Ash--Miller--Patzt show that the two structures are compatible and that $\mathrm{St}$ is a bialgebra in the resulting duoidal category \cite[Remark~1.4]{ash2024hopf}. The comparisons of Sections~\ref{sec:amp} and~\ref{sec:bcgp} then identify the Hopf algebras of \cite{ash2024hopf} and \cite{brown2024hopf} with a homological shadow of this bialgebra.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:sharbly}
The map $\mathrm{Sh}_\bullet(\mathbb{Z}^n) \to \mathrm{St}_n$ is a resolution of $\mathrm{St}_n$ by projective $\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})]$-modules.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For exactness, see \cite[Section 2]{ash2024hopf}. For projectivity, decompose $\mathrm{Sh}_k(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ over the $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$-orbits of generators. The setwise stabilizer of the unoriented simplex corresponding to a generator $[v_1, \ldots, v_{n+k}]$ is finite and acts by the sign of the induced permutation on the set of pairs $\{\{ \pm v_1 \}, \ldots, \{ \pm v_{n+k} \} \}$. Each orbit summand is thus induced from a character of a finite group. Characters of finite groups are projective over $\mathbb{Q}$, and induction preserves projectivity, so $\mathrm{Sh}_k(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ is projective.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:amp}
The sharbly product and coproduct on $\mathcal{H}$ coincide with the Steinberg product and coproduct.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $\Theta$ be the automorphism of $\bigoplus_{a,b} \mathrm{Sh}_k(\mathbb{Z}^a) \otimes \mathrm{Sh}_\ell(\mathbb{Z}^b)$ whose restriction to $\mathrm{Sh}_k(\mathbb{Z}^a) \otimes \mathrm{Sh}_\ell(\mathbb{Z}^b)$ is multiplication by $(-1)^{a\ell}$.  Composing with this automorphism converts the differential of the Ash--Miller--Patzt differential graded bialgebra, with Koszul signs governed by total degree, to that of the ordinary tensor product of resolutions.  

Note, in particular, that $\Theta$ is the identity on $\mathrm{Sh}_0$. Thus $\nabla \circ \Theta$ and $\Theta \circ \Delta$ are equivariant chain maps of the underlying resolutions, agreeing with $\nabla$ and $\Delta$ in degree zero. It remains to identify their degree-zero augmentations and apply the comparison lemmas of Sections~\ref{sec:comparison} and~\ref{sec:induced}.

For the product, let $\alpha_{m,n}\colon \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z}) \times \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{GL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{Z})$ be the block diagonal embedding. By Proposition~\ref{prop:sharbly}, $\mathrm{Sh}_\bullet(\mathbb{Z}^m) \otimes \mathrm{Sh}_\bullet(\mathbb{Z}^n) \to \mathrm{St}_m \otimes \mathrm{St}_n$ is a projective resolution over $\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z}) \times \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})]$, and $\nabla \circ \Theta$ is an $\alpha_{m,n}$-equivariant lift of $\mu_{m,n}$, since in degree zero it sends $[v_1, \dots, v_m] \otimes [w_1, \dots, w_n]$ to $\llbracket \phi(v_1), \dots, \phi(v_m), \psi(w_1), \dots, \psi(w_n) \rrbracket$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:product}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} it induces $(\alpha_{m,n}, \mu_{m,n})_*$ on coinvariants, so the sharbly product is $(\alpha_{m,n}, \mu_{m,n})_* \circ \times_{\mathrm{tot}}$, the Steinberg product.

For the coproduct, fix $0 \leq d \leq n$ and set $T_\bullet:=\mathrm{Sh}_\bullet(\mathbb{Z}^d)\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_\bullet(\mathbb{Z}^{n-d})$. Let $\Delta^{\langle d \rangle}\colon \mathrm{Sh}_\bullet(\mathbb{Z}^n) \to \mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})}T_\bullet$ be the rank-$d$ component of $\Theta \circ \Delta$ \cite[Section~2]{ash2024hopf}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:sharbly}, the source is a complex of projective $\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})]$-modules and $T_\bullet\to W_d$ is a projective, hence flat, $\mathbb{Q}[L_d]$-resolution; we regard $T_\bullet$ as a $P_d$-complex through $\pi_d$. The map $\Delta^{\langle d \rangle}$ lifts $\delta_d$, since in degree zero it is the $|S|=d$ part of Proposition~\ref{prop:coproduct}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}, $\kappa_{\mathrm{tot}}^{-1} \circ (\Delta^{\langle d \rangle})_* = \Delta_d$; summing over $d$, the sharbly coproduct is the Steinberg coproduct.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:rigid}
The map $c_n\colon \mathrm{Tot}(D^n) \to \widetilde{C}_*(\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_n)$ is a $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$-equivariant quasi-isomorphism. In particular, $H_*(\mathrm{Tot}(D^n)) \cong \mathrm{St}_n$, concentrated in degree $n$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
A flag $0 = U_0 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq U_p = \mathbb{Q}^n$ determines a rigidified diagram with $Q_{i,j} = (U_j \cap \mathbb{Z}^n)/(U_i \cap \mathbb{Z}^n)$. Every object of $\mathcal{T}_p(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ with this flag is uniquely isomorphic to this diagram, since each $Q_{0,i}$ has saturated image in $\mathbb{Z}^n$. Thus the functor $f_p$ assigning to an object its flag is an equivalence from $\mathcal{T}_p(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ to the discrete category of such flags.

After adjoining basepoints, the functors $f_p$ commute with the face and degeneracy maps in the $p$-direction. For $0 < i < p$, the face map $d_i$ deletes $U_i$ from the flag, and the degeneracy maps repeat a subspace. For $p > 0$, the face map $d_0$ sends a flag to the basepoint exactly when $U_1 \neq 0$, and $d_p$ does so exactly when $U_{p-1} \neq \mathbb{Q}^n$, as in $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_n$. The induced map on chains is $c_n$, since the nerve of a discrete category has no nondegenerate simplices in positive degrees. Replacing $\beta$ by $g\beta$ replaces each $U_i$ by $gU_i$, so $c_n$ is $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$-equivariant.

For each $p$, the equivalence of groupoids induces an isomorphism on the homology of their nerves. Taking reduced chains on each nerve and then normalizing in the $p$-direction preserves this property, since normalization is exact. Hence the map of double complexes inducing $c_n$ is a quasi-isomorphism in each column, and $c_n$ is a quasi-isomorphism by \cite[Lemma~2.7.3]{weibel1994introduction}. The last assertion follows from the homology of $\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_n$ recalled in Section~\ref{sec:tools}.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:bcgp-product}
Under the identification $^Q \! E^1_{n,k}\cong H_k(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z});\mathrm{St}_n)$ fixed in \eqref{eq:identification}, the product induced by $\mathbf{m}$ agrees with the Steinberg product. In particular, for $x$ in $H_j(\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z});\mathrm{St}_m)$ and $y$ in $H_k(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z});\mathrm{St}_n)$, it is given by
\[
x\cdot y=(\alpha_{m,n},\mu_{m,n})_*\bigl(x\times_{\mathrm{tot}}y\bigr).
\]
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Direct sum lifts to the rigidified groupoids $\mathcal{T}_p(\mathbb{Z}^m)\times\mathcal{T}_p(\mathbb{Z}^n)\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_p(\mathbb{Z}^{m+n})$ via
\[
\bigl(((Q_{i,j}),\beta),((Q'_{i,j}),\beta')\bigr)\longmapsto\bigl((Q_{i,j}\oplus Q'_{i,j}),\beta\oplus\beta'\bigr).
\]
This map is equivariant for the block diagonal inclusion $\alpha_{m,n}\colon\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z})\times\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{GL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{Z})$ and commutes with the simplicial structure and the outer-face conventions. Applying the Eilenberg--Zilber map in the two simplicial directions and totalizing gives an $\alpha_{m,n}$-equivariant chain map
\[
\tau_{m,n}\colon\mathrm{Tot}(D^m)\otimes\mathrm{Tot}(D^n)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Tot}(D^{m+n}).
\]
After passage to coinvariants, this is the map induced by the filtered direct sum $\mathbf{m}$ following the Eilenberg--Zilber map. Hence the product on $^Q \! E^1$ is the K\"unneth map followed by $(\tau_{m,n})_*$.

The maps $c_r$ commute with direct sum and with the shuffle maps, so the following diagram commutes:
\[
\xymatrix@C=3.5em{
\mathrm{Tot}(D^m)\otimes\mathrm{Tot}(D^n)
    \ar[rr]^-{\tau_{m,n}}
    \ar[d]_{c_m\otimes c_n}
&&
\mathrm{Tot}(D^{m+n})
    \ar[d]^{c_{m+n}}
\\
\widetilde{C}_*(\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_m)\otimes\widetilde{C}_*(\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_n)
    \ar[rr]_-{(\theta_{m,n})_*\circ\mathrm{EZ}}
&&
\widetilde{C}_*(\Sigma\mathrm{ST}_{m+n}).
}
\]
Indeed, a pair of rigidified diagrams collapses to flags $U_\bullet$ and $V_\bullet$, while their direct sum collapses to the flag $\phi(U_\bullet)+\psi(V_\bullet)$. The bottom map is therefore the chain map defining $\mu_{m,n}$ in Section~\ref{sec:product}. Under the identifications of Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, it follows that $H_{m+n}(\tau_{m,n})=\mu_{m,n}$.

The complexes in the source and target of $\tau_{m,n}$ are bounded below and free over the corresponding group rings, with homology concentrated in degree $m+n$. Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} therefore identifies the map induced by $\tau_{m,n}$ on coinvariant homology with $(\alpha_{m,n},\mu_{m,n})_*$.

It remains to compare the K\"unneth conventions. By Lemma~\ref{lem:kunneth-sign} with $a=m$ and $b=n$, on the summand of group-homological degrees $(j,k)$ the K\"unneth map of the coinvariant complexes is $(-1)^{mk}$ times the ordinary cross product, which is $\times_{\mathrm{tot}}$ by the definition in Section~\ref{sec:induced}. Consequently, the product on $^Q \! E^1$ is $(\alpha_{m,n},\mu_{m,n})_*\circ\times_{\mathrm{tot}}$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:phitilde}
Forgetting the rigidifications, $\widetilde\Phi$ is the Alexander--Whitney map following the chain map that $\Phi$ induces on the associated graded of the rank filtration. Under the collapse of Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, it covers $\mathrm{AW} \circ r$, with $r$ the splitting map of Section~\ref{sec:coproduct}.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By the rank inequality from the start of this section, when the terminal module has rank $n$ the two factors produced by $\Phi$ have total rank at most $n$, with equality exactly when the middle map $Q_{0,p}\to Q_{0,p+1}$ is an isomorphism; the unbalanced diagrams therefore map to the basepoint of the associated graded. The assignment is $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$-equivariant, $g$ carrying the $L$-summand to the $gL$-summand, and compatible with faces and degeneracies: the inner structure maps act factorwise, no structure map creates balance, and the two outer faces survive precisely when the corresponding outer faces of both factors survive, the rigidifications transported as in Section~\ref{sec:rigidified}. Forgetting $\beta$ thus recovers the chain map induced by $\Phi$ on the associated graded, followed by the Alexander--Whitney maps. After collapse, a balanced diagram becomes a flag with common middle term $L \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, sent to its front and quotient flags --- the defining formula for $r_{L \otimes \mathbb{Q}}$ in Section~\ref{sec:coproduct} --- so $\widetilde\Phi$ covers $\mathrm{AW} \circ r$.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:bcgp-coproduct}
Under the identification $^Q \! E^1_{n,k} \cong H_k(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z});\mathrm{St}_n)$ fixed in \eqref{eq:identification}, the coproduct induced by $\Phi$ agrees with the Steinberg coproduct of Section~\ref{sec:induced}. In particular, its rank-$d$ component is $\Delta_d$.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Fix $d$ and put
\[
X_\bullet := \mathrm{Tot}(\mathrm{es}(D^n)), \qquad T_\bullet := \mathrm{Tot}(D^d) \otimes \mathrm{Tot}(D^{n-d}), \qquad W_d := \mathrm{St}_d \otimes \mathrm{St}_{n-d}.
\]
Let
\[
\tau_d \colon X_\bullet \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ind}_{P_d}^{\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})} T_\bullet
\]
be the rank-$d$ component of $\widetilde\Phi$, followed by the inverse of the displayed induced-module isomorphism. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} applied to the edgewise subdivision, $X_\bullet$ is a bounded-below complex of free $\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})]$-modules with homology $\mathrm{St}_n$ concentrated in degree $n$; we identify $H_n(X_\bullet)$ with $\mathrm{St}_n$ by the collapse of Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} followed by $(\varphi_0)_*$, both quasi-isomorphisms. Likewise, by Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} and the K\"unneth theorem, $T_\bullet$ is a bounded-below complex of free $\mathbb{Q}[L_d]$-modules with homology $W_d$ concentrated in degree $n$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:phitilde} and the definition of $\delta$ in Section~\ref{sec:coproduct}, $\tau_d$ lifts $\delta_d$: that is, $H_n(\tau_d) = \delta_d$ under these identifications.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} with $N = n$, the map induced by $\tau_d$ on coinvariant homology is, through $(\varphi_0)_*$,
\[
\rho_d \circ (\mathrm{id}, \delta_d)_* \colon H_k(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z});\mathrm{St}_n) \longrightarrow H_k(L_d; W_d).
\]
The rank-$d$ component of the coproduct on $^Q \! E^1$ is this composite followed by the inverse K\"unneth map under the canonical identification
\[
(T_\bullet)_{L_d} \cong \mathrm{Tot}(C^d) \otimes \mathrm{Tot}(C^{n-d}).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:kunneth-sign} with $a=d$ and $b=n-d$, this K\"unneth map is, on the summand of degrees $(i,j)$, the sign $(-1)^{dj}$ times the ordinary cross product, hence $\times_{\mathrm{tot}}$; its inverse is therefore $\kappa_{\mathrm{tot}}^{-1}$. Hence the rank-$d$ component is
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{tot}}^{-1} \circ \rho_d \circ (\mathrm{id},\delta_d)_*=\Delta_d,
\]
the last equality by the definition of $\Delta_d$ in Section~\ref{sec:induced}. Summing over $d$ proves the proposition.

Transport the Quillen Hopf structure to $\mathcal{H}$ through the identification \eqref{eq:identification}. <!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The same proof gives a PID version of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Let $R$ be a PID with fraction field $F$, and define
\[
\mathcal{H}_R:=\bigoplus_{n,k\geq 0}H_k\bigl(\mathrm{GL}_n(R);\mathrm{St}(F^n)\bigr),
\]
where $\mathrm{St}(F^n)$ denotes the rational Steinberg module. Under the canonical identification of $\mathcal{H}_R$ with the $E^1$-page of the rank filtration on $BK(R)$, the sharbly Hopf structure for the trivial character coincides with the Hopf structure induced by the filtered Waldhausen product and coproduct.

Indeed, the sharbly Hopf structure is defined for every PID \cite[Theorem~3.1]{ash2024hopf}, while the filtered Waldhausen construction and the description of its $E^1$-page apply to Dedekind domains \cite[Section~1.7 and Remark~2.16]{brown2024hopf}. Over a PID, every finitely generated projective module is free, so the sum over projective-module isomorphism classes in the Dedekind-domain description of the $E^1$-page has one summand in each rank. Moreover, saturated submodules of $R^n$ are direct summands and form a single $\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$-orbit in each rank, and saturated submodules correspond to subspaces of $F^n$. Thus the rigidified model, the induced-module decomposition, and the comparisons of the product and coproduct carry over with $R$ and $F$ in place of $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$. The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sharbly} also carries over, since the stabilizer of a spanning sharbly is finite.
