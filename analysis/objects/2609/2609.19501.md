<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By the inner product properties (IP2), (IP3) we see that 
\[ 
\gamma_\nu = \text{\textlquill}  \mu -(c\chi) \theta_{G} ,\mu -(c\chi) \theta_{G} \text{\textrquill} _{\mathcal A} 
\] 
exists and satisfies 
\begin{align*} 
 \gamma_{\nu} &=\text{\textlquill}  \mu -(c\chi) \theta_{G} ,\mu -(c\chi) \theta_{G} \text{\textrquill} _{\mathcal A} \\ 
   &=\text{\textlquill}  \mu ,\mu \text{\textrquill} _{\mathcal A}+ -\overline{c} \text{\textlquill}  \mu ,\chi \theta_{G} \text{\textrquill} _{\mathcal A}-c \text{\textlquill}  \mu \chi \theta_{G} ,\mu \theta_{G} \text{\textrquill} _{\mathcal A}+|c|^2\text{\textlquill}  \mu \chi \theta_{G} ,\chi \theta_{G} \text{\textrquill} _{\mathcal A} \\ 
   &= \gamma_{\mu}-\overline{c}  (a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu) \chi)\theta_{G} - c (\overline{a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)} \chi)\theta_{G}+|c|^2 \chi\theta_{G} \\ 
   &= \gamma_{\mu}+ \left( |c|^2-\overline{c} a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)  - c \overline{a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)}\right) \chi\theta_{G} \,. 
\end{align*} 
By simple algebraic manipulations we find 
\begin{align*} 
|c|^2-\overline{c}  a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)  - c \overline{a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)} &= c \bar{c} -\overline{c}  a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)  - c \overline{a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)} +a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)  \overline{a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu) } -a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)  \overline{a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu) }  \\ 
 &= (c- a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu))\cdot \overline{ (c- a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu))} - a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu)  \overline{a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu) } \\ 
 &= \left| c- a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu) \right|^2- \left|a_{\chi}^{\mathcal A}(\mu) \right|^2 
\end{align*} 
Putting this together we obtain the first statement of the lemma. The second statement is an immediate consequence of the first statement after taking the Fourier transform and appealing to the Example ~\ref{ex-chi} discussed above.

\label{lem-cs} Let $\mu,\nu$ be translation bounded measures. Assume that $\text{\textlquill}  \mu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}},\text{\textlquill}  \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}$ and $\text{\textlquill}  \mu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}$ exist. Let $\tau : LP(G)\longrightarrow {\mathbb C}$ be positive linear functional. Then, 
$$\left|\tau(\text{\textlquill} \mu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}})\right|^2\leq \tau(\text{\textlquill} \mu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}})\cdot  \tau (\text{\textlquill}  \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) \,.$$
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
This is a variant of the usual proof of Cauchy--Schwarz inequality. For the convenience of the reader we include the details.  As the autocorrelations of $\mu$ and $\nu$ exist as well as $\text{\textlquill}  \mu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}$ we can use (IP3) to define 
\begin{eqnarray*} p(\lambda)&:=&\tau (\text{\textlquill}  \mu + \lambda \nu, \mu + \lambda\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}})\\ 
&=& \tau(\text{\textlquill}  \mu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) + \lambda \tau(\text{\textlquill}  \mu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) + \overline{\lambda} \tau(\text{\textlquill}  \nu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) + |\lambda|^2 \tau(\text{\textlquill} \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}). 
\end{eqnarray*} 
for $\lambda$ in the complex numbers. By (IP1) and positivity of $\tau$ we infer $p(\lambda)\geq 0$ for all $\lambda$. If $\tau(\text{\textlquill} \mu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) =0$ or $\tau (\text{\textlquill} \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) =0$ holds, we easily infer 
 $\tau (\text{\textlquill} \mu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) =0$ by taking suitable limits of $\lambda$. 
 
So, we can assume without loss of generality $\tau(\text{\textlquill} \mu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}})\neq 0$ and $ \tau(\text{\textlquill} \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}} ) \neq 0$. From this we find 
$$ 0\leq p(\lambda) = \tau(\text{\textlquill} \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) \left|\lambda +\frac{\tau(\text{\textlquill} \nu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}})}{\tau(\text{\textlquill}  \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}})}\right|^2  + \tau(\text{\textlquill} \mu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}) -   \frac{|\tau(\text{\textlquill} \nu,\mu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}}|^2}{\tau(\text{\textlquill}  \nu,\nu\text{\textrquill} _{{\mathcal A}})} . $$ 
Choosing $\lambda$ so that the first bracket vanishes then easily gives the desired statement.
