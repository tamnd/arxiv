<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass.CKP29V.1.1}
The sequences $\{X_i,\,i \in {\mathbb N}\}$, $\{Y_j,\,j \in {\mathbb N}\}$, $\{\Theta_i,\,\Delta_j\,,\; i,\,j \in {\mathbb N}\}$ are mutually independent.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{ass.CKP29V.2.1}
For $\{Z_i,\,i \in {\mathbb N}\}$, let suppose that there exist constants $C>0$, $x_0 >0$, such that it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.6}
\sup_{x \geq x_0} \sup_{n\geq 2} \sup_{a \in [x_0,\,x]} \dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} Z_i > x-a\;|\;Z_{n} = a \right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} Z_i > x-a \right)} \leq C\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem.CKP29V.2.1}
Assumption \ref{ass.CKP29V.2.1}, obviously contains the independence as special case. Further, it provides a general framework, that is consistent with the 
'insensitivity' of the single big jump principle, in the case of class $\mathcal{S}$ for the distributions of $\{Z_i,\,i \in {\mathbb N}\}$, see for example 
\cite[Th. 3.1]{ko:tang:2008} and relative discussions in \cite{jiang:gao:wang:2014}. For these reasons Assumption \ref{ass.CKP29V.2.1} and its extensions have been 
used in many applications of risk theory, see for example \cite{zhang:cheng:2017},  \cite{chen:li:cheng:2023}, \cite{chen:wang:cheng:yan:2023}. We refer the reader to \cite{yang:liu:huang:ma:2012} and \cite{zhang:cheng:2017}, for examples that satisfy Assumption \ref{ass.CKP29V.2.1}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
\label{ass.CKP29V.2.2}
Let suppose that the $\{Z_i,\,i \in {\mathbb N}\}$ are pairwise tail asymptotically independent, namely for any $i,\,j \in {\mathbb N}$, with  $i \neq j$, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.7}
\lim_{x_i \wedge x_j \to \infty} \mathbf{P}\left( Z_i > x_i\;|\;Z_j > x_j \right)=0\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
<!-- end assumption-3 -->
<!-- assumption-4: body_md -->
\label{ass.CKP29V.3.1}
The pair $(\Theta_1,\,\Delta_1)$ is independent of $\{(\Theta_i,\,\Delta_j)\,,\; i,\,j \in {\mathbb N}\,,\; i \wedge j \geq 2\}$.
<!-- end assumption-4 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem.CKP29V.3.1}
The dependence between insurer's two lines of business, under the Assumption \ref{ass.CKP29V.1.1}, stems only from the dependence between the $\{N(t),\,t \geq 0 \}$  
and $\{M(t),\,t \geq 0\}$. We can see that Assumption \ref{ass.CKP29V.3.1} contains a wide spectrum of counting processes, that escape from the common renewal driven 
models and their generalizations. A such kind of assumption, in combination with its application on risk models with subexponential claims, was used first time by \cite{cheng:yang:wang:2020}, while another one slightly stricter (with additional condition that the $\Theta_1,\,\Delta_1$ are independent each other), was used by \cite{chen:li:cheng:2023}. The assumption  that the $(\Theta_1,\,\Delta_1)$ are independent of the rest of inter-arrival times is very helpful from mathematical point of view, since it permits application of the Kesten inequalities. From practical point of view Assumption \ref{ass.CKP29V.3.1} is not much stricter than the condition: ' $\{\Theta_i,\,\Delta_j\,,\; i,\,j \in {\mathbb N}\}$ are arbitrarily dependent', since in many practical cases at the beginning of the operation of a portfolio-company, appear some 'irregularities' that get smoothed out by the time, see for example \cite{wang:2008}, for relative discussions.
<!-- end remark-2 -->

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem.CKP29V.3.3}
Theorem \ref{th.CKP29V.3.1} generalizes \cite[Lem. 4.4]{yang:li:2017}, with respect to (1) and (2) of subsection \ref{subsec.CKP29V.1.2}. The generalization with respect to (1), follows from the fact that if the $\{N(t),\,t \geq 0 \}$ and $\{M(t),\,t \geq 0\}$ are renewal processes, then \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.2} holds, and even for any fixed $T >0$. Indeed, in such a case, the delayed processes from \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.1} should be also renewal, and hence from \cite{stein:1946} they have analytic moment-generating functions near to zero.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem.CKP29V.3.4}
The statement of Theorem \ref{th.CKP29V.3.1} depends on the choice of $T>0$, due to condition \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.2}. However, as comes from the Remark \ref{rem.CKP29V.3.3}, in the renewal case, through Stein's lemma, for any fixed $T > 0$, there exists a properly chosen $\beta=\beta(T) >0$, such that relation \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.2} holds. 

The same is true for some quasi-renewal processes (i.e counting processes with identical but not necessarily independent inter-arrival times), see \cite[Lem. 2.2]{wang:cheng:2011}, and for some non-homogeneous renewal processes  (i.e. independent but not necessarily identical inter-arrival times), see 
\cite[Cor. 2.1]{bernackaite:siaulys:2015}. In such cases, (namely, when \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.2} holds for any fixed $T>0$), and following the path of our proof, we obtain \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.3} local uniformly for $T \in \Lambda :=\{t\;:\;\mathbf{P}(\Theta_1 \leq t\,,\;\Delta_1 \leq t) >0 \}$ (that means uniformly for $T \in \Lambda_{T^*}:= [0,\,T^*]\cap \Lambda$, for any fixed $T^* \in \Lambda$).

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{exam.CKP29V.a} 
Let consider that $\{\Theta_i\,,\;i \in {\mathbb N} \}$ are identically distributed and widely lower orthant dependent (WLOD) (see \cite{wang:wang:gao:2013} for introduction and examples of such kind of dependence), that means there exists a sequence of positive numbers $\{g_L^{\Theta}(n)\,,\;n \in {\mathbb N}\}$, called dominating sequence, such that for any 
$n \in {\mathbb N}$, $x_1,\,\ldots,\,x_n \in {\mathbb R}$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.A}
\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^n \{\Theta_i \leq x_i\} \right) \leq g_L^{\Theta}(n)\,\prod_{i=1}^n \mathbf{P}(\Theta_i \leq x_i)\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
and further we suppose that there exists some $0 < \varepsilon < -\log \mathbf{E}^{1/2}[e^{-2\,r_1\,p_1\,\Theta_1 }]$, such that it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.B}
\limsup_{n \to \infty} g_L^{\Theta}(n)\,e^{-\varepsilon\,n} < \infty\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent

Similarly, we suppose that $\{\Delta_j\,,\;j \in {\mathbb N} \}$ are identically distributed and WLOD with dominating sequence $\{g_L^{\Delta}(n)\,,\;n \in {\mathbb N}\}$, 
and there exists some $0 < \varepsilon^*< -\log \mathbf{E}^{1/2}[e^{-2\,r_2\,q_1\,\Delta_1}]$ such that it holds
\begin{eqnarray*}
\limsup_{n \to \infty} g_L^{\Delta}(n)\,e^{-\varepsilon^*\,n} < \infty\,.
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
Then the convergences in \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.4} are true.

{\bf Proof.}~
We shall prove only the convergence of the first sum, since the other follows similarly. Due to the fact that the $\{\Theta_i\,,\; i \in {\mathbb N}\}$ are 
identically distributed, we obtain that for any fixed $\gamma >1$ it holds
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,p_2}\,\mathbf{E}^{1/2}[e^{-2\,p_1\,r_1\,\tau_i}] = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,p_2}\,\mathbf{E}[e^{-2\,p_1\,r_1\,\Theta_1\,i}] \\[2mm]
&& \leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i^{\gamma\,p_2}\,g_L(i)\,\left(\mathbf{E}^{1/2}[e^{-2\,p_1\,r_1\,\Theta_1}] \right)^i < \infty\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
where at the second step we used \cite[Prop. 1.1(1)]{wang:wang:gao:2013}. The convergence at the last sum follows by relation \eqref{eq.CKP29V.B} in combination with the fact that 
$0 < \varepsilon < -\log \mathbf{E}^{1/2}[e^{-2\,p_1\,r_1\,\theta_1}]$.
~\hfill \qed

Let $Z_1,\,\ldots,\,Z_n\,,\;n \in {\mathbb N}$, non-negative, random variables with distributions $V_1,\,\ldots,\,V_n \in \mathcal{L}$, that satisfy Assumption \ref{ass.CKP29V.4.A^*}. We choose some large enough $N \in {\mathbb N}$ and for $x\wedge y >0$ we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.7b}
&&\mathbf{P}[D_1(t)  > x\,,\;D_2(t) >y] = \mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)} X_i\,e^{-r_1\,\tau_i} >x\,,\;\sum_{j=1}^{M(t)} Y_j\,e^{-r_2\,\sigma_j} >y\right) \\[2mm] \notag
&&=\left[\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} -\sum_{n=1}^{N} \sum_{m=1}^{N}  \right) + \sum_{n=1}^{N} \sum_{m=1}^{N} \right] \mathbf{P}\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\,e^{-r_1\,\tau_i} >x\,,\;\sum_{j=1}^{m} Y_j\,e^{-r_2\,\sigma_j} >y\,, \\[2mm] \notag

Let start with $J_1(x,\,T,\,N)$. Initially we obtain that it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.8}

&&=J_{11}(x,\,T,\,N)+J_{12}(x,\,T,\,N)\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
For $J_{11}(x,\,T,\,N)$, for any $\varepsilon >0$, we find
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.9} \notag
&&J_{11}(x,\,T,\,N) \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\,e^{-r_1\,\tau_1} >x\,,\;\sum_{j=1}^{m} Y_j\,e^{-r_2\,\sigma_1} >y\,,\;\tau_n \leq T\,,\; \sigma_m \leq T\right) \\[2mm] 
&&=  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} \int_0^T \int_0^T \mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\,e^{-r_1\,s} >x\,,\;\sum_{j=1}^{m} Y_j\,e^{-r_2\,t} >y\right)\\[2mm] \notag
&&\times \,\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n} \Theta_i \leq T-s\,,\;\sum_{j=2}^{m} \Delta_j \leq T-t \right)\,\mathbf{P}(\Theta_1 \in ds\,,\;\Delta_1 \in dt) \\[2mm] \notag
&&\leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} \int_0^T \int_0^T \mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i >x\,e^{r_1\,s}\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m} Y_j >y\,e^{r_2\,t}\right)\\[2mm] \notag
&&\times \,\mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) \geq n-1\,,\;M^*(T) \geq m-1 \right)\,\mathbf{P}(\Theta_1 \in ds\,,\;\Delta_1 \in dt) \\[2mm] \notag
&&\leq K_1\,K_2\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} (1+\varepsilon)^{n+m}\,\mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) \geq n-1\,,\;M^*(T) \geq m-1 \right)\\[2mm] \notag

At the fourth step the constants $K_1=K_1(\varepsilon) >0$ and $K_2=K_2(\varepsilon) >0$, are given by the application of Lemma \ref{lem.CKP29V.3.1} twice. Further we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} (1+\varepsilon)^{n+m}\,\mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) \geq n-1\,,\;M^*(T) \geq m-1 \right) \\[2mm]
&&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} (1+\varepsilon)^{n+m}\,\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty} \sum_{j=m-1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) =i\,,\;M^*(T) =j \right)  \\[2mm]

\end{eqnarray}\noindent
For $J_{221}(x,\,T,\,N)$ we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.17}
&&J_{221}(x,\,T,\,N) =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m}\\[2mm]  \notag
&&\,\int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T-s) \geq n-1\,,\;M^*(T -t) \geq m-1\right)\, \\[2mm]  \notag
&&\times \mathbf{P}(\Theta_1 \in ds\,,\;\Delta_1 \in dt)\leq \left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} n\,m \,\mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) \geq n-1\,,\;M^*(T) \geq m-1\right) \,\right)  \\[2mm]  \notag
&&\times \int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right] \,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent

Further we have 
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.18}
&&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} n\,m \,\mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) \geq n-1\,,\;M^*(T) \geq m-1\right)  \\[2mm]  \notag
&&= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty} n\,m \,\sum_{i=n-1}^{\infty} \sum_{j=m-1}^{\infty} \mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) =i\,,\;M^*(T) =j\right)  \\[2mm]  \notag
&&=\,\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \sum_{j=N}^{\infty} \mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) =i\,,\;M^*(T) =j\right) \sum_{n=1}^{i+1}  n\,\sum_{m=N+1}^{j+1} m   \\[2mm]  \notag
&& \leq \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \sum_{j=N}^{\infty} (1+ i)^2\,(1+j)^2\, \mathbf{P}\left(N^*(T) =i\,,\;M^*(T) =j\right)  \\[2mm]  \notag
&& =\mathbf{E}\left[(N^*(T) +1)^2\,(M^*(T)+1)^2\,{\bf 1}_{\{M^*(T) \geq N\}}\right]  \\[2mm]  \notag
&&\leq \mathbf{E}^{1/2}\left[(N^*(T) +1)^4\right] \,\mathbf{E}^{1/2}\left[(M^*(T)+1)^4\,{\bf 1}_{\{M^*(T) \geq N\}}\right] \,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
where at the second step we changed the order of summation, while at the last step we applied the H\"{o}lder inequality. From the Assumption \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.2}, for any $\delta \in (0,\,1)$ we can find some large enough $N$, such that from relations \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.17} and \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.18} is implied
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.19}
J_{221}(x,\,T,\,N) \leq \dfrac {\delta}2  \int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right]\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent

Similarly, due to symmetry, it holds
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.20}
J_{222}(x,\,T,\,N) \leq \dfrac {\delta}2  \int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right]\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
From relations \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.19} and \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.20}, in combination with relation \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.16} we obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.21}
J_{22}(x,\,T,\,N) \leq \delta \int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right]\,.
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
Putting \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.15} and \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.21} into \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.14} we find
\begin{eqnarray} \label{eq.CKP29V.2.22}
&&(1-\delta) \int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right] \lesssim\\[2mm] \notag
&&J_{2}(x,\,T,\,N) \lesssim \int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right]\,,
\end{eqnarray}\noindent
Putting \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.12} and \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.22} into \eqref{eq.CKP29V.2.7b} we obtain
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&(1-\delta)\,\int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right] \lesssim\\[2mm] \notag
&&\mathbf{P}[D_1(T)  > x\,,\;D_2(T) >y] \lesssim (1+\delta)\,\int_0^T \int_0^T \,\overline{F}\big(x\,e^{r_1\,s}\big)\,\,\overline{G}\big(y\,e^{r_2\,t}\big)\,\mathbf{E}\left[N(ds)\,M(dt)\right]\,,
\end{eqnarray*}\noindent
from which by letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ gives relation \eqref{eq.CKP29V.3.3}. 
~\hfill \qed

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem.CKP29V.4.1}
Lemmas \ref{lem.CKP29V.4.5} -\ref{lem.CKP29V.4.6} establish in some sense  the presence of the multivariate non-linear single big jump principle, in finite and infinite randomly weighted sums with weights $e^{-r_1\,\tau_i}$ and $e^{-r_2\,\sigma_j}$ for $i,\,j \in {\mathbb N}$. Such kind of problems, with more general random weights, 
were studied recently by \cite{li:2018}, \cite{yang:chen:yuen:2024}, \cite{konstantinides:passalidis:2025c}. Although the most of their conditions are general, the frame is somehow different. In \cite[Th. 2.3(2)]{yang:chen:yuen:2024} the result is close to that of Lemma \ref{lem.CKP29V.4.5*}, however the convergence there is valid as $x\wedge y \to 
\infty$.
