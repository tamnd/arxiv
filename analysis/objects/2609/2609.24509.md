<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
Let $\widehat{A}_k = \widehat{V}_k \widehat{S}_k \widehat{W}_k^T$ be an inexactly computed rank-$k$ truncated SVD of $A$ satisfying the bound \eqref{eq:inexactSVD}, and let $P$ and $Q$ be defined as in \eqref{eq:PQdef}.
Assume that $P^T\widehat{V}_k$ is invertible, and let $\mathcal{P}=\widehat{V}_k(P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}P^T$ be an interpolatory projector. Then 
 
\begin{equation*}
\|A-\mathcal{P}A\| \leq \|\widehat{V}_k\| \|(P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}\| (\theta\sigma_{k+1}+\phi).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{P}=\widehat{V}_k(P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}P^T$ be an interpolatory projector. From this we have
\[
\mathcal{P}\widehat{V}_k = \widehat{V}_k \rightarrow (I-\mathcal{P})\widehat{V}_k = 0.
\]
From this,
\begin{align*}
\|A-\mathcal{P}A\| = \|(I-\mathcal{P})A\| &= \|(I-\mathcal{P})(I-\widehat{V}_k \widehat{V}_k^\dagger)A\| \\
&\leq \|I-\mathcal{P}\| \|(I-\widehat{V}_k \widehat{V}_k^\dagger)A\|.
\end{align*}
We can then write, assuming $\mathcal{P}\neq 0$ or $I$,
\[
\|I-\mathcal{P}\| = \|\mathcal{P}\| = \|\widehat{V}_k (P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}P^T\| = \|\widehat{V}_k (P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}\| \leq \|\widehat{V}_k\|\|(P^T \widehat{V}_k)^{-1}\|;
\]
note that this differs from the result in \cite[Lemma 4.1]{sorensen2016deim} since we cannot assume $\|\widehat{V}_k\|=1$.
Thus altogether we have the bound
\[
\|A-\mathcal{P}A\| \leq \|\widehat{V}_k\| \|(P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}\| \|(I-\widehat{V}_k\widehat{V}_k^\dagger)A\|.
\]

We now seek to bound the last term on the right-hand side above. Writing $A = \widehat{A}_k + (A-\widehat{A}_k)$, we have
\begin{equation}
    \|(I-\widehat{V}_k\widehat{V}_k^\dagger)A\| = \|(I-\widehat{V}_k \widehat{V}_k^\dagger)(A-\widehat{A}_k)\| \leq \|A-\widehat{A}_k\| \leq \theta\sigma_{k+1} + \phi.
\end{equation}



Substituting this above, we have 
\[
\|A-\mathcal{P}A\| \leq \|\widehat{V}_k\| \|(P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}\| (\theta\sigma_{k+1}+\phi).
\]
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:CR}
Assuming \eqref{eq:inexactSVD} holds, for $C = AQ$ and $R = P^T A$ with $Q$ and $P$ defined in \eqref{eq:PQdef},
\begin{align}
  \|(I - CC^\dagger)A\| &\leq \|\widehat{W}_k\|\,\tilde\eta_q\,(\theta\sigma_{k+1}+\phi),
  \label{eq:C_res} \\
  \|A(I - R^\dagger R)\| &\leq \|\widehat{V}_k\|\,\tilde\eta_p\,(\theta\sigma_{k+1}+\phi).
  \label{eq:R_res}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma 4.2 in \cite{sorensen2016deim}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, $1 \le k < \min(m,n)$.  Suppose the DEIM index
sets $P$ and $Q$ are computed from $\widehat{V}_k$ and $\widehat{W}_k$
satisfying \eqref{eq:inexactSVD}, with amplification factors
$\tilde\eta_p$ and $\tilde\eta_q$ defined in \eqref{eq:etas}.  Let $C = AQ$, $R = P^T A$, and
$U = C^\dagger A R^\dagger$.  Then
\begin{equation}
  \|A - CUR\|
  \leq
  \bigl(\|\widehat{W}_k\|\,\tilde\eta_q + \|\widehat{V}_k\|\,\tilde\eta_p\bigr)
  \bigl(\theta\sigma_{k+1} + \phi\bigr).
  \label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The proof is analogous to the proof of Theorem 4.1 in \cite{sorensen2016deim}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:recovery}
When the SVD is computed exactly, we have $\|\widehat{V}_k\| = \|\widehat{W}_k\| = 1$,
$\tilde\eta_p = \eta_p$, $\tilde\eta_q = \eta_q$, $\theta = 1$, $\phi = 0$,
and~\eqref{eq:main} reduces to the result of Sorensen and Embree.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma_eta_exact_DEIM}
    For the (exact) DEIM selection algorithm derived above and under the inexact SVD assumptions, 
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \tilde{\eta}_p &\leq \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}}\frac{2^k}{\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{V}_k)}\\ 
        \tilde{\eta}_q &\leq \sqrt{\frac{nk}{3}}\frac{2^k}{\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{W}_k)}        
    \end{split}
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let us prove the result for $\tilde{\eta}_p$; the result for $\tilde{\eta}_q$ follows similarly. Assume $\widehat{V}_k$ is the approximation of the left singular space coming from the inexact SVD. Then we cannot assume orthonormality. Let $p = DEIM(\widehat{V}_k)$ denote the row index vector derived from the DEIM selection scheme described above. Let $P = I(:,p)$ so that $P^T\widehat{V}_k = \widehat{V}_k(p,:)$. The DEIM selection is precisely the index selection of LU decomposition with partial pivoting, so one can write (as in the proof of Lemma 4.4 from \cite{sorensen2016deim}): 
    \begin{equation*}
        \widehat{V}_k = LT
    \end{equation*}
    Let $L_1 \equiv L(1:k,1:k)$. Then $\widehat{V}_k(p,:) = L_1T$ and thus:
    \begin{equation*}
        \tilde{\eta}_p = \|(P^T\widehat{V}_k)^{-1}\| = \|(L_1T)^{-1}\| \leq \|T^{-1}\|\|L_1^{-1}\|
    \end{equation*}
    So we seek to bound $\|T^{-1}\|$ and $\|L_1^{-1}\|$. To bound $\|T^{-1}\|$, let $y \in \mathcal{R}^k$ be a unit vector such that $\|T^{-1}y\| = \|T^{-1}\|$. Then
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \|T^{-1}\| &= \|T^{-1}y\| \\
        &= \|\widehat{V}_k^\dagger \widehat{V}_k  T^{-1}y\|\\
        &\leq \|\widehat{V}_k^\dagger\| \|\widehat{V}_k  T^{-1}y\|\\
        &\leq \|\hat{V}_k^\dagger\| \|Ly\|\\
        &\leq \frac{\|Ly\|}{\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{V}_k)}\\
        &\leq \frac{\sqrt{m k}}{\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{V}_k)}
    \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
    The rest of the proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma 4.4 from \cite{sorensen2016deim} for bounding $\|Ly\|$ and $\|L_1^{-1}\|$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:eta-bound-fp}
Let $\hat p=\mathrm{DEIM}(\widehat{V}_k)$ be the index set produced when the DEIM index selection is implemented through GEPP applied to $\widehat{V}_k$ in floating point arithmetic with unit roundoff $u_{D}$, with selection matrix $\widehat{P}$ and  
$\rho_k$ defined above. If 
\begin{equation}
    ku_D < 1 \quad\text{and}\quad \left(1+ 2^k \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}} \right)\gamma^D_k\rho_k\|\widehat{V}_k\| < \sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k),
    \label{eq:assumptions}
\end{equation}
then $\widehat{P}^T \widehat{V}_k$ is nonsingular and
\begin{equation}
  \hat\eta_p \leq \frac{2^k \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}}}{\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k)- \left(1+2^k\sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}} \right) \gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\|}.
  \label{eq:eta-bound-fp}
\end{equation}
The analogous bound holds for $\hat\eta_q$ with $\widehat{W}_k,n$ in place of $\widehat{V}_k,m$, respectively.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
From \eqref{eq:finite-lu-backward}, we have
\[
\widehat{P}^T\widehat{V}_k = \widehat{L}{L}_1 \widehat{T} - \Delta V_1, \quad \|\Delta V_1 \| \leq \gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\|.
\]
By Weyl's inequality and the assumption in \eqref{eq:assumptions}, 
\[
\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k + \Delta V) \geq \sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k)-\gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\| > 0.
\]
Then $\widehat{V}_k + \Delta V$ has full column rank and $\widehat{T}$ is nonsingular. 

Since $\widehat{T}^{-1} = (\widehat{V}_k + \Delta V)^\dagger \widehat{L}$, the bound $\|\widehat{L}\|\leq \sqrt{mk}$ gives
\[
\| \widehat{T}^{-1}\|\leq \frac{\sqrt{mk}}{\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k-\gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k \|)}.
\]
Combining this with $\|\widehat{L}_1^{-1}\|\leq 2^k/\sqrt{3}$ gives
\[
\| (\widehat{L}_1 \widehat{T})^{-1}\| \leq \frac{2^k \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}}}{\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k)-\gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\|}.
\]
Applying Weyl's inequality again gives
\begin{align*}
\sigma_{min}(\widehat{P}^T\widehat{V}_k) &\geq \sigma_{min}(\widehat{L}_1 \widehat{T}) - \|\Delta V_1 \| \\
&\geq \frac{\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k)-\gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\|}{2^k \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}}}-\gamma^D_k\rho_k\|\widehat{V}_k\|\\
&= \frac{\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k) - \left(1+2^k\sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}}\right)\gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\|}{2^k \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}}},
\end{align*}
which is positive by \eqref{eq:assumptions} and which gives the bound \eqref{eq:eta-bound-fp}.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
We can use \eqref{eq:eta-bound-fp} to give guidance on how to choose $u_{D}$. 
We can say that the finite precision bound remains close to the exact arithmetic bound whenever
\[
\left(1+ 2^k\sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}} \right) \frac{\gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\|}{\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k)} \ll 1.
\]
Note that here we can only say that the two worst case bounds will be of similar magnitude; we can not necessarily say that the finite precision algorithm produces an amplification factor close to that obtained by the exact algorithm. Assuming $ku_D \ll 1$, we have $\gamma^D_k \approx ku_D$. Unless we have computed a very bad inexact SVD, we still expect $\Vert \widehat{V}_k\Vert$ and $\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{V}_k)$ to be close to 1 (we will make this more concrete in the following section). Then $\|\widehat{V}_k\|/\sigma_{min}(\widehat{V}_k)\approx 1$, and the above condition becomes 
 
\begin{equation}
k u_{D} \rho_k \left( 1+ 2^k \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}} \right) \ll 1.
\label{eq:uDEIM}
\end{equation}
Unless we have computed a very bad inexact SVD, we still expect $\Vert \widehat{V}_k\Vert$ and $\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{V}_k)$ to be close to 1 (we will make this more concrete in the following section). This means that we only need $u_{D} < 1/(k \rho_k)$. While we can not know $\rho_k$ ahead of time, it is expected that in most practical cases, this factor will be moderate. This means that we can get away with using potentially \emph{very} low precision in the DEIM selection algorithm.

If we also want to guarantee that the amplification factors $\widehat{\eta}_p$ do not grow significantly, we may require that, e.g., 
\[
\gamma^D_k \rho_k \|\widehat{V}_k\| \sqrt{\frac{mk}{3}}\;\frac{2^k}{\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{V}_k) - \gamma^D_k\rho_k\|\widehat{V}_k\|} \leq 1.
\]
 
On the left-hand side above, we have approximately $k \rho_k u_{D}$ multiplied by (approximately) the amplification factor for exact DEIM. This says that if we expect a very large amplification factor due to the DEIM algorithm itself, then we must use higher precision. Unfortunately, we can not know the amplification factor ahead of time. Experimentally, we have found that the amplification factors are usually moderate, which echoes the insight of \cite{sorensen2016deim} where the authors comment that they never observed exponential growth over very extensive testing. This suggests that low precision is in many cases suitable for DEIM.
