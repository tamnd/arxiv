A predicate $\mathcal{P}$ on properties
(``positive''), with the axioms of~\cite[Fig.~7]{J75}: \texttt{Ax1},
$\lfloor \mathcal{P}\varphi \wedge \mathcal{P}\psi \supset \mathcal{P}(\lambda x.\ \varphi x \wedge \psi x)\rfloor$;
\texttt{Ax2a}, $\lfloor \mathcal{P}\varphi \vee^{e} \mathcal{P}{\sim}\varphi\rfloor$;
\texttt{Ax2b}, $\lfloor \mathcal{P}\varphi\supset\Box\mathcal{P}\varphi\rfloor$;
\texttt{Ax3}, $\lfloor \mathcal{P}\,E\rfloor$; \texttt{Ax4}, $\lfloor \mathcal{P}\varphi \wedge (\varphi\supset_N\psi)
\supset \mathcal{P}\psi\rfloor$; and the generalised conjunction axiom
\[
\texttt{Ax1Gen}:\qquad \lfloor (\mathit{PosProps}\,\Phi \wedge
  \mathit{ConjOfPropsFrom}\,\varphi\,\Phi) \supset \mathcal{P}\varphi\rfloor,
\]
where $\Phi$ is a (world-dependent) set of properties, $\mathit{PosProps}\,\Phi$ says
that every member of $\Phi$ is positive, and $\mathit{ConjOfPropsFrom}\,\varphi\,\Phi =
\Box(\forall^{E} z.\ \varphi z \leftrightarrow \forall\psi.\ \Phi\psi\supset\psi z)$
says that $\varphi$ is necessarily the conjunction of $\Phi$.  This axiom formalises
G\"odel's footnote on ``any number of summands''~\cite{GoedelNotes}, following Anderson and
Gettings~\cite{AndersonGettings1996} and Fitting~\cite{Fitting2002}.  An axiom of that form
stands in G\"odel's own notes of 1952/53, as Kanckos and Lethen transcribe and reconstruct
them~\cite[p.~1019]{KanckosLethen2021}: in their notation $\Delta A$ is the conjunction of the
properties a second-order $A$ collects, and G\"odel's Axiom~2 says that $\Delta A$ is positive if
every property $A$ collects is positive; the positivity of being God-like is Theorem~1 of those
notes.  Axiom~2 carries no restriction on $A$, as the footnote carries none on the number of
summands.  Definitions: $G\,x =
\forall\varphi.\ \mathcal{P}\varphi\supset\varphi x$ (God-like); $\varphi\supset_N\psi =
\Box(\forall^{E} y.\ \varphi y\supset\psi y)$ (necessary property inclusion);
$\varphi\mathrel{\mathit{Ess}} x = \varphi x \wedge \forall\psi.\ \psi x \supset (\varphi\supset_N\psi)$
(essence, with the conjunct $\varphi x$ that repairs G\"odel's 1970 definition);
$E\,x = \forall\varphi.\ \varphi\mathrel{\mathit{Ess}} x\supset\Box\exists^{E} y.\ \varphi y$ (necessary
existence).  The conjunct $\varphi x$ is Scott's, but it was not new with him: G\"odel had it
already in the essence of one of his own earlier versions, the one Kanckos and Lethen
conjecture to be the latest of them~\cite[p.~1017]{KanckosLethen2021}.

As variant 2, but with the definitions of~\cite[Fig.~8]{J75}: essence is G\"odel's
original $\varphi\mathrel{\mathit{Ess}} x = \forall\psi.\ \psi x\supset(\varphi\supset_N\psi)$ and the

The two essences are not interdefinable, and it is worth saying which way the difference runs.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:esscompare}
Variant~2's essence implies variant~3's from the definitions alone (\texttt{Ess\_of\_Ess2}).  The
converse fails: with no axiom of the argument assumed, Nitpick finds a $\varphi$ that is an
essence of $x$ in the sense of~\cite[Fig.~8]{J75} and is not had by $x$ (\texttt{EssenceComparison}).  In the
theories of the argument the two coincide, $\varphi x$ being a theorem there
(\texttt{Ess\_iff\_Ess2}, and \texttt{Ess\_iff\_Ess2\_K} with no frame condition and hybrid-free).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From $\varphi x$ the property $\varphi$ is not $\lambda z.\bot$, and that inequality holds at
every world, so variant~2's essence carries the clause of~\cite[Fig.~8]{J75} under the $\Box$ and with it
variant~3's.  Conversely variant~3's gives variant~2's inclusion by dropping the clause, so
that only $\varphi x$ is wanting --- the clause says that $\varphi$ is not the empty property,

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:hybridfree}
A proof is \emph{hybrid-free} if every term it substitutes for a variable of one of the types
below stays within the object language.  These are the types the axioms quantify over beyond
the individuals, and they are of two kinds.  The \emph{world-lifted} types are those built from
$e$ and $\sigma$ that end in $\sigma$: a proposition $\sigma$, a property $\tau$, a
world-dependent set of properties $(e\to\sigma)\to\sigma$.  The others end in $o$ and take a
world-lifted argument, as a set of properties \emph{simpliciter} $(e\to\sigma)\to o$ does.  At a world-lifted type the substituted term must
be a term of $\mathcal{L}$ of that type.  The other types have no $\mathcal{L}$-terms at all, since
the formulas of $\mathcal{L}$ are world-lifted.  There the substituted term must express a
condition formed in
$\mathcal{L}$ --- the validity of formulas of $\mathcal{L}$, combined by the propositional
connectives and by quantification over anything but worlds --- as
$\{\psi\mid\lfloor \chi\,\psi\rfloor\}$ does, for a formula $\chi$ of $\mathcal{L}$ with a free property
variable.  Such a
substitution is made in an axiom instance, in instantiating the quantifier in the definition of
God-likeness, of essence or of necessary existence, in the set $\Phi$ of \texttt{Ax1Gen}, and in a lemma or a hypothesis applied
to such a term: wherever a variable of one of those types is instantiated.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:sound}
If the detector accepts every witness of a proof, then that proof is hybrid-free: every term
it substitutes at one of the types of Definition~\ref{def:hybridfree} is a term of
$\mathcal{L}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The clauses of the grammar are those of the definition of $\mathcal{L}$ above, one for one:
(i)~the connectives of the embedding, the polymorphic ones --- the possibilist quantifiers and
the lifted equalities --- at a type of $\mathcal{L}$, or at the type of worlds, where the
quantifier is the universal or the existential modality and the equality is flagged; (ii)~the
raw connectives, equality and quantification they unfold to, likewise restricted; (iii)~the
constants the theory declares, at a type of $\mathcal{L}$; and (iv)~variables of a type of
$\mathcal{L}$, bound in the term or outside it, and worlds bound in the term, the body of each
such binder mentioning no other.  A witness the detector accepts is built from these and
nothing else, and induction on the witness gives the rest: a subterm of type $\sigma$ speaks, under its binder, of
its own world of evaluation alone, so it is a term of $\mathcal{L}$ at that world; a raw
quantifier over worlds whose body does the same is $\mathsf{A}F$ or $\mathsf{E}F$ for such a
term $F$; and at the other types of $\mathcal{L}$ the term is a condition formed in
$\mathcal{L}$, which is what the definition asks there.  Definitions other than the
connectives are unfolded before the check, so no witness reaches outside $\mathcal{L}$ behind
a defined constant.  The grammar thus admits nothing the definition does not admit; it is a
whitelist, not a list of patterns a term must avoid.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:lgeneral}
An $\mathcal{L}$-general model is a Kripke model $(W, r, D, \mathit{existsAt})$ with a domain
$P_\tau$ for each type $\tau$ of $\mathcal{L}$ --- $P_e = D$, $P_\sigma \subseteq 2^W$,
$P_{e\to\sigma} \subseteq D \to P_\sigma$, and so on --- closed under $\mathcal{L}$: every term
of $\mathcal{L}$ of type $\tau$ with parameters from the domains denotes in $P_\tau$.  The
signature is interpreted on the domains.  It is a model of a set $T$ of sentences of
$\mathcal{L}$ --- the axioms of a variant, say --- if every sentence of $T$ holds in it, its
quantifiers at types of $\mathcal{L}$ ranging over the $P_\tau$.  A standard model is one with every $P_\tau$ full; a Henkin model of
HOL one closed under every term of HOL.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:lgeneral}
Let $T$ be a set of sentences of $\mathcal{L}$ and $\pi$ a proof in HOL of $\lfloor \varphi\rfloor$
from $T$, with $\varphi$ a term of $\mathcal{L}$.  Suppose that $\pi$ uses no constant of a
type of $\mathcal{L}$ outside the signature except through its definition, and that every term
of $\pi$ whose denotation is used as an element of a domain of a type of $\mathcal{L}$ is, after
unfolding the defined notions and folding back the connectives, a term of $\mathcal{L}$ with
parameters of types of $\mathcal{L}$.  The positions in question are the argument of a constant
of the signature, the argument of a variable of such a type and the instance of a quantifier at
such a type, wherever in $\pi$ they lie --- in a hypothesis, a motive, a side of an equation or
the statement itself.  Then $\lfloor \varphi\rfloor$ holds in every $\mathcal{L}$-general model of $T$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Fix an $\mathcal{L}$-general model $M$ of $T$.  Interpret the types of HOL standardly over the
worlds and individuals of $M$: the type of worlds by $W$, the individuals by $D$, $o$ by the two
truth values, and every function type by the full function space.  So every term of HOL
denotes, by the usual recursion modulo $\beta$- and $\eta$-conversion, and a term of a type
$\tau$ of $\mathcal{L}$ denotes a function on the full domains whether or not it lies in
$P_\tau$.  Three things are relativised to $M$.  A quantifier at a type $\tau$ of $\mathcal{L}$
ranges over $P_\tau$; every other quantifier ranges over its full domain.  Equality at a type of
$\mathcal{L}$ is agreement on $P$: identity at $e$, and at $\sigma$, whose argument type is the
type of worlds, not a type of $\mathcal{L}$, so that agreement on every world is identity; and
at a function type $\tau_1\to\tau_2$ of $\mathcal{L}$ the relation ``$f$ and $g$ agree, in this
sense, on every element of $P_{\tau_1}$''.  And a constant of the signature, as well as the value
of a variable at a function type $\tau\to\tau'$ of $\mathcal{L}$, which is a function on
$P_\tau$, is extended to the full domain by restriction: its value at an argument $F$ of the
full domain is its value at $F{\restriction}P_\tau$ whenever that restriction is an element of
$P_\tau$, and arbitrary otherwise, so that arguments which agree on $P$ receive the same value;
the connectives of the embedding and the defined constants are interpreted by their
definitions.  Under this interpretation the sentences
of $T$ hold, since $M$ is a model of $T$ with its quantifiers at types of $\mathcal{L}$ ranging
over the $P_\tau$.  What the hypothesis buys is this: wherever $\pi$ uses a term of a type of
$\mathcal{L}$ as an element of $P$ --- as the instance of a quantifier at that type, as the
argument of a constant of the signature, or as the argument of a variable of a type of
$\mathcal{L}$, which ranges over $P$; after unfolding the defined notions, an application of a
term of a type of $\mathcal{L}$ is one of these --- that term is a term of $\mathcal{L}$ with
parameters of types of $\mathcal{L}$, the parameters range over $P$, and by closure it denotes in
$P_\tau$.  Now the rules of HOL that $\pi$ uses.  $\forall$-elimination at a type of
$\mathcal{L}$, from $\forall x.\ A$ to $A[t/x]$: $t$ denotes in $P_\tau$, the range of the
quantifier; at every other type the range is full.  $\exists$-introduction likewise.
$\forall$-introduction and $\exists$-elimination: a derivation of $A$ for an arbitrary $x$ holds
for every element of the full domain, hence for every element of $P_\tau$.  Equality is
reflexive, and substitutive: from $s = t$ and $A(s)$ to $A(t)$, where $A$ applies $s$ to an
argument that argument is an element of $P$ and $s$, $t$ agree there; where $A$ hands $s$ to a
constant of the signature or to a variable of a type of $\mathcal{L}$, $s$ and $t$ are elements
of $P$ and equal as such, and a constant or variable extended by restriction gives the same
value to arguments that agree on $P$; and where $s$ is of type $e$ or $\sigma$ the two agree
outright.  A context of a type outside $\mathcal{L}$, a variable $h : \tau \to i$ say, could
tell $s$ from $t$, but it cannot be used: the Leibniz instance $\lambda x.\ h\,x = h\,s$ would
be an instance of a quantifier at a type of $\mathcal{L}$ with a parameter of a type outside
$\mathcal{L}$, which the hypothesis forbids.
Extensionality of functions at a type $\tau_1\to\tau_2$ of $\mathcal{L}$, from
$\forall x.\ f x = g x$ to $f = g$: the relativised premise says that $f$ and $g$ agree on
$P_{\tau_1}$, which is the relativised conclusion; at every other type it is the standard
extensionality of functions.  Extensionality of propositions, the Boolean rules and excluded
middle hold because $o$ is interpreted by the truth values.  Choice at a type other than those of
$\mathcal{L}$ is standard choice; a choice term at a type of $\mathcal{L}$ is not a term of
$\mathcal{L}$, so by the hypothesis it never stands where an element of $P$ is required, and
where it stands it denotes like any other term.  $\beta$- and $\eta$-conversion hold because the
interpretation is modulo them.  So every line of $\pi$ holds under the interpretation, and so
does the last, $\lfloor \varphi\rfloor$; and since $\varphi$ is a term of $\mathcal{L}$, whose quantifiers
range over the $P_\tau$, that is $\lfloor \varphi\rfloor$ holding in $M$.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:separating}
Let $\varphi := \mathsf{A}\,\exists p.\ (\mathsf{Atom}\,p \wedge p)$, ``every world lies in an
atom'', a sentence of $\mathcal{L}$ with $\mathsf{Atom}$ as above.  It holds in every standard
model, and the embedding proves it with a nominal: at a world $w$ take $p := \lambda u.\ u = w$;
$p$ holds at $w$, $p \neq \bot$, and $\mathsf{D}p$ fails at the one world of $p$, since every
$\psi$ that meets $p$ contains $w$.  The detector flags the witness \textsc{nominal} and
\textsc{rigid}.  Now let $W$ be the infinite $0$-$1$-sequences, let every world see every world,
let $D$ be a single individual that exists at every world, and $\mathcal{P}$ the empty set at every
world.  Let $G$ be the group of \emph{flips}, the maps $x \mapsto x \oplus d$ for a $0$-$1$-sequence
$d$, acting on $W$ and, by conjugation, on the full type hierarchy over $W$ and $D$; the flips
that leave the first $n$ coordinates alone form the subgroup $G_n$.  Call an object of type
$\tau$ \emph{finitely supported} if some $G_n$ fixes it, and let $P_\tau$ consist of the
hereditarily finitely supported objects: at a function type, the finitely supported functions
from $P_{\tau_1}$ to $P_{\tau_2}$.  This is the Fraenkel--Mostowski
construction~\cite[Ch.~15]{Jech2003} over $W$, in the form Pitts gives it~\cite{Pitts2013}.
Three facts.  (a)~The $P_\tau$ form an $\mathcal{L}$-general model $M$ of the empty set of
sentences: a term of $\mathcal{L}$ with finitely supported parameters denotes a finitely
supported object, by induction on terms, the case of a quantifier at a type $\tau$ because $G_n$
permutes $P_\tau$ and, once $n$ supports every parameter, fixes the parameters, so that the set
defined is fixed by $G_n$.  (b)~At type $\sigma$, finitely supported means \emph{finitary}:
membership depends on the first $n$ coordinates alone, since the flip of every coordinate beyond
$n$ at which $x$ and $y$ differ carries $x$ to $y$.  (c)~$M$ has no atoms: for $p \in P_\sigma$
with support $n$ and $w \in p$, flipping coordinate $n$ of $w$ gives a $w' \in p$, so that
$q := p \cap \{x \mid x_n \neq w_n\} \in P_\sigma$ is nonempty, inside $p$ and omits $w$; thus
$\mathsf{D}p$ holds at $w$ and $\mathsf{Atom}\,p$ fails.  In particular no singleton lies in
$P_\sigma$: $M$ is not a Henkin model of HOL, whose domains, as noted above, contain every
singleton.  Hence $\varphi$ is false at every world of $M$, and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:lgeneral} no proof of $\varphi$ meets the proposition's hypothesis: the
certificate excludes a sentence the embedding proves.  The propositional part of this --- a deep
embedding of $\mathcal{L}$ with its quantifiers over propositions, the family $P_\sigma$ and its
closure, the absence of atoms, and $\varphi$ false in $M$ and true in every standard model ---
is formalised in both systems (\texttt{Separation.thy}, \texttt{Separation.lean}); the higher types rest
on (a).  Two remarks.  No model with finitely many worlds separates: there the intersection of
the $p \in P_\sigma$ containing $w$ is a finite intersection, lies in $P_\sigma$ and is an atom
containing $w$, so $\varphi$ holds; a separating model is infinite.  And $M$ is not a model of
the axioms of variant~2, whatever $\mathcal{P}$ is taken to be: the modal collapse holds there
hybrid-free (Theorem~\ref{thm:consequences}(b)), so $X \subseteq \Box X$ for every
$X \in P_\sigma$, which fails for every finitary $X$ other than $\emptyset$ and $W$, since under
the total relation $\Box X$ is empty unless $X = W$.  Nothing in
Sections~\ref{sec:gex}--\ref{sec:neighbours} is touched; what the example shows is that the
question left open at the five places Section~\ref{sec:open} collects has content.  A frame with
an order separates as well: the irrationals under $<$, with $G$ the order-automorphisms of the
reals that map the rationals onto themselves, where a finitely supported set of type $\sigma$ is
a finite union of gaps between rationals of the support, Cantor's back-and-forth acting
transitively on the irrationals of each gap; that model is not formalised.
