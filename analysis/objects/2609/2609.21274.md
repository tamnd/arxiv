<!-- question-1: body_md -->
\label{quest:mc-dcpo-product}
Let $P$ and $Q$ be countable meet-continuous domains. Must
\[
 \sigma(P\times Q)=\sigma(P)\times\sigma(Q)
\]
hold?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- question-2: body_md -->
\label{quest:mc-dcpo-sober}
Is the Scott space of every countable meet-continuous domain sober?
<!-- end question-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:cofinal-chain}
Every countable directed subset $D$ of a poset contains a countable cofinal chain $C$, so that $\mathord{\downarrow}C=\mathord{\downarrow}D$. If $D$ has no largest element, $C$ can be chosen strictly ascending. The two sets have the same supremum whenever it exists.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
If $D$ has a largest element, use the corresponding singleton. Otherwise enumerate $D=\{d_n:n\in\mathbb N\}$ and set $c_0=d_0$. Given $c_n$, choose $e_n\in D$ with $e_n\nleq c_n$ and a common upper bound $c_{n+1}\in D$ of $c_n,d_{n+1},e_n$. Then $c_n<c_{n+1}$ and $d_i\le c_n$ for $i\le n$. Hence $C=\{c_n:n\in\mathbb N\}$ is cofinal in $D$, and $C,D$ have the same upper bounds.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:meet-mc}
If a dcpo $P$ has binary meets, condition \eqref{eq:general-mc} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meet-mc}
 x\wedge\bigvee D=\bigvee_{d\in D}(x\wedge d)
 ~(x\in P,\ D\subseteq P\text{ directed}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Assume \eqref{eq:general-mc} and put $z=x\wedge\bigvee D$. The set $\mathord{\downarrow}z\cap\mathord{\downarrow}D$ is generated downward by the directed set $\{z\wedge d:d\in D\}$. Its Scott closure is the principal ideal of the latter set's supremum. Thus \eqref{eq:general-mc} gives
\[
 z=\bigvee_{d\in D}(z\wedge d)=\bigvee_{d\in D}(x\wedge d),
\]
since $d\le\bigvee D$. Conversely, if \eqref{eq:meet-mc} holds and $x\le\bigvee D$, the directed set $\{x\wedge d:d\in D\}$ lies in $\mathord{\downarrow}x\cap\mathord{\downarrow}D$ and has supremum $x$. This implies \eqref{eq:general-mc}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:complete-tests}
A family $\mathcal D$ of directed subsets of a dcpo $P$ is \emph{complete for Scott openness} if an upper set $U\subseteq P$ is Scott open exactly when
\[
 \bigvee D\in U~\Longrightarrow~ D\cap U\ne\emptyset
 ~(D\in\mathcal D).
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:lattice-input}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item Every countable meet-continuous complete lattice admits a countable family of directed subsets complete for Scott openness.
\item If dcpos $P$ and $Q$ each admit such a countable complete family, then $\sigma(P\times Q)=\sigma(P)\times\sigma(Q)$.
\item Every countable meet-continuous complete lattice has a sober Scott space.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:L-dcpo}
A dcpo $P$ is an \emph{$L$-dcpo} if every principal ideal $\mathord{\downarrow}r$, $r\in P$, is a complete lattice in its induced order.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:principal-localization}
Let $P$ be a dcpo and $r\in P$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item The principal ideal $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ is a sub-dcpo, and its Scott topology is the subspace topology induced by $\Sigma P$.
\item $P$ is meet-continuous if and only if each $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ is meet-continuous.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Any directed subset of $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ has its supremum in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$, computed as in $P$. If $A$ is Scott closed in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$, it is lower in $P$, because $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ is lower, and it is closed under directed suprema in $P$. Thus $A$ is Scott closed in $P$. Conversely, intersections of Scott-closed subsets of $P$ with $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ are Scott closed in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$. This proves~(1).

Suppose $P$ is meet-continuous, $D\subseteq \mathord{\downarrow}r$ is directed and $x\in \mathord{\downarrow}r$ satisfies $x\le\bigvee D$. The set $\mathord{\downarrow}x\cap\mathord{\downarrow}D$ is contained in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$, and its downsets computed in $P$ and in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ agree. Apply \eqref{eq:general-mc} and the subspace equality in~(1) to obtain meet-continuity of $\mathord{\downarrow}r$. Conversely, for $x\le\bigvee D$ in $P$, set $r=\bigvee D$ and apply meet-continuity in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$; its inclusion into $P$ is continuous, so \eqref{eq:general-mc} follows.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:local-tests}
Let $P$ be a countable dcpo. Suppose that each $\mathord{\downarrow}r$, $r\in P$, admits a countable family $\mathcal D_r$ of directed sets complete for its Scott openness. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-test-union}
 \mathcal D_P=\bigcup_{r\in P}\mathcal D_r
\end{equation}
is a countable family of directed subsets of $P$ complete for Scott openness.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The family is countable, and each of its members has the same directed supremum in its principal ideal and in $P$. Necessity of the tests is immediate. Suppose that an upper set $U\subseteq P$ passes every test in $\mathcal D_P$. For each $r$, the upper set $U\cap\mathord{\downarrow}r$ passes every test in $\mathcal D_r$, and hence is Scott open in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$. Given a directed $D\subseteq P$ with $r=\bigvee D\in U$, work inside $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ to obtain $D\cap U\ne\emptyset$. Thus $U$ is Scott open in $P$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:L-dcpo-products}
Every countable meet-continuous $L$-dcpo admits a countable family of directed subsets complete for Scott openness. Consequently, for every finite family $P_1,\ldots,P_n$ of countable meet-continuous $L$-dcpos,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-dcpo-products}
 \sigma\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_i\right)
 =\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma(P_i).
\end{equation}
No global least elements are assumed.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For a countable meet-continuous $L$-dcpo $P$, Lemma~\ref{lem:principal-localization} makes every principal ideal $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ a countable meet-continuous complete lattice. Proposition~\ref{prop:lattice-input}\textup{(1)} supplies a countable complete test family on each $\mathord{\downarrow}r$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:local-tests} combines them into one for $P$. Proposition~\ref{prop:lattice-input}\textup{(2)} now proves the binary product equality.

Finite products are again countable $L$-dcpos, since
\[
 \mathord{\downarrow}(r_1,\ldots,r_n)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathord{\downarrow}r_i.
\]
Each principal ideal on the right is a meet-continuous complete lattice, with operations and directed suprema computed coordinatewise. Lemma~\ref{lem:principal-localization} therefore gives meet-continuity of the product. Induction proves \eqref{eq:L-dcpo-products}; the empty product is a singleton.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:adjoin-bottom}
Let $P_\bot=P\cup\{\bot\}$ be obtained from a dcpo $P$ by adjoining a new least element. Then $P_\bot$ is a dcpo,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bottom-scott}
 \sigma(P_\bot)=\sigma(P)\cup\{P_\bot\},
\end{equation}
where subsets of $P$ are viewed as subsets of $P_\bot$. Moreover, $P$ is meet-continuous if and only if $P_\bot$ is meet-continuous, and $\Sigma P$ is sober if and only if $\Sigma P_\bot$ is sober.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
If a directed set in $P_\bot$ has a point other than $\bot$, deleting $\bot$ leaves a nonempty directed set in $P$ with the same supremum. This proves directed completeness and \eqref{eq:bottom-scott}. For meet-continuity at an old point $x\in P$, the same deletion and the Scott-open neighbourhoods of $x$ reduce \eqref{eq:general-mc} to the corresponding condition in $P$. At $\bot$, the condition is automatic, since $\bot\in\mathord{\downarrow}\bot\cap\mathord{\downarrow}D$ for every nonempty directed $D$.

Every nonempty Scott-closed subset of $P_\bot$ has the form $\{\bot\}\cup A$, where $A$ is Scott closed in $P$. If $A\ne\emptyset$, this set is irreducible exactly when $A$ is irreducible, by \eqref{eq:bottom-scott}. Its being a principal ideal is likewise equivalent to $A$ being principal in $P$. The remaining nonempty case is $\{\bot\}$, itself principal. This proves the assertion about sobriety.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:bottom-free-local-products}
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_n$ be countable meet-continuous domains. Suppose that, for each $i$ and $r\in P_i$, every nonempty subset of $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ has a supremum in $\mathord{\downarrow}r$. Then
\[
 \sigma\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_i\right)
 =\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma(P_i).
\]
Neither global least elements nor least elements of the principal ideals are required.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The stated local condition is equivalent to $(P_i)_\bot$ being an $L$-dcpo. Indeed, for an old point $r$, the principal ideal in $(P_i)_\bot$ is $\{\bot\}\cup\mathord{\downarrow}_{P_i}r$. The assumed nonempty suprema, together with the new bottom, provide suprema of all its subsets, so it is a complete lattice. Conversely, the supremum of a nonempty subset of $\mathord{\downarrow}_{P_i}r$ in this lattice cannot be the new bottom, and is therefore a supremum in $\mathord{\downarrow}_{P_i}r$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:adjoin-bottom}, the lifted dcpos are countable, meet-continuous $L$-dcpos. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:L-dcpo-products} to their finite product. Each $P_i$ is Scott open in $(P_i)_\bot$, and $\prod_iP_i$ is Scott open in $\prod_i(P_i)_\bot$. On a Scott-open subset $O$ of a dcpo, the intrinsic Scott topology is the subspace topology: a directed set with supremum in $O$ has a cofinal tail in $O$, which tests openness inside $O$. Restricting the product equality for the lifted dcpos therefore gives the desired equality.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:bounded-complete-sober}
Let $P$ be a countable meet-continuous domain such that every nonempty upper-bounded subset of $P$ has a supremum in $P$. Then $\Sigma P$ is sober. Finite products of such dcpos also satisfy the Scott product equality. No least element of $P$ is assumed.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The product assertion follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:bottom-free-local-products}. For sobriety, let $B=P_\bot$. Then $B$ is a countable meet-continuous domain in which every upper-bounded subset, including the empty subset, has a supremum. Adjoin a new greatest element $\top$ to obtain $K=B\cup\{\top\}$. Every subset of $B$ bounded in $B$ has its old supremum, and every subset unbounded in $B$ has supremum $\top$. Thus $K$ is a complete lattice.

For $x,y\in B$, their meet exists in $B$: it is the supremum of their nonempty, upper-bounded set of common lower bounds. Hence $B$ satisfies the meet-continuity identity \eqref{eq:meet-mc}. These binary meets do not change on adjoining $\top$. A directed subset of $K$ not containing $\top$ has its supremum in $B$, whereas a directed subset containing $\top$ has a greatest member. These two cases, and $\top\wedge x=x$, verify \eqref{eq:meet-mc} for $K$. Therefore $K$ is a countable meet-continuous complete lattice, so $\Sigma K$ is sober by Proposition~\ref{prop:lattice-input}\textup{(3)}.

The subset $B$ is Scott closed in $K$, and its intrinsic Scott topology is the subspace topology. Closed subspaces of sober spaces are sober: an irreducible closed subset of a closed subspace is also irreducible and closed in the ambient space, and its generic point belongs to that subset. Thus $\Sigma B$ is sober. Lemma~\ref{lem:adjoin-bottom} now gives sobriety of $\Sigma P$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:upper-bound-sections}
For a dcpo $P$ and $A\in\Gamma(P)$, the following two conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $D_{A,x}\in\Gamma(P)$ for every $x\in P$.
\item $E_A$ is Scott closed in $P\times P$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Suppose~(1), and let $D\subseteq E_A$ be directed with $\bigvee D=(p,q)$. Fix $(u,v)\in D$. The tail of $D$ above $(u,v)$ is cofinal in $D$, and all its first coordinates belong to $B_{A,v}$. Their supremum is $p$, so $p\in B_{A,v}$. By symmetry, $v\in B_{A,p}$. This holds for every second coordinate of $D$. Since $B_{A,p}$ is Scott closed, $q\in B_{A,p}$, proving $(p,q)\in E_A$. Lowerness of $E_A$ now proves~(2).

Conversely, for fixed $x$, the map $y\mapsto(x,y)$ preserves directed suprema and has inverse image $D_{A,x}$ of $E_A$, so~(2) implies~(1).
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:irreducible-upper-bound-sober}
Let $P$ be a dcpo such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-product-hypothesis}
 \sigma(P\times P)=\sigma(P)\times\sigma(P).
\end{equation}
Then $\Sigma P$ is sober if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:irreducible-upper-bound-condition}
 \mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)\in\Gamma(P)
 ~\bigl(A\in\operatorname{Irr}_c(\Sigma P),\ x\in P\bigr).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
If $\Sigma P$ is sober, then $A=\mathord{\downarrow}a$ for some $a\in P$, and
\[
 D_{\mathord{\downarrow}a,x}=
 \begin{cases}
  \mathord{\downarrow}a,&x\le a,\\
  \emptyset,&x\nleq a.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:irreducible-upper-bound-condition} holds, even without \eqref{eq:square-product-hypothesis}.

Conversely, fix $A\in\operatorname{Irr}_c(\Sigma P)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:upper-bound-sections}, $E_A$ is Scott closed in $P\times P$. By \eqref{eq:square-product-hypothesis}, it is closed in the ordinary product topology. It contains the diagonal $\Delta_A=\{(a,a):a\in A\}$. For $x,y\in A$, every open rectangle about $(x,y)$ meets $\Delta_A$, because any two open sets meeting $A$ have an intersection meeting $A$. Hence
\[
 A\times A\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\sigma(P)\times\sigma(P)}\Delta_A\subseteq E_A.
\]
Consequently, $A$ is directed. Scott closedness gives $A=\mathord{\downarrow}\bigvee A$, proving sobriety.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:sequential-upper-bound}
For a countable dcpo $P$ satisfying \eqref{eq:square-product-hypothesis}, $\Sigma P$ is sober if and only if the following holds: whenever $A\in\operatorname{Irr}_c(\Sigma P)$, $x\in A$, and $y_0\le y_1\le\cdots$ has supremum $y$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sequential-upper-bound-lifting}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\bigl(\forall n\in\mathbb N\ \exists a_n\in A:\ x\le a_n,
                  \ y_n\le a_n\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{20mm}\Longrightarrow~
 \exists a\in A:\ x\le a,\ y\le a.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
For $x\notin A$, the set $D_{A,x}$ is empty because $A$ is lower. For $x\in A$, condition \eqref{eq:sequential-upper-bound-lifting} says exactly that the lower set $D_{A,x}$ is closed under suprema of nondecreasing sequences. By countability and Lemma~\ref{lem:cofinal-chain}, this is equivalent to its being Scott closed. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:irreducible-upper-bound-sober}.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:conditional-sobriety}
Let $P$ be a countable meet-continuous domain such that every nonempty subset of every principal ideal has a supremum in that ideal. In particular, $P$ may be an $L$-dcpo. If any one of the following conditions holds, then $\Sigma P$ is sober:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $\Sigma P$ is a strong $d$-space;
\item $\Sigma P$ has property $R$;
\item $\Sigma P$ is well-filtered and coherent;
\item $(P,\lambda(P))$ is upper-semicompact.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Corollary~\ref{cor:bottom-free-local-products} gives the square-product equality~\eqref{eq:square-product-hypothesis}. Under~(1), all the sets $D_{A,x}$ are Scott closed by \cite[Theorem~61]{LawsonXuLower}, so Proposition~\ref{prop:irreducible-upper-bound-sober} applies. Conditions~(2)--(4) each imply~(1), by \cite[Lemma~50\textup{(3)}, Proposition~33\textup{(2)}, and Proposition~42]{LawsonXuLower}; equivalently one may apply \cite[Theorem~76 and Corollary~77]{LawsonXuLower} directly.

\label{cor:finite-upper-bound-generators}

\]
Then $\Sigma P$ is sober.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:semilattice-isolation}
Let $P$ be a countable meet-continuous domain with binary meets, and let $(p_n)$ be an acsending sequence with supremum $r$. For every nonempty $S\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}r$, there exist $t\in S$ and $m\in\mathbb N$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semilattice-isolate}
 \{y\in S:y\wedge p_m=t\wedge p_m\}=\{t\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
We use the meet-coordinate argument of \cite[Lemma~3.1]{XuJi}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:meet-mc},
\[
 x=\bigvee_n(x\wedge p_n)~(x\le r).
\]
Distinct elements of $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ therefore differ at some coordinate, and a difference at one coordinate persists at all later coordinates: equality after meeting with $p_m$ implies equality after meeting with any $p_k\le p_m$.

Suppose \eqref{eq:semilattice-isolate} fails and enumerate all of $\mathord{\downarrow}r$ as $\{e_n:n\in\mathbb N\}$, allowing repetitions. Choose $w\in S$, put $m_0=0$ and $b_0=w\wedge p_0$. Recursively maintain a nonempty fibre
\[
 F_n=\{y\in S:y\wedge p_{m_n}=b_n\}.
\]
It contains at least two points, so choose $y_n\in F_n\setminus\{e_n\}$ and $m_{n+1}>m_n$ with
\[
 b_{n+1}:=y_n\wedge p_{m_{n+1}}\ne e_n\wedge p_{m_{n+1}}.
\]
Then $F_{n+1}$ contains $y_n$, and
\[
 b_{n+1}\wedge p_{m_n}=b_n,~ b_n\le b_{n+1}.
\]
Consequently $b=\bigvee_n b_n$ exists and belongs to $\mathord{\downarrow}r$. For every fixed $n$, repeated compatibility gives $b_k\wedge p_{m_n}=b_n$ for $k\ge n$, while the earlier terms are at most $b_n$. Equation~\eqref{eq:meet-mc} implies
\[
 b\wedge p_{m_n}=\bigvee_k(b_k\wedge p_{m_n})=b_n.
\]
Thus $b$ differs from $e_n$ at coordinate $m_{n+1}$ for every $n$, contradicting the enumeration of $\mathord{\downarrow}r$. No binary join or nondirected supremum has been used.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:semilattice-puncture}
Let $P$ be a countable meet-continuous domain with binary meets. For each nonprincipal ideal $J$ of $P$, there exist $s<t\le\bigvee J$ such that $[s,t)$ is directed and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semilattice-puncture}
 \down[s,t)\subseteq J,~ \bigvee[s,t)=t\notin J.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
The coordinate argument is that of \cite[Lemma~3.2]{XuJi}; the directedness argument below replaces the use of binary joins. Set $r=\bigvee J$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cofinal-chain}, choose a strictly ascending cofinal sequence $(p_n)$ in $J$. Since $J$ is nonprincipal, $r\notin J$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:semilattice-isolation} to $S=\mathord{\downarrow}r\setminus J$, and let $t,m$ be as there. Put $s=t\wedge p_m$. Then $s\in J$ and $s<t$.

If $s\le x\le t$, then $x\wedge p_m=s$. Equation~\eqref{eq:semilattice-isolate} forces every such $x<t$ to belong to $J$. Thus $[s,t)\subseteq J$.

For $x,y\in[s,t)$ choose $j\in J$ with $x,y\le j$. Then
\[
 x,y\le t\wedge j\in J,~ s\le t\wedge j<t,
\]
since $t\notin J$. This supplies a common upper bound in $[s,t)$ and proves directedness without a binary join. Finally, for $n\ge m$, the elements $t\wedge p_n$ lie in $[s,t)$ and have supremum $t$ by \eqref{eq:meet-mc}. Lowerness of $J$ completes \eqref{eq:semilattice-puncture}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:minimal-component-decomposition}
Let $P$ be an $L$-dcpo, and let $\operatorname{Min}(P)$ denote its set of minimal elements. For $x\in P$, put $b(x)=\min(\mathord{\downarrow}x)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $b(x)$ is the unique minimal element of $P$ below $x$, and $x\le y$ implies $b(x)=b(y)$;
\item the sets $P_m=\mathord{\uparrow}m$, $m\in\operatorname{Min}(P)$, form a partition of $P$ into Scott-open and Scott-closed sub-dcpos, with no comparable points in distinct parts;
\item $P$ has a least element if and only if $\operatorname{Min}(P)$ is a singleton.
\end{enumerate}
No countability or meet-continuity assumption is needed.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
The element $b(x)$ exists because $\mathord{\downarrow}x$ is a complete lattice. If $z\le b(x)$, then $z\in\mathord{\downarrow}x$, so $b(x)\le z$ and $z=b(x)$. Thus $b(x)$ is minimal in $P$. If $m$ is any minimal element below $x$, then $b(x)\le m$ and hence $b(x)=m$.

If $x\le y$, then $b(y)\le x$, so $b(x)\le b(y)$. Also $b(x)\le y$, so $b(y)\le b(x)$. This proves~(1). Every point lies in exactly one $P_m$, and~(1) excludes comparabilities between different parts. Each $P_m$ is upper by definition. It is also lower: if $z\le x\in P_m$, then $b(z)=b(x)=m$ and hence $z\in P_m$.

For a nonempty directed $D\subseteq P$, write $s=\bigvee D$. Every $d\in D$ satisfies $d\le s$, so $b(d)=b(s)$. Consequently, if $s\in P_m$, then $D\subseteq P_m$; this proves Scott openness. If instead $D\subseteq P_m$, choose $d\in D$ and observe that $m\le d\le s$, whence $s\in P_m$. Together with lowerness this proves Scott closedness and the sub-dcpo assertion in~(2). Finally,~(1) shows that a unique minimal element lies below every point; the converse is immediate. This proves~(3).
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:finite-support-transfer}
Let $(P_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of nonempty dcpos such that
\[
 \sigma\!\left(\prod_{i\in F}P_i\right)
 =\prod_{i\in F}\sigma(P_i)
 ~(F\subseteq I\text{ finite}).
\]
If $J=\{i\in I:P_i\text{ has no least element}\}$ is finite, then
\[
 \sigma\!\left(\prod_{i\in I}P_i\right)
 =\prod_{i\in I}\sigma(P_i).
\]
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
The case $I=\emptyset$ is immediate. Put $P=\prod_{i\in I}P_i$ and, for $i\notin J$, let $\bot_i$ be the least element of $P_i$. For $x\in P$ and a finite set $F$ with $J\subseteq F\subseteq I$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-support-with-exceptions}
 x_i^F=
 \begin{cases}
  x_i,&i\in F,\\
  \bot_i,&i\notin F.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The finite subsets $F$ containing $J$ form a directed set under inclusion. The corresponding points $x^F$ are directed, with supremum $x$: every coordinate is retained once $F$ contains its index.

Write $P_F=\prod_{i\in F}P_i$, let $\pi_F:P\to P_F$ be the projection, and let $e_F:P_F\to P$ insert $\bot_i$ in every coordinate outside $F$. Both maps preserve directed suprema. For every $W\in\sigma(P)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-cylinder-with-exceptions}
 W=\bigcup_{\substack{F\subseteq I\text{ finite}\\J\subseteq F}}
       \pi_F^{-1}\bigl(e_F^{-1}(W)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $x\in W$ implies $x^F\in W$ for some such $F$ by Scott openness, and membership in a set on the right implies $x^F\in W$ and $x^F\le x$, hence $x\in W$. The set $e_F^{-1}(W)$ is Scott open in $P_F$ and is therefore open in its finite product topology by hypothesis. Each cylinder on the right of~\eqref{eq:finite-cylinder-with-exceptions} is consequently open in $\prod_i\sigma(P_i)$. This proves one inclusion; Scott continuity of the coordinate projections gives the reverse inclusion.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:arbitrary-L-products}
Let $(P_i)_{i\in I}$ be any family of nonempty countable meet-continuous domains, each of which is an $L$-dcpo. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arbitrary-L-product}
 \sigma\!\left(\prod_{i\in I}P_i\right)
 =\prod_{i\in I}\sigma(P_i)
\end{equation}
holds if and only if the set
\[
 I_0=\{i\in I:P_i\text{ has no least element}\}
\]
is finite. Neither $I$ nor the whole product is required to be countable. The necessity of the finiteness condition holds for arbitrary nonempty $L$-dcpos, without countability or meet-continuity.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Suppose first that $I_0$ is finite. Theorem~\ref{thm:L-dcpo-products} gives the required equality on every finite subproduct, so Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-support-transfer} proves~\eqref{eq:arbitrary-L-product}.

For the converse, only the $L$-dcpo hypothesis is used. Suppose $I_0$ is infinite. For each $i\in I$, choose $m_i\in\operatorname{Min}(P_i)$ and put $m=(m_i)_{i\in I}$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-component-product}
 W=\mathord{\uparrow}m=\prod_{i\in I}\mathord{\uparrow}m_i.
\end{equation}
This is an upper subset of $P=\prod_iP_i$. If a nonempty directed set $D\subseteq P$ has supremum $s\in W$, then for every $d\in D$ and $i\in I$,
\[
 b_i(d_i)=b_i(s_i)=m_i,
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-component-decomposition}, since $d_i\le s_i$ and $m_i\le s_i$. Hence $m_i\le d_i$ for every $i$, so in fact $D\subseteq W$. Therefore $W\in\sigma(P)$.

However, $W$ is not open in the ordinary product topology. A basic product neighbourhood $N$ of $m$ restricts only finitely many coordinates, say those in $F$. Choose $j\in I_0\setminus F$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-component-decomposition}\textup{(3)}, the factor $P_j$ has a minimal element $n_j\ne m_j$. Replace just the $j$-th coordinate of $m$ by $n_j$, obtaining a point $z\in N$. Distinct minimal elements are incomparable, so $z\notin W$. Thus no basic product neighbourhood of $m$ is contained in $W$, which contradicts~\eqref{eq:arbitrary-L-product}.
<!-- end proof-18 -->
<!-- corollary-5: body_md -->
\label{cor:arbitrary-powers}
For a nonempty countable meet-continuous $L$-dcpo $P$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $P$ has a least element;
\item $\sigma(P^{\mathbb N})=\prod_{n\in\mathbb N}\sigma(P)$;
\item $\sigma(P^I)=\prod_{i\in I}\sigma(P)$ for every index set $I$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-5 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Theorem~\ref{thm:arbitrary-L-products} gives~(1)$\Rightarrow$(3), and~(3)$\Rightarrow$(2) is immediate. If $P$ has no least element, every factor in its countable power lacks one, so the same theorem excludes~(2).
<!-- end proof-19 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{ex:antichain-infinite-product}
Let $A=\{a,b\}$ carry the equality order. Every directed subset of $A$ is a singleton, so $A$ is meet-continuous, and every principal ideal is a singleton complete lattice. Thus $A$ is a finite meet-continuous $L$-dcpo. Its countable order power $A^{\mathbb N}$ is again an antichain, so its Scott topology is discrete. The ordinary product of the discrete topologies on the factors is not discrete, since a basic neighbourhood restricts only finitely many coordinates. Consequently,
\[
 \prod_{n\in\mathbb N}\sigma(A)\subsetneq\sigma(A^{\mathbb N}).
\]
This is the elementary obstruction observed in \cite[Section~5]{XuJi}. Theorem~\ref{thm:arbitrary-L-products} shows that, within the present class of factors, infinitely many factors without least elements account for every failure of the arbitrary-product equality.
