<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
For any $h \in \prod_{\nu \nmid \infty}\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathcal{O}_\nu)$ and $k \in \mathrm{GL}(n,F)$, $L(g)=L(kgh)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The first claim is clear from the definition. For the second, observe that $x \in I(kg) \Leftrightarrow xk \in I(g)$. Thus
\begin{align*}
L(kg) &= \rho(I(g)k^{-1}) \cdot (kg)_\infty    \\
&= \rho(I(g))k^{-1}_\infty(kg)_\infty \\
&= \rho(I(g)) g_\infty = L(g).
\end{align*}
The second equality above holds because $\rho(xk^{-1}) = \rho(x)k^{-1}_\infty$ for any $x \in F^n$.
\qed
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
In fact, it holds that $L(g) = L(g')$ if and only if $g,g'$ represent the same coset in $$\mathrm{GL}(n,F) \backslash \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{A}_F) / \prod_{\nu \nmid \infty} \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathcal{O}_\nu).$$
See e.g., \cite[Sec. 2.3.2]{dBPTW26} (where they factor on the right by a little more to normalize the determinant and rotational factor).

<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
This follows from e.g. \cite[Lemma 2.3]{Kim23}.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        
\qed

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:module}
Let $M$ be a module lattice given by a pseudo basis $M=\mathfrak b_1 \mathbf{v}_1 \oplus \ldots \oplus \mathfrak b_n \mathbf{v}_n$ 
where the $\mathbf{v}_i \in (F\otimes\mathbb{R})^n$'s are linearly independent, 
and the $\mathfrak b_i$'s are fractional ideals.
Let $g \in \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{A}_F)$ such that $M=L(g)$.
Then:
$$ \eta(g) = \min_{\substack{\text{nonzero } \mathbf{v} \in (F\otimes\mathbb{R})^n \\  \text{fractional ideal } {\mathfrak b}, {\mathfrak b} \mathbf{v} \subseteq M }} N(\mathfrak b) H_\infty( \mathbf{v}),$$
which is $\le \min_{1 \le i \le n} N(\mathfrak b_i) H_\infty( \mathbf{v}_i).$
Furthermore:
$$ d^{-d/2} |\Delta_F|^{-1/2} \lambda_1(M)^d \le \eta(g) \le d^{-d/2} \lambda_1(M)^d \le n^{d/2} (\det M)^{1/n}$$
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\cite[Lemma 2.2]{LPSW19} proved inequalities relating $\lambda_1(M)$
and a quantity called $\lambda_1^{\mathcal{N}}(M)$, which is slightly different from $\eta(g)$,
but the same arguments show our inequalities. \qed
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The notion of height allows some flexibility. For example, heights at infinite places can be replaced by any $L^p$-norm. They can also be twisted by a linear transformation, in the sense of \cite{Thu93} It may be interesting to ask whether some heights are more suitable than others for algorithmic purposes.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemma:diagonal}
Let $g\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{A}_F)$ have an Iwasawa decomposition $g=uak$, and denote $a = \mathrm{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$. Then
\begin{align*}
\eta(g)                                      
\geq \min_{1 \leq i \leq n} H(a_i).
\end{align*}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose a vector $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_n) \in F^n$, and let $j$ be the largest index such that $w_j \neq 0$. First, notice that for each place $\nu$, right multiplication by $K_\nu$ preserves the local height at $\nu$. Therefore
               
$H(wuak)=H(wua).$
             
Since the $j$-th entry of $wua$ is $w_ja_j$, we have
\begin{align*}
H(wua) \geq H(w_ja_j) = H(a_j),
\end{align*}
where the last equality is by the product formula.
\qed

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:LLL-reduced-2}
We say that $g \in \mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{A}_F)$ is \emph{(LLL-)reduced} with respect to the above parameters if it has an Iwasawa decomposition $g= uak$ for which the following points hold
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item (size reduced) 
                                                                                                                                                                             
There exists $\tau_\sigma > 0$ for each of the $d$ embeddings $\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}$ such that
\[\tau_\sigma \leq C^\frac{1}{d}, \text{ } \tau_\sigma|m_\sigma| \leq \mu \text{ and }
H_\textbf{f}(m,1) \leq\prod_{\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}\tau_\sigma.\]


\item (Lov\'asz condition) $\delta \leq H((m,\alpha))$, or equivalently $\delta H((a_1,0)) \le H((m a_1,a_2))$.
                                                                           

\item (unit reduced) The entries of                                                                                     
$$\mathrm{Log}(\alpha_\infty) - \frac{1}{d}\log H_\infty(\alpha_\infty)\cdot(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_\mathrm{real},\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_\mathrm{complex})$$ are bounded by $A$.

\item (class reduced) $\alpha$ is integral (that is, $\alpha_\nu\in\mathcal{O}_\nu$ for all finite $\nu$) and $B^{-1} \leq H_\textbf{f}(\alpha)$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:swap_bound}
Algorithm \ref{alg:lll_c} terminates. In fact, if for some $\kappa>0$, 
$\eta(g) \geq \kappa$, then it takes at most
                                                                          
$$\frac{\log {H(v_1)} - \log{\kappa}}{-\log \delta}$$
swaps to terminate. (Recall that we are denoting by $v_1$ the first row vector of $g$.)
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
From the descriptions provided above, it is clear that only Line 6, the row swapping, of Algorithm \ref{alg:lll_c} has the possibility of changing $E(g)$. 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                
                                                                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  

When a swap occurs, $H(v_1)=H((a_1,0))$ is replaced by $H(v_2)=H((m a_1,a_2))$. Since the Lov\'asz condition fails, we have $\delta H(v_1) >H(v_2)$, and thus $H(v_1)$ decreases at least by a factor of $\delta$. Since $H(v_1)$ cannot be lower than $\kappa$, this completes the proof.
\qed

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lemma:lowest_ht}
Suppose $g \in \mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{A}_F)$ corresponds to the pseudobasis $\mathfrak{b}_1\mathbf{v}_1+\mathfrak{b}_2\mathbf{v}_2$
where $\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2\in\rho(\mathcal{O}_F^2)$, i.e.,
\begin{align*}
g_\infty = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{v}_1 \\ \mathbf{v}_2 \end{pmatrix}, g_\nu=\begin{pmatrix} \pi_\nu^{-\mathrm{ord}_\nu\mathfrak{b}_1} & \\ & \pi_\nu^{-\mathrm{ord}_\nu\mathfrak{b}_2}\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}
for each $\nu\nmid\infty$. Then
               
$\eta(g) \geq N(\mathfrak{b}_1+\mathfrak{b}_2).$
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let us write $\beta_i$ for the finite adele such that $(\beta_i)_\nu = \pi_\nu^{\mathrm{ord}_\nu\mathfrak{b}_i}$, so that
\begin{align*}
g_\textbf{f} = \begin{pmatrix} \beta_1^{-1} & \\ & \beta_2^{-1} \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align*}
Also let $\beta \in \mathbb{A}_\textbf{f}$ be such that $\beta_\nu = \pi_\nu^{\min(\mathrm{ord}_\nu\mathfrak{b}_1,\mathrm{ord}_\nu\mathfrak{b}_2)}$. 

Take any $x_1,x_2 \in F$, not both zero. We have
\begin{align*}
H((x_1,x_2)g) = H_\textbf{f}((x_1\beta_1^{-1},x_2\beta_2^{-1}))H_\infty(x_1\mathbf{v}_1+x_2\mathbf{v}_2).
\end{align*}
First notice
\begin{align*}
H_\textbf{f}((x_1\beta_1^{-1},x_2\beta_2^{-1}))\geq H_\textbf{f}(x_1\beta^{-1},x_2\beta^{-1}) \geq H_\textbf{f}((x_1,x_2))H_\textbf{f}(\beta^{-1}).
\end{align*}
                             
{}

As for $H_\infty(x_1\mathbf{v}_1+x_2\mathbf{v}_2)$, by considering a single nonzero coordinate of $x_1\mathbf{v}_1+x_2\mathbf{v}_2$, it is bounded from below by $H_\infty(y)$, where $y$ is some element of the ideal generated by $x_1,x_2$. By the product formula,
\begin{align*}
1 = H_\textbf{f}(y)H_\infty(y) \leq H_\textbf{f}((x_1,x_2))H_\infty(y).
\end{align*}
Combining the last two bounds, we have $H((x_1,x_2)g) \geq H_\textbf{f}(\beta^{-1})$, which completes the proof.
\qed

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:rhf_bound}
Let $A, B, C$ denote the parameters from Algorithm~\ref{alg:lll_c} and define
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Q}
  Q = \bigl(\delta^{2/d} - \mu^2\bigr)^{-d/4} (CB)^{1/2} e^{Ad/2}.
\end{equation}
If $g$ is reduced, then
\begin{equation} \label{ineq:ratio}
  \frac{H(a_1)}{H(a_2)} \le Q^2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{ineq:Hermite}
  H(v_1) \le Q\, H(\det g)^{1/2},
\end{equation}

bit-sizes remain bounded by a polynomial in the input bit-size, \(n\), $d$, and
\(\log \Delta_K\) throughout the algorithm.

The coefficient ideals \(I_i\) affect the vectors \(b_j\) only indirectly, through
the reduction coefficients. In the corrected size reduction step, the coefficient
\(x_{ij}\) is chosen in the fractional ideal \(I_iI_j^{-1}\). Hence the effect of the
update
\[
b_j \leftarrow b_j-x_{ij}b_i
\]
is controlled by the geometry of the lattice \(I_iI_j^{-1}\). In particular, once the
pseudo-basis is scaled, the norms \(N(I_i)\) are uniformly bounded above and below,
which yields a uniform control on \(N(I_iI_j^{-1})\), and therefore on the growth of
the vectors \(b_j\).

Using our notations, \cite{LPSW19} compare $H(a_1)$ with $H(a_2)$, while we compare $H((a_1,0))$ with $H((ma_1,a_2))$. By analogy with the rational case, our condition is a Lov\'asz condition, while their condition corresponds to a more relaxed Siegel condition.
    \item \textbf{Swap:} A pleasant feature obtained by expressing our algorithm using the adelic formalism is the following: the swapping procedure is simply a swap, corresponding to left-multiplication by $\begin{pmatrix}
        0 & 1\\1 &0
    \end{pmatrix}\in \mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{A}_F)$. In pseudo-basis formalism however, swaps are not that simple, as they require extra care to preserve the module structure. It is interesting to note that the procedure used for ``swapping'' in \cite{LPSW19} (see steps 4 and 5 of Algorithm 4.3) is essentially the same as our own \texttt{adelic-swap}.
