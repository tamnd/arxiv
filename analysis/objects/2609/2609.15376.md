Let $(K,v)$ and $(L,w)$ be henselian valued fields of mixed characteristic 
        $(0,p)$
        such that $\mathcal{O}_v/(p)$ and $\mathcal{O}_w/(p)$ are semiperfect. Then, we have
    $$\underbrace{(K,v) \equiv (L,w)}_{\text{in }\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{val}} \Longleftrightarrow \underbrace{\mathcal{O}_v/(p) \equiv \mathcal{O}_w/(p)}_{\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}}\textrm{ and } \underbrace{(vK,v(p)) 
    \equiv (wL,w(p))}_{\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oag}}\cup\{c\}},$$
        and 
        $$\underbrace{(K,v) \equiv_\exists (L,w)}_{\text{in }\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{val}} \Longleftrightarrow \underbrace{\mathcal{O}_v/(p) \equiv_\exists \mathcal{O}_w/(p)}_{\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}}\textrm{ and } \underbrace{(vK,v(p)) \equiv_\exists (wL,w(p))}_{\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oag}}\cup\{c\}},$$
        where $c$ is a constant symbol interpreted as the value of $p$.
        Moreover, when $vK$ and $wL$ are regular, the value group data on the right hand side can be omitted in both equivalences.

\label{ex:structure}
    Let $(K,v)$ be the completion of $ {\big(\mathbb{Q}_p(\sqrt[p-1]{p},\sqrt[p^\infty]{p}),v_p\big)}$ 
    and 
    $(L,w)$ be the completion of ${\big(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty}),v_p\big)}$. Then, $(K,v)$ and $(L,w)$ are well-known 
    examples of perfectoid fields
    whose tilts are both isomorphic to
    the completed perfect hull $(F,u)$ of $(\mathbb{F}_p(\!(t)\!),v_t)$.
    In fact, we have
    \begin{align*}\mathcal{O}_v/(p) & \overset{{p} \mapsto t}{\cong} \mathcal{O}_u/(t^{p-1}) \cong \mathbb{F}_p[t^{1/p^\infty}]/(t^{p-1}) \overset{t+1 \mapsto \mu_p}{\cong} 
    \mathcal{O}_w/(p)
    \end{align*}
    and $$(vK,v(p)) \cong (\mathbb{Z}[1/p],p-1)\cong (wL,w(p)).$$
    In particular,
    $$\mathcal{O}_v/(p) \equiv \mathcal{O}_w/(p) \textrm{ and }(vK,v(p))\equiv (wL,w(p))$$
    holds.
    However, as $\sqrt[p]{p} \in K$ and $\sqrt[p]{p} \notin L$ we obtain 
    $$\underbrace{(K,v) \not\equiv (L,w).}_{\textrm{ in }\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}} \textrm{ (or even $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}$)}}$$
    Thus, it is necessary to add further structure on $\mathcal{O}_v/(p)$ and/or $(vK,v(p))$
    to have any hope of an AKE-type principle. Moreover, as in this example 
    $vK$ and $wL$ are both
    regular, the $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}$-theory of $\mathcal{O}_v/(p)$ (resp.~$\mathcal{O}_w/(p)$) completely
    determines the $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oag}}\cup\{c\}$-theory of $(vK,v(p))$ (respectively that of $(wL,w(p))$), see the proof of \cite[Theorem 5.1.2]{jahnke-kartas2023beyond}.

We show, for each roughly deeply ramified valued field \( (K,v) \) of mixed characteristic \((0,p)\), that \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p^n)\) (with \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}\)) is interpretable in \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p)\) (with \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}\)), where the defining formulas do not depend on the choice of \( (K,v) \).

        We first claim that for any such field \( (K,v) \), 
        \[
        \begin{array}{rrcl}
        \omega_{n}\colon &\left(\mathcal{O}_v/(p)\right)^n & \longrightarrow & \mathcal{O}_v/(p^n)\\
        &(x_0+p\mathcal{O}_v,\ldots,x_{n-1}+p\mathcal{O}_v) & \longmapsto & W_{n-1}(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})+p^n\mathcal{O}_v.
        \end{array}
        \]
        is a well-defined surjective map.
        The fact that $\omega_{n}$ is well-defined follows from \Cref{lem:warm.up}.
        To see that $\omega_{n}$ is surjective, take $y\in \mathcal{O}_v$.
        By induction, we construct $x_0, \ldots, x_{n-1}\in \mathcal{O}_v$ such that 
        \[
        \omega_{n} (x_0 + p\mathcal{O}_v, \dotsc, x_{n-1}+ p\mathcal{O}_v) = y+p^n\mathcal{O}_v, \]
        more precisely, such that for $i= 0 \ldots, n$ we have
        \[
        y - x_0^{p^{n-1}} - px_1^{p^{n-2}} - \ldots - p^{i-1}x_{i-1}^{p^{n-i}}\in p^{i}\mathcal{O}_v.
        \]
 
        (For $i=0$ this statement is vacuous.)
        Suppose that $x_0, \ldots, x_{i-1}$ have been constructed, for some $i \in \{0, \ldots, n-1\}$.
        Write 
        \[
        y - x_0^{p^{n-1}} - px_1^{p^{n-2}} - \ldots - p^{i-1}x_{i-1}^{p^{n-i}} = p^{i}z
        \]
        for some $z\in \mathcal{O}_v$.
        Since $\mathcal{O}_v/(p)$ is semiperfect, there exists $x_{i}\in \mathcal{O}_v$ such that $x_{i}^{p^{n-1-i}} \equiv z\bmod p\mathcal{O}_v$, so
        \[
        y - x_0^{p^{n-1}} - px_1^{p^{n-2}} - \ldots - p^{i-1}x_{i-1}^{p^{n-i}}- p^{i}x_{i}^{p^{n-1-i}}\in p^{i+1}\mathcal{O}_v,
        \]
        as desired.
        
        We need to argue that for every unnested atomic formula in \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}\), there is a quantifier-free defining formula in \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}\).
        In fact, it is enough to check this for \enquote{\(X=Y\)}, \enquote{\(X+Y=Z\)} and \enquote{\(X\cdot Y=Z\)}: one has \(W_n(0,\ldots,0)=0\), \(W_n(1,0,\ldots,0)=1\) and \(W_n(-1,0,\ldots,0)=-1\) if \(p\) is odd and \(W_n(1,\ldots,1)=-1\) if \(p=2\), see \cite[Exercise on page 135]{vdd}. This means that \(0\) and \(1\) are uniformly \( \varnothing \)-definable without quantifiers, and similarly for subtraction since it is multiplying by \(-1\).

        By definition of the \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}\)-structure on \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p)\), the defining formula for \enquote{\(X=Y\)} is just \(\ker_n(\underline{X};\underline{Y})\).

        By \cite[Lemma~6.5]{vdd}, there are \(\mathbb{Z}\)-polynomials \[S_0,S_1,\ldots,P_0,P_1,\ldots\in \mathbb{Z}[X_0,X_1,\ldots Y_0,Y_1,\ldots]\] (where for each \(i\) we have \(S_i,P_i\in \mathbb{Z}[X_0,\ldots,X_i,Y_0,\ldots,Y_i]\)) such that 
        the defining formula for \enquote{\(X+Y=Z\)} is given by 
        \[
        \ker_n(S_0(\underline{X},\underline{Y}),\ldots,S_{n-1}(\underline{X},\underline{Y});\underline{Z})
        \] 
        and the defining formula for \enquote{\(X\cdot Y=Z\)} is given by
        \[
        \ker_n(P_0(\underline{X},\underline{Y}),\ldots,P_{n-1}(\underline{X},\underline{Y});\underline{Z}).
        \]
        Note that the defining formulas just depend on \(n\) and \(p\) and not on the choice of \( (K,v) \).
        Thus \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p^n)\) is uniformly interpretable in the 
        $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}$-structure \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p)\).

        To see that $\operatorname{res}_{n, m}\colon \mathcal{O}_v/(p^n)\to \mathcal{O}_v/(p^m)$ is $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}$-interpretable for $n\geq m$, we simply note that
        \[
        \operatorname{res}_{n,m}(\omega_n(x_0, \ldots, x_{n-1})) = \omega_m(x_0^{p^{n-m}}, \ldots, x_{m-1}^{p^{n-m}})
        \]
        and the map $(\mathcal{O}_v/(p))^n\to (\mathcal{O}_v/(p))^m,\ (x_0, \ldots, x_{n-1})\mapsto (x_0^{p^{n-m}}, \ldots, x_{m-1}^{p^{n-m}})$ is clearly quantifier-free $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}$-definable.

Let 
        \[
        \varphi\colon \left(\mathcal{O}_v/(p),\ldots\right) \hookrightarrow \left(\mathcal{O}_w/(p),\ldots\right)
        \]
        be an embedding of \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}\)-structures.
        For each \(n\in\mathbb{N}\), $\varphi$ induces a unique map \(\varphi_n\) as follows:
        \[
        \begin{array}{ccc}
            \varphi_n\colon \mathcal{O}_v/(p^n) & \hookrightarrow & \mathcal{O}_w/(p^n) \\
            \omega_{n}(\underline{x}) & \mapsto & \omega_{n}(\varphi(\underline{x}))
        \end{array}
        \]
        for any \(\underline{x}\in (\mathcal{O}_v/(p))^n\).
        As \(\varphi\) is an \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}\)-embedding, we have
        \[
        \ker_n(\varphi(\underline{x});\varphi(\underline{y})) \Leftrightarrow \ker_n(\underline{x};\underline{y}).
        \]
        Since \(\ker_n\) is interpreted as the kernel of \(\omega_{n}\), this shows that \(\varphi_n\) is well-defined and injective.
        
        Moreover, \(\varphi_n\) is a ring homomorphism.
        Indeed, since we have the uniform quantifier-free interpretation from \cref{lem:interpretability} (see also \cref{rem:kostas}), 
        there is a quantifier-free \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}\)-formula \(\chi_{+,n}\) such that
        \[
        \omega_{n}(\underline{x})+\omega_{n}(\underline{y})=\omega_{n}(\underline{z}) \text{ in \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p^n)\)} \iff \mathcal{O}_v/(p) \models \chi_{+,n}(\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{z})
        \]
        for all \(\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{z}\in (\mathcal{O}_v/(p))^n\), and 
        \[
        \omega_{n}(\underline{x})+\omega_{n}(\underline{y})=\omega_{n}(\underline{z}) \text{ in \(\mathcal{O}_w/(p^n)\)} \iff \mathcal{O}_w/(p) \models \chi_{+,n}(\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{z})
        \]
        for all \(\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{z}\in (\mathcal{O}_w/(p))^n\).
        Thus, if \(\underline{x},\underline{y}\in(\mathcal{O}_v/(p))^n\), and \(\omega_{n}(\underline{x})+\omega_{n}(\underline{y})=\omega_{n}(\underline{z}) \text{ in \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p^n)\)}\) for some \(\underline{z}\in (\mathcal{O}_v/(p))^n\), then by the above we have 
        \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p)\models \chi_{+,n}(\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{z})\), and since \(\varphi\) is an \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}\)-embedding and \(\chi_{+,n}\) is quantifier-free, we get \(\mathcal{O}_w/(p)\models \chi_{+,n}(\varphi(\underline{x}),\varphi(\underline{y}),\varphi(\underline{z}))\).
        Now again by the above and applying the definition of \(\varphi_n\), we get
        \begin{align*}
        \varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{x}))+\varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{y}))&=\omega_{n}(\varphi(\underline{x}))+\omega_{n}(\varphi(\underline{y}))=\omega_{n}(\varphi(\underline{z}))\\&=\varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{z}))=\varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{x})+\omega_{n}(\underline{y}))
        \end{align*}
        Similarly, using the defining formulas for \enquote{\(X\cdot Y=Z\)}, \enquote{\(X=1\)}, and \enquote{\(\operatorname{res}_{n,m}(X)=Y\)}, one proves in the same way that 
        \begin{align*}
        	\varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{x})) \cdot \varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{y})) &= \varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{x}) \cdot \omega_{n}(\underline{y})),\\
        	\varphi_n(1)&=1, \text{ and}\\
            \varphi_m(\operatorname{res}_{n,m}(\omega_{n}(\underline{x})))&=\operatorname{res}_{n,m}(\varphi_n(\omega_{n}(\underline{x}))).
        \end{align*}
        Thus, the \(\varphi_n\) are ring embeddings compatible with the inverse system maps, and by the universal property of inverse systems we obtain an embedding
        \[\Phi \colon \mathcal{O}_v\cong\varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}_v/(p^n)\hookrightarrow \varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}_w/(p^n)\cong\mathcal{O}_w.\]
        Note that if two $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Witt}}$-embeddings $\varphi$ and $\psi$ induce the same
        maps $\varphi_n$ respectively $\psi_n$ for all $n$, then $\Phi$ 
        and $\Psi$ (the embedding of valuation rings induced by $\psi$) coincide by the universal property of inverse systems.
        
        Now $\Phi$ induces a unique embedding of fraction fields 
        $$K=\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_v) \hookrightarrow L=\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_w).$$ 
        Moreover, $\mathcal{O}_\nu:=\Phi(\mathcal{O}_v) \subseteq \Phi(K)$ is a refinement of 
        $\mathcal{O}_\omega = \mathcal{O}_w \cap \Phi(K)$. 
        Note that, for any refinement $\mathcal{O}_\nu \subseteq \mathcal{O}_\omega$, the induced map $\mathcal{O}_\nu/(p) \to \mathcal{O}_\omega/(p)$
        has kernel $$(p\mathcal{O}_\omega \cap \mathcal{O}_\nu)/p\mathcal{O}_\nu = p\mathcal{O}_\omega/p\mathcal{O}_\nu \cong \mathcal{O}_\omega/\mathcal{O}_\nu$$ where the first equality follows from 
        $p\mathcal{O}_\omega \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_\omega \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_\nu \subseteq  \mathcal{O}_\nu$. As the injectivity of the composition
        $\mathcal{O}_\nu/(p) \rightarrow \mathcal{O}_\omega/(p) \rightarrow \mathcal{O}_w/(p)$ implies 
        that of $\mathcal{O}_\nu/(p) \rightarrow \mathcal{O}_\omega/(p)$, we conclude
        $\mathcal{O}_\nu=\mathcal{O}_\omega$.
        
        Hence, $\Phi$ is in fact
        an embedding $\Phi\colon (K,v) \hookrightarrow (L,w)$ of valued fields,
        and is the unique such that induces $\varphi$.

They lift to embeddings \(\Phi\) and \(\Phi'\) between \( (K,v) \) and \( (L,w) \) whose composition (in both ways) is a lift of the identity map of \(\mathcal{O}_v/(p)\) respectively \(\mathcal{O}_w/(p)\). 
        By uniqueness, there is only one lift of the identity map, namely the identity. 
        Thus, \(\Phi'=\Phi^{-1}\) and \(\Phi\) is an isomorphism.

Consider
    $K = \mathbb{C}_p$.
    We take a compatible system $(x_q)_{q\in \mathbb{Q}}$ of roots of $p$, so that $x_q^{q^{-1}} = p$.
    On $R_1 = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}/(p)$, we can then take $s_1\colon \mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\to R_1$ to be the map $q\mapsto \operatorname{res}_1(x_q)$ for $q < 1$ and $q\mapsto 0$ for $q \geq 1$.
    Let $X$ be the set $s_1([0,1)\cap \mathbb{Q})$, which is definable in $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{QE}$ as 
    \[
    X = \{\xi\in \mathcal{O}_v/(p)\mid \xi\neq 0 \text{ and } S_1(v(\xi), \xi) \text{ holds}\}.
    \]
    Then the inverse image
    \[
    \operatorname{res}_1^{-1}(X) = \bigsqcup_{q\in [0,1)\cap \mathbb{Q}} x_q + p\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}
    \]
    is not a finite union of Swiss cheeses, and hence cannot be $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(\mathbb{C}_p)$-definable in $\mathbb{C}_p$, by Holly's theorem~\cite[Corollary 3.8]{holly}.
    Thus also $X$ itself is not $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}$-definable, even with parameters.
