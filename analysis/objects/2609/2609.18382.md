<!-- head-1: body_md -->
The goal of this work is to develop
a stable and exactly symmetric discretization of the MCS formulation in two space dimensions,
where the Clough--Tocher macroelement split is exploited
to design a new family of finite element spaces for the stress variable~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$.
In this construction,
the symmetry of the stress tensor is exactly imposed as a built-in property,
rather than being imposed weakly.
More precisely,
given an integer~$k \geq 1$,
the discrete stress space consists of \textbf{exactly symmetric} and trace-free
tensor-valued functions,
whose components are
piecewise polynomial of order~$k-1$
on the Clough--Tocher refinement.
In particular,
no higher-order interior bubble enrichment is required,
unlike in the original MCS construction.
For the velocity and pressure variables,
we will consider the same spaces as those in~\cite{Gopalakrishnan-Lederer-Schoberl:2020},
defined on the original (unrefined) mesh:
as discrete velocity space,
vector-valued piecewise polynomial functions of order~$k$
with square-integrable divergence;
as discrete pressure space,
discontinuous piecewise polynomials of order~$k-1$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The notation $\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}$ in the definition of~$H(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\operatorname{\mathbf{div}},\Omega)$
is motivated by the three-dimensional counterpart of the space.
Indeed,
given a vector-valued function~$\mathbf{v} = (v_1, v_2, v_3)$,
we introduce the differential operators
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{div} \mathbf{v} :=\partial_1 v_1 + \partial_2 v_2 + \partial_3 v_3, \qquad
\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}} \mathbf{v} := (\partial_2 v_3 - \partial_3 v_2, \partial_3 v_1 - \partial_1 v_3, \partial_1 v_2 - \partial_2 v_1)^T,
\end{equation*}
and, for a tensor-valued function~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}$,
we define~$\operatorname{\mathbf{div}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ row-wise.
The three-dimensional counterpart of the space in~\eqref{Hcurldiv-2d} is then
\begin{equation*}
H(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\operatorname{\mathbf{div}},\Omega) := \{ \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}} \in L^2(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}) \, | \, \operatorname{\mathbf{curl}} \operatorname{\mathbf{div}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}} \in  H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^3)\}.
\end{equation*}
This provides the motivation for the notation $H(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\operatorname{\mathbf{div}},\Omega)$,
and although the present work is restricted to the two-dimensional setting,
we retain this notation for consistency.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:dev-sym-basis-Ki}
Let~${K_i}$ be an element of~$\mathcal{C}_h$.
Then,
the set $\{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i),\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)\}$
is a basis for~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0({K_i})$.
In particular,
for any given~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$,
there exist polynomials~$r_{k-1}^i$ and~$s_{k-1}^i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$
such that~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ admits the following representation:
\begin{equation*}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h = r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since the set $\{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i),\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)\}$
has two elements,
it suffices to show that they are linearly independent,
which directly follows from
the orthogonality property~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym}.
Using that
$\{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i),\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)\}$
is a basis for~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0({K_i})$,
writing
$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i}) = \mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})\otimes\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0({K_i})$
yields the desired decomposition.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
A natural alternative choice for the degrees of freedom
for the local discrete stress space~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$
would be  to consider only two families of bulk moments
of degree up to order~$k-1$,
associated with the normal-normal
and normal-tangential components, respectively, i.e.,
\begin{subequations}
\begin{equation}
\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x\qquad\qquad
    \forallq_{i} \in \mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i}),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation} \label{dofs:natural-bulk}
\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x\qquad\qquad
    \forallq_{i} \in \mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i}).
\end{equation}
\end{subequations}
The unisolvence of these functionals follows immediately
from the same arguments used in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lemma:unisolvence},
since $\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)$ and $\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)$ form a basis for~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0({K_i})$.
However,
we decompose the second family of bulk functionals~\eqref{dofs:natural-bulk}
into the two groups~\eqref{dofs:bulk-k} and~\eqref{dofs:bulk-kmo-lambda}
because the second term on the right-hand side in the definition of~$b_2$ in~\eqref{btwo}
requires control of the normal-tangential component on the edges.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:decomposition-polynomial-spaces}

The space of polynomials~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$ admits the following decomposition
\begin{equation} \label{decomposition-polynomial-spaces}
\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i}) = {\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}}))}\oplus\lambda_i\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We first show that the sum on the right-hand side of~\eqref{decomposition-polynomial-spaces} is direct:
let~$p_{k-1}$ belong to
$\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}}))\cap\lambda_i\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$.
Then,
there exist~$q^F_{k-1}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}})$
and~$q_{k-2}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$ such that
$p_{k-1} = \mathcal{L}_{k-1}(q^F_{k-1}) = \lambda_iq_{k-2}$.
Restricting the second identity to~${F_{{K_i}}}$
and using the definition of~$\mathcal{L}_{k-1}$
together with the fact that~$\lambda_i$ vanishes on~${F_{{K_i}}}$,
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
q_{k-1}|_{F_{{K_i}}}  = \mathcal{L}_{k-1}(q^F_{k-1}){|_{F_{{K_i}}}} = (\lambda_iq_{k-2})|_{F_{{K_i}}} = 0.
\end{equation*}
Hence~$p_{k-1} = \mathcal{L}_{k-1}(0) = 0$,
the sum is direct,
and we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}}))\oplus\lambda_i\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i}))
= \dim(\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}})))+\dim(\lambda_i\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})).
\end{equation*}
It remains to show that the sum space has the same dimension as~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$. By construction,
the extension operator~$\mathcal{L}_{k-1}$ of~\eqref{Lcal}
is injective,
since it is a right inverse of the trace map from~${K_i}$ to~${F_{{K_i}}}$.
Hence,
$\dim\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}})) = \dim\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}})$.
Moreover,
$
\dim(\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i}))
= {k(k+1)}/{2}
= k + {(k-1)k}/{2}.
$
                   
                    
                    
                         
                 
Since both spaces in the direct sum are trivially contained in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$,
the sum space is a subspace of~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$ with the same dimension.
Hence,
\eqref{decomposition-polynomial-spaces} follows.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:unisolvence}
Let~${K_i}$ be an element of~$\mathcal{C}_h$.
Then,
the set of functionals $\psi^{{F_{{K_i}}}}$, $\psi^{{K_i}}_1$, and~$\psi^{{K_i}}_2$ of~\eqref{dofs}
is a unisolvent set of degrees of freedom for the space~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$
be such that the moments~\eqref{dofs} vanish.
The set of functionals in~\eqref{dofs}
has the same cardinality as $\dim \underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$.
Indeed,
a direct computation
and~\eqref{dim-sigmakmo}
show
\begin{equation} \label{dimensions-check}
\begin{split}
\dim \mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}}) + \dim \mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i}) + \dim \mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})
&= k + \frac{k(k+1)}{2} + \frac{(k-1)k}{2} \\
&= k(k+1)
\overset{\eqref{dim-sigmakmo}}{=} \dim \underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Therefore,
it suffices to prove that~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ is identically zero over~${K_i}$.

We detail the proof for $k-1 \geq 1$.
The lowest-order case $k-1 = 0$ can be treated similarly
and is simpler,
since we no longer have the bulk moment of~\eqref{dofs:bulk-kmo-lambda}
among the degrees of freedom,
and the polynomial coefficients appearing in~\eqref{decomposition-polynomial-spaces} are constants.

Let $k-1 \geq 1$
and let~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$ be such that
the moments~\eqref{dofs} vanish.
By Lemma~\ref{lemma:dev-sym-basis-Ki},
there exist polynomials~$r_{k-1}^i$ and~$s_{k-1}^i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$
such that the restriction of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ on each~${K_i}$
admits the following representation
\begin{equation} \label{decomposition-taubfh}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h{}|_{{K_i}} = r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i).
\end{equation}
We proceed by proving that both polynomial coefficients vanish.

We first show that~$r_{k-1}^i$ vanishes.
Since the bulk moments~\eqref{dofs:bulk-k} vanish,
by applying the orthogonality property~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym}
and the identity~\eqref{dev:frob-norm},
we deduce that
for all~$q_{i}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$
the following identity holds true
\begin{equation*} 
\begin{split}
0 
\overset{\eqref{dofs:bulk-k}}{=}
\int_{K_i}  \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x
&\overset{\eqref{decomposition-taubfh}}{=}
\int_{K_i}  r_{k-1}^i \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\qquad+ \int_{K_i} s_{k-1}^i \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) :  \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\overset{\eqref{dev:frob-norm},\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym}}{=}
\frac12 \int_{K_i}  r_{k-1}^i q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Since~$r_{k-1}^i$ is in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$,
choosing~$q_{i} = r_{k-1}^i$ in
the above display yields~$r_{k-1}^i = 0$.
Hence,
the decomposition~\eqref{decomposition-taubfh} reads as
\begin{equation} \label{decomposition-taubfh-rkmoizero}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h|_{{K_i}} = s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i).
\end{equation}

We next show that~$s_{k-1}^i$ vanishes.
Since $\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ is symmetric,
its normal-tangential component on~${F_{{K_i}}}$ is
\begin{equation} \label{nt-tauh-on-FKi}
\begin{split}
(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{nt}{}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}
&
= \operatorname{\bf{t}}_i^T(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}) \operatorname{\bf{n}}_i
= \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h|_{{F_{{K_i}}}} : (\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)
= \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h|_{{F_{{K_i}}}} : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)
\\
&\!\!\!\!\overset{\eqref{decomposition-taubfh}}{=}
(r_{k-1}^i{}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{k-1}^i{}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)): \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) \\
&\overset{\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},\eqref{sym:frob-norm}}{=}
0 + \frac{1}{2}s_{k-1}^i{}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Since the edge moments~\eqref{dofs:edge} vanish 
and $2(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{nt}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}} = s_{k-1}^i|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}$ belongs to $\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}})$,
using the display above,
we deduce
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
0
\overset{\eqref{dofs:edge}}{=}
(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{nt}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}
\overset{\eqref{nt-tauh-on-FKi}}{=} 
\frac12s_{k-1}^i{}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}},
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
i.e., $s_{k-1}^i$ vanishes on the edge~${F_{{K_i}}}$.
In particular,
due to the decomposition~\eqref{decomposition-polynomial-spaces},
there exists a polynomial~$\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$
such that
\begin{equation} \label{skmoi-decomposition}
s_{k-1}^i = \lambda_i \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i,
\end{equation}
and the decomposition~\eqref{decomposition-taubfh-rkmoizero} reads as
\begin{equation} \label{tau-lambda-stildekmti}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h|_{{K_i}}
= \lambda_i \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i).
\end{equation}

Since the bulk moments~\eqref{dofs:bulk-kmo-lambda} vanish as well,
by applying the identity~\eqref{sym:frob-norm}
we deduce that,
for all~$q_{k-2}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$,
the following identity holds true
\begin{equation*} 
\begin{split}
0 
\overset{\eqref{dofs:bulk-kmo-lambda}}{=}
\int_{K_i}  \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h :  \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) \lambda_iw_i \, \mathrm{d}x
&\overset{\eqref{tau-lambda-stildekmti}}{=}
\int_{K_i} \lambda_i \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) :  \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) \lambda_iw_i \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\overset{\eqref{sym:frob-norm}}{=}
\frac12 \int_{K_i} \lambda_i^2 \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i w_i \, \mathrm{d}x.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Since~$\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i$ is in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$,
and the barycentric coordinate~$\lambda_i$ is positive on~${K_i}$,
taking~$w_i = \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i$ in the above display yields~$\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i = 0$.
Using~\eqref{skmoi-decomposition},
we deduce that~$s_{k-1}^i = 0$.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
In the standard MCS method~\cite{Gopalakrishnan-Lederer-Schoberl:2020-b},
a suitable combination of the covariant and contravariant Piola transformations
is employed to map the stress space from the reference element to the physical element,
while preserving the normal-tangential component
and the trace-free property.
In the present setting,
we would further need to preserve the symmetry of the stress tensor,
but considering the symmetric version of the above mapping is not sufficient:
a direct computation shows that,
although it preserves symmetry,
it no longer preserves the normal-tangential component.
Instead of pursuing the construction of a completely different mapping,
we rely on alternative arguments in the analysis,
which therefore does not require any specific mapping for the space~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemma:dev-n1-n2-basis}
Given~$K$ in~$\mathcal{T}_h$,
there exist two distinct indices $\mathtt{i},\mathtt{j}$ in $\{1,2,3\}$
such that the set
$\{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}),\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})\}$
forms a basis for~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0(K)$.
In particular,
for any given~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}(K)$,
there exist polynomials~$\ell_{k-1}$ and~$m_{k-1}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)$
such that~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ admits the following representation:
\begin{equation*}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h = \ell_{k-1} \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}) + m_{k-1} \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}).
\end{equation*}
Moreover,
the following estimate holds true
\begin{equation} \label{strict-CS}
|\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})|
< |\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}})||\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})|
= \frac12.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The shape-regularity assumption guarantees that
there exists at least a pair of distinct indices $(\mathtt{i},\mathtt{j})$ in $\{1,2,3\}$,
such that the corresponding normal vectors $\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}$ and $\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}$
are neither parallel nor orthogonal.
Consider the lowest-order local space $\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0(K)$.
Using the same dimension count as in~\eqref{dimensions-check},
we obtain $\dim \underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0(K) = 2$.
Since the set $\{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}),\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})\}$
comprises two elements,
it suffices to show that they are linearly independent.

Let~$\alpha$ and~$\beta$ be in~$\mathbb{R}$.
Assume that
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{0}
= \alpha\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}) + \beta \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})
= \alpha(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}} - \frac{1}{d}\underline{\mathbf{I}}) + \beta(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}} - \frac{1}{d}\underline{\mathbf{I}}),
\end{equation*}
where we have applied the definition of the deviatoric operator~$\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}$.
Applying the above identity to the tangential vector~$\operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}}$,
and using the orthogonality property between $\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}$ and $\operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}}$
together with the identity
$(\mathbf{a}\otimes\mathbf{b})\mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c})\mathbf{a}$
for all $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{c}$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$,
gives
\begin{equation*} \label{alpha-beta-basisn1n2}
\begin{split}
0
= \alpha(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}} - \frac{1}{d}\underline{\mathbf{I}})\operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}} + \beta(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}} - \frac{1}{d}\underline{\mathbf{I}})\operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}}
= \alpha(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}} \cdot \operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}})\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}} - \frac{\alpha+\beta}{d}\operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Since~$\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}$ and~$\operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}}$ are not parallel,
the above identity is satisfied if and only if both the coefficients
$\alpha(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}} \cdot \operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}})$
and
$(\alpha+\beta)/d$
vanish;
since~$\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}$ and~$\operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}}$ are not orthogonal,
$\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}} \cdot \operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}} \neq 0$,
which,
combined with $\alpha(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}} \cdot \operatorname{\bf{t}}_{\mathtt{j}})=0$,
yields~$\alpha = 0$.
Using this identity in $(\alpha+\beta)/d = 0$
in turn implies~$\beta = 0$.
Therefore,
the tensors $\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}})$ and $\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})$
are linearly independent and form a basis of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0(K)$.

The existence of the representation
$
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h = \ell_{k-1} \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}) + m_{k-1} \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}),
$
for~$\ell_{k-1}$ and~$m_{k-1}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)$,
follows from the decomposition
$
\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}(K) = \mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)\otimes\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^0(K).
$
The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality yields
$
|\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})|
\leq |\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}})||\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})|.
$
Since
$\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}})$ and $\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})$
are linearly independent,
equality cannot hold in the above display,
which combined with the identity~\eqref{dev:frob-norm},
yields the assertion.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lemma:norm-equivalence}
Let~$K$ be an element in~$\mathcal{T}_h$
and~$\mathbf{v}_h$ be in~$\mathbf{V}_{h,0}^k$.
Consider its symmetric gradient~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)$
and let~$\ell_{k-1}$ and~$m_{k-1}$ be the polynomials in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)$
of its representation on~$K$; cf.~\eqref{eps-vh:dev-dev-representation-dev}.
Then,
the following norm equivalence holds true
\begin{equation} \label{norm-equivalence:statement}
{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
\simeq \sum_{i = 1}^3\big({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2\big).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We first show the upper bound.
The decomposition~\eqref{eps-vh:dev-dev-representation-dev},
the identity~\eqref{dev:frob-norm},
and
the triangle inequality,
give
\begin{equation} \label{norm-equivalence:leq}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
&= {\left\|{\ell_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}) + m_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&\lesssim {\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
= \sum_{i=1}^3 \big({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2\big).
\end{split}
\end{equation}

We now show the lower bound.
Using the decomposition~\eqref{eps-vh:dev-dev-representation-dev},
expanding the square,
applying identity~\eqref{dev:frob-norm},
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
and the Young inequality,
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
&= {\left\|{\ell_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}) + m_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&\geq \frac12{\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 + \frac12{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&\qquad        - 2  |\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})| {\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)} \\
&\geq \frac12{\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 + \frac12{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&\qquad        - 2|\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})| \Big(\frac12{\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
                    + \frac12{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2\Big) \\
&= \big(\frac12-|\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})|\big)
    \sum_{i=1}^3 \big({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 \big) .
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Since by estimate~\eqref{strict-CS} we have
$|\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}})| < 1/2$,
the coefficient in the above display
is positive,
and we deduce
\begin{equation} \label{norm-equivalence:geq}
{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \gtrsim  \sum_{i=1}^3 \big({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2\big).
\end{equation}
Combining the bounds~\eqref{norm-equivalence:leq} and~\eqref{norm-equivalence:geq},
the assertion follows.

\end{split}
\end{equation*}
for all~$\mathbf{v}_h$ in~${\bf{V}}_h^k$ and~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h$ in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}$.

\label{lemma:discrete-inf-sup-bt}

\begin{equation} \label{inf-sup:i}
b_2(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h) \gtrsim {\left\|{\mathbf{v}_h} \right\|}_{1,\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}},h}^2
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{inf-sup:ii}

\end{equation}

    \gtrsim{\left\|{\mathbf{v}_h} \right\|}_{1,h}.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We detail the proof for $k-1 \geq 1$.
The lowest-order case $k-1 = 0$ can be treated similarly
and is simpler,
since all the estimates below that rely on the combination of
the degrees of freedom~\eqref{dofs:edge} and~\eqref{dofs:bulk-kmo-lambda}
can be directly obtained by using just the edge moments~\eqref{dofs:edge}.

Let~$\mathbf{v}_h$ be any given function in~$\mathbf{V}_{h,0}^k$;
we proceed by constructing a stress function~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}$
that satisfies the estimates~\eqref{inf-sup:i}--\eqref{inf-sup:ii}.
Let~$\gamma > 0$ be a positive constant to be fixed later.
We define~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$
by fixing the corresponding degrees of freedom in~\eqref{dofs} as follows.
We impose:
\begin{itemize}
\item on every edge~$F$ in~$\mathcal{F}_h$, for all~$p_{i}^F$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(F)$,
\begin{equation}  \label{stability-dofs:1}
\int_{F} (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{{nt}} p_{i}^F \, \mathrm{d}s = \frac{1}{h} \int_{F} \Pi_0^{F}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]p_{i}^F \, \mathrm{d}s;
\end{equation}
\item on every subelement~${K_i}$ in~$\mathcal{C}_h$, for all~$q_{i}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$,
\begin{equation} \label{stability-dofs:2}
\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x = - \gamma\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h) : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x;
\end{equation}
\item on every subelement~${K_i}$ in~$\mathcal{C}_h$, for all~$w_i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$,
\begin{equation} \label{stability-dofs:3}
\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Now let~$K$ be any fixed element in~$\mathcal{T}_h$,
and let~${K_i}$ be in~$\mathcal{C}_h(K)$.
Since~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ belongs to~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}$,
as discussed in Lemma~\ref{lemma:dev-sym-basis-Ki},
there exist polynomials~$r_{k-1}^i$ and~$s_{k-1}^i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$
such that the restriction of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ on~${K_i}$
admits the following representation:
\begin{equation} \label{tau-decomposition}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h|_{{K_i}} = r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i).
\end{equation} 
Similarly,
since~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)$ belongs to~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}(K)$,
as discussed in Corollary~\ref{corollary:decomposition-epsvh},
there exist polynomials~$\ell_{k-1}$ and $m_{k-1}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)$
such that the restriction of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)$ on~$K$
admits the following representation:
\begin{equation} \label{eps-decomposition}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)|_{K} = \ell_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}) + m_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}).
\end{equation}
Since the subelements~${K_i} \in \mathcal{C}_h(K)$ are subsets of $K$,
we can restrict the representation~\eqref{eps-decomposition}
to each~${K_i}$.

We now give alternative representations
for the coefficients of the decomposition~\eqref{tau-decomposition} above,
starting from~$s_{k-1}^i$.
Since $(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{{nt}}|_{F}$
belongs to~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(F)$,
identity~\eqref{stability-dofs:1},
entails that,
for all~$F$ in~$\mathcal{F}_h$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
\frac{1}{h}\Pi_0^{F}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]
\overset{\eqref{stability-dofs:1}}{=}
(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{{nt}}|_{F} 
&\overset{\eqref{tau-decomposition}}{=}
\big(r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)\big)_{{nt}}|_{F} 
\overset{\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},\eqref{sym:frob-norm}}{=}
\frac{1}{2}s_{k-1}^i|_{F}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
In particular,
for each~${K_i}$ in~$\mathcal{C}_h(K)$
with corresponding external edge~${F_{{K_i}}}$ in~$\mathcal{F}_h$,
it holds
\begin{equation}  \label{skmoi-value-on-edge}
\frac{1}{h}\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]
= \frac{1}{2}s_{k-1}^i|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}.
\end{equation}
Since~$s_{k-1}^i$ belongs to~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$,
the above display combined with the decomposition~\eqref{decomposition-polynomial-spaces},
yield the existence of a polynomial~$\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$
such that
\begin{equation} \label{skmoi-stildekmti}
s_{k-1}^i = \frac{2}{h}\mathcal{L}_0(\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]) + \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i,
\end{equation}
where
$\mathcal{L}_0(\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right])$ denotes
the constant extension of $\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]$ from~${F_{{K_i}}}$ over~${K_i}$
as defined in~\eqref{Lcal}.

We now also give a representation for the polynomial coefficient~$r_{k-1}^i$ of~\eqref{tau-decomposition}.
Inserting the \eqref{tau-decomposition}
in~\eqref{stability-dofs:2},
and applying the orthogonality property~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},
we obtain that,
for all~$q_{i}$ in $\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$,
the following identity holds true
\begin{equation*} \label{rkmoi-integral-value}
\int_{{K_i}} r_{k-1}^i \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x = - \gamma\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h) : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x.
\end{equation*}
Since the polynomial coefficient $r_{k-1}^i$
belongs to~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$
and the symmetric gradient $\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)|_{{K_i}}$
and the tensor $\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)$
have entries in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$, and~$\mathbb{P}_0({K_i})$,
respectively,
the above display
combined with the identity~\eqref{dev:frob-norm}
yields
\begin{equation} \label{rkmoi-value}
\begin{split}
r_{k-1}^i
&= -2\gamma \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) : \underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)|_{{K_i}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

We are now ready to prove the estimates in~\eqref{inf-sup:i-ii},
starting with~\eqref{inf-sup:i}.
Plugging~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$ and~$\mathbf{v}_h$
in the bilinear form~$b_2(\cdot,\cdot)$ of~\eqref{btwo},
we have
\begin{equation*} \label{bt-Ione-Itwo}
\begin{split}
b_2(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h) 
= -\sum_{K \in \mathcal{T}_h} \sum_{i=1}^3 \int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h) \, \mathrm{d}x
    + \sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \int_{F} (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{{nt}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right] \, \mathrm{d}s 
=: I_1 + I_2.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}

We start by estimating~$I_1$.
Consider the constants
$B_{ij} := \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_j\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_j)$;
in particular,
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives~$|B_{ij}| \leq 1$.
Note that below,
one of the two indices will always be fixed to~$\mathtt{i}$ or~$\mathtt{j}$
of Lemma~\ref{lemma:dev-n1-n2-basis}.
Using the representations~\eqref{tau-decomposition} of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$
and~\eqref{eps-decomposition} of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)$  on each~${K_i}$,
the orthogonality~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},
and the identity~\eqref{rkmoi-value},
we have
\begin{equation} \label{Ione}
\begin{split}
-\sum_{i=1}^3& \int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h) \, \mathrm{d}x 
\overset{\eqref{tau-decomposition}}{=}
-\sum_{i=1}^3 \int_{{K_i}} (r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)+s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)) : \underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h) \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\overset{\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},\eqref{eps-decomposition}}{=} 
-\sum_{i=1}^3 \int_{{K_i}} 
   r_{k-1}^i\,(\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h))
   + s_{k-1}^i\,(\ell_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{i}} + m_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{j}}) \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\overset{\eqref{rkmoi-value}}{=}
\sum_{i=1}^3 \int_{{K_i}} 
   2\gamma(\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h))^2 
   \, \mathrm{d}x
   -\sum_{i=1}^3 \int_{{K_i}} s_{k-1}^i\,(\ell_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{i}} + m_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{j}}) \, \mathrm{d}x\\
&=: {I_{K,1}} + {I_{K,2}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We estimate the two terms on the right-hand side separately.

We first estimate~$I_{K,1}$.
Since~$\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)$ is a polynomial,
a scaling argument combined with~$|{K_i}|=|K|/3$
and the shape-regularity assumption
yield
that there exists a positive constant~$C_{\text{eq},2}$ such that
\begin{equation*}
{\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}
\leq C_{\text{eq},2} {\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})},
\end{equation*}
which implies
\begin{equation} \label{IKone:first-estimate}
I_{K,1} 
= \sum_{i=1}^3 2\gamma
  {\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2
\geq 2\gammaC_{\text{eq},2}^2 \sum_{i=1}^3  
  {\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2.
\end{equation}
Consider the constants
$D_{ij} := \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_j\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_j)$;
with this notation,
identity~\eqref{dev:frob-norm} reads as $|D_{ii}|=1/2$.
Note that below,
the two indices will always be fixed to~$\mathtt{i}$ and~$\mathtt{j}$ of Lemma~\ref{lemma:dev-n1-n2-basis}.
Neglecting a non-negative term,
using the representation~\eqref{eps-decomposition} of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)$ on~$K$
and the definition of~$D_{ij}$,
and expanding the squares,
we obtain
\begin{equation} \label{sumdev-eps}
\begin{split}
\sum_{i = 1}^3&{\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 
\geq \sum_{i \in\mathtt{i},\mathtt{j}}{\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&\overset{\eqref{eps-decomposition}}{=} 
\sum_{i \in\mathtt{i},\mathtt{j}}{\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):(\ell_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{i}})+m_{k-1}\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_{\mathtt{j}}))} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&={\left\|{\ell_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{i}} + m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 
     + {\left\|{\ell_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}+ m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{j}\mathtt{j}}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&={\left\|{\frac12\ell_{k-1} + m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 
     + {\left\|{\ell_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}+ \frac12m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 \\
&= \frac14{\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} + |D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|^2{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} + \int_K\ell_{k-1}m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}\, \mathrm{d}x \\
        &\quad +|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|^2{\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} + \frac14{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} +\int_K\ell_{k-1}m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}\, \mathrm{d}x \\
&=\Big(\frac14+|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|^2\Big)({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)})+ 2\int_K\ell_{k-1}m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}\, \mathrm{d}x \\
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We estimate the last term on the right-hand side.
The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and
the Young inequality
yield
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
2\int_K\ell_{k-1}m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}\, \mathrm{d}x
&\geq - 2\int_K|\ell_{k-1}m_{k-1} D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|\, \mathrm{d}x 
\geq - 2|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|{\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2(K)} \\
&\geq - 2|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|\big(\frac12{\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} + \frac12{\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}\big).
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Using the estimate above in~\eqref{sumdev-eps}
and applying Lemma~\ref{lemma:norm-equivalence}
with the notation of~\eqref{norm-equivalence:explicit-constants},
gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
&\sum_{i = 1}^3{\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 
\geq \Big(\frac14+|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|^2-|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|\Big)({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}) \\
&\qquad= \Big(\frac12-|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|\Big)^2({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)})
\geq \Big(\frac12-|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|\Big)^2c_{\text{eq},1}^2{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)} \\
&\qquad=: C_{\text{eq},3}^2{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Note that due to~\eqref{strict-CS}, $|D_{\mathtt{i}\mathtt{j}}|<1/2$ and
consequently, $C_{\text{eq},3}$ is a positive constant.
Inserting the above display in~\eqref{IKone:first-estimate},
we obtain
\begin{equation} \label{IKone}
I_{K,1} \geq 2\gammaC_{\text{eq},2}^2C_{\text{eq},3}^2{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2.
\end{equation}

We now estimate~$I_{K,2}$.
Inserting the representations~\eqref{tau-decomposition} of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$
and~\eqref{skmoi-stildekmti} of~$s_{k-1}^i$ on~${K_i}$
in identity~\eqref{stability-dofs:3},
we obtain that,
for all~$w_i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$
it holds
\begin{equation} \label{zero-sum-Lcal-skmoi}
\begin{split}
0
&\overset{\eqref{stability-dofs:3}}{=}
\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\overset{\eqref{tau-decomposition}}{=}
\int_{{K_i}} (r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)) : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\overset{\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},\eqref{skmoi-stildekmti}}{=}
\int_{{K_i}} 0 + \big(\frac{2}{h}\mathcal{L}_0(\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]) + \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i\big) \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\overset{\eqref{sym:frob-norm}}{=}
\frac12 \int_{{K_i}} \big(\frac{2}{h}\mathcal{L}_0(\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]) + \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i\big)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Choosing
$w_i = \widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i$ in the above display
and applying
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
and the stability estimate~\eqref{stability-Lcal} of the constant extension operator~$\mathcal{L}_0$,
give
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2
&\overset{\eqref{zero-sum-Lcal-skmoi}}{\lesssim}
\frac{1}{h}\int_{{K_i}}|\mathcal{L}_0(\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right])\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i| \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\leq \frac{1}{h}{\left\|{\mathcal{L}_0(\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right])} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}{\left\|{\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\overset{\eqref{stability-Lcal}}{\lesssim}
h^{-\frac12}{\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}{\left\|{\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}.
\end{split} 
\end{equation*}
Then,
taking the $L^2$ norm on both sides of the identity~\eqref{skmoi-stildekmti}
and applying 
the triangle inequality,
the above display,
the stability estimate~\eqref{stability-Lcal},
and the shape-regularity assumption,
yield
the existence of a positive constant~$\widetilde{C}$
such that
\begin{equation} \label{skmoi-leq-PizF}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{s_{k-1}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
&\leq{\left\|{\widetilde{s}_{k-2}^i\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + \frac{2}{h}{\left\|{\mathcal{L}_0(\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right])} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\leq \widetilde{C} h^{-\frac12}{\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Considering now the term~$I_{K,2}$ in~\eqref{Ione},
applying the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
the triangle inequality,
the estimates~\eqref{skmoi-leq-PizF}
and~$|B_{ij}| \leq 1$ for~$ij = \{i\mathtt{i},i\mathtt{j}\}$,
the weighted Young inequality with parameter~$\delta > 0$,
and Lemma~\ref{lemma:norm-equivalence}
with the notation of~\eqref{norm-equivalence:explicit-constants},
we have
\begin{equation} \label{IKtwo}
\begin{split}
|I_{K,2}|
&\leq \sum_{i=1}^3 {\left\|{s_{k-1}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} {\left\|{\ell_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{i}} + m_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{j}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\leq \sum_{i=1}^3 {\left\|{s_{k-1}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \big({\left\|{\ell_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{i}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + {\left\|{m_{k-1} B_{i\mathtt{j}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}\big) \\
&\leq \sum_{i=1}^3 \widetilde{C} \frac{1}{h^{\frac12}} {\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})} \big({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}\big) \\
&\leq \widetilde{C}\frac{1}{2\delta}\sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{h} {\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}
        + \delta\sum_{i=1}^3 \big({\left\|{\ell_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 + {\left\|{m_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2\big) \\
&= \frac{\widetilde{C}}{2\delta}\sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{h} {\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}
        + \deltaC_{\text{eq},1}^2{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Summing over the elements~$K$ in~$\mathcal{T}_h$
and plugging~\eqref{IKone} and~\eqref{IKtwo} in~\eqref{Ione},
since one edge in~$\mathcal{F}_h$ is shared at most by two elements in~$\mathcal{T}_h$,
gives
\begin{equation} \label{Ione-final}
\begin{split}
I_1
&\geq \sum_{K\in \mathcal{T}_h}(I_{K,1} - |I_{K,2}|) \\
&\geq \Big(2\gammaC_{\text{eq},2}^2C_{\text{eq},3}^2-\deltaC_{\text{eq},1}^2\Big) \sum_{K\in \mathcal{T}_h} {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
            - \frac{\widetilde{C}}{\delta}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_h}\frac{1}{h}{{\left\|{\Pi_0^{F}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}}_{L^2(F)}^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}


We now estimate~$I_2$.
Using the identity~\eqref{skmoi-value-on-edge}
and the~$L^2$-orthogonality of~$\Pi_0^{F}$,
we have
\begin{equation} \label{Itwo}
I_2 
= \sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \frac{1}{h}\int_F \Pi_0^{F}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right] 
  \cdot \left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right] \, \mathrm{d}s
= \sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \frac{1}{h}
  {\left\|{\Pi_0^{F}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2(F)}^2.
\end{equation}

Combining the estimates~\eqref{Ione-final} and~\eqref{Itwo}
for~$I_1$ and~$I_2$,
gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
b_2(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,&\mathbf{v}_h)
= I_1 + I_2 \geq \sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_h}(I_{K,1} - |I_{K,2}|) + I_2 \\
&\geq \Big(2\gammaC_{\text{eq},2}^2C_{\text{eq},3}^2-\deltaC_{\text{eq},1}^2\Big)\sum_{K \in \mathcal{T}_h}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
  + \Big(1 - \frac{\widetilde{C}}{\delta}\Big)
  \sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h}
  \frac{1}{h}{\left\|{\Pi_0^{F}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2(F)}^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
By choosing the positive parameters~$\delta$ and~$\gamma$ as
\begin{equation} \label{delta-gamma}
\delta = 2\widetilde{C},
\quad \text{and}\quad
\gamma = 2\widetilde{C}\Big(\frac{C_{\text{eq},1}}{C_{\text{eq},2}C_{\text{eq},3}}\Big)^2,
\end{equation}
and using the identity~\eqref{deveps-equal-eps},
we conclude
\begin{equation*}
b_2(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h)
\gtrsim  \sum_{K \in \mathcal{T}_h} {\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h))} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
  + \sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h}
  \frac{1}{h}{\left\|{\Pi_0^{F}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2(F)}^2,
\end{equation*}
which yields estimate~\eqref{inf-sup:i}.

We next show~\eqref{inf-sup:ii}.
Using the representation~\eqref{tau-decomposition} of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h$
and the orthogonality property~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym} twice and
choosing~$q_{i} = r_{k-1}^i$ in~\eqref{stability-dofs:2},
applying
the estimate~\eqref{skmoi-leq-PizF},
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
the weighted Young inequality with positive parameter~$\eta >0$,
and adding some non-negative terms,
yield
\begin{equation} \label{intermediate-tauhnorm}
\begin{split}
&{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
\overset{\eqref{tau-decomposition}}{\leq}
\sum_{i=1}^3 {\left\|{r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 + \sum_{i=1}^3{\left\|{s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^3 \int_{{K_i}}r_{k-1}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)r_{k-1}^i\, \mathrm{d}x + \sum_{i=1}^3{\left\|{s_{k-1}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 \\
&\overset{\eqref{stability-dofs:2},\eqref{sym:frob-norm}}{=}
\sum_{i=1}^3 - \gamma\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)r_{k-1}^i \, \mathrm{d}x
    + \frac12\sum_{i=1}^3 {\left\|{s_{k-1}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2 \\
&\overset{\eqref{skmoi-value-on-edge}}{\leq}
\sum_{i=1}^3 \gamma{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2(K)} {\left\|{r_{k-1}^i \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
    +  \frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^3\frac{1}{h} {\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})} \\
&{\leq}
\frac{\eta}{2}\gamma^2{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}
    +  \frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{h} {\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})} \\
&\qquad    + \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{2\eta}({\left\|{r_{k-1}^i \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})}
        + {\left\|{s_{k-1}^i \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Then,
combining the above display
with
$\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) : s_{k-1}^i \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)= 0$
due to the orthogonality property~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
&\overset{\eqref{intermediate-tauhnorm},\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym}}{\leq}
\frac{\eta}{2}\gamma^2{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}
    +  \frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{h} {\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})} \\
&\qquad    + \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{2\eta}\Big(
            {\left\|{r_{k-1}^i \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})}
          + {\left\|{s_{k-1}^i \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\qquad   + 2\int_{{K_i}} r_{k-1}^i \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) : s_{k-1}^i \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) \, \mathrm{d}x\Big) \\ 
&= \frac{\eta}{2}\gamma^2{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}
    + \frac{\widetilde{C}}{2} \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{h} {\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}
    + \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{2\eta}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})},
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
where the constant~$\gamma$ is fixed as in~\eqref{delta-gamma}.
Using the identity~\eqref{deveps-equal-eps}
in the above display
yields
\begin{equation*} \label{upper-bound-taubfh}
\big(1-\frac{1}{2\eta}\big) {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2
\leq \frac12(\eta\gamma^2 + \widetilde{C}) \Big({\left\|{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h))} \right\|}_{L^2(K)}^2 + \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{1}{h}{\left\|{\Pi_0^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{t}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}\Big).
\end{equation*}
Choosing~$\eta = 1$,
both the coefficients
$(1-1/(2\eta))$ and $\eta\gamma^2 + \widetilde{C}$ are positive;
hence,
summing over the elements~$K$ in~$\mathcal{T}_h$,
yields~\eqref{inf-sup:ii}.

The inf-sup estimate~\eqref{discrete-inf-sup:btwo}
follows from~\eqref{inf-sup:i-ii} and
the norm equivalence~\eqref{norm-equivalence-h-dev}.
This concludes the proof.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We detail the proof for $k-1 \geq 1$.
The lowest-order case $k-1 = 0$ can be treated similarly
and is simpler.
We split the proof in three steps.
\paragraph*{Stability estimates.}
We first prove the local stability estimate.
Let~${K_i}$ be an element of~$\mathcal{C}_h$
and~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ be a stress function in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}$.
Lemma~\ref{lemma:dev-sym-basis-Ki} entails that
there exist polynomials~$r_{\mathcal{I}}^i$ and~$s_{\mathcal{I}}^i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$
such that
$\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|_{{K_i}})$ admits the following representation
\begin{equation} \label{IcalKi-representation}
\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|_{{K_i}}) = r_{\mathcal{I}}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{\mathcal{I}}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i).
\end{equation}
Hence,
the triangle inequality
and identities~\eqref{dev:frob-norm}-\eqref{sym:frob-norm}
imply that
\begin{equation} \label{triangle-IcalKi}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|_{{K_i}})} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
&\leq {\left\|{r_{\mathcal{I}}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
    + {\left\|{s_{\mathcal{I}}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
    ({\left\|{r_{\mathcal{I}}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
    +{\left\|{s_{\mathcal{I}}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We estimate the two terms on the right-hand side separately.

As for the first term,
inserting the representation~\eqref{IcalKi-representation}
in~\eqref{interpolation:bulk-kmo}
and applying the orthogonality property~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},
we obtain that,
for all~$q_{i}$ in $\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$,
the following identity holds true
\begin{equation*} \label{rIi-integral-value}
\int_{{K_i}} r_{\mathcal{I}}^i \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x
= \int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} : \operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) q_{i} \, \mathrm{d}x.
\end{equation*}
The above display
and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
yield that
\begin{equation*} 
\begin{split}
&\frac12{\left\|{r_{\mathcal{I}}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}^2
= \int_{{K_i}}r_{\mathcal{I}}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i):\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)r_{\mathcal{I}}^i\, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\qquad= \int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i)r_{\mathcal{I}}^i \, \mathrm{d}x 
\lesssim {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} {\left\|{r_{\mathcal{I}}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Therefore,
\begin{equation} \label{rIi-estimate}
{\left\|{r_{\mathcal{I}}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \lesssim {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}.
\end{equation}

As for the second term,
since $(\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|_{{K_i}}) )_{{nt}}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}$
belongs to~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({F_{{K_i}}})$,
the identity~\eqref{interpolation:edge},
the orthogonality property~\eqref{orthogonality:dev-sym},
the identity~\eqref{sym:frob-norm},
the symmetry of~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$,
and the fact that $\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)$ is constant on~${F_{{K_i}}}$,
entail
\begin{equation} \label{sI-value-on-edge}
\frac{1}{2}s_{\mathcal{I}}^i|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}
= (\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|_{{K_i}}))_{{nt}}|_{{F_{{K_i}}}}
= \Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}})
=  \Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i).
\end{equation}
Since~$s_{\mathcal{I}}^i$ belongs to~$\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({K_i})$,
the above display yields the existence of~$\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$
such that
\begin{equation} \label{sI-stildeI}
s_{\mathcal{I}}^i = 2\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}})) + \tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i,
\end{equation}
where
$\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}}))$ denotes
the constant extension of $\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}})$ from~${F_{{K_i}}}$ over~${K_i}$
as defined in~\eqref{Lcal}.
Then,
the triangle inequality yields
\begin{equation} \label{triangle-sIi}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{s_{\mathcal{I}}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
\lesssim {\left\|{\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}})} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
    +{\left\|{\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Using the stability of the extension operator~$\mathcal{L}_{k-1}$ of~\eqref{stability-Lcal},
the stability of the $L^2$-projection on~${F_{{K_i}}}$, cf., e.g.~\cite[Thm.~18.16]{Ern-Guermond:1}, 
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
identity~\eqref{sym:frob-norm},
the multiplicative trace inequality; see, e.g., \cite[Lem.~12.15]{Ern-Guermond:1},
and the shape-regularity assumption,
give
\begin{equation} \label{Lcal-estimate}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
&\lesssim h^{\frac12}{\left\|{\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}}} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})} 
\lesssim h^{\frac12}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}}} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}
\lesssim h^{\frac12}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})} \\
&\lesssim {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + h_K{\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right|}_{H^1({K_i})}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Inserting the representations~\eqref{IcalKi-representation} of~$\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$
and~\eqref{sI-stildeI} of~$s_{\mathcal{I}}^i$ on~${K_i}$
in identity~\eqref{interpolation:bulk-kmt-lambda},
we obtain that,
for all~$w_i$ in~$\mathbb{P}_{k-2}({K_i})$
it holds
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
\int_{{K_i}} \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} &: \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i\, \mathrm{d}x
=\int_{{K_i}} \mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}) : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&= \int_{{K_i}} (r_{\mathcal{I}}^i\operatorname{\underline{\mathbf{dev}}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i) + s_{\mathcal{I}}^i\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)) : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&= \int_{{K_i}} \big(2\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}})) + \tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i\big) \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i) : \underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)w_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x \\
&= \frac12\int_{{K_i}} \big(2\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}})) + \tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\big)\lambda_iw_i\lambda_i \, \mathrm{d}x.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Choosing~$w_i = \tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}$ in~\eqref{interpolation:bulk-kmt-lambda} and
using the representation~\eqref{sI-stildeI},
the identity~\eqref{sym:frob-norm},
and the estimate~\eqref{Lcal-estimate},
give
\begin{equation*} 
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})}
&= 2\int_{{K_i}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:\underline{\bf{sym}}(\operatorname{\bf{n}}_i\otimes\operatorname{\bf{t}}_i)\,\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i\, \mathrm{d}x
    - \int_{{K_i}}\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}}))\,\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i\, \mathrm{d}x \\
&\lesssim \Big({\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
    + {\left\|{\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(\Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}}))} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}\Big)
    {\left\|{\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\lesssim\Big({\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + h{\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right|}_{H^1({K_i})}\Big)
     {\left\|{\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})},
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
hence,
the following estimate holds true
\begin{equation} \label{stildeIi-estimate}
{\left\|{\tilde{s}^i_{\mathcal{I}}\lambda_i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \lesssim {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + h_{{K_i}}{\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right|}_{H^1({K_i})}.
\end{equation}
Inserting estimates~\eqref{Lcal-estimate} and~\eqref{stildeIi-estimate} in~\eqref{triangle-sIi}
gives
\begin{equation} \label{sIi-estimate}
{\left\|{s_{\mathcal{I}}^i} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \lesssim {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + h_{{K_i}}{\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right|}_{H^1({K_i})}.
\end{equation}

Combining the estimates~\eqref{rIi-estimate} for~$r_{\mathcal{I}}^i$
and~\eqref{sIi-estimate} for~$s_{\mathcal{I}}^i$
in~\eqref{triangle-IcalKi}
with the quasi-uniform assumption,
we obtain the stability estimate
\begin{equation} \label{IcalKi-stability}
{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
\lesssim {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + h_K{\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right|}_{H^1({K_i})}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Approximation estimates.}
We first derive the edge approximation estimate.
By~\eqref{sI-value-on-edge},
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
{\left\|{(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_{{nt}}} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}
={\left\|{(I - \Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}})(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{nt}})} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}.
\end{equation*}
Standard approximation properties of the $L^2$-projection on edges; see, e.g., \cite[Rem.~18.17]{Ern-Guermond:1}
and regularity assumptions on~$\mathcal{T}_h$,
yields
\begin{equation*}
{\left\|{q - \Pi_{k-1}^{{F_{{K_i}}}} q} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})} \lesssim h^{s-\frac12}{\left|{q} \right|}_{H^s({K_i})}
\qquad\qquad \forall q \in H^s({K_i}).
\end{equation*}
Combining the two displays above
with the regularity assumptions on~$\mathcal{T}_h$ gives
\begin{equation*}
{\left\|{(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_{{nt}}} \right\|}_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}
\lesssim h^{s-\frac12}{\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right|}_{H^s({K_i})},
\end{equation*}
which proves the local approximation estimate on~${F_{{K_i}}}$.

We next derive the volume approximation estimate.
By estimate~\eqref{IcalKi-stability},
the interpolation operator~$\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}$ is continuous and
by the unisolvency of the degrees of freedom, cf. Lemma~\ref{lemma:unisolvence},
it preserves polynomials in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$, i.e.,
\begin{equation} \label{preservation-polynomials}
\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h - \mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h = 0
    \qquad \forall \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h \in \underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i}).
\end{equation}
Since the $L^2$-projection preserves both the symmetric and trace-free properties,
given~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ in~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}({K_i})$
its $L^2$-projection $\underline{\mathbf{q}}_{k-1}:=\Pi_{k-1}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})$ belongs to~$\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}({K_i})$.
Then,
the triangle inequality,
the preservation property~\eqref{preservation-polynomials} and
the stability property~\eqref{IcalKi-stability} of the interpolation operator $\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}$,
and the approximation properties of the $L^2$-projection $\Pi_{k-1}$, cf., e.g., \cite[Thm.~18.16]{Ern-Guermond:1},
yield
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
&\leq {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\mathbf{q}}_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
    + {\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{q}}_{k-1} - \mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&= {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\mathbf{q}}_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}
    + {\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{{K_i}}(\underline{\mathbf{q}}_{k-1} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\lesssim {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\mathbf{q}}_{k-1}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} + h_{{K_i}}{\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\mathbf{q}}_{k-1}} \right|}_{H^1({K_i})}
\lesssim h^s {\left|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right|}_{H^s({K_i})},
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
which proves the local approximation estimate on~${K_i}$.

The global estimate~\eqref{global-approximation:sigma}
follows by summing the local estimate
over all elements~${K_i}$ in~$\mathcal{C}_h$.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{theorem:error-estimates}
Let~$(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\mathbf{u},p)$ and $(\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h,\mathbf{u}_h,p_h)$
be the solutions to~\eqref{strong-mixed} and~\eqref{variational-formulation:discrete}.
Assume that $\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ belongs to $H^1(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\cap H^{m-1}(\mathcal{C}_h,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$,
$\mathbf{u}$ to $H^1(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^d)\cap H^{m}(\mathcal{T}_h,\mathbb{R}^d)$,
and $p$ to $L^2_0(\Omega,\mathbb{R})\cap H^{m-1}(\mathcal{T}_h,\mathbb{R})$.
Then,
for~$s := \min(m-1,k)$
the following estimate holds true
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} + {\left\|{\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_h} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h} + \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{p-p_h} \right\|}_{Q_h}
    \lesssim h^s\Big({\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{H^{s}(\mathcal{C}_h)} + {\left\|{\mathbf{u}} \right\|}_{H^{s+1}(\mathcal{T}_h)} +{\left\|{p} \right\|}_{H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_h)} \Big)
\end{equation*}

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
The proof follows along the same lines as those of~\cite[Thm.~6.3]{Gopalakrishnan-Lederer-Schoberl:2020};
we include it here for completeness.

The triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation} \label{error-estimate:triangle}
\begin{split}
&\frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} + {\left\|{\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_h} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h} + \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{p-p_h} \right\|}_{Q_h} \\
&\qquad\qquad\lesssim \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} + {\left\|{\mathbf{u}-\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u}} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h} + \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{p-\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p} \right\|}_{Q_h} \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad +\frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} + {\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_h} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h} + \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p-p_h} \right\|}_{Q_h} \\
&\qquad\qquad\lesssim \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} + {\left\|{\mathbf{u}-\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u}} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h} + \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{p-\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p} \right\|}_{Q_h} \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad +\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\left|\!\left|\!\left| (\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h,\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_h,\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p-p_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right|.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Using the approximation estimates~\eqref{approximation:V-Q} and~\eqref{global-approximation:sigma},
the first three terms on the right-hand side of the above display can be bounded as needed.
As for the latter term,
using Lemmata~\ref{lemma:inf-sup-product-space}
and~\ref{lemma:consistency},
we deduce
\begin{equation} \label{sup:error-estimates}
\begin{split}
&\left|\!\left|\!\left| (\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h,\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}_h,\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p - p_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right| \\
&\qquad\qquad \leq
    \sup_{(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,q_h)\in\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h^{k-1}\times{\bf{V}}_h^k\timesQ_h^{k-1}}
    \frac{B(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u},\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p - p; \underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,q_h)}{\left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,q_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right|}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We now estimate the numerator.
As for the first three terms on the right-hand side of~\eqref{bilinear-form:B},
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality yields  
\begin{equation} \label{a-bone:estimate}
\begin{split}
&a(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h) + b_1(\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u},q_h) + b_1(\mathbf{v}_h,\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p - p) \\
&\quad\lesssim \Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} \Big)
    + \Big(\sqrt{\nu}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}{\left\|{q_h} \right\|}_{Q_h}\Big) \\
    &\qquad+ \Big(\sqrt{\nu}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}_h} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p - p} \right\|}_{Q_h}\Big) \\
&\quad\leq \left|\!\left|\!\left| (\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} -\mathbf{u},\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p - p) \right|\!\right|\!\right|\left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,q_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right|.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
As for the latter two terms on the right-hand side of~\eqref{bilinear-form:B},
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
and
the discrete trace inequality~\eqref{trace-inequality},
give
\begin{equation*} \label{btwo:estimate}
\begin{split}
&b_2(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\mathbf{v}_h) + b_2(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}) \\
&\lesssim \sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \sqrt{h}{\left\|{(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_{{nt}}} \right\|}_{L^2(F)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}{\left\|{\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{\operatorname{\bf{t}}}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2(F)} 
    +\sum_{{K_i} \in \mathcal{C}_h} {\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\quad+ \sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \sqrt{h}{\left\|{(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h)_{{nt}}} \right\|}_{L^2(F)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}{\left\|{\left[\!\left[(\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u})_{\operatorname{\bf{t}}}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}_{L^2(F)} 
    +\sum_{{K_i} \in \mathcal{C}_h} {\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u})} \right\|}_{L^2({K_i})} \\
&\lesssim \left(\sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \frac{h}{\nu}{\left\|{(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_{{nt}}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(F)}\right)^{\frac12}
          \left(\sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \frac{\nu}{h}{\left\|{\left[\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_h)_{\operatorname{\bf{t}}}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2(F)}\right)^{\frac12} \\
    &\quad+ \left(\sum_{{K_i} \in \mathcal{C}_h} \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})}\right)^{\frac12}
             \left(\sum_{K \in \mathcal{T}_h}\nu{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{v}_h)} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}\right)^{\frac12} \\
    &\quad+ \left(\sum_{{K_i} \in \mathcal{C}_h} \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})}\right)^{\frac12}
             \left(\sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} \frac{\nu}{{h}}{\left\|{\left[\!\left[(\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u})_{\operatorname{\bf{t}}}\right]\!\right]} \right\|}^2_{L^2(F)}\right)^{\frac12} \\
    &\quad+ \left(\sum_{{K_i} \in \mathcal{C}_h} \frac{1}{\nu}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h} \right\|}^2_{L^2({K_i})}\right)^{\frac12}
             \left(\sum_{K \in \mathcal{T}_h}{\left\|{\underline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u})} \right\|}^2_{L^2(K)}\right)^{\frac12}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Starting from the right-hand side of the above display,
the definition of the norm~$\left|\!\left|\!\left| (\cdot,\cdot,\cdot) \right|\!\right|\!\right|$ of~\eqref{norm-three-bars}
and the estimate
$\left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,0) \right|\!\right|\!\right| \leq \left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,p_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right|$,
yield
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
b_2(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\mathbf{v}_h) &+ b_2(\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}) \\
&\lesssim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\left(\left(\sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} {h}{\left\|{(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_{{nt}}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(F)}\right)^{\frac12}
                +{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} \right)
                \left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,0) \right|\!\right|\!\right| \\
    &\quad+ \sqrt{\nu}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h} 
                \left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,0) \right|\!\right|\!\right| \\
&\lesssim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\left(\left(\sum_{F \in \mathcal{F}_h} {h}{\left\|{(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_{{nt}}} \right\|}^2_{L^2(F)}\right)^{\frac12}
                +{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}} \right\|}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h} \right)
                \left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,p_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right| \\
    &\quad+ \sqrt{\nu}{\left\|{\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}} \right\|}_{{\bf{V}}_h} 
                \left|\!\left|\!\left| (\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_h,\mathbf{v}_h,p_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right|.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Combining the above display with estimates~\eqref{a-bone:estimate} in~\eqref{sup:error-estimates},
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
&\left|\!\left|\!\left| (\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_h,\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}_h,\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p - p_h) \right|\!\right|\!\right| \\
&\qquad\qquad\lesssim \left|\!\left|\!\left| (\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\mathcal{I}_{{\bf{V}}_h}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u},\mathcal{I}_{Q_h} p - p) \right|\!\right|\!\right|
    + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\left(\sum_{{F_{{K_i}}} \in \mathcal{F}_h} {h}{\left\|{(\mathcal{I}_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_h}\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} - \underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_{{nt}}} \right\|}^2_{L^2({F_{{K_i}}})}\right)^{\frac12}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Inserting the estimate above in~\eqref{error-estimate:triangle},
and applying the approximation
estimates~\eqref{approximation:V-Q}, \eqref{local-approximation:sigma}, and~\eqref{global-approximation:sigma},
the assertion follows.
