<!-- proof-1: body_md -->

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{full_lapid_mao}
Assume that the different $\mathfrak{d}_F$ splits in $E$. Assume that $\pi$ is only ramified at split primes. Let $\phi= \phi_\pi^\circ \otimes \phi_\infty \in \pi$ and $\phi'= \phi_{\pi^\vee}^\circ \otimes \phi_\infty' \in \pi^\vee$, where $\phi_\pi^\circ \in \pi_f$ and $\phi_{\pi^\vee}^\circ \in \pi_f^\vee$ are the respective essential vectors of $\pi$ and $\pi^\vee$, and $\phi_\infty \in \pi_\infty$ and $\phi_\infty' \in \pi_\infty^\vee$ are some archimedean forms. Then:

$$
\langle \phi,\phi' \rangle = C \cdot {L(\chi_{E/F},1) \cdot L^{imp}(\pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)} \cdot \prod_{v \in S_\infty} [ W_{\phi_v}, W_{\phi_v'}]_v
$$

where $C = 2^k \cdot D_F^{n(n-1)/4} \cdot u_{E/F} \cdot \prod_{\wp \in S_\pi} \left( 1 - N\wp^{-n} \right)^{-1}$ and:
$$
u_{E/F} = \prod_{v \in S_{E/F}} \frac{\prod_{i=2}^{n} L_v(\chi_{E/F}^i,i)}{L_v(\pi_v,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)} \cdot I^{\natural}_v(\phi_v,\phi_v')^{-1}
$$
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For our choice of $\phi$ and $\phi'$, the set $S$ in \ref{Lapid_Mao_conjecture} can be chosen to be:
$$
S = S_\pi \cup S_{E/F} \cup S_{F} \cup S_\infty
$$
By assumptions, all the places in $S_\pi$, $S_F$ and $S_\infty$ are split in $E$. Let $v$ a place of $F$ which splits in $E$. In that case, $U(F_v) \simeq \ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(F_v)$. We recall that $(\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(F_v) \times\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(F_v), \ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(F_v))$ is a Gelfand pair i.e. $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(F_v)}(\pi_v \otimes \pi_v^\vee, \mathbb{C})$ is a $1$-dimensional vector space (see \cite[Theorem A]{Bernstein84} for non-archimedean places \cite[Theorem 8.2.5]{AG09} for archimedean places). Thus, in the above theorem, at each place $v \in S$ which is split in $E$, one can choose $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle_v$ to be $(\phi,\phi') \mapsto [W_\phi,W_{\phi'}]_v$ where $[\cdot,\cdot]_v$ is the pairing defined by:
\begin{equation}
\label{whittaker_pairing}
[ W,W' ]_v = \int_{N_{n}(F_v)\backslash P_{n}(F_v)}W(p) {W'}(p)dp
\end{equation}
for $W \in \mathcal{W}(\pi_v,\psi_{v})$ and $W \in \mathcal{W}(\pi_v^\vee,\psi_{v}^{-1})$ (for the equivariance of this pairing, see \cite[Proof of Proposition 3.1]{Zh14}). Here $P_n(F_v)$ is the mirabolic subgroup, consisting of matrices in $\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(F_v)$ whose last row is equal to $(0,\dots,0,1)$. For this choice of $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle_v$, one has (see \cite[Lemma 4.4]{LM15}),:
$$
I_v(\phi_v,\phi_v') = W_{\phi_v}(e) \cdot W_{\phi_v'}(e)
$$
Assume moreover that $v$ is finite. In \cite[Lemma 4.4]{LM15}, the Haar measure on $P_n(F_v)$ in (\ref{whittaker_pairing}) is normalized so that $\ensuremath{\mathrm{vol}}(P_n(\mathcal{O}_v))= \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \zeta_{F_v}(i)^{-1}$. Thus \cite[Lemma 3.2]{TR_BC}, where the Haar measure is normalized so that $\ensuremath{\mathrm{vol}}(P_n(\mathcal{O}_v))= 1$, gives:
$$
\langle \phi_v,\phi_v' \rangle_v = \frac{L^{imp}(\pi_v \times \pi_v^\vee,1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\zeta_{F_v}(i)} = \frac{L_{v}(\chi_{E/F},1) \cdot L^{imp}(\pi_v, \ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)}{ \prod_{i=1}^{n-1}L_{v}(\chi_{E/F}^i,i)}
$$
and the formula follows from \ref{Lapid_Mao_conjecture}. \\

Now assume that $v \in S_F$ (and thus $v$ is split by assumption). One still have that:
$$
I^{\natural}_v(\phi_v,\phi_v') = \frac{W_{\phi,v}(e) \cdot W_{\phi',v}(e)}{[W_{\phi,v},W_{\phi',v}]_v}
$$
with $W_{\phi,v} \in \mathcal{W}(\pi_v,\psi_v)$ and $W_{\phi',v} \in \mathcal{W}(\pi_v^\vee,\psi_v^{-1})$. By definition of $\phi$, we have:
$$
W_{\phi,v}(g) = W_{\pi_v}^\circ(\mathrm{diag}(d_v^{n-1},\dots,d_v,1)g)
$$

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{rationality_uE}
For every $\varsigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$, one has that:
$$
\varsigma(u_{E/F}(\pi_f)) = u_{E/F}({}^\varsigma \pi_f)
$$
In particular, $u_{E/F}(\pi_f) \in \mathbb{Q}(\pi_f) := \mathbb{C}^{S(\pi_f)}$ with $S(\pi_f) = \{ \varsigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}), {}^\varsigma \pi_f = \pi_f \}$.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The proof of this result is implicitly contained in \cite{Che25}. We recall it here. The constant $u_{E/F}$ is a product of local factors. To prove the lemma, it suffices to prove the $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$-equivariant formula for each local factor. Thus, let $v \in S_{E/F}$ and let $w \mid v$. We write $L/K$ for $E_w/F_v$. To simplify the notation, we will now adopt local notation, and omit the subscript $v$. Accordingly, we will write $\pi = \pi_v$, $\psi = \psi_v$ for the unramified additive character of $K = F_v$, 
$\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle$ for $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle_v :\pi_v \times \pi_v^\vee \to \mathbb{C} $ and $I(\cdot,\cdot)$ for $I_v(\cdot,\cdot)$. 



Let $\varsigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$. We recall that the local action of $\varsigma$ on $\pi = \pi_v$ is defined as follows. Let $V$ be the representation space of $\pi$. Then ${}^\varsigma \pi$ is the representation acting on ${}^\varsigma V:= V \otimes_{\mathbb{C},\varsigma^{-1}} \mathbb{C}$ via $\pi$ on the first factor. Let $a_{\varsigma} \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times$ be such that $\varsigma(\psi(x)) = \psi(a_{\varsigma}x)$ for all $x \in K$. Then, one has that:
$$
\varsigma(\psi_N(n)) = \psi_N\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix} a_{\varsigma} & &\\  & 1& \\ &&a_{\varsigma}^{-1} \end{smallmatrix}\right) n \left(\begin{smallmatrix} a_{\varsigma}^{-1} & &\\  & 1& \\ &&a_{\varsigma} \end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)
$$
for $n \in N$. The representation ${}^\varsigma \pi$ is generic and the map $t_{\varsigma} : W \mapsto {}^\varsigma W$ defined by:
$$
{}^\varsigma W : g \mapsto \sigma(W(\left(\begin{smallmatrix} a_{\varsigma}^{-1} & &\\  & 1& \\ &&a_{\varsigma} \end{smallmatrix}\right)g))
$$
is a $U(K)$-equivariant isomorphism $\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi) \buildrel\sim\over\rightarrow \mathcal{W}({}^\varsigma \pi,\psi)$ which is $\sigma$-semi-linear:
$$
t_{\varsigma}( \lambda \cdot W) = \varsigma(\lambda) \cdot t_{\varsigma}(W), \quad \lambda \in \mathbb{C}.
$$
We also define the $U(K)$-equivariant isomorphism $t_\varsigma : \pi \buildrel\sim\over\rightarrow {}^\varsigma \pi$, which sends $\phi \in \pi$ to the unique form ${}^\varsigma \phi \in {}^\varsigma \pi$ such that $W_{{}^\varsigma \phi} = {}^\varsigma W_\phi$. Since $\ensuremath{\mathrm{diag}}(a_{\varsigma},1, a_{\varsigma}^{-1}) \in U(K) \cap \ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_3}(\mathcal{O}_L)$, we  see that the spherical form $\phi_{{}^\varsigma \pi}^\circ$ of ${}^\varsigma\pi$ (with respect to $\psi$) is:
$$
\phi_{{}^\varsigma\pi}^\circ = {}^\varsigma\phi_{\pi}^\circ
$$
where $\phi_{\pi}^\circ$ is the spherical vector of $\pi$ (with respect to $\psi$). Let $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle_{\varsigma} : {}^\varsigma \pi \times {}^\varsigma \pi^\vee \to \mathbb{C}$  be the $U(K)$-equivariant pairing defined by:
$$
\langle {}^\varsigma \phi, {}^\varsigma \phi' \rangle_{\varsigma} = \varsigma(\langle \phi, \phi' \rangle)
$$
Thus, we have that $\varsigma(\langle \phi_{\pi}^\circ, \phi_{\pi^\vee}^\circ \rangle) = \langle \phi_{{}^\varsigma\pi}^\circ, \phi_{{}^\varsigma \pi^\vee}^\circ \rangle_{\varsigma}$. We now denote by ${}^\varsigma I(\cdot,\cdot)$ the local Whittaker functionnal constructed using $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle_{\varsigma}$. Then, one has \cite[Formula (3.11)]{Che25} that:
$$
\varsigma(I(\phi, \phi')) = {}^\varsigma I( {}^\varsigma \pi(\ensuremath{\mathrm{diag}}(a_{\varsigma},1, a_{\varsigma}^{-1})({}^\varsigma \phi), {}^\varsigma \pi^\vee(\ensuremath{\mathrm{diag}}(a_{\varsigma},1, a_{\varsigma}^{-1}))({}^\varsigma \phi'))
$$
for any $\phi \in \pi$ and $\phi' \in \pi^\vee$. Consequently, we see that $\varsigma(I(\phi_{\pi}^\circ, \phi_{\pi^\vee}^\circ)) = {}^\varsigma I(\phi_{{}^\varsigma\pi}^\circ, \phi_{{}^\varsigma \pi^\vee}^\circ)$. Finally, since $\varsigma(L(\pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)) = L({}^\varsigma \pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)$, we get:
$$
\varsigma \left( I^\natural(\phi_{\pi}^\circ, \phi_{\pi^\vee}^\circ) \cdot L(\pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1) \right) = I^\natural(\phi_{{}^\varsigma\pi}^\circ, \phi_{{}^\varsigma \pi^\vee}^\circ) \cdot L({}^\varsigma \pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)
$$
and the lemma follows.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{non-endo_lambda_rank}
Let $\pi \in \mathrm{Coh}(U,\mu,K_f)$ which is non-endoscopic. Let $\lambda_\pi : h^S \to \mathcal{O}$ be the Hecke eigenstystem associated to $\pi$. Assume that the space of ${K_f}$-fixed vectors in $\pi_f$ is $1$-dimensional. Then:

$$
In particular, the top ($q=t$) and bottom ($q=b$) cuspidal cohomology groups are Hecke modules of $\lambda_\pi$-rank $1$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Write $\Pi = \otimes_v \Pi_v$ for the $L$-packet of $\pi$. If $v$ is an archimedean place of $F$, then $U_v$ is split and thus $\# \Pi_v = 1$. Moreover, since $\pi$ is non-endoscopic, it follows from \cite[Theorem 13.1.1.(1)]{ARU3} that $\# \Pi_v = 1$ when $v$ is a finite place. Hence $\Pi$ is the singleton $\{ \pi \}$.


Let $\sigma \in \mathrm{Coh}(U,\mu,K_f)$ and let $\lambda_\sigma : h^S \to \mathcal{O}$ be the associated Hecke eigensystem. Let $\Sigma = \otimes_v \Sigma_v$ denote the $L$-packet of $\sigma$. Assume that:

i.e that $\lambda_\sigma(T) = \lambda_\pi(T)$ for all $T \in h^S$. This implies that $\Sigma_v = \Pi_v$ for all $v \notin S \cup S_\infty(F)$. The lemma then follows from (\ref{g-K_dim}).

\label{SBC_surjectivity}

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The proof uses a similar argument that Carayol in \cite{Carayol}. More precisely, similarly to \cite[Section 2.2]{Carayol}, we can construct a Galois representation $\rho: \ensuremath{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{E}/E) \to \ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(\mathrm{\mathbf{T}})$ with coefficient in $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}} = \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_E$ such that if $w \notin S_\Pi \sqcup S_p(E)$ is a finite place of $E$, we have:
$$
\det(X I_n - \rho(\ensuremath{\mathrm{Frob}}_{w})) = \sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i q_w^{i(i-1)/2} T_{w,i} X^{n-i}
$$
Alternatively, one can deduce the existence of this representation from \cite[Corollary 5.4.4]{Scholze15}. Then for all $w \in S_{E,s} - (S_\Pi \sqcup S_p(E))$, the polynomial $\det(X I_n - \rho(\ensuremath{\mathrm{Frob}}_{w}))$ has coefficients in $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_s := \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{E,s}$. But this set of prime ideals is of density $1$, so by the Chebotarev's density theorem, $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{E}/E)$ is topologicaly generated by $\{ \ensuremath{\mathrm{Frob}}_{w}, \,w \in S_{E,s} - (S_\Pi \sqcup S_p(E)) \}$. In particular $T_{w,i} \in \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_s$, for all $w \notin S_\Pi \sqcup S_p(E)$ and $0 \leq i \leq n$. Since these operators generate $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}$, this proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{spherical_ramified_factor} Assume that $E_w/\mathbb{Q}_v$ is ramified and let $\psi_w^\circ$ be a non-trivial unramified additive character of $E_w$ which is trivial on $\mathbb{Q}_v$. Then:
$$
\Psi_v(s,\Phi_v,W_{\Pi_w}^\circ) = L(\Pi_v, \mathrm{As},s)
$$
where $W_{\Pi_w}^\circ \in \mathcal{W}(\Pi_w, \psi_w^\circ)$ is the spherical Whittaker function of the Whittaker model of $\Pi_w$ with respect to $\psi_w^\circ$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We give a proof which is similar to the proof of Jacquet-Shalika (when $v$ is split) and Flicker (when $v$ is inert), using Shintani's formula for the diagonal values of the spherical Whittaker functions. To simplify notation, we denote by $L/K$ the extension of local fields $E_w/F_v$, and by $W$ the spherical Whittaker function $W_{\phi,v}:= W_{\Pi_w}^\circ$. Let $\varpi_K$ (resp. $\varpi_L$) be an uniformizer of $K$ (resp. $L$), and let $\delta_{B_n(K)}$ (resp. $\delta_{B_n(L)}$) be the modulus character of $B_n(K)$ (resp. ${B_n(L)}$). If $\lambda = (\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n) \in \mathbb{Z}_n$, $\varpi_K^\lambda \in Z_n(K)$ is defined to be $\mathrm{diag}(\varpi_K^{\lambda_1},\dots,\varpi_K^{\lambda_n})$, and $\varpi_L^\lambda \in Z_n(L)$ is defined similarly. Moreover if $\lambda$ is such that $\lambda_1 \geq \cdots \geq \lambda_n$, $s_\lambda(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ denotes the Schur polynomial associated to $\lambda$. Finally, we define $\alpha = (\alpha_i)_{i=1}^n$ to be the $n$-tuple of complex numbers $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ such that the standard $L$-function of $\Pi_w$ writes:
$$
L(\Pi_w,s) = \prod_{i=1}^n (1-\alpha_i q^{-s})^{-1}
$$
where $q = \# (\mathcal{O}_L/(\varpi_L))$. Then Shintani's formula \cite[Theorem]{Shintani76} is:
$$
W_{\Pi_w}^\circ(\varpi^\lambda) = \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \delta_{B_n(L)}^{1/2}(\varpi^\lambda) s_\lambda(\alpha) & \mbox{if } \lambda_1 \geq \cdots \geq \lambda_n \\
        0 & \mbox{otherwise.}
    \end{array}
\right.
$$

Recall that the Haar measures on $\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(K)$ and $N_n(K)$ are normalized so that $\ensuremath{\mathrm{vol}}(\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_n}(\mathcal{O}_K)) = \ensuremath{\mathrm{vol}}(N_n(\mathcal{O}_K)) = 1$ (since $\Pi$ is unramified at $w$). Similarly to the proof of \cite[Proposition p.305]{Flicker88}, we have that:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_v(W, \Phi_v,s)  &=  \sum_{\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n} W(\varpi_K^\lambda) \delta_{B_n(K)}^{-1}(\varpi_K^\lambda) \Phi_v(0,\dots,0,\varpi_K^{\lambda_n}) |\varpi_K^\lambda|_K^s \\
& =  \sum_{\lambda_n \geq 0} W(\varpi_L^{2\lambda}) \delta_{B_n(K)}(\varpi_K^\lambda) q^{-s\ensuremath{\mathrm{Tr}}\lambda} \\
& =  \sum_{\lambda} \delta_{B_n(L)}^{1/2}(\varpi_L^{2\lambda}) s_{2\lambda}(\alpha) \delta_{B_n(K)}(\varpi_K^\lambda) q^{-s\ensuremath{\mathrm{Tr}}\lambda} \\
\end{aligned}
$$
where the last sum is indexed by the $n$-uples $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ such that $\lambda_1 \geq \dots \geq \lambda_n \geq 0$. One then checks:
$$
\delta_{B_n(L)}^{1/2}(\varpi_L^{2\lambda}) = q^{-\sum_{i=1}^n (n+1-2i)\lambda_i} = \delta_{B_n(K)}(\varpi_K^{\lambda})
$$

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We now prove \ref{SBC_divisibility}, using the congruence number formalism introduced in the last paragraphs. We consider the $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_E$-module $M = H^5_{cusp}(Y_E(K_f),\mathcal{L}_{\mu_E}(\mathcal{O}))_{\mathfrak{m}_\Pi}$, and the linear form $\ensuremath{\mathrm{L}}: M \to \mathcal{O}$ obtained by restricting to $M$ the linear form $\ensuremath{\mathrm{L}}$ defined in paragraph~\ref{linear_forms_SBC}. We begin by situation (I). Since $\Pi$ is self-conjugate, we have that $t \in \mathfrak{m}_\Pi \iff t^\sigma \in \mathfrak{m}_\Pi$, and the action of $\sigma$ on $h_{G_E}(K_f,\mathcal{O})$ induces an action on $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{G_E}= h_{G_E}(K_f,\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}_\Pi}$ and $\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_E$. Then, $M$ also inherits from an action of $\sigma$ and this action is semi-linear, in the sense of \S\ref{congruence_modules_with_an_involution}. We have seen that $\ensuremath{\mathrm{L}}$ is naturally $\sigma$-invariant. \ref{lf_vanishing_SBC} implies that $\ensuremath{\mathrm{L}}$ is cancelled out by $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ker }}(\theta_{SBC})$ where $\theta_{{SBC}}: \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_E \to \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_U$ is the stable base change transfer defined in \S\ref{hecke_SBC}. because by definition of the $\sigma$-periods, the cohomology class $\delta^+_\sigma(W_{\phi_f})/\Omega_5(\Pi,\sigma,+)$ is an $\mathcal{O}$-base of $M_{\lambda_\Pi}[+]$, and the last equality follows from \ref{explicit_linear}.  \\

<!-- theorem-12: body_md -->
\label{divisibilite_unitaire}
Let $\pi$ be a self-dual non-endoscopic cohomological cuspidal automorphic representation of $U_E(\mathbb{A})$ which is globally generic. Assume that $\pi$ is only ramified at split primes. Let $\Pi = \mathrm{SBC}(\pi)$ be its strong stable base change to $\ensuremath{\mathrm{GL}_3}(\mathbb{A}_E)$. Assume that the Galois representation associated with $\Pi$ is residually absolutely irreducible. Assume that the cohomological weight $\mu$ of $\pi$ is $p$-small, that $p \nmid 6N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{n}) h_E(\mathfrak{n})D_E$ and that $p\notin S_\partial$. Then, under the assumption $(H = \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_U)$, we have the following divisibility:
$$
\nu_\pi \cdot \Omega_2(\pi) \cdot \Omega_3(\pi) \;  \mid \; \Omega_5(\Pi,\sigma,-)
$$
where $\nu_\pi := \eta_\pi \cdot \eta_\pi(M_{\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}})[+]^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}$.
<!-- end theorem-12 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
First, from \ref{cn_decomposition}, we have a factorization of congruence numbers:
$$
\eta_{\Pi}(M^*)[\pm] \sim \eta_{\pi}(M^*_T)[\pm] \cdot \eta_{\Pi}^{\#}(M^*)[\pm] 
$$
where $M = H_{cusp}^5(Y_E(K_f), \mathcal{L}_{\mu_E}(\mathcal{O}))_{\mathfrak{m}_\Pi}$. One has that $\eta_{\Pi}(M^*)[\pm] = \eta_{\Pi}(M)[\pm]$ and that $\eta_{\pi}(M_T^*)[\pm]$ divides the Hecke congruence number $\eta_{\pi}$ of $\pi$. Thus we have the following relation of congruence numbers:
\begin{equation}
\label{CN_equality}
\eta_{\Pi}(M)[+] \, \sim \, \nu_\pi^{-1} \cdot \eta_{\pi} \cdot \eta_{\Pi}^\#(M^*)[+]
\end{equation}
where $\nu_\pi := \eta_\pi \cdot \eta_\pi(M_{\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}})[+]^{-1}$ and $M_{\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_\mathbb{Q}}$ is defined in \S\ref{sss_relative}. Then, assumption $(H = \mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_U)$ implies that $\eta_{\pi}(H^3_\mathfrak{m}) \sim \eta_{\pi}$, where $\eta_\pi(H^3_\mathfrak{m})$ is the congruence number of $\pi$ on $H^3_\mathfrak{m}$. Thus, \ref{unitary_adjoint_L_value} (for $F=\mathbb{Q}$) implies that, under our hypothesis on $p$, we have that:
$$
\eta_\pi \sim \frac{\Lambda^{imp}(\pi, \ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)}{\Omega_2(\pi) \cdot \Omega_3(\pi)}
$$
Next, recall from \ref{adjoint_L_value} that we have:
$$
\eta_{\Pi}(M)[+] \sim \frac{\Lambda^{imp}(\Pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)}{\Omega_5(\Pi,\sigma,+)\cdot\Omega_5(\Pi,\sigma,-)}
$$
From these two adjoint $L$-value formulas, from \ref{SBC_divisibility} and from (\ref{CN_equality}), we get the following divisibility:
$$
\frac{\Lambda^{imp}(\Pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},s)}{\Omega_5(\Pi,\sigma,+)\cdot\Omega_5(\Pi,\sigma,-)} \, \mid \, \nu_\pi^{-1} \cdot \frac{\Lambda^{imp}(\pi, \ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},1)}{\Omega_2(\pi) \cdot \Omega_3(\pi)} \times \frac{\Lambda^{imp}(\pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}} \otimes \chi_E,s)}{\Omega_5(\Pi,\sigma,+)}
$$
\ref{divisibilite_unitaire} then follows from the following factorization of completed imprimitive $L$-functions (see (\ref{decomposition_ad_imp})):
$$
\Lambda^{imp}(\Pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},s) = \Lambda^{imp}(\pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}},s) \times \Lambda^{imp}(\pi,\ensuremath{\mathrm{Ad}} \otimes \chi_E,s)
$$
