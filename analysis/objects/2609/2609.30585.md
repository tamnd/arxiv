<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{gap}
The \emph{spectral gap}, $\lambda^{(n)}$, of the infinitesimal generator \eqref{eq:generator} is the smallest positive eigenvalue of $-\ensuremath{\mathcal L}_n$ , and is given by the variational
principle
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:5}
  \lambda^{(n)} : = \inf_{\substack{f:\Omega_n \to {\ensuremath{\mathbb R}}  \\ f \neq \textrm{const}}}\frac{\ensuremath{\mathcal D}_n(f)}{{\rm Var}_{\pi_n}(f)},
\end{align}
where $\ensuremath{\mathcal D}_n(f)= -\pi_n\left(f\ensuremath{\mathcal L}_n f\right)$ is the Dirichlet form of
the process.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The process is well-defined on the infinite lattice, and we denote the associated spectral gap by $\lambda$. 
    It turns out that $\lambda = \inf_n \lambda^{(n)} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \lambda^{(n)}$ \cite{HTbook}*{Proposition 3.11},\cite{CMRT}*{Lemma 2.11, Proposition 2.13}.
    The exponential decay critical parameter is given by $\tilde{q}_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U}) = \inf\{q>0\,:\, \lambda > 0\}$.
    If we define $\tau_0$ to be the first time the origin is updated in the infinite volume process, then the ergodicity critical parameter is given by $q_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U}) = \inf\{q>0\,:\, {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _\pi(\tau_0 <\infty) = 1\}$, where $\pi$ is the infinite volume product Bernoulli measure with parameter $1-q$. 
    For \emph{oriented} KCM $\tilde{q}_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U}) = q_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U})$, see for example \cite{Hartarskybootstrap}*{Corollary 1.8} and \cite{HTbook}*{Section 7.1}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
For $t \;\geqslant\; 0$, let
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:dn}
 d_n(t):=\sup_{\eta\in\Omega_n}\|{\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _\eta(\eta(t) \in \cdot)-\pi_n\|_{\rm{TV}},
\end{align}
where 
\[
\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm{TV}} = \sup_A|\mu(A)-\nu(A)| = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\eta\in\Omega_n}|\mu(\eta)-\nu(\eta)|\,.
\] 
Then, the \emph{total variation mixing time} of the $\ensuremath{\mathcal U}$-KCM on $\Lambda_n$ is defined by
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:tmix}
 t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon):=\inf\{t \;\geqslant\; 0\,:\,d_n(t)\;\leqslant\; \varepsilon\}.
\end{align}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{th:main}
Let $\ensuremath{\mathcal U}$ be an oriented update family and assume $\inf_n \lambda^{(n)} > 0$, which holds for  $q\in(q_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U}),1)$. Then the mixing time of the $\ensuremath{\mathcal U}$-KCM on $\Lambda_n$, with facilitating boundary conditions, exhibits pre-cutoff in linear time. More precisely, there exists a constant $C=C(q,d,\ensuremath{\mathcal U})\;\geqslant\; 1$ such that, for all $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ and all $n$ sufficiently large (depending on $\varepsilon$)
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:main}
    C^{-1} n \;\leqslant\; t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon) \;\leqslant\; C n\,.
\end{align}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Recall, a sequence of Markov chains is said to exhibit pre-cutoff if
   \[
   \sup_{\epsilon \in (0,1/2)}\limsup_{n \to \infty}\frac{t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\epsilon)}{t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(1-\epsilon)} < \infty\,,
   \]
   see for example \cite{Levin2008}. Theorem \ref{th:main} shows that the ratio is bounded above by a constant $C^2$ independent of $\epsilon$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The restriction to $q \;\geqslant\; q_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U})$ is necessary for the result to hold in the North-East model. 
   Indeed, for $q<q_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U})$ the North-East process has a relaxation time, and hence mixing time, which is exponentially large in $n$ \cite{CMRT}*{Theorem 6.16}.
   The same is expected to hold for general nontrivial oriented models.
   A lower bound on the relaxation time should follow by adapting the arguments in \cite{CMRT}, since there exist macroscopic stable regions under the stationary distribution by comparison with the associated bootstrap percolation process \cite{HTbook}*{Chapter 7}.
   The critical case, $q=q_c(\ensuremath{\mathcal U})$, remains an open problem.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:oriented}
The update family $\ensuremath{\mathcal U}$ is \emph{oriented}, and the corresponding KCM is \emph{oriented}, if there exists a direction $v\in S^{d-1}$ such that $\cup_{U\in\ensuremath{\mathcal U}}U \subset {\ensuremath{\mathbb H}} _v$. 
Equivalently, there is a $v\in S^{d-1}$ such that
\[
       \langle u,v\rangle<0 \qquad\text{for every }u\in\ensuremath{\mathcal N}.
\]
For one such $v$, set
\[
   \delta_v:=\min_{u\in\ensuremath{\mathcal N}}\bigl(-\langle u,v\rangle\bigr)>0.
\]
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:killed-contraction}
The lowest eigenvalue of $-H^x$ is uniformly positive. In particular, it is not smaller than $\kappa := \frac{1}{2}( p \wedge q) \lambda > 0$. Hence, for all $f \in L^2(\pi_n)$ and $t\ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercivity}
       -\langle f,H^x f\rangle_{\pi_n}\ge \kappa\|f\|_{2,\pi_n}^2,
       \qquad
       \|Q_t^x\|_{2\to2}\;\leqslant\; e^{-\kappa t}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By homogeneity, fix an eigenfunction $f$ of $-H^x$, such that $\|f\|_{2,\pi_n} = 1$.
   Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to $[Q_t^x f(\eta)]^2$ gives
   \begin{align*}
      \pi_n\left( [Q_t^x f]^2 \right) \;\leqslant\; \max_\eta {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\eta}(\tau_x\ge t)\;\leqslant\; \frac{1}{\min_{\eta}\pi_n(\eta)}{\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}(\tau_x\ge t)\,,
   \end{align*}
   where, in the first inequality, we used stationarity of $\pi_n$.
For $i\in\{0,1\}$, let $\tau_x^{i}$ be the hitting time of the set $\{\eta\,:\, \eta_x = i\}$,  and
observe that $\tau_x \;\leqslant\; \tau_x^0 \vee \tau_x^1$.
Thus, for any $t\ge 0$,
\begin{align*}
   {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}(\tau_x\ge t) 
   &\;\leqslant\; {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}(\tau_x^0\ge t) + {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}(\tau_x^1\ge t).
\end{align*}
By a standard result for reversible Markov chains, see for example \cite{Aldous-Fill}*{Lemma 3.35}, we have
\begin{align*}
   {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}(\tau_x^1\ge t) \;\leqslant\; e^{-\lambda^{(n)}\, p\, t}, \qquad \textrm{and} \qquad {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}(\tau_x^0\ge t) \;\leqslant\; e^{-\lambda^{(n)}\, q\, t}.
\end{align*}
Hence,
\begin{align*}
   \liminf_{t\to\infty} -\frac{1}{t}\log \pi_n\left( [Q_t^x f]^2 \right) \ge  ( p \wedge q) \lambda^{(n)} \ge ( p \wedge q) \lambda >0.
\end{align*}
Since $f$ is chosen to be an eigenfunction of $-H^x$, we have $Q_t^x f = e^{-t \mu} f$ for some eigenvalue $\mu$ of $-H^x$. 
Hence, the lowest eigenvalue of $-H^x$ is at least $\kappa = \frac{1}{2}( p \wedge q) \lambda >0$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:failure-string}
Fix $x_1,\ldots,x_r\in\Lambda_n$ (repeated sites are allowed) and a sequence of non-overlapping time intervals
\[
       0\;\leqslant\; s_1<t_1\;\leqslant\; s_2<t_2\;\leqslant\;\cdots\;\leqslant\; s_r<t_r\,,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:failure-string}
       {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^r \{\text{no legal ring at $x_j$ during }[s_j,t_j)\}\right)
          \;\leqslant\; \exp\left\{-\kappa\sum_{j=1}^r(t_j-s_j)\right\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $P_t=e^{t \ensuremath{\mathcal L}_n}$. The Markov property at the deterministic interval endpoints, and the definition of the killed semigroup give,
\begin{align*}
 {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} _{\pi_n}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^r\{\text{there is no legal ring at $x_j$ during }[s_j,t_j)\} \right) = \\
  =\langle \mathbf{1},P_{s_1}Q_{t_1-s_1}^{x_1}P_{s_2-t_1}Q_{t_2-s_2}^{x_2}
       \cdots P_{s_r-t_{r-1}}Q_{t_r-s_r}^{x_r}\mathbf{1}\rangle_{\pi_n}.
\end{align*}
Since $\|P_t\|_{2\to2}\;\leqslant\;1$, and Proposition \ref{prop:killed-contraction} gives $\|Q_t^x\|_{2\to2}\;\leqslant\; e^{-\kappa t}$, Cauchy--Schwarz and submultiplicativity yield \eqref{eq:failure-string}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:permanent}
Suppose $\sigma \sim \pi_n$ independently of the graphical construction\footnote{To simplify notation, we include this initial independent configuration in ${\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} $.}. 
For each initial configuration $\eta \in \Omega_n$ let $(\eta(t))_{t\;\geqslant\; 0}$ and $(\sigma(t))_{t\;\geqslant\; 0}$ be driven by the same graphical construction. Then, for every $k\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $G_k\subseteq G_{k+1}$;
 \item if $x\in G_k\,$, then $\ensuremath{\mathcal N}_x\subseteq G_k$;
 \item if $x\in G_k\,$, then $\eta_x(t)=\sigma_x(t)$ for every $t\ge ks$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
$(1)$ is immediate by construction.
$(2)$ follows by induction; assume it holds for $G_k$, if $x \in G_{k+1}\setminus G_k$, then $\ensuremath{\mathcal N}_x \subseteq G_k\subseteq G_{k+1}$.
 For $(3)$, fix $\eta \in \Omega_n$ an arbitrary initial configuration, suppose $(3)$ holds for $G_k$ and fix $x\in G_{k+1}\setminus G_k$. 
 By construction, $\ensuremath{\mathcal N}_x\subseteq G_k$, so every site $y\in\ensuremath{\mathcal N}_x$ satisfies $\eta_y(t)=\sigma_y(t)$ for every $t\ge ks$.
 Therefore, the constraints under $(\eta(t))_{t\;\geqslant\; 0}$ and $(\sigma(t))_{t\;\geqslant\; 0}$ at $x$ agree throughout $I_k$. 
 At the first legal ring of $(\sigma(t))_{t\;\geqslant\; 0}$ at $x$ in $I_k$, the same ring is legal for $(\eta(t))_{t\;\geqslant\; 0}$, and the two chains adopt the same spin at $x$. 
 All subsequent rings at $x$ are simultaneously legal or not in both chains, so equality at $x$ is conserved. 
 Sites already in $G_k$ remain coupled by induction.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:witness}
For every $K\ge1$ and $x\in\Lambda_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness-inclusion}
             \{x\notin G_K\}\subseteq
                \bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_K(x)}F_\gamma.
\end{equation}
Moreover every $\gamma\in\Gamma_K(x)$ has at most $R_n$ spatial moves and therefore at least $K-R_n$ stays.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix an outcome with $x\notin G_K$ and set $z_K=x$. 
We will construct a witness path $\gamma=(z_0,\ldots,z_K)\in\Gamma_K(x)$ backwards in time such that the corresponding failure event $F_\gamma$ occurs.
We proceed by induction.
Fix $j+1 \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ and suppose $z_{j+1}\notin G_{j+1}$.
Since $G_j\subseteq G_{j+1}$, also $z_{j+1}\notin G_j$. Fix once and for all an ordering of $\ensuremath{\mathcal N}$. 
If there is $u\in\ensuremath{\mathcal N}$ such that $z_{j+1}+u\in\Lambda_n\setminus G_j$, choose the first such $u$ and set $z_j=z_{j+1}+u$. If no such move exists, then $\ensuremath{\mathcal N}_{z_{j+1}}\subseteq G_j$. 
Since $z_{j+1}\notin G_{j+1}$, recursion \eqref{eq:Wrecursion} forces the absence of a legal ring at $z_{j+1}$ during $I_j$; set $z_j=z_{j+1}$. Thus each stay creates the corresponding failure event, proving \eqref{eq:witness-inclusion}. The move bound follows from \eqref{eq:Rn} and the preceding argument.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:incomplete}
For every $K\ge R_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incomplete}
       {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} (G_K\ne\Lambda_n)
          \;\leqslant\; n^d (1+N)^K\exp\{-\kappa s(K-R_n)\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For a fixed $x\in \Lambda_n$, Lemma \ref{lem:witness} and a union bound give
\[
      {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} (x\notin G_K)\;\leqslant\;\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_K(x)}{\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} (F_\gamma).
\]
At each backward step there is one stay choice and at most $|\ensuremath{\mathcal N}|$ move choices, so $|\Gamma_K(x)|\;\leqslant\; (1+|\ensuremath{\mathcal N}|)^K$. For a fixed candidate path, the sequence of sites and time intervals in \eqref{eq:Fgamma} are deterministic. By Lemma \ref{lem:failure-string},
\[
        {\ensuremath{\mathbb P}} (F_\gamma)\;\leqslant\; e^{-\kappa s|S(\gamma)|}
             \;\leqslant\; e^{-\kappa s(K-R_n)}.
\]
A union bound over the $n^d$ sites in $\Lambda_n$ proves \eqref{eq:incomplete}.
