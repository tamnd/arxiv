<!-- repeatedmaintheorem-1: body_md -->
The group \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) is exotic, and
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)}=\overline{\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)}=\operatorname{GL}(Q).
\]
<!-- end repeatedmaintheorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let \(R\) be a unital ring. A \emph{Sylvester matrix rank function}
on \(R\) is an \(\mathbb R_{\geq0}\)-valued function \(\operatorname{rk}\) on
all finite rectangular matrices over \(R\), satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(SM\arabic*)]
\item \(\operatorname{rk}(0)=0\) and \(\operatorname{rk}(1_R)=1\);
\item \(\operatorname{rk}(AB)\leq\min\Set*{\operatorname{rk}(A),\operatorname{rk}(B)}\) for compatible matrices;
\item \(\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{diag}(A,B)=\operatorname{rk}(A)+\operatorname{rk}(B)\);
\item \(\operatorname{rk}\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&B\end{pmatrix}\geq\operatorname{rk}(A)+\operatorname{rk}(B)\)
for matrices of the indicated sizes.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A \emph{representation} of a topological group \(G\) on
\(\mathcal H\) is a homomorphism \(\pi:G\to U(\mathcal H)\)
that is \emph{strongly continuous}: the map \(g\mapsto\pi(g)\xi\) is
continuous for each \(\xi\in\mathcal H\).
For a discrete group this continuity condition is automatic.
In particular,
\(\pi(gh)=\pi(g)\pi(h)\) and
\(\pi(g^{-1})=\pi(g)^{-1}=\pi(g)^*\).
The representation is \emph{trivial} if
\(\pi(g)=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H}\) for every \(g\in G\).
The group \(G\) is \emph{exotic} if every representation
of \(G\) is trivial.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:kazhdan-separation}
Let \(G\) be a discrete group and let \((S,\varepsilon)\) be a Kazhdan pair.
Then, for every unitary representation
\(\pi:G\to U(\mathcal H)\) and any \(\xi\in\mathcal H\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kazhdan-separation}
 \sup_{g\in G}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|
 \leq
 \frac{2}{\varepsilon}
 \sup_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\xi-\xi\|.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Put \(\eta=(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H}-P_G)\xi\).
If \(\eta=0\), the assertion is immediate. Otherwise the restriction of \(\pi\) to
\((\mathcal H^G)^\perp\) has no nonzero invariant vectors, so the
Kazhdan assumption gives
\[
 \varepsilon\|\eta\|
 \leq
 \sup_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\eta-\eta\|
 =
 \sup_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\xi-\xi\|.
\]
On the other hand, for every \(g\in G\),
\[
 \|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|
 =
 \|\pi(g)\eta-\eta\|
 \leq2\|\eta\|.
\]
Combining the two inequalities proves the claim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:integer-matrices-kazhdan}
  Let \(r\geq1\), let \(G=(\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z),+)\) and let \(V=\{0,1\}^r\).
  For \(v\in V\), set \(H_v = \Set*{
   vw^{\mathsf T}
    
  \nonscript\:\delimsize\vert\allowbreak\nonscript\:\mathopen{} 

   w\in\mathbb Z^r
 }\).
    
      
    
         
                   
           
                
     
    
Then
\[
 \rho(\{H_v\}_{v\in V},G)\leq\frac12.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 S=\bigcup_{v\in V}H_v
 =
 \Set*{
   vw^{\mathsf T}
    
  \nonscript\:\delimsize\vert\allowbreak\nonscript\:\mathopen{} 

   v\in\{0,1\}^r,\;
   w\in\mathbb Z^r
 },
\]
then \((S,1)\) is a Kazhdan pair for \(G\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For \(1\leq i,j\leq r\), we have
  \[
    e_{ij}=e_i e_j^{\mathsf T}\in H_{e_i}.
  \]
Thus the subgroups \(H_v\), \(v\in V\), generate \(G\).

Let \(\pi:G\to U(\mathcal H)\) belong to \(\operatorname{Rep}_0(G)\).
For \(v\in V\), write \(P_v=P_{H_v}\) for the orthogonal
projection onto \(\mathcal H^{H_v}\). We shall prove that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-projection-average}
 \frac1{2^r}\sum_{v\in V}
 \|P_v\xi\|^2
 \leq
 \frac12\|\xi\|^2
 \qquad(\xi\in\mathcal H).
\end{equation}

Write \(\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T)\) for the additive group of
\(r\times r\) matrices with entries in \(\mathbb T\).
Identify the Pontryagin dual of \(G\) with this group,
using the convention from \Cref{sec:unitary-preliminaries}:
\[
 \chi_N(\Theta)
 =\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}\sum_{j,k=1}^r n_{jk}\theta_{jk}\right),
\]
where \(N=(n_{jk})\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z)\) and
\(\Theta=(\theta_{jk})\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T)\).
For \(v\in V\) and \(w\in\mathbb Z^r\), \(\chi_{vw^{\mathsf T}}(\Theta)=\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\,v^{\mathsf T}\Theta w)\).
Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_v^\perp
 &=\Set*{\Theta\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T) 
  \nonscript\:\delimsize\vert\allowbreak\nonscript\:\mathopen{} 

                 v^{\mathsf T}\Theta w=0\text{ for every }w\in\mathbb Z^r}\\
 &=\Set*{\Theta\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T) 
  \nonscript\:\delimsize\vert\allowbreak\nonscript\:\mathopen{} 
 v^{\mathsf T}\Theta=0}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last equality follows by taking \(w\) to be each standard
basis vector; the equalities are in \(\mathbb T\) and
\(\mathbb T^{1\times r}\), respectively.

Let \(\nu_\xi\) be the scalar spectral measure associated with
\(\xi\).
By \eqref{eq:spectral-fixed-space},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-fixed-projection}
 \|P_v\xi\|^2=\nu_\xi(H_v^\perp).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
 \nu_\xi(\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T))=\|\xi\|^2,
 \qquad
 \nu_\xi(\{0\})=0,
\]
since \(\pi\) has no nonzero \(G\)-invariant vectors.

For \(\Theta\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T)\), let
\[
 V_\Theta
 =
 \Set*{
   v\in V
    
  \nonscript\:\delimsize\vert\allowbreak\nonscript\:\mathopen{} 

   v^{\mathsf T}\Theta=0
 }.
\]
We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cube-half}
 |V_\Theta|\leq2^{r-1}
 \qquad(\Theta\neq0).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if \(\Theta\neq0\), choose \(i\) such that \(e_i^{\mathsf T}\Theta\neq0\).
Partition \(V\) into the \(2^{r-1}\) pairs
\[
 \{v,v+e_i\},
 \qquad v_i=0.
\]
At most one member of each pair belongs to \(V_\Theta\), since
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Theta=(v+e_i)^{\mathsf T}\Theta=0 \Longrightarrow e_i^{\mathsf T}\Theta=0,
\]
contrary to the choice of \(i\).
This proves \eqref{eq:cube-half}.

Using \eqref{eq:matrix-fixed-projection} and finite summation,
we obtain
\[
\frac1{2^r}\sum_{v\in V}\|P_v\xi\|^2
 =
 \frac1{2^r}\sum_{v\in V}\nu_\xi(H_v^\perp)=
 \int_{\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T)}
 \frac{|V_\Theta|}{2^r}\,d\nu_\xi(\Theta).
\]
Since \(\nu_\xi(\{0\})=0\), \eqref{eq:cube-half} gives
\[
 \frac1{2^r}\sum_{v\in V}\|P_v\xi\|^2
 \leq
 \frac12
 \nu_\xi(\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T)\setminus\{0\})
 =
 \frac12\|\xi\|^2.
\]
By \eqref{eq:codistance-projection} and the arbitrariness of
\(\pi\in\operatorname{Rep}_0(G)\), this proves
\[
 \rho(\{H_v\}_{v\in V},G)\leq\frac12.
\]

Finally, since the subgroups \(H_v\) generate \(G\),
\eqref{eq:codistance-kazhdan} yields
\[
 \kappa(G,S)
 \geq
 \sqrt{2\left(1-\frac12\right)}=1.
\]
Therefore \((S,1)\) is a Kazhdan pair.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:integer-matrix-displacement}
  Let \(r\geq1\), let \(\sigma:(\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z),+)\longrightarrow U(\mathcal H)\)
  be a unitary representation.
  Then, for every \(\xi\in\mathcal H\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-conclusion}
 \sup_{N\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z)}
 \|\sigma(N)\xi-\xi\|
 \leq
 2
 \sup_{\substack{v\in\{0,1\}^r\\ w\in\mathbb Z^r}}
 \|\sigma(vw^{\mathsf T})\xi-\xi\|.
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->

the Kazhdan pair in \Cref{prop:integer-matrices-kazhdan}.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:direct-averaging}
The Kazhdan pair in \Cref{prop:integer-matrices-kazhdan} can
also be obtained directly by averaging. Use \(G,V,S\) and
\(V_\Theta\) as in the proposition and its proof, and put
\(F_M=\{-M,\ldots,M\}^r\) for positive integers \(M\).
For \(\Theta\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T)\) and \(v\in V\), the product
of the one-dimensional character averages gives
\[
 \frac1{|F_M|}\sum_{w\in F_M}\overline{\chi_{vw^{\mathsf T}}(\Theta)}
 \longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
  1,&v^{\mathsf T}\Theta=0,\\
  0,&v^{\mathsf T}\Theta\neq0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Let \(\pi:G\to U(\mathcal H)\) have no nonzero invariant
vectors, and let \(\eta\in\mathcal H\). Its spectral measure
satisfies \(\nu_\eta(\{0\})=0\) and
\(\nu_\eta(\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T))=\|\eta\|^2\).
Using \(|z-1|^2=2-2\operatorname{Re} z\) for \(|z|=1\), the spectral
formula and dominated convergence give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac1{2^r|F_M|}
   \sum_{v\in V}\sum_{w\in F_M}
   \|\pi(vw^{\mathsf T})\eta-\eta\|^2\\
 &\qquad=2\int_{\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb T)}
       \left(1-\frac{|V_\Theta|}{2^r}\right)\,d\nu_\eta(\Theta)
 \geq\|\eta\|^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The averaged squared character differences are bounded by \(4\),
and the last inequality follows from \eqref{eq:cube-half}.
Each finite average is at most
\(\sup_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\eta-\eta\|^2\), even though repeated
matrices may occur in the sum. Hence
\[
 \|\eta\|\leq\sup_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\eta-\eta\|.
\]
Since this holds for every \(\pi\in\operatorname{Rep}_0(G)\), \((S,1)\) is a Kazhdan pair.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:roots}
Every strongly continuous unitary representation of
\(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) is trivial on
\(\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)\).
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let \(\pi:\operatorname{GL}(Q)\to U(\mathcal H)\) be a strongly continuous
unitary representation. Fix \(k\) with \(n=n_k\geq2\), distinct
indices \(i,j\in\Set{1,\ldots,n}\), and \(a\in R\), and let
\[
 u=1_n+ae_{ij}\in\operatorname{E}_n(R).
\]
It suffices to prove that \(\pi(\iota_k(u))=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H}\),
since these elements generate \(\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)\).

For each \(\ell>k\), set \(r=n_\ell/n\) and define the additive map \(T_r:\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{M}_{nr}(R)\) by
\[
 T_r(N)=N\otimes(ae_{ij}).
\]
Since \(i\ne j\), we have \((ae_{ij})^2=0\). Integer coefficients
are central in \(R\), so for all \(N,N'\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z)\),
\[
 T_r(N)T_r(N')=NN'\otimes(ae_{ij})^2=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 (1_{nr}+T_r(N))(1_{nr}+T_r(N'))=1_{nr}+T_r(N+N'),
\]
and \(1_{nr}+T_r(N)\) is invertible with inverse
\(1_{nr}-T_r(N)\).
Hence
\[
\sigma_r(N)=\pi\bigl(\iota_\ell(1_{nr}+T_r(N))\bigr).
\]
defines a unitary representation of the discrete additive group \(M_r(\mathbb Z)\).

For \(v,w\in\mathbb Z^r\), the factorization
\[
 T_r(vw^{\mathsf T})
 =(v\otimes ae_i)(w^{\mathsf T}\otimes e_j^{\mathsf T})
\]
expresses this matrix as the product of an \(nr\times1\) column
and a \(1\times nr\) row. The Sylvester rank axioms therefore give \(\operatorname{rk}(T_r(vw^{\mathsf T}))\leq1\), and hence
\[
 d\bigl(\iota_\ell(1_{nr}+T_r(vw^{\mathsf T})),1_Q\bigr)
 =\operatorname{rk}_\mu\bigl(\iota_\ell(T_r(vw^{\mathsf T}))\bigr)=\operatorname{rk}_{nr}(T_r(vw^{\mathsf T}))\leq\frac1{nr}.
\]
This bound is uniform in \(v,w\), regardless of their integer entries.

Fix \(\xi\in\mathcal H\).
For \(t>0\), set
\[
 \beta_\xi(t)=\sup\Set*{\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\| 
  \nonscript\:\delimsize\vert\allowbreak\nonscript\:\mathopen{} 

             g\in\operatorname{GL}(Q),\ d(g,1_Q)\leq t}.
\]
Unitarity gives \(0\leq\beta_\xi(t)\leq2\|\xi\|\), and strong
continuity at the identity implies \(\beta_\xi(t)\to0\) as
\(t\downarrow0\). The preceding rank bound gives
\[
 \|\sigma_r(vw^{\mathsf T})\xi-\xi\|
 \leq\beta_\xi\bigl((nr)^{-1}\bigr)
 \qquad(v,w\in\mathbb Z^r).
\]
Applying \Cref{cor:integer-matrix-displacement} to \(\sigma_r\) therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{N\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z)}\|\sigma_r(N)\xi-\xi\|
 \leq2\beta_\xi\bigl((nr)^{-1}\bigr).
\]
On the other hand, evaluating at \(1_r\) gives the image of \(u\)
under \(\phi_{\ell,k}\):
\[
 1_{nr}+T_r(1_r)=1_r\otimes(1_n+ae_{ij})
 =\phi_{\ell,k}(u).
\]
By \eqref{eq:canonical-compatibility},
\[
 \sigma_r(1_r)=\pi\bigl(\iota_\ell(\phi_{\ell,k}(u))\bigr)
 =\pi(\iota_k(u)).
\]
Since \(nr=n_\ell\to\infty\), we conclude that
\[
 \|\pi(\iota_k(u))\xi-\xi\|
 \leq2\beta_\xi(n_\ell^{-1})\longrightarrow0.
\]
The left-hand side is independent of \(\ell\), so it is zero.
As \(\xi\) was arbitrary, \(\pi(\iota_k(u))=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H}\).
This proves that \(\pi\) is trivial on \(\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)\).

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:correction}
  Let \((R,\operatorname{rk})\) be a rank ring.
For \(X,Y\in\operatorname{M}_n(R)\) and integer \(r\geq2\), there is
\(U\in\operatorname{E}_{nr}(R)\) with
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_{nr}(1_r\otimes X-U)
 \leq2(1-1/r)\operatorname{rk}_n(1_n-XY)+\frac1r.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The identities
  \begin{align*}
    \begin{pmatrix}1_n&0\\-Y&1_n\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1_n&X\\Y&1_n\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1_n&-X\\0&1_n\end{pmatrix}
    & =\operatorname{diag}(1_n,1_n-YX),\\
    \begin{pmatrix}1_n&-X\\0&1_n\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1_n&X\\Y&1_n\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1_n&0\\-Y&1_n\end{pmatrix}
 & =\operatorname{diag}(1_n-XY,1_n)
  \end{align*}
give, by invariance under elementary multiplication and block additivity,
\[
 \operatorname{rk}(1_n-XY)=\operatorname{rk}(1_n-YX).
\]
Consider the elementary block product
\[
 W=
 \begin{pmatrix}1_n&X\\0&1_n\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1_n&0\\-Y&1_n\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1_n&X\\0&1_n\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-1_n\\1_n&0\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}2X-XYX&XY-1_n\\1_n-YX&Y\end{pmatrix}\in \operatorname{E}_{2n}(R).
\]
Each upper block matrix is a product of the elementary matrices
\(1_{2n}+x_{ij}e_{i,n+j}\); the lower block matrix is treated in
the same way, using the entries of \(-Y\).
The off-diagonal block matrix also belongs to \(\operatorname{E}_{2n}(R)\), since
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-1_n\\1_n&0\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1_n&-1_n\\0&1_n\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}1_n&0\\1_n&1_n\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}1_n&-1_n\\0&1_n\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We have
\[
 W-\operatorname{diag}(X,Y)
 =\begin{pmatrix}X-XYX&XY-1_n\\1_n-YX&0\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1_n-XY&0\\0&1_n-YX\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}X&-1_n\\1_n&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
the product inequality and block additivity give
\[
 \operatorname{rk}(W-\operatorname{diag}(X,Y))
 \leq\operatorname{rk}(1_n-XY)+\operatorname{rk}(1_n-YX)=2\operatorname{rk}(1_n-XY).
\]
For \(1\leq h<r\),
                                
                          
define
\begingroup
\setlength{\arraycolsep}{4pt}
\[
 W_h=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1_{(h-1)n}&0&0&0\\
  0&2X-XYX&0&XY-1_n\\
  0&0&1_{(r-h-1)n}&0\\
  0&1_n-YX&0&Y
 \end{pmatrix},\quad
 D_h=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1_{(h-1)n}&0&0&0\\
  0&X&0&0\\
  0&0&1_{(r-h-1)n}&0\\
  0&0&0&Y
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
\endgroup
Each zero block has the size determined by its block row and column.
Blocks of size zero are omitted when \(h=1\) or \(h=r-1\).
The elementary factorization of \(W\), applied to block indices
\(h,r\), gives \(W_h\in\operatorname{E}_{nr}(R)\).
Moreover,
\[
 W_h-D_h = \begin{pmatrix}
  0_{(h-1)n}&0&0&0\\
  0&X-XYX&0&XY-1_n\\
  0&0&0_{(r-h-1)n}&0\\
  0&1_n-YX&0&0_n
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \operatorname{rk}(W_h-D_h)=\operatorname{rk}(W-\operatorname{diag}(X,Y))\leq2\operatorname{rk}(1_n-XY).
\]
Set \(U=W_1\cdots W_{r-1}\), which belongs to \(\operatorname{E}_{nr}(R)\).
The identity
\[
 U-D_1\cdots D_{r-1}
 =\sum_{h=1}^{r-1}W_1\cdots W_{h-1}(W_h-D_h)D_{h+1}\cdots D_{r-1}
\]
is valid in this order over a noncommutative ring, with empty products
equal to the identity.
Rank subadditivity and the product inequality give
\[
 \operatorname{rk}(U-D_1\cdots D_{r-1})
 \leq\sum_{h=1}^{r-1}\operatorname{rk}(W_h-D_h)
 \leq2(r-1)\operatorname{rk}(1_n-XY).
\]
Finally,
\[
 D_1\cdots D_{r-1}
 =\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{X,\ldots,X}_{r-1\text{ copies}},Y^{r-1}),
\]
so
\[
 \operatorname{rk}(1_r\otimes X-D_1\cdots D_{r-1})
 =\operatorname{rk}(X-Y^{r-1})\leq n.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{rk}_{nr}(1_r\otimes X-U)
 &\leq\frac{\operatorname{rk}(1_r\otimes X-D_1\cdots D_{r-1})
                +\operatorname{rk}(D_1\cdots D_{r-1}-U)}{nr}\\
 &\leq\frac{n+2(r-1)\operatorname{rk}(1_n-XY)}{nr}\\
 &=2(1-1/r)\operatorname{rk}_n(1_n-XY)+\frac1r.
 \end{aligned}
\]

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:density}
The subgroups \(\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)\) and \(\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)\) are dense in
\(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\). More precisely,
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)}
 =\overline{\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)}=\operatorname{GL}(Q),
\]
where closures are taken in the rank metric on \(Q\).
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Fix \(g\in\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\).
By density of \(\bigcup_{k\geq0}\iota_k(\operatorname{M}_{n_k}(R))\subset Q\),
choose \(k\geq0\), put \(n=n_k\), and take
\(X,Y\in\operatorname{M}_n(R)\), writing
\(x=\iota_k(X)\) and \(y=\iota_k(Y)\), such that
\[
 d(g,x)<\varepsilon,\qquad d(g^{-1},y)<\varepsilon.
\]
The identity
\[
 1_Q-xy=(g-x)g^{-1}+x(g^{-1}-y)
\]
and \eqref{eq:canonical-rank} give
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_n(1_n-XY)
 =\operatorname{rk}_\mu(1_Q-xy)
 \leq d(g,x)+d(g^{-1},y)<2\varepsilon.
\]

Since \(\mu\) is unbounded, choose \(\ell>k\) such that
\(r=n_\ell/n\geq2\) and \(1/r\leq\varepsilon\).
By \Cref{lem:correction}, there is \(U\in\operatorname{E}_{nr}(R)\) with
\(\operatorname{rk}_{nr}(1_r\otimes X-U)\leq5\varepsilon\).

Now \(\iota_\ell(U)\in\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)\), and
\(\iota_\ell(1_r\otimes X)=\iota_k(X)=x\). Hence
\[
 d(g,\iota_\ell(U))
 \leq d(g,x)+d(x,\iota_\ell(U))=d(g,x)+\operatorname{rk}_{nr}(1_r\otimes X-U)
 <6\varepsilon.
\]
As \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary,
\(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)}\).

For the reverse inclusion, let \((u_j)\) be a sequence in
\(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) converging to \(u\in Q\). Multiplication by units
preserves rank, so
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d(u_j^{-1},u_m^{-1})
 &=\operatorname{rk}_\mu\bigl(u_j^{-1}(u_m-u_j)u_m^{-1}\bigr)\\
 &=\operatorname{rk}_\mu(u_m-u_j)=d(u_j,u_m)\longrightarrow0
 \quad(j,m\to\infty).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \((u_j^{-1})\) is Cauchy. Completeness of \(Q\) gives
\(v\in Q\) with \(u_j^{-1}\to v\), and continuity of multiplication gives
\[
 uv=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_ju_j^{-1}=1_Q,\qquad
 vu=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_j^{-1}u_j=1_Q.
\]
Hence \(u\in\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) with \(u^{-1}=v\), proving that
\(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) is closed in \(Q\). Since
\(\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(Q)\), it follows that
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{E}_\mu(R)}\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(Q).
\]
Together with the first inclusion, this proves the assertion.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:additive}
The additive group \((Q,+)\) is exotic.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let \(\pi:(Q,+)\to U(\mathcal H)\) be a representation.
Fix \(k\geq0\), put \(n=n_k\), and take \(X\in\operatorname{M}_n(R)\).
For \(\ell>k\), put \(r=n_\ell/n\) and define
\[
 \sigma_r:(\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z),+)\longrightarrow U(\mathcal H),
 \qquad \sigma_r(N)=\pi\bigl(\iota_\ell(N\otimes X)\bigr).
\]
This is a representation of the discrete additive group
\((\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z),+)\): for \(N=(n_{pq})\in\operatorname{M}_r(\mathbb Z)\),
\(N\otimes X=(n_{pq}X)_{p,q}\) uses only integer scalar multiples.
For \(v,w\in\mathbb Z^r\), the factorization
\[
 (vw^{\mathsf T})\otimes X
   =(v\otimes 1_n)X(w^{\mathsf T}\otimes 1_n)
\]
and \eqref{eq:canonical-rank} give
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_\mu\bigl(\iota_\ell((vw^{\mathsf T})\otimes X)\bigr)
 =\operatorname{rk}_{nr}((vw^{\mathsf T})\otimes X)
 \leq\frac{\operatorname{rk}(X)}{nr}\leq\frac1r.
\]
For a fixed \(\xi\in\mathcal H\) and \(t>0\), put
\[
 \gamma_\xi(t)=\sup\Set*{\|\pi(x)\xi-\xi\| 
  \nonscript\:\delimsize\vert\allowbreak\nonscript\:\mathopen{} 
 x\in Q,\ \operatorname{rk}_\mu(x)\leq t}.
\]
Strong continuity at zero gives \(\gamma_\xi(t)\to0\) as
\(t\downarrow0\), and the preceding rank bound gives
\[
 \sup_{\substack{v\in\{0,1\}^r\\w\in\mathbb Z^r}}
       \|\sigma_r(vw^{\mathsf T})\xi-\xi\|
 \leq\gamma_\xi(1/r).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \iota_\ell(1_r\otimes X)
 =\iota_\ell(\phi_{\ell,k}(X))=\iota_k(X).
\]
Thus \Cref{cor:integer-matrix-displacement} yields
\[
 \|\pi(\iota_k(X))\xi-\xi\|
 =\|\sigma_r(1_r)\xi-\xi\|
 \leq2\gamma_\xi(1/r)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here \(\ell\to\infty\), so \(r=n_\ell/n\to\infty\), whereas
the left side is independent of \(\ell\). It is therefore zero
for every \(\xi\), proving \(\pi(\iota_k(X))=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H}\).
The union of the images \(\iota_k(\operatorname{M}_{n_k}(R))\) is dense in
\(Q\), so the representation is trivial.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:small-subgroup-width}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\) and every positive integer
\(m>\varepsilon^{-1}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-subgroup-width}
 \operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)\subseteq T(B_\varepsilon)^m.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Fix \(g=\iota_k(u)\) with \(u\in\operatorname{GL}_{n_{k}}(R)\).
Since \(1/m<\varepsilon\) and \(n_\ell/n_k\to\infty\), choose
\(\ell\geq k\) such that \(r=n_\ell/n_{k}\geq m\) and
\[
 \frac1m+\frac1r<\varepsilon.
\]
Partition \(\{1,\ldots,r\}\) into nonempty sets
\(I_1,\ldots,I_m\) with \(|I_s|\leq\lceil r/m\rceil\).
In \(\operatorname{M}_{rn_{k}}(R)\), let \(p_s\) be the diagonal block
idempotent with block \(1_{n_{k}}\) at positions in \(I_s\) and
block \(0_{n_{k}}\) elsewhere. Set
\[
 A=1_r\otimes u,\qquad
 v_s=1_{rn_k}-p_s+p_sAp_s.
\]
The matrix \(A\) commutes with each \(p_s\), and its inverse is
\(1_r\otimes u^{-1}\). Hence
\[
 (p_sAp_s)(p_sA^{-1}p_s)
 =(p_sA^{-1}p_s)(p_sAp_s)=p_s.
\]
Thus \(p_sAp_s\) is a unit in the corner with identity \(p_s\),
and \(v_s\) is a unit in \(\operatorname{M}_{rn_k}(R)\).

Put \(e_s=\iota_\ell(p_s)\).
These are idempotents of \(Q\).
Applying \(\iota_\ell\) to \(p_sAp_s\) and \(p_sA^{-1}p_s\) shows that
\(\iota_\ell(p_sAp_s)\) is invertible in \(e_sQe_s\), with inverse
\(\iota_\ell(p_sA^{-1}p_s)\). In particular,
\[
 \iota_\ell(v_s)\in\Gamma_Q(e_s).
\]
This argument does not require \(\iota_\ell\) to be injective.
By block additivity and \eqref{eq:canonical-rank},
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_\mu(e_s)
 =\frac{\operatorname{rk}(p_s)}{rn_{k}}
 =\frac{|I_s|n_{k}}{n_\ell}
 =\frac{|I_s|}{r}
 \leq\frac{\lceil r/m\rceil}{r}
 \leq\frac1m+\frac1r<\varepsilon.
\]
                                                                
                                                          
The bound now gives
\(\Gamma_Q(e_s)\subseteq B_\varepsilon\), so
\(\iota_\ell(v_s)\in T(B_\varepsilon)\).
The matrices \(v_s\) act on disjoint groups of blocks; their
product is exactly \(A\). Therefore
\[
 g=\iota_\ell(1_r\otimes u)
   =\prod_{s=1}^m\iota_\ell(v_s)\in T(B_\varepsilon)^m.
\]
The choices of \(m\) and \(\varepsilon\) were independent of
\(k\) and \(u\), proving \eqref{eq:small-subgroup-width}.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:bourbaki}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the least positive integer \(N\) such that
\(\operatorname{GL}(Q)=B_\varepsilon^N\) is
\[
 N=\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor+1.
\]
In particular, \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) is Bourbaki bounded.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The upper bound follows by taking
\(m=\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor+1\) in \eqref{eq:ball-width}.

For optimality, the integer matrices
\[
 A_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
satisfy
\(\det A_j=\det(A_j-1_j)=1\) for \(j=2,3\).
For any \(n=n_k\geq2\), write \(n=2a+3b\) with nonnegative
integers \(a,b\), and form the block diagonal matrix \(A\) with
\(a\) copies of \(A_2\) and \(b\) copies of \(A_3\).
Both \(A\) and \(A-1_n\) are invertible over \(\mathbb Z\), hence
over \(R\) via its canonical coefficient map. Consequently
\(g=\iota_k(A)\) is a unit with \(d(g,1_Q)=1\).
Every product of \(N\) elements of \(B_\varepsilon\) has
distance strictly less than \(N\varepsilon\) from \(1_Q\).
Thus \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)=B_\varepsilon^N\) requires \(N\varepsilon>1\),
which proves the asserted minimum. Every identity neighborhood
contains such a ball, so Bourbaki boundedness follows with
\(F=\{1_Q\}\).

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:no-escape}
Every escape function on \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) is zero.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let \(f\) be an escape function with escape neighborhood \(U\).
Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(B_\varepsilon\subseteq U\),
and a positive integer \(m>\varepsilon^{-1}\).
If \(H\leq\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) lies in \(B_\varepsilon\), all positive powers
of each \(h\in H\) lie in \(U\). By
\eqref{eq:escape-definition}, \(f(h)<\delta\) for every
\(\delta>0\), and hence \(f(h)=0\).
Thus \(f\) vanishes on \(T(B_\varepsilon)\), and subadditivity
makes it vanish on \(T(B_\varepsilon)^m\).
By \eqref{eq:ball-width} this set is dense in \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\), so
continuity gives \(f=0\).

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:escape-homomorphisms}
Let \(H\) be a Hausdorff topological group with the escape
property. Every continuous homomorphism \(\psi:\operatorname{GL}(Q)\to H\)
is trivial.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
As in \cite[Lemma~6.7]{SchneiderGeometry}, the pullback of an
escape function \(f\) on \(H\) is an escape function on \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\):
the inverse image under \(\psi\) of an escape neighborhood
for \(f\) verifies \eqref{eq:escape-definition} for \(f\circ\psi\).
Thus \(f\circ\psi=0\) by \Cref{cor:no-escape}.
For each identity neighborhood \(V\) in \(H\), choose \(f\)
with \(f^{-1}([0,1))\subseteq V\). Then \(\psi(\operatorname{GL}(Q))\subseteq V\).
The intersection of all identity neighborhoods in a Hausdorff
group is \(\{1_H\}\), proving the assertion.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:extreme-amenability}
The additive group \((Q,+)\) is extremely amenable.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:unit-extreme-amenability}
The following conditions on \(R\) are equivalent, with the matrix
groups equipped with the discrete topology:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(R\) is locally finite;
\item \(\operatorname{GL}_n(R)\) is amenable for every \(n\geq1\);
\item \(\operatorname{GL}_{n_k}(R)\) is amenable for every \(k\);
\item \(\operatorname{E}_3(R)\) is amenable.
\end{enumerate}
Under these conditions, \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\), endowed with the rank topology,
is extremely amenable.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
If \(R\) is locally finite, any finite subset of \(\operatorname{GL}_n(R)\) is
contained in \(\operatorname{GL}_n(S)\) for some finite unital subring
\(S\subseteq R\): take \(S\) containing the entries of the matrices
and their inverses. Hence \(\operatorname{GL}_n(R)\) is a locally finite group,
so (1) implies (2). Clearly (2) implies (3). Since \((n_k)\) is
unbounded, choose \(k\) with \(n_k>3\). The embedding
\(A\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(A,1_{n_k-3})\) identifies \(\operatorname{E}_3(R)\) with a
subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}_{n_k}(R)\), proving (3) implies (4).

Assume (4), and let \(S\subseteq R\) be a finitely generated
subring containing \(1_R\). The group \(\operatorname{E}_3(S)\) is amenable as a
subgroup of \(\operatorname{E}_3(R)\), and has Property~\textup{(T)} by
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{EJ}. It is therefore finite by
\cite[Theorem~1.1.6]{BekkaHarpeValette}. The injection
\(s\mapsto1_3+se_{12}\) from \(S\) into \(\operatorname{E}_3(S)\) shows that
\(S\) is finite. This proves (4) implies (1).

Under these conditions, \(\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)\) is amenable as a discrete
group, being the increasing union of the homomorphic images
\(\iota_k(\operatorname{GL}_{n_k}(R))\). Thus every continuous action of
\(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\) on a nonempty compact Hausdorff space admits a
\(\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)\)-invariant regular Borel probability measure.
Density of \(\operatorname{GL}_\mu(R)\), proved in \Cref{prop:density}, and
continuity of the action make this measure \(\operatorname{GL}(Q)\)-invariant:
for each continuous function on the compact space, its integral
against the translated measure depends continuously on the group
element. Now apply \Cref{thm:main,lem:dynamics}.
