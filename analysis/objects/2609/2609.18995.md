<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let \(d\ge 1\), \(r\ge 2\), and \(\ell\ge 1\). If \(\mathcal{H}\) is an \(\ell\)-bounded set system with \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{H})\le d\) and no \(r\)-sunflower, then
\[
\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert\le \sum_{k=0}^{\ell}(50dr)^{k}<2(50dr)^{\ell}.
\]
In particular, every \(\ell\)-uniform set system \(\mathcal{H}\) with \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{H})\le d\) and \(\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert>(50dr)^{\ell}\) contains an \(r\)-sunflower.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:sauer}
Let \(\mathcal{G}\) be a set system on \(X\) with \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{G})\le d\), and let \(E\subseteq X\) have size \(m\ge d\ge 1\). Then
\[
\lvert\{G\cap E:G\in\mathcal{G}\}\rvert\le \sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{m}{i}\le \left(\frac{em}{d}\right)^{d}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:independence}
If \(J\) is a graph with \(N\) vertices and \(e(J)\) edges, then
\[
\alpha(J)\ge \sum_{v\in V(J)}\frac{1}{\deg_{J}(v)+1}\ge \frac{N^{2}}{N+2e(J)}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-entropy}
Let \(\mathcal{G}\) be a \(q\)-spread family with \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{G})\le d\), where \(0<q<1\) and \(d\ge 1\). Let \(G\) be chosen uniformly at random from \(\mathcal{G}\), and put \(\mu_{x}=\mathbb{P}(x\in G)\). If \(E\subseteq X\) has size \(m\ge d\), then
\[
\sum_{x\in E}\mu_{x}\le \frac{d\log\left(\frac{em}{d}\right)}{\log\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->

Set \(R=G\cap E\). Thus \(R\) records exactly which elements of \(E\) occur in the random set \(G\). If \(A\subseteq E\) is a possible value of \(R\), then the event \(R=A\) implies \(A\subseteq G\), and therefore
\[
\mathbb{P}(R=A)\le \mathbb{P}(A\subseteq G)\le q^{\lvertA\rvert}.
\]
For \(A\ne\varnothing\), the second inequality is exactly the spread condition. For \(A=\varnothing\), it is the trivial inequality \(\mathbb{P}(R=\varnothing)\le 1=q^{0}\). Taking logarithms gives
\[
\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(R=A)}\ge \lvertA\rvert\log\frac{1}{q}.
\]
We multiply this inequality by \(\mathbb{P}(R=A)\) and sum over all possible values \(A\) of \(R\). By the definition of entropy,
\[
H(R)\ge \log\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)\sum_{A\in\operatorname{supp}(R)}\mathbb{P}(R=A)\lvertA\rvert
=\log\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)\sum_{x\in E}\mu_{x}.
\]
To see the last equality directly, the middle sum is the average size of \(R\). An element \(x\in E\) contributes one to this size precisely when \(x\in G\), which occurs with probability \(\mu_{x}\).

It remains to bound the same entropy from above. Since every member of \(\mathcal{G}\) is chosen with positive probability, the possible values of \(R\) are exactly the sets \(G\cap E\) with \(G\in\mathcal{G}\). Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:sauer} and the general entropy bound~\eqref{eq:concavity} give
\[
H(R)\le\log\lvert\operatorname{supp}(R)\rvert
=\log\lvert\{G\cap E:G\in\mathcal{G}\}\rvert
\le d\log\left(\frac{em}{d}\right).
\]
Comparing the lower and upper bounds for \(H(R)\) proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:square-sum}
Let \(\mathcal{G}\) be \(q\)-spread with \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{G})\le d\), where \(d\ge 1\) and \(0<q\le\frac{1}{2}\). If \(G\) is chosen uniformly at random from \(\mathcal{G}\) and \(\mu_{x}=\mathbb{P}(x\in G)\), then
\[
\sum_{x\in X}\mu_{x}^{2}<49dq.
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For every \(x\in X\), the spread condition applied to \(\{x\}\) gives \(\mu_{x}\le q\). We separate the elements according to the size of this probability. For each integer \(j\ge 0\), let
\[
E_{j}=\{x\in X:2^{-j-1}q<\mu_{x}\le 2^{-j}q\}
\]
and set \(m_{j}=\lvertE_{j}\rvert\). The sets \(E_{j}\) partition the elements for which \(\mu_{x}>0\), elements with \(\mu_{x}=0\) do not affect the desired sum. Moreover, every \(x\in E_{j}\) satisfies \(\mu_{x}^{2}\le 4^{-j}q^{2}\). It therefore remains to control the number \(m_{j}\) of elements in each group.

Fix \(j\ge 0\) with \(m_{j}\ge d\). Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:local-entropy} to \(E_{j}\), and using \(\mu_{x}>2^{-j-1}q\) for every \(x\in E_{j}\), gives
\[
m_{j}2^{-j-1}q\log\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)
<\sum_{x\in E_{j}}\mu_{x}\log\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)
\le d\log\left(\frac{em_{j}}{d}\right).
\]
Put \(L=\log\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)\) and \(u_{j}=\frac{m_{j}2^{-j-1}q}{d}\). The definition of \(u_{j}\) gives \(\frac{m_{j}}{d}=\frac{u_{j}2^{j+1}}{q}\), and hence
\[
\log\left(\frac{em_{j}}{d}\right)=1+\log u_{j}+(j+1)\log 2+L.
\]
Thus the preceding inequality becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uj}
u_{j}L<L+(j+1)\log 2+1+\log u_{j}.
\end{equation}

\begin{claim}\label{claim:uj}
If \(m_{j}\ge d\), then \(u_{j}\le 4(j+2)\).
\end{claim}

\begin{poc}
Suppose that \(u_{j}>4(j+2)\). Since \(j\ge 0\), this implies \(u_{j}>8\), and in particular \(\log u_{j}>0\). Also, \(q\le\frac{1}{2}\) gives \(L\ge\log 2\). After dividing \eqref{eq:uj} by \(L\), we use \(\frac{(j+1)\log 2}{L}\le j+1\), \(\frac{1}{L}\le\frac{1}{\log 2}<2\), and \(\frac{\log u_{j}}{L}\le\log_{2}u_{j}\). It follows that
\[
u_{j}<j+4+\log_{2}u_{j}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(\log_{2}u\le\frac{u}{2}\) holds for \(u\ge 8\), so \(u_{j}<2j+8\). On the other hand, our assumption gives \(u_{j}>4j+8\ge 2j+8\), a contradiction.
\end{poc}

Returning to the definition of \(u_{j}\), Claim~\ref{claim:uj} gives
\[
m_{j}=\frac{du_{j}2^{j+1}}{q}\le \frac{8d(j+2)2^{j}}{q}
\]
whenever \(m_{j}\ge d\). Since every element of \(E_{j}\) contributes at most \(4^{-j}q^{2}\), such a group contributes at most
\[
\sum_{x\in E_{j}}\mu_{x}^{2}\le m_{j}4^{-j}q^{2}\le 8dq(j+2)2^{-j}.
\]
Summing over all groups with \(m_{j}\ge d\), and using \(\sum_{j\ge 0}(j+2)2^{-j}=6\), we obtain a total contribution of at most \(48dq\).

If instead \(m_{j}<d\), then the same pointwise bound gives
\[
\sum_{x\in E_{j}}\mu_{x}^{2}\le m_{j}4^{-j}q^{2}<d4^{-j}q^{2}.
\]
The total contribution of these groups is therefore less than \(dq^{2}\sum_{j\ge 0}4^{-j}=\frac{4}{3}dq^{2}\le\frac{2}{3}dq\), where the last inequality uses \(q\le\frac{1}{2}\). Adding the two contributions yields
\[
\sum_{x\in X}\mu_{x}^{2}<48dq+\frac{2}{3}dq=\frac{146}{3}dq<49dq,
\]
as required.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:matching}
Let \(\mathcal{G}\) be a finite family of nonempty sets. If \(\mathcal{G}\) is \(q\)-spread, \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{G})\le d\), \(d\ge 1\), and \(0<q\le\frac{1}{2}\), then
\(
\nu(\mathcal{G})>\frac{1}{49dq}.
\)
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Define a graph \(J\) whose vertices are the members of \(\mathcal{G}\), with two distinct members joined by an edge exactly when they intersect. Thus an independent set in \(J\) is the same as a pairwise disjoint subfamily of \(\mathcal{G}\).

Write \(N=\lvert\mathcal{G}\rvert\), choose a member \(G\) uniformly from \(\mathcal{G}\), and put \(\mu_{x}=\mathbb{P}(x\in G)=\frac{d_{\mathcal{G}}(\{x\})}{N}\). Consider the sum of \(\lvertA\cap B\rvert\) over all ordered pairs \((A,B)\in\mathcal{G}^{2}\). Since every member of \(\mathcal{G}\) is nonempty, each of the \(N\) pairs \((A,A)\) contributes at least one. Moreover, every edge \(\{A,B\}\) of \(J\) gives the two ordered pairs \((A,B)\) and \((B,A)\), each of which also contributes at least one. It follows that
\[
N+2e(J)\le\sum_{A,B\in\mathcal{G}}\lvertA\cap B\rvert.
\]
We now count the sum on the right by first fixing \(x\in X\). There are \(d_{\mathcal{G}}(\{x\})\) choices of \(A\) containing \(x\), and the same number of choices for \(B\). Thus \(x\) is counted in exactly \(d_{\mathcal{G}}(\{x\})^{2}\) ordered pairs, and consequently
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{A,B\in\mathcal{G}}\lvertA\cap B\rvert
=\sum_{x\in X}d_{\mathcal{G}}(\{x\})^{2}
=N^{2}\sum_{x\in X}\mu_{x}^{2}
<49dqN^{2},
\end{equation*}
where the last inequality follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:square-sum}. Combining the two estimates and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:independence}, we obtain
\[
\alpha(J)\ge\frac{N^{2}}{N+2e(J)}>\frac{1}{49dq}.
\]
Since independent sets in \(J\) are precisely the pairwise disjoint subfamilies of \(\mathcal{G}\), we have \(\nu(\mathcal{G})=\alpha(J)\), which proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:maximal-kernel}
Let \(0<q<1\), and let \(\mathcal{H}\subseteq\binom{X}{\ell}\) satisfy \(\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert>q^{-\ell}\). Then there is a set \(C\subseteq X\) with \(\lvertC\rvert<\ell\) such that
\[
\mathcal{G}=\{H\setminus C:H\in\mathcal{H},\ C\subseteq H\}
\]
is a nonempty \(q\)-spread, \((\ell-\lvertC\rvert)\)-uniform family with \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{G})\le\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{H})\).
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose an inclusion-maximal set \(C\subseteq X\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dense-kernel}
d_{\mathcal{H}}(C)\ge \lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert q^{\lvertC\rvert}.
\end{equation}
Such a choice is possible because \(X\) is finite and \(C=\varnothing\) satisfies \eqref{eq:dense-kernel}.

The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:dense-kernel} is positive, so \(C\) is contained in at least one member of \(\mathcal{H}\). In particular, \(\lvertC\rvert\le\ell\). Suppose that \(\lvertC\rvert=\ell\). Since \(\mathcal{H}\) is \(\ell\)-uniform, the only member of \(\mathcal{H}\) that can contain \(C\) is \(C\) itself, and hence \(d_{\mathcal{H}}(C)=1\). On the other hand,
\[
d_{\mathcal{H}}(C)\ge\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert q^{\ell}>q^{-\ell}q^{\ell}=1,
\]
a contradiction. Consequently, \(\lvertC\rvert<\ell\).

Every member of \(\mathcal{H}\) containing \(C\) produces, after \(C\) is removed, a set of size \(\ell-\lvertC\rvert\). Moreover, the original member can be recovered by adding \(C\) back, so distinct members of \(\mathcal{H}\) produce distinct members of \(\mathcal{G}\). Thus \(\mathcal{G}\) is nonempty and \((\ell-\lvertC\rvert)\)-uniform, and
\(
\lvert\mathcal{G}\rvert=d_{\mathcal{H}}(C).
\)

We next verify the spread condition. Let \(\varnothing\ne T\subseteq X\setminus C\). If \(C\cup T\) also satisfied \eqref{eq:dense-kernel}, it would be a proper extension of \(C\) satisfying the same condition, contrary to the maximal choice of \(C\). Hence
\[
d_{\mathcal{H}}(C\cup T)<\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert q^{\lvertC\rvert+\lvertT\rvert}.
\]
A member of \(\mathcal{G}\) contains \(T\) exactly when the corresponding member of \(\mathcal{H}\) contains \(C\cup T\). Using \eqref{eq:dense-kernel}, we therefore obtain
\[
d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)=d_{\mathcal{H}}(C\cup T)
<\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert q^{\lvertC\rvert}q^{\lvertT\rvert}
\le\lvert\mathcal{G}\rvert q^{\lvertT\rvert}.
\]
This is precisely the \(q\)-spread condition.

It remains to compare the VC-dimensions. Suppose that \(D\subseteq X\setminus C\) is shattered by \(\mathcal{G}\). For every \(A\subseteq D\), there is a member \(G\in\mathcal{G}\) such that \(G\cap D=A\). By the definition of \(\mathcal{G}\), there is an \(H\in\mathcal{H}\) containing \(C\) for which \(G=H\setminus C\). Since \(D\cap C=\varnothing\),
\[
H\cap D=(H\setminus C)\cap D=G\cap D=A.
\]
Thus every subset of \(D\) also occurs as the intersection of \(D\) with a member of \(\mathcal{H}\), so \(D\) is shattered by \(\mathcal{H}\). We conclude that \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{G})\le\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{H})\).
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We first prove the assertion for an \(\ell\)-uniform family. Set \(Q=50dr\) and \(q=Q^{-1}\). Since \(d\ge 1\) and \(r\ge 2\), we have \(q\le\frac{1}{2}\).

Suppose that \(\mathcal{H}\subseteq\binom{X}{\ell}\), that \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{H})\le d\), and that \(\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert>Q^{\ell}=q^{-\ell}\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:maximal-kernel}, there is a set \(C\) such that
\[
\mathcal{G}=\{H\setminus C:H\in\mathcal{H},\ C\subseteq H\}
\]
is a nonempty \(q\)-spread family with VC-dimension at most \(d\). Lemma~\ref{lem:matching} gives
\[
\nu(\mathcal{G})>\frac{1}{49dq}=\frac{50r}{49}>r.
\]
In particular, \(\mathcal{G}\) contains \(r\) pairwise disjoint members \(G_{1},\ldots,G_{r}\). For each \(i\), the definition of \(\mathcal{G}\) gives a member \(H_{i}=C\cup G_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\). Every \(G_{i}\) is disjoint from \(C\), and \(G_{i}\cap G_{j}=\varnothing\) whenever \(i\ne j\). Consequently,
\[
H_{i}\cap H_{j}=(C\cup G_{i})\cap(C\cup G_{j})=C
\]
for every \(i\ne j\). Thus \(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}\) form an \(r\)-sunflower with kernel \(C\). We have proved that every \(\ell\)-uniform family with no \(r\)-sunflower has at most \(Q^{\ell}\) members.

Now let \(\mathcal{H}\) be \(\ell\)-bounded, and partition it according to the sizes of its members. For \(0\le k\le\ell\), let \(\mathcal{H}_{k}=\mathcal{H}\cap\binom{X}{k}\). Each \(\mathcal{H}_{k}\) is a subfamily of \(\mathcal{H}\). Removing members cannot create a shattered set, so \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal{H}_{k})\le d\). Likewise, an \(r\)-sunflower in \(\mathcal{H}_{k}\) would also be an \(r\)-sunflower in \(\mathcal{H}\), and hence no \(\mathcal{H}_{k}\) contains one. The uniform case therefore gives \(\lvert\mathcal{H}_{k}\rvert\le Q^{k}\) for \(1\le k\le\ell\). The family \(\mathcal{H}_{0}\) contains at most the empty set, so \(\lvert\mathcal{H}_{0}\rvert\le 1=Q^{0}\). It follows that
\[
\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert
=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\lvert\mathcal{H}_{k}\rvert
\le\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}Q^{k}
=\frac{Q^{\ell+1}-1}{Q-1}
<2Q^{\ell},
\]
where the final inequality uses \(Q\ge 2\). Substituting \(Q=50dr\) completes the proof.
