<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem: colim}
    Let $B$ be normal in $M$ over $A$. Let $\{B_i\}_{i \in I}$ be a family of normal subsets of $B$ which form a directed system under inclusion and such that $\cup_{i \in I}B_i = B$. Then $\varprojlim \text{Aut}(B_i/A) \cong \text{Aut}(B/A)$ and moreover if $G$ is an $A$-definable group then $$\varinjlim \operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B_i/A,G(B_i)) \cong \operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A,G(B)).$$
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Note that $\varinjlim B_i$ is the disjoint union of the $B_i$ modulo the relation of eventual equality. Each $B_i$ includes into $B$ which induces a map from $\varinjlim B_i$ to $B$. It follows immediately from the definitions that this is well defined and injective. Surjectivity follows from the assumption that $\cup_{i \in I}B_i = B$. So there is a canonical bijection from $\varinjlim B_i$ to $B$.

    As $\{B_i\}_{i \in I}$ forms a directed system, $\{\text{Aut}(B_i/A)\}_{i \in I}$ forms an inverse system. For each index $i$, the quotient homomorphism $Aut(B/A) \to \text{Aut}(B_i/A)$ is surjective and the required compatibilities hold so that we have a map $Aut(B/A) \to \varprojlim\text{Aut}(B_i/A)$. Injectivity follows by noting that if two automorphism of $Aut(B/A)$ differ, then they differ on some $B_i$. To check surjectivity, let $(\sigma_i)_{i\in I} \in \varprojlim\text{Aut}(B_i/A)$. We define an automorphism of $B$ whose image is $(\sigma_i)_{i\in I}$. For $b \in B$, there is an $i \in I$ such that $b\in B_i$, so define $\sigma(b) = \sigma_i(b)$. To see that this is well defined independent of the choice of $B_i$, let $i,j \in I$ such that $b \in B_i \cap B_j$. Then there is some $B_k$ containing both $B_i$ and $B_j$. Then $\sigma_i(b) = \pi_{ki}(\sigma_k)(b) = \sigma_k(b) = \pi_{kj}(\sigma_k)(b) = \sigma_j(b)$ where $\pi_{ki}:\text{Aut}(B_k/A) \to \text{Aut}(B_i/A)$ and $\pi_{kj}:\text{Aut}(B_k/A) \to \text{Aut}(B_j/A)$ are the projections. As $\sigma$ restricts to an elementary permutation on each $B_i$, and $B = \cup_{i \in I}B_i$, $\sigma$ is an elementary permutation. So $\text{Aut}(B/A) \cong \varprojlim \text{Aut}(B_i/A)$.

    By Theorem \ref{thm: SES}, as the $\{\text{Aut}(B_i/A)\}_{i \in I}$ form an inverse system of groups, $\{\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(B_i/A, G(B_i))\}_{i \in I}$ forms a directed system of pointed sets. The inclusion of each $B_i$ in $B$ induces again via Theorem \ref{thm: SES} a map from $\varinjlim \operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B_i/A,G(B_i))$ to $\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A,G(B))$. As $\cup_{i\in I}B_i = B$, this map is surjective as the definability data for any cocycle in $\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A,G(B))$ must be contained in from some $B_i$. Injectivity is immediate: If for some indices  $i,j \in I$ there are class in $\operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B_i/A,G(B_i))$ and $\operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B_i/A,G(B_j))$ which have the same image in $\operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A,G(B))$, then they must be equal in $\operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B_k/A,G(B_k))$ where $B_i,B_j \subseteq B_k$. So $\varinjlim \operatorname{H}^1_{\operatorname{def}}(B_i/A,G(B_i)) \cong \operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A,G(B))$.

\label{internality}
Let $Y$ and $X$ be a $A$-definable sets. Let $\psi_Y(\bar{z})$ and $\varphi_X(\bar{w})$ be $A$-definable formulas defining $Y$ and $X$ respectively.  We say that $Y$ is internal to $X$ if there is a small subset of parameters $B \subset \bar{M}$ such that $Y \subseteq \operatorname{dcl}(B,X,A)$.
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
$Y$ is internal to $X$ means that, for each $\bar{a} \in Y$, there is an $A$-formula, $\varphi_{\bar{a}}(\bar{y}_{\bar{a}},\bar{x}_{\bar{a}},\bar{z})$, a tuple $\bar{b}_{\bar{a}} \in B^{|\bar{y}_{\bar{a}}|}$ (the variable lengths can change depending on the $\bar{a}$) and a tuple $\bar{c}_{\bar{a}} \in X^{|\bar{x}_a|}$ such that $\models \varphi_{\bar{a}}(\bar{b}_{\bar{a}},\bar{c}_{\bar{a}},\bar{a})$ and $\bar{a}$ is the unique element of $\bar{M}$ satisfying the above formula.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{Y definability lemma}
Let $Y$ be $X$-internal as above. Then there exists an $A$-definable function $f(\bar{y},\bar{x})$ and a tuple of elements $\bar{b}$ from $Y$ such that for each $\bar{a} \in Y$, there exists a tuple $\bar{c}$ from $dcl(X,A)$ such that $f(\bar{b},\bar{c}) = \bar{a}$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->

        \item Let $A \subseteq B = \operatorname{dcl}(A,b)$ be a definable Galois extension relative to $G$. Let $(Q,H)$ be the associated $A$-definable torsor. As $H$ lives on $G$, $H(M) = H(\operatorname{mcl}(A))$. Then by Lemma \ref{lem: mcl points}, $Q(M) = Q(\operatorname{mcl}(A))$. As $B$ is generated by a $M$-point of $Q$, $B$ is contained in $\operatorname{mcl}(A)$. This is a slight strengthening of Fact \ref{fact: mcl} (3).
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop: struct of M}
    Let $A$ be a $\operatorname{dcl}$-closed set of parameters. Let $X$ be an $A$-definable set such that $X(M) = X(\operatorname{mcl}(A))$. Then each $A^{X\text{DG}_n}$ is contained in $\operatorname{mcl}(A)$ and we have $$A \subseteq A^{X\text{DG}_1} \subseteq A^{X\text{DG}_2} \cdots \subseteq A^{X\text{DG}_\infty} \subseteq \operatorname{mcl}(A) \subseteq M.$$ Furthermore each $A^{X\text{DG}_n}$ is normal.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
This follows from Theorem \ref{thm: mcl points} (3). Normality follows because each conjugate under $\text{Aut}(M/A)$ of an $X$-definable Galois extension is again an $X$-definable Galois extension.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem: IDG form directed system}
    Let $B$ and $C$ be iterated definable Galois extensions of $A$ of finite type relative to $X$ in $M$. Then $\operatorname{dcl}(B,C)$ is again an iterated definable Galois extension of $A$ of finite type relative to $X$ in $M$.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
We follow Lemma 2 of \cite{magid2022completepicardvessiotclosure}. Let $A=B_0 \subseteq B_1 \subseteq \cdots B_m=B$ and $A=C_0 \subseteq C_1 \subseteq \cdots C_n=C$ witness that $B$ and $C$ are iterated definable Galois extensions of finite type. The claim is proved by induction on $n$. If $n$ is $0$ then $C=A$ and $\operatorname{dcl}(B,C) = B$. Assume true for $n-1$. So $\operatorname{dcl}(B_m,C_{n-1})$ is an iterated definable Galois extension of $A$. By assumption $C_n = \operatorname{dcl}(C_{n-1},c_n)$ where $tp(c_n/C_{n-1})$ is weakly orthogonal to $X$ and strongly internal to $X$. Then by Fact \ref{weak orth fact}, $tp(c_n/B_m,C_{n-1})$ is still weakly orthogonal to $X$ as $X(M) = X(A)$. Adding parameters preserves strong internality to $X$. So $\operatorname{dcl}(B,C)$ is a definable Galois extension of $tp(c_n/B_m,C_{n-1})$ of finite type. So $\operatorname{dcl}(B,C)$ is still an iterated definable Galois extension of finite type.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem: finite type iterated is dg}
    Let $X(M)=X(A)$. A normal iterated definable Galois extension $B$ of $A$ of finite type relative to $X$ must be a definable Galois extension of finite type
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Let $b$ $\operatorname{dcl}$-generate $B$ over $A$. Then by normality and strong homogeneity any other realization of the type of $b$ over $A$ must be contained in $\operatorname{dcl}(A,b,X(A))$. Again as $X(M)=X(A)$, $tp(b/A)$ is weakly orthogonal to $X$ by Fact \ref{weak orth fact}.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop: idg colim}
    Let $G$ be an $A$ definable group living on an $A$-definable set $X$. Assume $X(M)=X(A)$. Then $$\varinjlim \operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A, G(B)) \cong \operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(A^{XDG_1}/A, G(A^{XDG_1}))$$ where the limit is taken over the family of normal iterated definable Galois extensions of finite type $B$ of $A$ relative to $X$, equivalently, the family of definable Galois extensions of finite type.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{lem: finite type iterated is dg}, a normal iterated definable Galois extension of finite type $B$ of $A$ relative to $X$ is a definable Galois extension of finite type. Then by Lemma \ref{lem: IDG form directed system}, the family of definable Galois extensions of finite type $B$ of $A$ relative to $X$ form a directed system. It is clear that their union is $A^{XDG_1}$. The result then follows from Lemma \ref{lem: colim}.

<!-- theorem-16: body_md -->
\label{thm: idg colim}
    Let $G$ be an $A$ definable group living on an $A$-definable set $X$. Assume $X(M)=X(A)$. Then $$\varinjlim \operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A, G(B)) \cong \operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(A^{XDG_\infty}/A, G(A^{XDG_\infty}))$$ where the limit is taken over the family of normal closures of iterated definable Galois extensions of finite type $B$ of $A$ relative to $X$.
<!-- end theorem-16 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{lem: IDG form directed system}, the family of normal closures of iterated definable Galois extensions of finite type $B$ of $A$ relative to $X$ form a directed system. It is clear that their union is $A^{XDG_\infty}$. The result then follows from Lemma \ref{lem: colim}.

<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
Let $X$ be an $A$-definable set satisfying $X(A)= X(M)$. Let $G$ be an $A$-definable group living on $X$.  The set of parameters $A$ is said to be bounded with respect to $G$ and $X$ if there exists an iterated definable Galois extension of finite type relative to $X$ whose normal closure $B$ satisfies $$\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(B/A, G(B)) \cong \operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(A^{XDG_\infty}/A, G(A^{XDG_\infty})).$$
