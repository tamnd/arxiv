<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:picpi}
A collection $\mathcal{I}_p=\{I_j\}_{j=1}^{J}$ is called a
collection of \emph{Prediction-Interval-Conditional Prediction Intervals\xspace (PICPIs\xspace)} with respect to $p$ if,
for every $j\in[J]$ with $\mathbb{P}(p(X)\inI_j)>0$,
\begin{align}
\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid p(X)\inI_j)\inI_j.
\label{eq:picpi-definition}
\end{align}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:algorithm_population}
Suppose $\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ are drawn i.i.d. from the
distribution of $(X,Y)$ and $p$ is fixed independently of these observations.
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the calibration sample,
every interval returned by \textsc{Calibration} in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:calibration_population} is a PICPI\xspace with respect to
$p$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proofsketch-1: body_md -->
We use Hoeffding's inequality on $\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid p(X)\in I)$ and a union bound over $K^2$ candidate intervals. For the full proof, see  Supplementary Material~\ref{proof_thm:algorithm_population}.
<!-- end proofsketch-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A random variable $Z$ over $[0,1]$ is $\lambda$-\emph{regular} if $\mathbb{P}(Z \in A) \geq \lambda \cdot \mu_B(A)$ for any measurable set $A$, where $\mu_B$ is the Lebesgue measure.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:consistency-prediction}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           
Consider $\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{m+n}$  i.i.d.\ data points such that 
$n \geq 2^{-1/2} \lambda^{-1} \log(3n/\delta) $.
                                                                           
Use $m$ data points to construct the predictor $p: \mathcal{X} \to [0,1]$ and $n$ data points to produce PICPIs\xspace $\mathcal{I}_p$  via Algorithm~\ref{alg:calibration_population}. 
Suppose $X \sim \mu$, $p(X)$ is $\lambda$-regular, and
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}_\mu\Big[\Big| p (X) - \mathbb{P}(Y = 1 \mid X)\Big|\Big] \leq \epsilon, \qquad \text{for some  $\epsilon>0$.}
\end{align*}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     
For any $\delta,\delta_1 > 0$, with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the randomness of $\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$,
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{P}_{x \sim \mu}\Big(\exists [a,b] \in \mathcal{I}_p ~s.t.~ \underbrace{p(x) \in [a,b]}_{\text{enclose prediction}} ~\text{and}~ \underbrace{|b-a| \leq C(n,\epsilon,\delta,\delta_1)}_{\text{bounded width}}  \Big) \geq 1-\delta_1,
\end{align}
where 
\begin{align}\label{eq:width_function}
    C(n,\epsilon,\delta,\delta_1) = c_0 \log(1/\delta_1) \cdot \Big(\Big(\underbrace{\frac{ \log(n/\delta)}{\lambda n}\Big)^{1/3}}_{\text{calibration error}}  + \underbrace{\frac{\epsilon}{\delta_1}}_{\text{prediction error}}\Big)
\end{align}
for a universal constant $c_0$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proofsketch-2: body_md -->
First, we construct an initial collection of intervals of desired length and coverage by applying Lemma~\ref{lem:dist-regularity} to the distribution of $p(X)$. That is, there exist intervals $\{[a_j,b_j]\}$ where the conditional mean prediction $\mathbb{E}[p(X) \mid p(X)\in [a_j, b_j]]$ is bounded away from its own endpoints. Then we transfer this property 
                                                                            
    to the conditional mean outcome $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid p(X)\in [a_j, b_j]]$ (by setting $U=p^*(X)$ and $V=p(X)$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional-dist-shift}). Upon discarding a small amount of mass, the retained intervals satisfy the desired PICPI\xspace property. We then show that these intervals can be produced by Algorithm~\ref{alg:calibration_population}. Define $A_1$ to be the event that every interval contains sufficiently many samples, and $A_2$ the event that the empirical label average is close to the conditional mean outcome. Under $A_1\cap A_2$, the empirical estimate is sufficiently far from both endpoints for the algorithm to accept. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sample-size-interval},

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:dist-regularity}
For any $\epsilon, \delta > 0$, and any distribution $\nu$ over $[0,1]$, there exist disjoint intervals $[a_i,b_i], i \in [L]$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{(Well-centered conditional means)}  
    The conditional mean of $Z$ on $[a_i,b_i]$ lies away from the interval boundaries by at least $\epsilon$ (except for the endpoints $0$ or $1$). That is, for any $i \in [L]$, 
    \begin{align}\label{eq:regular-condition}
        a_i + \epsilon \cdot \mathbbm{1}(a_i \neq 0) \leq \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \nu}[Z \mid Z \in [a_i,b_i]] \leq b_i - \epsilon \cdot \mathbbm{1}(b_i \neq 1).
    \end{align}
    \item 
    \emph{(Coverage guarantee)}  
    The union of these intervals covers all but a $\delta$-fraction of the probability mass. That is, \begin{align}\label{eq:regular-condition-coverage}
        \mathbb{P}_{Z \sim \nu}\Big(Z \notin \cup_{i \in [L]} [a_i,b_i]\Big) \leq \delta.
    \end{align}
                                                                    
    \item \emph{(Bounded lengths)}  
    Each interval has a bounded length. That is, for any $i \in [L]$,
    \begin{align}\label{eq:regular-condition-length}
        4\epsilon \leq \Big|b_i - a_i\Big| \leq 56 \epsilon \cdot \log(3/\delta).
    \end{align}                                                                 
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We provide a constructive proof. Discretize $[0,1]$ into $K = \lfloor1/(4\epsilon)\rfloor$ subintervals of length $4\epsilon$ and form a directed graph $G=(V,E)$ on $V = [K]$. We draw a directed edge $i \to j$ ($|i-j|=1$) if either the conditional mean of $Z$ in bin $i$ $\mathbb{E}[Z \mid Z \in B_i]$ is closer to the neighboring endpoint on the side of bin $j$, or the probability mass of $Z$ in bin $i$ is not substantially greater than bin $j$ (i.e., $\mathbb{P}(Z\in B_i)\le 3\mathbb{P}(Z\in B_j)$). By construction, every vertex has at least one outgoing edge, and there exists  bidirectional edges. 
Group consecutive bidirectional edges into maximal blocks. Partition each block into groups of two vertices. Notice that the edges between blocks split into a leftward chain followed by a rightward chain. Attach them to the neighboring blocks, truncated at a radius of $\lceil\log(3/\delta)\rceil$. This would give the desired intervals.
We then show these intervals satisfy the stated properties. 
The boundedness of widths follows from truncation of the chain. 
The conditional mean property comes from the edge construction and total expectation. Finally, outside these intervals, the absence of reverse edges implies exponential mass decay to bound uncovered mass by $\delta$. For the full proof see
Supplementary Material~\ref{proof_lem:dist-regularity}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:conditional-dist-shift}
Let $U,V$ be random variables jointly over $[0,1]$, such that $\mathbb{E}[|U-V|] \leq \epsilon > 0$. Then for any $\delta > 0$, integer $L$ and intervals $[a_i,b_i], i = 1,\dots, L$ with disjoint interiors such that $\mathbb{P}\Big(V \in \cup_{j \in [L]} [a_j,b_j]\Big) > 1- \delta$, 
there exists a subset $\{i_1,\dots,i_J\}$ of $L$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{(Conditional distribution on selected intervals)} Conditioning on a selected interval, the discrepancy of the expected value between $U$ and $V$ is controlled by $2\epsilon/\delta$.
 That is, for any $j \in [J]$, 
    \begin{align}\label{eq:distribution-shift}
        \Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[U\mid V \in [a_{i_j},b_{i_j}]\Big] - \mathbb{E}\Big[V \mid V \in [a_{i_j},b_{i_j}]\Big]\Big| \leq 2\epsilon/\delta.
    \end{align}
    \item \emph{(Coverage guarantee)}
    The intervals not selected carry at most a $\delta$-fraction of the probability mass. That is,
\begin{align}\label{eq:distribution-shift-coverage}
        \mathbb{P}\Big(V \in \cup_{j \in [L] \setminus \{i_1,\dots,i_J\}} [a_j,b_j]\Big) \leq \delta.
    \end{align}
    Consequently, the union of the selected intervals has probability mass $1-2\delta$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proofsketch-4: body_md -->
By applying the triangle inequality to each interval $w$ violating Eq.~\eqref{eq:distribution-shift}, the conditional mean absolute deviation satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\vert{}U - V\vert{} \mid V \in [a_w, b_w]] > 2\epsilon/\delta$. Accounting for endpoint overlaps ($\sum_w \mathbbm{1}(V \in [a_w, b_w]) \leq 2$), we have $2\epsilon \ge \sum_w \mathbb{E}[|U-V| \mathbbm{1}(V \in [a_w, b_w])] > \mathbb{P}(V \in \bigcup_w [a_w, b_w]) \cdot (2\epsilon/\delta)$, yielding total violating mass bounded by $\delta$. The full proof is provided in Supplementary Material~\ref{proof_lem:conditional-dist-shift}.

    \epsilon_1 = &~ \epsilon_\mathrm{stat} + \frac{4\epsilon}{\delta_1},\\

If $K=1$, the interval $[0,1]$ is returned by the algorithm and covers all prediction values, proving the result directly with $C(n,\epsilon,\delta,\delta_1)=1$. For $K \geq 2$, the proof consists of three steps. 

\paragraph{Step 1. } 
We show that there exists $J \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ and disjoint intervals $[a_j,b_j], j \in [J]$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For any $j \in [J]$, 
    \begin{align}\label{eq:consistency-existence-calibration}

    \item

        \mathbb{P}\Big(p(X) \notin \cup_{j \in [J]} [a_j,b_j]\Big) \leq \delta_1;
    \end{align}
    \item For any $j \in [J]$,
    \begin{align}\label{eq:consistency-existence-length}   
        4\epsilon_1 \leq \Big|b_j - a_j\Big| \leq 56\epsilon_1 \cdot \log(6/\delta_1).
    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
First, applying Lemma~\ref{lem:dist-regularity} with $\nu$ being the distribution of $p(X)$, uncovered mass budget $\delta_1$, and accuracy parameter $\epsilon_1$, we see that there exist disjoint intervals $[a_i,b_i], i \in [L]$ that satisfy 
\begin{align*}

\end{align*}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:consistency-existence-length}, and
\begin{align}\label{eq:regularity-pm}
    a_i + \epsilon_1 \cdot \mathbbm{1}(a_i \neq 0) \leq \mathbb{E}[p(X)\midp(X) \in [a_i,b_i]] \leq b_i - \epsilon_1 \cdot \mathbbm{1}(b_i \neq 1).
\end{align}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional-dist-shift}, there exist $i_1,\dots,i_J \in [L]$ such that for any $j \in [J]$, 
\begin{align}\label{eq:conditional-dist-shift-pm}
    \Big|\mathbb{P}(Y=1\midp(X) \in [a_{i_j},b_{i_j}])  - \mathbb{E}[p(X)\midp(X) \in [a_{i_j},b_{i_j}]] \Big| \leq 4\epsilon/\delta_1 
\end{align}
and
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{P}\Big(p(X) \in \cup_{j' \notin \{i_1,\dots,i_J\}} [a_{j'},b_{j'}]\Big) \leq \delta_1/2.
\end{align*}
Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-dist-shift-pm} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:regularity-pm}, we have
\begin{align*}

    & \leq  \mathbb{E}[p(X)\midp(X) \in [a_{i_j},b_{i_j}]] + 4\epsilon/\delta_1 \\

We present a constructive proof based on discretization of $[0,1]$. To simplify notation, we abbreviate $\mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \nu}$ as $\mathbb{E}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{Z \sim \nu}$ as $\mathbb{P}$.


\paragraph{Step 1: Construct the graph.}
Consider $[(i-1)/K, i/K], i = 1,2, \dots,K$ with $K = \lfloor 1/(4\epsilon) \rfloor$. Without loss of generality assume $K\ge2$.
Construct a directed graph $G = (V,E)$ where $V = [K]$ and let the edges be defined as follows: $1 \rightarrow 2, K-1 \leftarrow K$, where there is a directed edge $i \rightarrow j$ iff both of the following conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $|i-j| = 1$, and
    \item Either \begin{align}\label{eq:edge-condition-1}
   \mathbb{P}(Z\in [(i-1)/K, i/K]) \text{ and } \Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[Z\mid Z \in [(i-1)/K, i/K]\Big] - \frac{i-0.5}{K}\Big) \cdot (j-i) \ge 0 ,
\end{align}
or 
\begin{align}\label{eq:edge-condition-2}
    \mathbb{P}\Big(Z \in [(i-1)/K, i/K]\Big) \leq 3 \cdot \mathbb{P}\Big(Z \in [(j-1)/K, j/K]\Big).
\end{align}
\end{itemize}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge-condition-1} can be interpreted as: if $\mathbb{E}\Big[Z\mid Z \in [(i-1)/K, i/K]\Big]$ is closer to $(i-1)/K$, then draw a directed edge $i-1 \leftarrow i$; if $\mathbb{E}\Big[Z\mid Z \in [(i-1)/K, i/K]\Big]$ is closer to $i/K$, then draw a directed edge $i \rightarrow i+1$. It directs an edge toward the side of the bin on which its conditional mean lies.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge-condition-2} can be interpreted as: we draw a directed edge to $i \rightarrow j$ (for $j = i-1$ or $i+1$) if the probability mass on $[(i-1)/K, i/K]$ is not substantially greater than that of $[(j-1)/K, j/K]$. It directs an edge toward a neighboring bin whose mass is not substantially smaller.

By construction, it is obvious that every vertex has at least one outgoing edge since for positive-mass vertex Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge-condition-1} holds either for $j = i-1$ or $j=i+1$, and for zero-mass vertex Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge-condition-2} holds for both neighbors.


There is at least one bidirectional adjacent pair. The existence of such pair follows from a continuity argument: since 
$1 \rightarrow 2, K-1 \leftarrow K$, and every vertex has at least one outgoing edge, let $k^*\in \{2,\dots,K\}$ be the smallest integer such that there exists an edge $k^* -1 \leftarrow k^*$. Then $k^* -1 \rightarrow k^*$ because every vertex has at least one outgoing edge, and if the outgoing edge for $k^*-1$ is $k^*-1 \rightarrow k^*-2$, then  $k^*$ would not be the smallest vertex as defined. This means that $k^* - 1 \leftrightarrows k^*$ forms a bidirectional pair, thus confirming the existence.

\paragraph{Case 2:  }

    \geq &~ \frac{s_i-0.5}{K} \\
    \geq &~ a_i + \epsilon.

\paragraph{Case 3: }

\begin{align*}

The first step comes from the law of total expectation and $[(j-1)/K, j/K], j=s_i,\dots, t_i$ forms a partition of $[a_i,b_i]$; the second step comes from pulling the first term out $j=s_i$ and then using $\mathbb{E}[Z\mid Z \in [(j-1)/K, j/K]] \geq \mathbb{E}\Big[Z\mid Z \in [s_i/K, (s_i+1)/K]\Big]$ for all $j \geq s_i+1$; the third step comes from Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge-condition-2}; the last step comes from $a_i = (s_i-1)/K,$ $ \epsilon \le 1/(4K)$, so $ a_i + \epsilon \le (s_i-3/4)/K$.

\end{align*}

\begin{align*}

\end{align*}

\begin{align*}    \sum_{d=\lceil\log(3/\delta)\rceil+1}^{ l_i-u_i}q_{ l_i-d} < \sum_{d=\lceil\log(3/\delta)\rceil+1}^{ l_i-u_i}3^{-d} q_{ l_i} .

The analogous argument on the right chain ($d\in\{1,\dots,v_i-r_i\}$) gives

Fix a class $g\in[G]$ and abbreviate
\[
\pi:=\pi_g,\qquad
t:=t_g,\qquad
J:=J_g,\qquad
S:=S_g,
\]
\[
m_j:=m_{g,j},\qquad
a_j:=a_{g,j},\qquad
b_j:=b_{g,j},
\qquad j=1,\ldots,J.
\]
Define the disjoint events
\[
A_0
:=
\left\{
p_g(X)\notin \bigcup_{j=1}^{J}[a_j,b_j]
\right\},
\qquad
A_j
:=
\bigl\{
p_g(X)\in [a_j,b_j]
\bigr\},
\quad j=1,\ldots,J.
\]
Let
\[
\mu_0
:=
\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y(X)=g \mid A_0\bigr)\in[0,1],
\qquad
\mu_j
:=
\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y(X)=g \mid A_j\bigr)\in[a_j,b_j],
\quad j=1,\ldots,J,
\]
where the inclusion $\mu_j\in[a_j,b_j]$ follows from the definition of PICPIs\xspace.
Since the events $A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_J$ partition the sample space,
\begin{align}
\pi
=
\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y(X)=g\bigr)
=
t\mu_0+\sum_{j=1}^{J} m_j\mu_j.
\label{eq:mc_partition_identity}
\end{align}

Now,
\begin{align}
&~\mathbb{P}\Bigl(Y(X)\notin C_{\mathrm{mc}}(X;S),\,Y(X)=g\Bigr)
\nonumber\\
=
&~t\mu_0+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j\mu_j,
\label{eq:mc_miscoverage_numerator}
\end{align}
because class $g$ is omitted precisely on the uncovered region $A_0$ and on those intervals $A_j$ with $j\notin S$.
Dividing Eq.~\eqref{eq:mc_miscoverage_numerator} by $\pi$ gives
\begin{align}
\mathbb{P}\Bigl(Y(X)\notin C_{\mathrm{mc}}(X;S)\mid Y(X)=g\Bigr)
=
\frac{t\mu_0+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j\mu_j}{\pi}.
\label{eq:mc_conditional_error_exact}
\end{align}

We first prove the upper-endpoint bound.
Since $\mu_0\le 1$ and $\mu_j\le b_j$ for every $j\notin S$,
\[
t\mu_0+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j\mu_j
\le
t+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j b_j.
\]
Combining with Eq.~\eqref{eq:mc_conditional_error_exact} yields
\[
\mathbb{P}\Bigl(Y(X)\notin C_{\mathrm{mc}}(X;S)\mid Y(X)=g\Bigr)
\le
\Gamma_{g}^{\mathrm{up}}(S_g).
\]

Next we prove the lower-endpoint bound.
By Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mc_partition_identity} and \eqref{eq:mc_miscoverage_numerator},
\[
t\mu_0+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j\mu_j
=
\pi-\sum_{j\in S} m_j\mu_j.
\]
Since $\mu_j\ge a_j$ for all $j\in S$,
\[
t\mu_0+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j\mu_j
\le
\pi-\sum_{j\in S} m_j a_j.
\]
Combining again with Eq.~\eqref{eq:mc_conditional_error_exact} gives
\[
\mathbb{P}\Bigl(Y(X)\notin C_{\mathrm{mc}}(X;S)\mid Y(X)=g\Bigr)
\le
\Gamma_{g}^{\mathrm{low}}(S_g).
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb{P}\Bigl(Y(X)\notin C_{\mathrm{mc}}(X;S)\mid Y(X)=g\Bigr)
\le
\Gamma_{g}(S_g).
\]

It remains to prove exactness.
Define
\[
U:=t+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j b_j,
\qquad
L:=\pi-\sum_{j\in S} m_j a_j.
\]
We show that the miscoverage numerator in Eq.~ \eqref{eq:mc_miscoverage_numerator} can attain $\min\{U,L\}$.

\emph{Case 1: $U\le L$.}
Set
\[
\mu_0=1,
\qquad
\mu_j=b_j \ \text{ for all } j\notin S.
\]
Then the miscoverage numerator equals $U$.
To make Eq.~\eqref{eq:mc_partition_identity} hold, we need to choose $\mu_j\in[a_j,b_j]$ for $j\in S$ so that
\[
\sum_{j\in S} m_j\mu_j=\pi-U.
\]
This is possible because
\[
U\le L=\pi-\sum_{j\in S} m_j a_j
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\pi-U\ge \sum_{j\in S} m_j a_j,
\]
and, since any admissible law must satisfy
\[
\pi
\le
t+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j b_j+\sum_{j\in S} m_j b_j
=
U+\sum_{j\in S} m_j b_j,
\]
we also have
\[
\pi-U\le \sum_{j\in S} m_j b_j.
\]
Hence $\pi-U$ lies in the interval
\[
\left[\sum_{j\in S} m_j a_j,\ \sum_{j\in S} m_j b_j\right],
\]
so such a choice of $\{\mu_j\}_{j\in S}$ exists.
Therefore equality is attained with value
\[
\frac{U}{\pi}
=
\Gamma_{g}^{\mathrm{up}}(S_g)
=
\Gamma_{g}(S_g).
\]

\emph{Case 2: $L<U$.}
Set
\[
\mu_j=a_j \ \text{ for all } j\in S.
\]
Then the retained intervals contribute exactly $\sum_{j\in S} m_j a_j$, so by Eq.~\eqref{eq:mc_partition_identity} the miscoverage numerator must equal $L$.
It remains to show that one can choose $\mu_0\in[0,1]$ and $\mu_j\in[a_j,b_j]$ for $j\notin S$ so that
\[
t\mu_0+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j\mu_j=L.
\]
The attainable range of the left-hand side is
\[
\left[\sum_{j\notin S} m_j a_j,\ t+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j b_j\right].
\]
Because any admissible law must satisfy
\[
\pi\ge \sum_{j=1}^{J} m_j a_j,
\]
we have
\[
L
=
\pi-\sum_{j\in S} m_j a_j
\ge
\sum_{j\notin S} m_j a_j.
\]
And by assumption,
\[
L<U=t+\sum_{j\notin S} m_j b_j.
\]
Hence $L$ lies in the attainable range, so equality is attained with value
\[
\frac{L}{\pi}
=
\Gamma_{g}^{\mathrm{low}}(S_g)
=
\Gamma_{g}(S_g).
\]

This proves exactness in both cases.

Let $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{PI}}$ denote the event that all intervals returned by Algorithm~1 are genuine disjoint PICPIs\xspace simultaneously.
By assumption,
\[
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{PI}})\ge 1-\delta_{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]

For each class $g\in[G]$ and each candidate interval $I\in\mathcal{A}_{p_g}$, define
\[
Z_k^{(g,I)}
:=
\mathbbm{1}\bigl(p_g(X_k)\in I\bigr),
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,n_{\mathrm{cal}}.
\]
Conditioned on the data-generating distribution, these are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random variables with mean
\[
m_{g}(I)
:=
\mathbb{P}\bigl(p_g(X)\in I\bigr).
\]
Hence Hoeffding's inequality gives, for every fixed $(g,I)$,
\[
\mathbb{P}\left(
\left|
\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{cal}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\mathrm{cal}}} Z_k^{(g,I)}
-
m_{g}(I)
\right|
>
\varepsilon_m
\right)
\le
2e^{-2n_{\mathrm{cal}}\varepsilon_m^2}.
\]
Applying a union bound over all candidate intervals in all classes yields
\[
\mathbb{P}\left(
\sup_{g\in[G]}
\sup_{I\in\mathcal{A}_{p_g}}
\left|
\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{cal}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\mathrm{cal}}} Z_k^{(g,I)}
-
m_{g}(I)
\right|
>
\varepsilon_m
\right)
\le
2N_{\mathrm{cand}}e^{-2n_{\mathrm{cal}}\varepsilon_m^2}
=
\delta_m.
\]
Therefore, with probability at least $1-\delta_m$, we have simultaneously for every class $g$ and every candidate interval $I\in \mathcal{A}_{p_g}$,
\[
\left|
\widehat m_{g}(I)-m_{g}(I)
\right|
\le
\varepsilon_m.

m_{g,j}

\ge

\qquad
