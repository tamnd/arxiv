<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:add-vertex-outside-end}
Let $G$ be a graph with $\kappa(G)=k$, let $S$ be a $k$-cutset of $G$ and let $F$ be an end of $G$ to $S$. <!-- proof-2: body_md -->

For every $u\in Q\setminus X$, the vertex $u$ has at least $k$ neighbors in $V(G)$. Since $|X_G|\le k-1$, at least one of these neighbors lies in $V(G)\setminus X_G$. Thus every vertex of $Q\setminus X$ is adjacent to the connected graph $G-X_G$. Consequently, $G'-X$ is connected. Therefore, $G'$ is $k$-connected.

Since no vertex of $Q$ has a neighbor in $F$, we have $N_{G'}(F)=N_G(F)=S$. Hence $S$ remains a $k$-cut of $G'$.
