<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $(\Omega,g)$ be a prescribed compact connected smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension $n\geq3$ with nonempty smooth boundary.
Let
\[
 q_1,q_2\in C^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),\qquad
 0\notin\operatorname{Spec}_{D}(-\Delta_g+q_j),\quad j=1,2.
\]
If their Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-data}
 \Lambda_{g,q_1}=\Lambda_{g,q_2},
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-conclusion}
 q_1=q_2\quad\text{on }\Omega.
\end{equation}
The maps are compared with the same boundary identification and the same density induced by the prescribed metric.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:cap}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, there is a compact connected smooth extension $N$ of $\Omega$ with a common smooth metric and smooth potentials, still denoted $g,q_1,q_2$, such that both Dirichlet operators on $N$ are invertible.
The extended potentials are equal on the open added region $E=N^\circ\setminus\Omega$.
If $R_j=(-\Delta_g+q_j)_{D,N}^{-1}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap-solutions}
 (R_1F)|_E=(R_2F)|_E\qquad (F\in C_c^\infty(E)).
\end{equation}
Their symmetric Green kernels satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap-kernels}
 G_1(x,y)=G_2(x,y)\qquad (x,y\in E,\ x\ne y).
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\smallskip\noindent\emph{1. Smooth exterior coefficients.}\ 
Extend the known metric and $q_1$ smoothly across a collar of the boundary, and use the same exterior extension for $q_2$.
The pasting is smooth by~\eqref{eq:flat-q}.
For a collar of thickness $\epsilon$, denote the extended manifold by $N_\epsilon$.
A smooth collar diffeomorphism $J_\epsilon:\Omega\to N_\epsilon$, equal to the identity away from the collar, pulls these coefficients back to ones converging smoothly to the original coefficients as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{2. The extension remains nonresonant.}\ 
The pulled-back Dirichlet operators therefore satisfy
\[
 \|\mathcal L_{j,\epsilon}-\mathcal L_{j,0}\|_{H_0^1\to H^{-1}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Since $\mathcal L_{j,0}$ is invertible, openness of the set of bounded invertible operators gives invertibility for all sufficiently small $\epsilon$.
Fix one thickness valid for both potentials and set $N=N_\epsilon$.
This choice uses nonresonance only and permits negative Dirichlet eigenvalues.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{3. A weak transmission argument.}\ 
Let $F\in C_c^\infty(E)$ be real and set $w_1=R_1F$.
Its trace $h=w_1|_{\partial\Omega}$ is smooth.
Since the source is outside $\Omega$, $w_1$ is $L_{g,q_1}$-harmonic there.
Let $v_2$ solve the original $L_{g,q_2}$ Dirichlet problem on $\Omega$ with trace $h$.
Define $\widetilde w$ to equal $v_2$ in $\Omega$ and $w_1$ on $E$.

More precisely, $v_2-w_1|_\Omega\in H_0^1(\Omega)$.
Its zero extension to $N$, added to $w_1$, is an element of $H_0^1(N)$ and is exactly $\widetilde w$.
For a real $\varphi\in H_0^1(N)$, split the weak forms across the original boundary.
The exterior forms and sources coincide.
By~\eqref{eq:weak-DN}, the difference of the interior contributions is
\[
 \big\langle (\Lambda_{g,q_2}-\Lambda_{g,q_1})h,
                 \varphi|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \int_N\bigl(\langle \mathrm{d}\widetilde w,\mathrm{d}\varphi\rangle_g+
                  q_2\widetilde w\varphi\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}V_g
       =\int_E F\varphi\,\mathrm{d}V_g.
\]
Thus $\widetilde w=R_2F$ by invertibility on $N$.
In particular the two solutions agree on $E$, proving~\eqref{eq:cap-solutions}.
This proof contains no minimization of an indefinite quadratic form.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{4. Equality of Green kernels.}\ 
Self-adjointness of the two inverses gives symmetric distribution kernels normalized by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Green-normalization}
 L_{g,q_j,x}G_j(x,y)=\delta_y^g(x),\qquad
 R_jF(x)=\int_NG_j(x,y)F(y)\,\mathrm{d}V_g(y).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:cap-solutions}, tested also in the first variable against functions supported in $E$, gives equality of these kernels on $E\times E$ as distributions.
Elliptic regularity makes them smooth away from the diagonal, proving~\eqref{eq:cap-kernels}.
The kernels need not be positive.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:cross}
Let $D_i$ be bounded connected coordinate domains and let $S_i\Subset D_i$ be nonempty open sets.
Suppose a product solution is given consistently on
\[
 (D_1\times S_2)\cup(S_1\times D_2),
\]
with the sum of its two $L^2$ norms at most $M$.
Suppose $O_i\Subset D_i$ are open sets and every $L_i$-solution $w$ on $D_i$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-interpolation}
 \|w\|_{L^2(O_i)}\leq C\|w\|_{L^2(S_i)}^{\gamma_i}
                         \|w\|_{L^2(D_i)}^{1-\gamma_i},
 \qquad 0<\gamma_i<1,\quad\gamma_1+\gamma_2>1.
\end{equation}
Then the compatible function extends as a product solution to $O_1\times O_2$.
Its $C^m$ norm on every smaller compact subset is bounded by $C_mM$.
The constants are uniform when the coefficient bounds, compact containments, interpolation constants and the positive margin $\gamma_1+\gamma_2-1$ are uniform.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We may replace $O_i$ by $O_i\cup S_i$.
Indeed, if $a=\|w\|_{L^2(S_i)}$ and $b=\|w\|_{L^2(D_i)}$, then $a\leq b$ and $a\leq a^{\gamma_i}b^{1-\gamma_i}$.
The interpolation inequality remains valid on the union with a larger constant.
Both comparison sets used below therefore belong to the region of convergence.
Let $\mathcal H_1(D_1)$ be the closed subspace of $L^2(D_1)$ consisting of distributional $L_1$-solutions.
Restriction
\[
 R:\mathcal H_1(D_1)\longrightarrow L^2(S_1)
\]
is compact by interior elliptic estimates and injective by unique continuation.
The compact positive operator $R^*R$ has an orthonormal eigenbasis $(w_j)$ in $\mathcal H_1(D_1)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-singular}
 (w_i,w_j)_{L^2(S_1)}=\mu_j^2\delta_{ij},\qquad 0<\mu_j\leq1.
\end{equation}
Every fixed power of $j$ is an upper decay bound for these singular values: for each integer $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-decay}
 \mu_j\leq C_N(1+j)^{-N}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, multiply a solution by a fixed smooth cutoff supported in $D_1$ and equal to one near $S_1$.
Its $H^k$ norm is bounded by its $L^2(D_1)$ norm.
Fourier truncation in a containing cube gives a rank $O(A^n)$ approximation with error $O(A^{-k})$.
The minimax characterization of singular values gives $\mu_j\leq C_kj^{-k/n}$.
Taking $k$ sufficiently large proves~\eqref{eq:cross-decay}.
This argument uses interior estimates only.

For $y\in S_2$, expand the first given piece as
\[
 F(x,y)=\sum_jw_j(x)b_j(y),\qquad \|b_j\|_{L^2(S_2)}\leq M.
\]
Its coefficients extend to $D_2$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-coefficients}
 b_j(y)=\mu_j^{-2}\int_{S_1}F(x,y)\overline{w_j(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,
 \qquad \|b_j\|_{L^2(D_2)}\leq M\mu_j^{-1}.
\end{equation}
They solve $L_2b_j=0$, and~\eqref{eq:cross-singular} shows agreement with the original coefficients on $S_2$.
Applying~\eqref{eq:cross-interpolation} yields
\[
 \|w_j\|_{L^2(O_1)}\leq C\mu_j^{\gamma_1},\qquad
 \|b_j\|_{L^2(O_2)}\leq CM\mu_j^{\gamma_2-1}.
\]
Thus the series converges absolutely in $L^2(O_1\times O_2)$, because its summands are bounded by $CM\mu_j^{\gamma_1+\gamma_2-1}$ and~\eqref{eq:cross-decay} makes their sum finite.
More explicitly, put $\eta=\gamma_1+\gamma_2-1>0$ and choose $N$ with $N\eta>1$.
Then
\[
 \sum_j\|w_j(x)b_j(y)\|_{L^2(O_1\times O_2)}
 \leq CM C_N^\eta\sum_j(1+j)^{-N\eta}<\infty.
\]
For a uniform margin $\eta\geq\eta_0>0$, choose one $N$ with $N\eta_0>1$.
The slice equalities initially hold almost everywhere by Fubini's theorem; after passage to the limit, elliptic regularity makes these distributional equalities pointwise on the overlaps.
The limit solves both equations distributionally.
Interior elliptic estimates for their sum give smoothness and the stated compact-subset bounds.

We verify agreement with the second piece as well.
If $z\in L^2(S_1)$ is orthogonal to the closure of $R\mathcal H_1(D_1)$, then
\[
 y\longmapsto\int_{S_1}F(x,y)\overline{z(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
\]
solves $L_2w=0$ on $D_2$ and vanishes on $S_2$.
Hence it vanishes on $D_2$.
Every second-piece slice therefore belongs to that closed range.
Its expansion in the orthonormal basis $(w_j/\mu_j)$ has coefficients $\mu_jb_j(y)$ by~\eqref{eq:cross-coefficients}.
The same series is consequently the given second piece on $S_1\times O_2$.
It agrees with the first piece on $O_1\times S_2$ by its construction.
No positive lower bound for $\mu_j$ has been used, so all estimates have the claimed uniformity.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:strict-product}
Let $\rho$ be smooth near $z_0=(x_0,y_0)$ with $\rho(z_0)=0$ and $\mathrm{d}_x\rho,\mathrm{d}_y\rho\ne0$.
The norms and Hessians below use the two principal metrics.
Define
\[
 a_i=\frac{\nabla_i\rho}{|\nabla_i\rho|^2},\qquad H=\operatorname{Hess}\rho.
\]
Suppose at $z_0$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-product-condition}
 H((a_1,-a_2),(a_1,-a_2))+H((z_1,z_2),(z_1,z_2))>0
\end{equation}
whenever $z_i\perp a_i$ and $|z_i|=|a_i|$.
Every product solution given on the side $\{\rho>0\}$ near $z_0$ extends to a neighborhood of $z_0$.
The construction uses data on two compact product sets strictly inside that side.
The output neighborhoods and their compact-subset bounds are uniform under small smooth perturbations preserving the strict inequalities and the chosen data bounds.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\smallskip\noindent\emph{1. Splitting the Hessian between the factors.}\ 
We find real symmetric forms $P_i$ and $K>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hessian-splitting}
 P_i(a_i,a_i)+P_i(z_i,z_i)>0,
 \qquad H+K\,\mathrm{d}\rho\otimes \mathrm{d}\rho>P_1\oplus P_2.
\end{equation}
Here and below a strict matrix inequality means positive definiteness.
We give the finite-dimensional separation argument.
The dual tests for the open cone consisting of positive definite forms plus the admissible $P_1\oplus P_2$ are positive semidefinite matrices $D$ whose diagonal blocks have the form
\[
 D_{ii}=d_i a_i\otimes a_i+S_i,
 \quad d_i\geq0,\quad S_i\geq0\text{ on }a_i^\perp,
 \quad\operatorname{tr}S_i=d_i|a_i|^2.
\]
Indeed, the dual cone in each block is generated by $a_i\otimes a_i+z_i\otimes z_i$ with $z_i$ as in the hypothesis.
Normalize $\operatorname{tr}D=1$.

It suffices to show $H:D>0$ on the normalized tests with $D\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0$.
The block equations imply $d_1=d_2=d$ and that every mixed normal--tangential block vanishes.
To verify this directly, write $\ell_1=\mathrm{d}_x\rho$ and $\ell_2=\mathrm{d}_y\rho$ for the partial covectors, so $\ell_i(a_i)=1$ and $\ell_i$ vanishes on $a_i^\perp$.
Then
$D_{11}\ell_1=d_1a_1$, $D_{22}\ell_2=d_2a_2$, and $D\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0$ forces $D_{12}\ell_2=-d_1a_1$ and $D_{21}\ell_1=-d_2a_2$.
Pairing these identities with $\ell_1,\ell_2$ and using symmetry gives $d_1=d_2$.
They also give the claimed vanishing of all normal--tangential components.
Thus
\[
 D=d(a_1,-a_2)\otimes(a_1,-a_2)+D_\perp,
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}(D_\perp)_{ii}=d|a_i|^2.
\]
Here $D_\perp\geq0$ acts on the two tangential spaces.
The value $d=0$ would force $D=0$, which is impossible after normalization.

Among positive semidefinite tangential matrices with these two prescribed traces, every extreme point has rank one.
In fact, on an image of rank $r\geq2$, symmetric perturbations have dimension $r(r+1)/2>2$.
One can therefore preserve both traces with a nonzero symmetric perturbation; sufficiently small perturbations of either sign preserve positivity.
This excludes such a matrix from being extreme.
The rank-one matrices are $d(z_1,z_2)\otimes(z_1,z_2)$ with $|z_i|=|a_i|$.
The hypothesis gives $H:D>0$ on these extreme tests, and hence on their compact convex hull.
Compactness gives a positive minimum for the normalized tests with $D\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0$.
For all other tests, increasing $K$ makes $H:D+K\,D(\mathrm{d}\rho,\mathrm{d}\rho)$ positive uniformly; a compactness argument proves this assertion by taking a subsequence if it failed.
Convex separation now yields~\eqref{eq:Hessian-splitting}.

Prescribe the gradients $\mathrm{d}_i\rho$ and the Hessians $P_i$ of functions $\theta_i$ vanishing at the two respective points.
These gradients are nonzero and~\eqref{eq:Hessian-splitting} makes each function a strict weight after shrinking the neighborhoods.
Taylor's formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-gap}
 \theta_1(x)+\theta_2(y)
 \leq\widetilde\rho(x,y)-c(|x-x_0|^2+|y-y_0|^2),
 \qquad \widetilde\rho=\rho+K\rho^2/2.
\end{equation}
The positive side of $\widetilde\rho$ is that of $\rho$ near $z_0$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{2. The cross sets and the interpolation exponents.}\ 
Use local coordinates $(s_i,z_i)$ with $s_i=\theta_i$.
Choose $\kappa>0$ sufficiently small that $\varphi(s_i)=s_i-\kappa s_i^2$ is still a strict weight.
Then choose successively a small lateral radius $R$, a height $\ell\ll cR^2$, and $e\ll\kappa\ell^2$.
Set
\[
 D_i=\{-\ell<s_i<\ell+4e,\ |z_i|<R\}.
\]
Choose a cutoff supported in this cylinder, equal to one in the inner lateral half and for $-\ell+3e<s_i<\ell+2e$, with bottom transition in $(-\ell+2e,-\ell+3e)$ and top transition in $(\ell+2e,\ell+3e)$.
Let $S_i\Subset D_i$ contain slightly enlarged neighborhoods of its top and lateral derivative supports, and an inner slice near $s_i=\ell+5e/4$.

The products $D_1\times S_2$ and $S_1\times D_2$ lie strictly on the given side.
On a top slice the sum of the two $s$ coordinates is positive.
On a lateral slice this sum is greater than $-2\ell$, while the quadratic gap in~\eqref{eq:weight-gap} is bounded below by a fixed multiple of $R^2$.
The choices therefore give a positive margin.
By keeping the cutoff supports compact and, if necessary, slightly reducing the cylinder end faces, both data sets have closures in $\{\rho>\epsilon\}$ for one $\epsilon>0$.

Apply~\eqref{eq:strict-Carleman} to the cutoff times an $L_i$-solution.
Normalize its $L^2(D_i)$ norm to one.
Interior estimates bound the bottom derivative terms by that norm with weight $\varphi(-\ell+3e)$.
The other derivative terms are bounded by its $L^2(S_i)$ norm with weight at most $\varphi(\ell+3e)$.
On a smaller inner cylinder starting at $s_i=-e$, the weight is at least $\varphi(-e)$.
Absorbing the polynomial factors for $\tau\geq\tau_0$ gives
\[
 \|w\|_{L^2(O_i)}
 \leq C\left(\mathrm{e}^{-a\tau}\|w\|_{L^2(D_i)}
              +\mathrm{e}^{b\tau}\|w\|_{L^2(S_i)}\right),
\]
where $a=\varphi(-e)-\varphi(-\ell+3e)>0$ and $b=\varphi(\ell+3e)-\varphi(-e)>0$.
Balancing the exponentials proves~\eqref{eq:cross-interpolation} with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-exponent}
 \gamma_i=\frac{\varphi(-e)-\varphi(-\ell+3e)}
                  {\varphi(\ell+3e)-\varphi(-\ell+3e)}>\frac12.
\end{equation}
At $e=0$ this quotient is $1/2+\kappa\ell/2$; the choice $e\ll\kappa\ell^2$ preserves the strict inequality.
An explicit margin follows from
\[
 a+b=2\ell(1-6\kappa e),\qquad
 a-b=2\kappa\ell^2-8e+16\kappa e^2.
\]
Choosing $\kappa\ell<1/8$ and $0<e<\kappa\ell^2/16$ gives $a,b>0$ and $\gamma_i=a/(a+b)>1/2$.
When the small norm is positive, the balancing value is
\[
 \tau=(a+b)^{-1}\log\frac{\|w\|_{L^2(D_i)}}{\|w\|_{L^2(S_i)}}.
\]
If the small norm is zero, unique continuation gives $w=0$.
If the balancing parameter is below $\tau_0$, the same estimate follows by increasing $C$ and using the normalized large norm.
The smaller output cylinder may include a connected inner tube from the origin to the top slice.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{3. Construction and agreement.}\ 
Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} now produces a product solution near $z_0$.
It agrees with the old solution on the nearby positive side.
Indeed, from a sufficiently close positive-side point, increase $s_2$ into its top slice.
The partial derivative $\partial_{s_2}\rho$ is positive after decreasing the neighborhood, so the segment remains in the given side and in the larger output cylinder.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{1. Polynomial norms and the degree factors.}\ 
Each operator changes the angular degree by exactly one, so different input degrees have orthogonal images for either operator.
It suffices to work on a fixed degree $k\geq1$.
Identify that component with a harmonic homogeneous polynomial $v(z)$ of degree $k$ on a tangent space.
In the Fischer inner product, monomials $z^\alpha$ are orthogonal and have squared norm $\alpha!$.
Differentiation $a_i=\partial_{z_i}$ is adjoint to multiplication $M_i=z_i$ on the full polynomial space.

On harmonic homogeneous polynomials the Fischer squared norm is the normalized sphere squared norm multiplied by
\[
 N_k=n(n+2)\cdots(n+2k-2),\qquad N_0=1.
\]
One direct verification uses integration with density $(2\pi)^{-n/2}\mathrm{e}^{-|z|^2/2}$.
For harmonic homogeneous $v$, the differentiated exponential satisfies
$v(\partial_z)\mathrm{e}^{-|z|^2/2}=(-1)^kv(z)\mathrm{e}^{-|z|^2/2}$;
the trace terms vanish because $\Delta_zv=0$.
Integration by parts identifies this integral inner product with the Fischer one on degree $k$.
Polar integration gives the radial moment $N_k$, proving the norm formula.

Set $d=k+n/2-1$.
If $q_i$ are harmonic of degree $k$ and $s=\sum_i a_iq_i$, the harmonic projection of $\sum_iM_iq_i$ is
\[
 \Pi_{k+1}\sum_iM_iq_i
       =\sum_iM_iq_i-\frac{|z|^2}{2d}s.
\]
Indeed, $s$ is harmonic of degree $k-1$,
$\Delta_z\sum_iM_iq_i=2s$, and $\Delta_z(|z|^2s)=4ds$.
The second term is orthogonal to harmonic polynomials because multiplication by $|z|^2$ is adjoint to $\Delta_z$.
Its squared norm is $d^{-1}\|s\|_F^2$, where $F$ denotes the Fischer norm.
Expanding the first squared norm using the multiplication and differentiation adjoints gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic-projection-norm}
 \left\|\Pi_{k+1}\sum_iM_iq_i\right\|_F^2
 =\sum_i\|q_i\|_F^2+
     \sum_{i,j}(a_jq_i,a_iq_j)_F
       -\frac1d\left\|\sum_i a_iq_i\right\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
Since $N_{k+1}/N_k=2d+2$ and $N_k/N_{k-1}=2d$, the sphere adjoint is
$A_i^*|_{\mathcal H_k}=(2d+2)\Pi_{k+1}M_i$.

At degree $k$, write $D_i^\pm=\pm\nabla_i+kF_i$.
Formula~\eqref{eq:harmonic-projection-norm} shows that $N_k$ times the left side of~\eqref{eq:raising-estimate} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raising-expanded}
 (2d+2)\left[\|D^+v\|_F^2+
    \sum_{i,j}(a_jD_i^+v,a_iD_j^+v)_F-\frac1d\left\|\sum_i a_iD_i^+v\right\|_F^2\right]
       -2d\left\|\sum_i a_iD_i^-v\right\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
All pairings here include integration in the center variable.
We take real parts of the cross pairings when the functions are complex.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{2. Integration by parts and curvature terms.}\ 
Let $C_{ij}=2k(\operatorname{Hess}_\gamma F_\delta)_{ij}$ and define
\[
 C[av]=\int_Y\sum_{i,j}C_{ij}(a_iv,a_jv)_F\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma.
\]
Covariant integration by parts yields
\begin{align}
 \sum_{i,j}(a_jD_i^+v,a_iD_j^+v)_F
  &=\left\|\sum_i a_iD_i^-v\right\|_F^2-C[av]
          +O(k^2)\|v\|_F^2,\label{eq:raising-ibp-one}\\
 \left\|\sum_i a_iD_i^+v\right\|_F^2
  &\leq k\|D^-v\|_F^2-C[av]+O(k^2)\|v\|_F^2,
       \label{eq:raising-ibp-two}\\

  &=\|D^+v\|_F^2+\int_Y\operatorname{tr}C\,|v|_F^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma.
       \label{eq:raising-ibp-three}
\end{align}
To check the first identity, move a $D_i^+$ across the pairing and commute $D_i^-D_j^+$ into $D_j^+D_i^- -C_{ij}$, including the curvature commutator of the horizontal covariant derivatives.
The reordered pairing is the displayed divergence square.
For the second identity the same operations leave a pairing of $(a_iD_j^-v)_{i,j}$ with its transposition.
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds it by $k\|D^-v\|_F^2$, since
$\sum_i\|a_iw\|_F^2=k\|w\|_F^2$
on degree $k$.
A curvature commutator acts as a sum of rotations on degree-$k$ polynomials, of norm $O(k)$; the two contractions contribute one further factor $k$.
Thus both error bounds are $O(k^2)\|v\|_F^2$ with fixed geometric constants.
Expanding the two squares and integrating the cross terms proves~\eqref{eq:raising-ibp-three}.

\begin{align}\label{eq:raising-intermediate}
 (2+2/d)\left[d\|D^+v\|_F^2-
       \left\|\sum_i a_iD_i^+v\right\|_F^2-dC[av]\right]
 +2\left\|\sum_i a_iD_i^-v\right\|_F^2
       +O(k^3)\|v\|_F^2.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{3. Retaining both derivative strengths.}\

 \leq{}&(1-\zeta)(k+n-1)|D^+v|_F^2\notag\\

                         +8k^3\beta^2|v|_F^2\right),
 \qquad\zeta=c_1/\beta.

$\sum_i a_iM_i=k+n-1$

$|\sum_iF_ia_iv|_F^2\leq k\beta^2|v|_F^2$.

 -(2+2/d)\left[(d-1+\vartheta)C[av]
       +(1-\vartheta)k\int_Y\operatorname{tr}C\,|v|_F^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma\right].
\end{equation}
The two coefficients are positive for $n\geq3$, $k\geq1$.
The inequality
$\operatorname{Hess}_\gamma F_\delta\leq-c_0\beta\gamma$
and the contraction identity imply that~\eqref{eq:raising-Hessian-form} is at least
$c k^3\int_Y\beta|v|_F^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma$.
The choices can be made uniformly in $k$.
Take $0<\vartheta<\min\{1/2,(n-2)/(4(n-1))\}$, giving $d-k-\vartheta(n-1)\geq(n-2)/4$.
Since $d-1+\vartheta\geq k/2$, the Hessian form is bounded below by $c_H k^3\int\beta|v|_F^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma$ for a constant $c_H>0$ independent of $k$.
The negative scalar contribution from~\eqref{eq:raising-convex-bound} is at most $C\vartheta c_1 k^3\int\beta|v|_F^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma$.
First fix $c_1>0$ with $C\vartheta c_1<c_H/4$.
Then increase $\beta_0$ to absorb the curvature error and to ensure $\zeta\leq1$ and $2-2(2+2/d)\vartheta\zeta\geq0$ for every $k\geq1$.
The last expression is the remaining coefficient of the divergence square, and its uniform nonnegativity follows from $d\geq3/2$.
We retain positive multiples of
\[
 \|D^+v\|_F^2,\qquad
 \int_Y\frac{k}{\beta}|D^+v|_F^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma,\qquad
 k^3\int_Y\beta|v|_F^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma.
\]

Finally,
\[
 \|D^+v\|_F^2
 =\|\nabla v\|_F^2+k^2\int\beta^2|v|_F^2
               -k\int\Delta_\gamma F_\delta\,|v|_F^2
 \geq\|\nabla v\|_F^2+k^2\int\beta^2|v|_F^2.
\]
For the weighted derivative use the pointwise bound
\[
 \frac{k}{\beta}|\nabla v|_F^2
       \leq2\frac{k}{\beta}|D^+v|_F^2+2k^3\beta|v|_F^2.
\]
Division by $N_k$ gives exactly the four quantities in~\eqref{eq:N-norm} at this degree, up to fixed constants, since $(1+k)^s$ and $k^s$ are comparable for $k\geq1$.
Sum the orthogonal components.
Approximation by finite spherical harmonic sums proves the assertion for every smooth $\varphi$ in the statement.

The proof is complete.

\label{lem:coercivity}
For the operator $\mathcal{P}_\delta$ in~\eqref{eq:density-PDE}, with $Q=\gamma^{-1}$, choose $\beta_0$ sufficiently large and $\varepsilon_0>0$ sufficiently small in terms of the fixed smooth coefficients and the constants in~\eqref{eq:profile-bounds}.

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-coercivity}
 \mathcal{N}_\delta(\varphi)
       \leq C\|\mathcal{P}_\delta\varphi\|_{L^2(S_\gamma Y)}
\end{equation}

A dagger denotes the full adjoint for $\mathrm{d}V_\gamma(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_y(\omega)$.
A star still denotes the sphere adjoint only.
Covariant integration and the degree shift $BA_i=A_i(B-1)$ give
\[
 Z:=\mathcal{T}^\flat-\mathcal{T}^\dagger=\sum_iF_iA_i.
\]
The expansion of the two squared norms is the exact identity
\begin{align}\label{eq:squared-norm-identity}
 \|(2\mathcal{T}+U)\varphi\|_2^2
  -\|(2\mathcal{T}^\flat+U^\dagger)\varphi\|_2^2
  =4\bigl(\|\mathcal{T}\varphi\|_2^2
          -\|\mathcal{T}^\flat\varphi\|_2^2\bigr)
             +(\mathcal E\varphi,\varphi),
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error-operator}
 \mathcal E=2[\mathcal{T}^\dagger,U]+2[U^\dagger,\mathcal{T}]
                 +[U^\dagger,U]-2\bigl(UZ+(UZ)^\dagger\bigr).
\end{equation}
For example, substitute $\mathcal{T}^\flat=\mathcal{T}^\dagger+Z$ in the mixed terms on the left.
They become the first two commutators and the last two products in~\eqref{eq:error-operator}.
This also verifies the adjoint signs.

We prove the form bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error-form-bound}
 |(\mathcal E\varphi,\varphi)|\leq C\varepsilon
                              \mathcal{N}_\delta(\varphi)^2.
\end{equation}
To track all derivative orders, say a coefficient family has indices $(m,s)$ if the coefficient of $\partial_y^\alpha$ has angular order $m-|\alpha|$ and size $O(\beta^{s-|\alpha|})$, with the derivative allowances already specified.
Products add these indices.
Taking a center adjoint differentiates a coefficient, which multiplies its allowance by $\beta$ while removing one center derivative; hence it preserves the indices.
The leading angular symbols here are scalar.
Their commutator loses one angular order, and a center derivative hitting a coefficient likewise loses one total derivative order.
Thus a commutator loses one in the first index and preserves the sum of the second indices.

The indices of $\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^\dagger,U,Z$ are respectively
\[
 (2,1),\qquad(2,1),\qquad(2,0),\qquad(1,1).
\]
Every $U$ carries a factor $\varepsilon$, and $\varepsilon\leq1$.
Hence~\eqref{eq:error-operator} has total indices at most $(3,1)$ and a factor $O(\varepsilon)$.
We must also exclude a third derivative in the center variable; a total-order statement alone does not do this.

Use a smooth orthonormal sphere trivialization, and write the center density as $\mu(y)\mathrm{d}y$.
The sphere measure is fixed in this trivialization.
By~\eqref{eq:second-base-coefficient},
\[
 U=a^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+B^i\partial_i+C,
 \qquad a^{ij}=rQ^{ij}=a^{ji}\in\mathbb{R}.
\]
The coefficients $B^i,C$ are angular operators.
Direct integration by parts gives
\begin{align*}
 U^\dagger-U={}&
  \bigl(2\mu^{-1}\partial_j(\mu a^{ij})-B^i-(B^i)^*\bigr)\partial_i\\
 &+\mu^{-1}\partial_i\partial_j(\mu a^{ij})
    -\mu^{-1}\partial_i\bigl(\mu(B^i)^*\bigr)+C^*-C.
\end{align*}
In particular, this difference has at most one center derivative.
In $[\mathcal{T}^\dagger,U]$ and $[U^\dagger,\mathcal{T}]$, a possible third-center coefficient is a commutator of an angular coefficient with the scalar $a^{ij}$, and therefore is zero.
In $[U^\dagger,U]=[U^\dagger-U,U]$, the same argument cancels the possible third-center coefficient.
The product $UZ$ has at most two center derivatives.
For example, write $U^\dagger-U=V^i\partial_i+V^0$.
The coefficient of a third center derivative in $[U^\dagger-U,U]$ is a sum of $[V^i,a^{jk}]$.
It vanishes because $a^{jk}$ is a scalar function of the center alone.
Fourth center derivatives already canceled in passing to this commutator.
The same calculation applies to the one-center leading terms in the other two commutators; a derivative hitting a coefficient leaves at most two center derivatives.
Converting horizontal derivatives to coordinate derivatives changes only lower center orders and adds bounded angular differential operators.
Therefore every term of $\mathcal E$ belongs to one of the following three classes, or a lower class:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error-three-types}
 \varepsilon\beta^{-1}\Psi^1\partial_y^2,
 \qquad \varepsilon\Psi^2\partial_y,
 \qquad \varepsilon\beta\Psi^3.
\end{equation}

For the first class integrate once in the center variable.
The main form is bounded by
\[
 C\varepsilon\int_Y\beta^{-1}\|\partial_y\varphi\|_{1/2}^2\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma.
\]
Differentiating its coefficient costs one factor $\beta$ and leaves an $\varepsilon\Psi^1\partial_y$ term.
This includes the weight derivative: $|\mathrm{d}\beta|\leq C\beta^2$ implies $|\mathrm{d}(\beta^{-1})|\leq C$.
For the second class the angular Sobolev pairing gives
\begin{align*}
 |(\varepsilon\Psi^2\partial_y\varphi,\varphi)|
 &\leq C\varepsilon\int_Y
          \|\partial_y\varphi\|_{1/2}\|\varphi\|_{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma\\
 &\leq C\varepsilon\int_Y
    \bigl(\beta^{-1}\|\partial_y\varphi\|_{1/2}^2
                       +\beta\|\varphi\|_{3/2}^2\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}V_\gamma.
\end{align*}
The third class has form bound
$C\varepsilon\int_Y\beta\|\varphi\|_{3/2}^2\mathrm{d}V_\gamma$.
The lower term produced by the first integration satisfies the same bounds.
The difference between a coordinate derivative and a horizontal derivative is a bounded angular first derivative; its contribution is again controlled by the last two terms of~\eqref{eq:N-norm}, since $\beta\geq1$.
These estimates prove~\eqref{eq:error-form-bound}.
The angular pairings use $\Psi^1:H^{1/2}\to H^{-1/2}$, $\Psi^2:H^{1/2}\to H^{-3/2}$, and $\Psi^3:H^{3/2}\to H^{-3/2}$.
Together with the single center integration by parts, they control all three classes without requiring two center derivatives of $\varphi$ in $\mathcal{N}_\delta$.
They use finitely many angular symbol seminorms and the stated finite center derivative allowances.

Choose $\varepsilon_0$ so that~\eqref{eq:error-form-bound} is smaller than half the positive term supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:raising} in~\eqref{eq:squared-norm-identity}.
The second squared norm on the left is nonnegative, so
\[
 \mathcal{N}_\delta(\varphi)\leq C\|(2\mathcal{T}+U)\varphi\|_2.
\]
Finally, the bounded order-one family $J$ satisfies
\[
 \|J\varphi\|_2\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^2(Y;H^1(S_y))}
                   \leq C\beta_0^{-1}\mathcal{N}_\delta(\varphi).
\]
Increase $\beta_0$ to absorb it and use~\eqref{eq:P-decomposition}.
If the preceding estimate has constant $C_*$ and the bound for $J$ has constant $C_J$, take $\beta_0\geq2C_*C_J$.
Then
\[
 \mathcal{N}_\delta(\varphi)
 \leq C_*\|\mathcal{P}_\delta\varphi\|_2
       +\tfrac12\mathcal{N}_\delta(\varphi).
\]
Moving the last term to the left gives a constant $2C_*$ independent of $\delta$.
This proves~\eqref{eq:full-coercivity} for the actual operator, including all its second-center and angular terms.

The proof is complete.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{1. The constant component is fixed exactly.}\ 
Equation~\eqref{eq:reproduction} gives
\[
 \int_{S_y}(\psi_\delta-1)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_y=0.
\]
Therefore $\varphi_\delta=\chi(\psi_\delta-1)$ has zero fiber mean and is compactly base-supported.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{2. The operator applied to the constant is bounded.}\ 
In~\eqref{eq:P-decomposition}, $\mathcal{T}1=0$ because $B1=0$ and $\nabla1=0$.
The terms of $U$ with a base derivative also vanish on $1$.
Its remaining angular operator has uniformly bounded symbol seminorms of order two, with coefficient $O(\varepsilon)$.
Applied to the fixed smooth function $1$, it is bounded in $L^2$ uniformly in $\delta$.
The same holds for $J1$, by its fixed order-one bounds.

 \|\chi\mathcal{P}_\delta1\|_{L^2}\leq C.
\end{equation}
The cancellation preceding~\eqref{eq:P-decomposition} is important: it prevents a spurious term growing like $\beta_\delta$ in this estimate.

Since $\chi$ depends on the base alone, angular operators commute with it.

\[
 [2\mathcal{T},\chi]z
       =2\sum_iA_i^*((\nabla_i\chi)z).
\]
For the second-base term,
\[
 [rQ^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j,\chi]z
       =2rQ^{ij}(\partial_i\chi)\partial_jz
                       +rQ^{ij}(\partial_i\partial_j\chi)z.
\]
The first-base terms of $U$ contribute bounded angular order-one operators on the same fixed cutoff region.
The zero-base term and $J$ commute with $\chi$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:commutator-bound}
 \|[\mathcal{P}_\delta,\chi]z\|_{L^2}
 \leq C_\chi\left(
     \|z\|_{L^2(D_\chi;H^1(S_y))}
          +\|r_\delta\nabla z\|_{L^2(S_\gamma D_\chi)}\right).
\end{equation}
All coefficients in this inequality are bounded independently of $\delta$; for example the mixed coefficients have size $O(r\beta)$, which is bounded under~\eqref{eq:coercivity-conditions}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} bounds the two norms on the right for $z=\psi_\delta-1$.
This uses only the known lower region and fixed-positive-separation region described in~\eqref{eq:cutoff-support}; no bound for the continued kernel at uncontrolled shrinking separation is substituted here.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-density-equation}
 \mathcal{P}_\delta\varphi_\delta
  =-\chi\mathcal{P}_\delta1
       +[\mathcal{P}_\delta,\chi](\psi_\delta-1).

\smallskip\noindent\emph{1. Normalization on one common coordinate neighborhood.}\ 
Choose the $n+1$ sources constructed in Section~\ref{subsec:source-jets}.
Shrink the coordinate neighborhood so that $w_j>1/2$ and the differentials of $v_1^k=u_1^k/w_1$ form a basis.
The transformed objects in~\eqref{eq:normalized-objects} satisfy the local data specified in Section~\ref{subsec:sphere-geometry}.
In particular the normalized metrics and kernels agree on the known side, by the assumed equalities for $G_j$ and $w_j$.
The two transformed metrics are pointwise conformal on the whole common neighborhood, not merely on the known side.

Choose the geometry, profile parameters and one spatial dilation as in Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}.
The finite family of normalized functions is fixed before these choices.
The density bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:density-bound} is available for every $\delta>0$, with that same spatial dilation.
We now keep all notation in the rescaled coordinate chart until the final step.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{2. Reproduction of constants gives a first-order remainder.}\ 
For each $k$, equations~\eqref{eq:transfer-representation} and~\eqref{eq:reproduction} imply
\begin{align}\label{eq:paired-difference}
 v_{2,\lambda}^k(y)-v_{1,\lambda}^k(y)
   =\int_{S_y}\psi_\delta(y,\omega)
       \bigl[&v_{1,\lambda}^k(\exp_y^\gamma(r_\delta(y)\omega))\notag\\
             &-v_{1,\lambda}^k(y)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_y(\omega).
\end{align}
On the fixed compact set containing these balls, the first function has bounded gradient.
Along the radial geodesic, the fundamental theorem of calculus therefore gives
\[
 |v_{1,\lambda}^k(\exp_y^\gamma(r_\delta\omega))-v_{1,\lambda}^k(y)|
       \leq C_k r_\delta(y).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz with the probability measure $\mathrm{d}\sigma_y$ gives
\[
 |v_{2,\lambda}^k(y)-v_{1,\lambda}^k(y)|
       \leq C_k r_\delta(y)\|\psi_\delta(y,\cdot)\|_{L^2(S_y)}.
\]
Multiply by $\chi/r_\delta$, square and integrate.
The fixed coordinate volume and $\mathrm{d}V_\gamma$ are comparable, so Lemma~\ref{lem:density-bound} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-order-moment}
 \left\|\frac{\chi(v_{2,\lambda}^k-v_{1,\lambda}^k)}{r_\delta}
                \right\|_{L^2(Y)}\leq C_k.
\end{equation}
Only constant reproduction was used.
No coordinate reproduction, Hessian spanning condition or second-order Taylor cancellation is needed.

Choose a connected neighborhood $V_0$ of the rescaled contact point with compact closure in $\{\chi=1,t<0\}$.
Such a set exists because $\chi=1$ near $0$ and $t(0)<0$.
On this fixed closure, $t\leq-a$ for some $a>0$.

\[
 \sup_{V_0}r_\delta\leq R_*\delta^p \mathrm{e}^{-pa/\delta}\longrightarrow0.

\[
 \|v_{2,\lambda}^k-v_{1,\lambda}^k\|_{L^2(V_0)}
      \leq C_k\sup_{V_0}r_\delta\longrightarrow0.
\]
The left side is independent of $\delta$.

Only the representing spheres and densities vary.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-ratios}
 v_1^k=v_2^k=:v^k,\qquad 1\leq k\leq n.
\end{equation}
The original first derivatives remain a basis on this neighborhood by their initial choice.
The neighborhood can be chosen to meet $W$.

 \Delta_gv^k+2\langle \mathrm{d}\log w_j,\mathrm{d}v^k\rangle_g=0.

 \langle \mathrm{d}\log(w_1/w_2),\mathrm{d}v^k\rangle_g=0,

\smallskip\noindent\emph{1. A nonempty region of equality.}\ 
By Section~\ref{subsec:boundary-jets} and Lemma~\ref{lem:cap}, form the common fixed nonresonant extension $N$, the exterior collar $E$, and its chosen component $E_0$.
Fix $U_0\Subset E_0$ as above.
Let $W$ be the connected component containing $E_0$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maximal-equality-region}
 \operatorname{int}_{N^\circ}\{x:q_1(x)=q_2(x)\}.
\end{equation}
This is a nonempty connected open set.
The purpose of the remainder of the proof is to show $W=N^\circ$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{2. All source solutions agree on this entire region.}\ 
For every real $f\in C_c^\infty(U_0)$, the difference $R_1f-R_2f$ vanishes on $E_0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:cap}.
On $W$ it solves the common homogeneous equation: the source terms cancel, including on $U_0$, and the potentials are equal there.
Unique continuation on the connected set $W$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-equality-W}
 R_1f=R_2f\quad\hbox{on }W
                 \qquad(f\in C_c^\infty(U_0)).
\end{equation}

We also need the Green kernels on $W\times W$, not just the source solutions.
Fix $x\in W$.
By~\eqref{eq:source-equality-W} and the common volume density, the kernels agree as distributions in the second variable on $U_0$, and hence pointwise there away from a possible pole.
On $W\setminus\{x\}$ their difference solves the same homogeneous equation in that variable, because the potentials coincide on $W$.
Since $n\geq3$, a path through $x$ can be diverted inside a punctured coordinate ball, so $W\setminus\{x\}$ is connected.
The set $U_0\setminus\{x\}$ contains a nonempty open subset.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Green-equality-W}
 G_1(x,y)=G_2(x,y)\quad(x,y\in W,\ x\ne y).
\end{equation}
This step does not require a nonresonant Dirichlet problem on $W$ itself.
It compares the two inverses on the fixed compact extension $N$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{3. A smooth touching ball at an interior frontier.}\ 
Suppose that $W\ne N^\circ$.
Connectedness of $N^\circ$ implies that the relative frontier of $W$ in $N^\circ$ is nonempty.
Choose $p_0$ on it.
Since $\overline{U_0}\Subset W$, take a coordinate chart centered at $p_0$, disjoint from $\overline{U_0}$, whose image contains $\overline{B(0,R)}$.

Choose $c$ in the coordinate image of $W$ with $|c|<R/4$.
Let
\[
 F=\overline{B(0,R/2)}\setminus W
\]
within that chart.
It is compact and contains $0$.
Since $c\in W$ and $W$ is open, its distance $r=\operatorname{dist}(c,F)$ is positive; also $r\leq|c|<R/4$.
The distance is attained at a point $p\in F$.
The closed ball $\overline{B(c,r)}$ lies in $B(0,R/2)$ because $|c|+r<R/2$.
Its open interior is contained in $W$, while its sphere meets the complement at $p$.

Near $p$, the sphere is a smooth hypersurface and its inner side lies in $W$.
This supplies precisely the one-sided smooth geometry required in Lemma~\ref{lem:local-recovery}.
No smoothness of the whole frontier of~\eqref{eq:maximal-equality-region} has been assumed.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{4. Elimination of the frontier.}\ 
The potentials agree on $B(c,r)$ by construction.
Equations~\eqref{eq:source-equality-W} and~\eqref{eq:Green-equality-W} provide the paired source solutions and the common Green data there.
The touching point is outside $\overline{U_0}$, so Section~\ref{subsec:source-jets} supplies the local normalizing functions and their first derivatives.
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-recovery} gives equality of the potentials on a connected neighborhood of $p$ meeting $B(c,r)$.

This neighborhood lies in the interior of the potential-equality set and meets its component $W$.
It must belong to the same component.
In particular $p\in W$, contradicting its choice in $F$.
Therefore the supposed interior frontier cannot exist, and $W=N^\circ$.

The potentials are consequently equal throughout the interior of the fixed extension.
Smoothness, and hence continuity at the boundary, gives equality on all of $N$.
Restricting to the original $\Omega$ proves~\eqref{eq:main-conclusion}.
