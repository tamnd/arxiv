<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{qaxioms}
A quandle is a set $Q$ equipped with a binary  operation $*$ (and a right-inverse operation $\bar{*}$) satisfying the following three axioms (for any $a, b, c \in Q$): 
\begin{enumerate}  
\item \text{Idempotence:}\footnote{The related notion of automorphic sets \cite{Brieskorn1988} or racks \cite{fenn1992racks} is obtained by relaxing the idempotence axiom. These become relevant when discussing invariants of framed links.}  $$a * a = a$$    
\item \text{Right-invertibility:}  $$(a * b) \;\bar{*}\; b = a = (a \;\bar{*}\; b) * b $$   
\item \text{Right-self-distributivity:}  
 $$ (a * b) * c = (a * c) * (b * c) $$  
 \end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Given a $\Lambda$-module $M$, the Alexander quandle over $M$ is defined by the operation 
\[
  a * b = t \, a + (1  -  t) \, b  
\] 
where $t$ is an invertible element of the $\Lambda$-module (in the case of knots, $t$ is the generator of deck transformations of the infinite cyclic cover of the knot  complement). These are also referred to as \emph{affine Alexander quandles}.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
The \emph{Alexander module} of $L$ is then defined to be  
\[
  A(L) \;=\; H_1\!\bigl(\widetilde{X}_L;\,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)
\]
as a $\Lambda_m$-module.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
A \emph{multivariate Alexander quandle} is a $\Lambda_m$-module $Q$
equipped with the following binary operation (using the standard notation $a \equiv \bar{x}_a$), applied to a positive crossing, where `a' refers to the understrand:   
\begin{eqnarray}
  a \;*\; b \;=\; t_b\,a + (1 - t_a)\,b  
\label{foxmqdef1}
\end{eqnarray}  
with the constraint  $t_{(a \,*\, b)} = t_a$.

The inverse operation, applied to negative crossings with `a' once again being the understrand, is given by: 
\begin{eqnarray}
   a \;\bar{*}\; b \;=\; t_b^{-1}\,a + t_b^{-1} \, (t_a - 1)\,b   
\label{foxmqdef2}
\end{eqnarray}  
with the corresponding constraint being  $t_{(a \,\bar{*}\, b)} = t_a$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Recall that the direct sum $\mathbb{Z}_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_3$ consists of ordered pairs
with component-wise addition:
\[
  \mathbb{Z}_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_3 = \{(a,b) : a \in \mathbb{Z}_2,\; b \in \mathbb{Z}_3\}
\]
with elements
\[
  \{(0,0),\; (0,1),\; (0,2),\; (1,0),\; (1,1),\; (1,2)\}
\]
and group operation
$(a,b) + (a',b') = (a + a' \bmod 2,\; b + b' \bmod 3)$.
The total order is $2 \cdot 3 = 6$. 

In contrast, consider the disjoint union of $\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $\mathbb{Z}_3$ as sets,
where each element is tagged by an index indicating its component of
origin:    
\[
  (1,\mathbb{Z}_2) \bigsqcup \, (2,\mathbb{Z}_3) = \{(1,0),\;(1,1),\;(2,0),\;(2,1),\;(2,2)\} 
\]
where $(i, a)$ denotes a "tagged" element of the set, with $i$, an index labelling an algebra, and $a$, an element of the algebra labelled by $i$.  The disjoint union of $\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $\mathbb{Z}_3$ above has total cardinality $2 + 3 = 5$.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
The \emph{disjoint union} (or tagged-element construction) on cyclic groups
$\mathbb{Z}_{n_1}, \ldots, \mathbb{Z}_{n_m}$ is the set
\[
  {\cal X} = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m} \mathbb{Z}_{n_i} = \{(i,a) : 1 \le i \le m,\; a \in \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}\}
\]
equipped with the partial binary operation $+_{\cal X}$ defined by
\[
  (i,a) +_{\cal X} (i,b) = (i,\; a + b \bmod n_i)
\]
for elements in the same component, and undefined for elements $(i,a)$
and $(j,b)$ with $i \neq j$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{defgcygr}
Let $n_1, \ldots, n_m$ be positive integers. We define the \emph{groupoid}  $\mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m)$ as a category, whose objects are:  
\[ \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G}) = \{1, 2, \ldots, m\}  \]
with morphisms:  
 \[
          \mathrm{Hom}(i,j) =
          \begin{cases}
            \mathbb{Z}_{n_i} & \text{if } i = j, \\
            \varnothing & \text{if } i \neq j.
          \end{cases}
        \]
  For $a, b \in \mathrm{Hom}(i,i) = \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$,  compositions are defined by
        \[
          b \circ a = a + b \pmod{n_i}.
        \]
 subject to associativity   
        \[
          (c \circ b) \circ a = c \circ (b \circ a) \pmod{n_i},
        \]
     for any $a, b, c \in \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$.  
     
Furthermore, for each object $i$, there exists an identity morphism  $\mathrm{id}_i = 0 \in \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$, such that  for any $a \in \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$,
        \[
          a \circ \mathrm{id}_i =  a =  \mathrm{id}_i \circ a 
        \]

And,  for each $a \in \mathrm{Hom}(i,i) = \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$, the inverse morphism is defined as 
        $a^{-1} = -a \pmod{n_i}$,  satisfying 
        \[
          a \circ a^{-1} = \mathrm{id}_i = a^{-1} \circ a
        \]
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The groupoid $\mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m)$ is a disjoint union of delooping   groupoids of cyclic groups (see \cite{nlab:groupoid}). More precisely, as an isomorphism of groupoids, we have:   
\[
  \mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m) \;\cong\; \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m} B\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}  
\]
where $B\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$ is the delooping groupoid of $\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$ and
$\bigsqcup$ denotes the disjoint union (or the coproduct in the category of
groupoids).  Thus, $\bigsqcup_i B\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$ has object  set $\{1, \ldots, m\}$ (one object from each $B\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$, which we
label by $i$), and
\[
  \mathrm{Hom}_{\bigsqcup_i B\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}}(i,j) =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathbb{Z}_{n_i} & \text{if } i = j, \\
    \varnothing & \text{if } i \neq j,
  \end{cases}
\]
which coincides with the morphism sets of $\mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m)$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The groupoid $\mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m)$  we have constructed here, can be interpreted as a means to simultaneously encode $m$ independent deck groups such that the generator $t_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq m$) of the $i^{th}$ deck group is associated to the $i^{th}$ link component.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a groupoid. The \emph{integral groupoid ring}
$\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ is defined as follows:

As an abelian group 
\[
  \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}] = \bigoplus_{g \in \mathrm{Mor}(\mathcal{G})} \mathbb{Z} \cdot g,
\]
the free abelian group on the set of all morphisms of $\mathcal{G}$. Elements
are formal finite $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combinations $\sum_g a_g \cdot g$ with
$a_g \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Multiplication is defined on basis elements by  
\[
  g \cdot h =
  \begin{cases}
    g \circ h & \text{if } \operatorname{sr}(g) = \operatorname{tr}(h), \\
    0         & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
\]
where $\operatorname{sr}, \operatorname{tr} : \mathrm{Mor}(\mathcal{G}) \to \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})$ denote the source and target
maps, and extended to all of $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ by $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinearity.
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
From the above definition, one can check that the multiplication on $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ is indeed associative.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{ludef}
The \emph{local units} of $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ are defined as:       
For each object $x \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})$, the identity morphism
$e_x := \mathrm{id}_x$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}  
  \item $e_x^2 = e_x$ (idempotence).
  \item $e_x \cdot g = g$ for all $g$ with $\operatorname{tr}(g) = x$.
  \item $g \cdot e_x = g$ for all $g$ with $\operatorname{sr}(g) = x$.
  \item $e_x \cdot e_y = 0$ for $x \neq y$ (orthogonality).
\end{enumerate}
The set $\{e_x : x \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})\}$ forms a complete system of
orthogonal local idempotents. Moreover, when $\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})$ is finite, the sum
$\sum_{x \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})} e_x$ acts as a two-sided identity on
$\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop-tdg1} 
For the totally disconnected groupoid $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m)$, defined in Definition~\ref{defgcygr},  the integral groupoid ring takes the form
\[
  \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}] = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{m} \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]  
\]
as an abelian group, where $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$ denotes the integral group
ring of $\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$. The multiplication is given by: for
$\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$ and $\beta \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_j}]$,
\[
  \alpha \cdot \beta =
  \begin{cases}
    \alpha\beta & \text{if } i = j, \\
    0           & \text{if } i \neq j,
  \end{cases}
\]
where $\alpha\beta$ denotes multiplication in $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\mathcal{G}$ is totally disconnected, we have
$\mathrm{Mor}(\mathcal{G}) = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m} \mathrm{Hom}(i,i) = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m} \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$.
The free abelian group on this set decomposes as
$\bigoplus_i \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$.

For morphisms $g \in \mathrm{Hom}(i,i)$ and $h \in \mathrm{Hom}(j,j)$, we have
$\operatorname{sr}(g) = \operatorname{tr}(g) = i$ and $\operatorname{sr}(h) = \operatorname{tr}(h) = j$. The condition $\operatorname{sr}(g) = \operatorname{tr}(h)$
holds if and only if $i = j$. When $i = j$, the product
$g \cdot h = g \circ h$ is the group operation in $\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$, extended
linearly to give multiplication in $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
From Proposition~\ref{prop-tdg1}, it follows that for finite $m$, we have an isomorphism of rings
\[
  \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}] \;\cong\; \prod_{i=1}^{m} \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}],
\]
where multiplication is component-wise.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
A \emph{$\mathbb{Z}$-algebroid} (also called an \emph{Ab-enriched category} or
a \emph{ringoid}) is a category $\mathcal{A}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}  
  \item Each hom-set $\mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{A}(x, y)$ carries the structure of an
        abelian group.
  \item Composition
        $\mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{A}(y,z) \times \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{A}(x,y) \to \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{A}(x,z)$
        is $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinear.  
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, the composition map satisfies the associativity and unit axioms of a category.
<!-- end definition-9 -->
<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
A \emph{functor of $\mathbb{Z}$-algebroids} (or \emph{Ab-enriched functor})    
$F : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ is a functor such that each map
$\mathrm{Hom}_F (x,y) : \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{A}(x,y) \to \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{B}(Fx, Fy)$ is a group  homomorphism, and is compatible with compositions and units in $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$.
<!-- end definition-10 -->
<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
Given a groupoid $\mathcal{G}$, its \emph{free $\mathbb{Z}$-linearization} is the
$\mathbb{Z}$-algebroid $\mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ defined by:
\begin{enumerate}  
  \item $\mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]) = \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})$.

  \item Composition given by $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinear extension of groupoid

\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-12: body_md -->
\label{def:alg-module}
A \emph{left $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$-module} is an abelian group $M$ together with
a $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinear action $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}] \times M \to M$, written
$(\alpha, \mathfrak{m}) \mapsto \alpha \cdot \mathfrak{m}$, satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}  
  \item  The action 
        $(\alpha\beta) \cdot \mathfrak{m} = \alpha \cdot (\beta \cdot \mathfrak{m})$ is associative 
        for all $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$, $\mathfrak{m} \in M$.
  \item Local unitality decomposes $M$ as a direct sum
        \[
          M = \bigoplus_{x \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})} M_x,
          \quad \text{where } M_x := e_x \cdot M
          = \{\mathfrak{m} \in M : e_x \cdot \mathfrak{m} = \mathfrak{m}\} 
        \]  
        where the set $\{ e_x \}$ are idempotent and orthogonal local units of $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ (from Definition \ref{ludef}).  
  \item Each morphism $g : x \to y$ in
        $\mathcal{G}$ defines a graded left action, which realizes a group
        homomorphism $g_* : M_x \to M_y$.
  \item  Since $\mathcal{G}$ is a groupoid, each
        $g_*$ becomes an isomorphism with an inverse  
        $(g^{-1})_* : M_y \to M_x$.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, a morphism  $f : M \to N$ of left $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$-modules is a group
homomorphism satisfying $f(\alpha \cdot \mathfrak{m}) = \alpha \cdot f(\mathfrak{m})$ for all
$\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$, $\mathfrak{m} \in M$.

which directly follows from the from component-wise multiplication in
$\prod_i \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop-id}  
For  $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m)$ (Definition~\ref{defgcygr}), and  $M$  a $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$-module, with   $M_i := e_i \cdot M$ for each  $i$, we have that:  
\begin{enumerate} 
  \item Each $M_i$ realizes a $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$-module.
  \item The idempotents $\{ e_i \}$ uniquely specify the module decomposition  
        \[
          M \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{m} M_i.
        \]         
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
(1) The subset $e_i M = \{e_i \cdot \mathfrak{m}: \mathfrak{m}\in M\}$ is an abelian
subgroup of $M$. For $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$ and $e_i \mathfrak{m}\in M_i$,
define
\[
  \alpha \cdot (e_i m) := \tilde{\alpha} \cdot \mathfrak{m} 
\]
where $\tilde{\alpha} = (0, \ldots, \alpha, \ldots, 0) \in
\prod_j \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_j}] \cong \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ with $\alpha$ in position $i$.
Since $e_i$ acts as the identity on $M_i$ and annihilates $M_j$ for
$j \neq i$, this is well-defined and gives a $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$-module
structure.

(2) By completeness, every $\mathfrak{m} \in M$ can be written as
\[
  \mathfrak{m} = 1 \cdot \mathfrak{m}= \left(\sum_{i=1}^{m} e_i\right) \cdot \mathfrak{m}
  = \sum_{i=1}^{m} e_i \mathfrak{m}
\]
with $e_i \mathfrak{m} \in M_i$. Furthermore, by orthogonality, if $\sum_i \mathfrak{m}_i = 0$ with
$\mathfrak{m}_i \in M_i$, then applying $e_j$ gives $\mathfrak{m}_j = 0$. Thus, $\sum_i \mathfrak{m}_i$ is linearly independent for every $\mathfrak{m}$, which uniquely specifies the   decomposition of $M$ as a $\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$-module.

<!-- definition-13: body_md -->
\label{defZbmod}  
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a groupoid. A \emph{left $\mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$-module} is an
Ab-enriched functor to the category of abelian groups: 
\[
  M : \mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}] \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ab}  
\]
Such that, for each object $i \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})$, this assigns an abelian group $M_i$. 

And, for each morphism $a \in \mathrm{Hom}(i,j)$, this assigns a group homomorphism $M(a) : M_i \to M_j$. 

Furthermore, the above satisfy two functoriality axioms referring to the identity and composition of morphisms:  
\begin{enumerate}
    \item  $M(id_i) = \mathrm{id}_{M_i}$ for all objects $i$.
    \item  $M(b \circ a) = M(b) \circ M(a)$ for all composable morphisms $a, b$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-13 -->
<!-- definition-14: body_md -->
A \emph{morphism of left $\mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$-modules} is an Ab-enriched natural transformation
$\eta : M \Rightarrow N$, consisting of group homomorphisms
$\eta_x : M_x \to N_x$ for each $x \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})$ such that for
every $\alpha \in \mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}](x,y)$ the following compatibility condition holds: 
\[
  \eta_y \circ M(\alpha) = N(\alpha) \circ \eta_x.
\]
The module category is denoted by:  
\[
   \mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]\textbf{-Mod}  := \mathbf{Ab\text{-}Fun}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}],\, \mathbf{Ab}).
\]
<!-- end definition-14 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop4.1v1}, we have:  
\[
  \mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]\textbf{-Mod}  \;\cong\; \mathbf{Fun}(\mathcal{G},\, \mathbf{Ab}).
\]
Therefore, specifying a $\mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$-module is equivalent to specifying an
ordinary functor $\mathcal{G} \to \mathbf{Ab}$.

  \item $M(a+b) = M(a) \circ M(b)$ 
\end{enumerate}

\label{mamdef}
Let $\mathcal{G} = \mathcal{G}(n_1, \ldots, n_m)$ be the totally disconnected groupoid with  hom-sets $\mathrm{Hom} (i,i)$ as cyclic groups $\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$.  
A \emph{multivariable Alexander (left) module} is an $\mathbf{Ab}$-enriched functor from the $\mathbb{Z}$-algebroid  $\mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}]$ to the category of abelian groups
\[
  M \;:\; \mathbf{Z}[\mathcal{G}] \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ab}
\]
Equivalently, it is a locally unital left module over the groupoid ring

where each   $M_i = e_i M$  is a $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}]$-module, with a choice of generator $t_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$ for each $i$,  determining an isomorphism

where the polynomial $p_i (t_i)$ is such that $t_i$ is invertible in the quotient.

\label{defele}

<!-- definition-18: body_md -->
\label{def:maquand}
Given $M$ a multivariate Alexander module, and 
\[
  X(M) \;:=\; \mathrm{Ob}\!\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}} U \circ M \right) \;=\; \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{|\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})|} M_i  
\]
its tagged set, i.e., the object set of the category
of elements of the underlying set-valued functor $U \circ M$,  a  \emph{multivariate Alexander quandle} on $M$ is a quandle $(X(M), *, \bar{*})$ satisfying the axioms (Definition~\ref{qaxioms}) of idempotence, right-invertibility, and right-self-distributivity, together with the following conditions: 

\begin{enumerate}  
  \item  The constraints:  
  $$t_{(a * b)} = t_{a}  \qquad \mbox{and}  \qquad  t_{(a \bar{*} b)} = t_a$$ 
  ensuring that the operation is closed on each fiber $M_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq |\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G})|$), whenever $a \in M_i$, for every $b \in M$, such that $a * b \in M_i$  (likewise, for the inverse operation).   

  \item  A general affine operation:    
   \[
          (a_1, a_2) * (b_1, b_2) \;=\; \bigl(a_1, \; T_{ a_1, b_1 }\, a_2 + (1 - F_{ a_1, b_1 })\, b_2 \bigr)  
        \]
  where the tagged elements $a$ and $b$ are explicitly denoted as $(a_1, a_2)$ and  $(b_1, b_2)$ respectively, with tagging labels $a_1, b_1$; and the matrix elements $T_{ a_1, b_1 }$, $F_{ a_1, b_1 }$ are subject to the 3 quandle axioms and invertibility within the module.  
  
With inverse operation:  
  \[
          (a_1, a_2) \; \bar{*} \; (b_1, b_2) \;=\; \bigl(a_1, \; (T_{ a_1, b_1 })^{-1}\; a_2 + (T_{ a_1, b_1 })^{-1} \, (F_{ a_1, b_1 } - 1)\; b_2 \bigr)   
        \]

\item   When $a_1 = b_1$, that is, both $a$ and $b$ belong to the same fiber  $M_i$, associated to the invertible element $t_i$ (equivalently, $t_a$), the quandle operation reduces to the Alexander operation: 
  \[
          (a_1, a_2) * (a_1, b_2) \;=\; \bigl(a_1, \; t_{i}\, a_2 + (1 - t_{i})\, b_2 \bigr) 
        \]
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
In Traldi's construction \cite{traldi1}, the Alexander module $M_A(L)$ of a $m$-component link is a single module over the commutative Laurent polynomial ring $\Lambda_m = \mathbb{Z}[t_1^{\pm 1}, \ldots, t_m^{\pm 1}]$, presented as the cokernel of a map associated to a link diagram. The total multivariate Alexander quandle is then a subset of the module $M_A(L)$, defined by the  epimorphism appearing in the link module sequence  (see   \cite{crowell1969elementary, hillman1981alexander}),  and all $m$ variables act on all module elements simultaneously \cite{traldi1}. 

In our construction here, the quandle is defined on $X(M)$, which is the object set of the category of elements  $\int_{\mathcal{G}} U \circ M$, and not on a subset of the module $M$. Moreover, each variable $t_i$ acts only on its own 
fiber $M_i$, defined by the action of $\mathbb{Z}_{n_i}$ on $M_i$.

When $m = 1$, both definitions recover the standard Alexander quandle.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
Given $M$ a (finite) multivariate Alexander module with each $M_i$ of fixed order $n$, the matrices $T = (T_{ a_1, b_1 })$ and $F = (F_{ a_1, b_1 })$ are completely specified by Definition~\ref{def:maquand}.  In particular, idempotence implies $T_{ a_1, a_1 } = F_{ a_1, a_1 } = t_a$,  and  right-self-distributivity with $t_{(a * b)} = t_{a}$, determines three general solution classes of multivariate quandle operations:  
\begin{eqnarray*}
  &\text{(i) }& T = F, \qquad  \;\,\   T_{ a_1, b_1 } = t_b   \\  
  &\text{(ii) }& T = F, \qquad  \;\,\  T_{ a_1, b_1 } = \delta_{ a_1, b_1 } \, t_a + (1 - \delta_{ a_1, b_1 })   \\  
  &\text{(iii) }& T_{ a_1, b_1 } = t_b, \hspace{,5cm}  F_{ a_1, b_1 } = t_a
\end{eqnarray*}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Given $a, b \in X(M)$, let us express a multivariate Alexander quandle operation of finite order as a specification of the pair:      
$$a * b = (a_1 \bullet b_1, a_2 \bullet b_2)$$   
where 
\begin{eqnarray}
a_1 \bullet b_1 &:=& a_1 \pmod{m}  \label{pr1}  \\
a_2 \bullet b_2 &:=& T(a_1, b_1) a_2 + (1 - F(a_1, b_1)) b_2 \pmod{n}   \label{pr2} 
\end{eqnarray}  
Here $T (a_1, b_1)$ and $F (a_1, b_1)$ are elements of $m \times m$ matrices (the notation used here is simply a more readable form of $T_{a_1, \, b_1}$). We now show that the above matrix elements are determined by imposing the quandle axioms. Moreover, the condition in eq.~(\ref{pr1})  turns  out to be a constraint on the parameters $t_i$.  

Firstly, notice that idempotence implies that the diagonal entries of $T$ and $F$ are identical. Next, imposing right self-distributivity yields the following terms on the left-hand side (of the self-distributivity axiom): 
\begin{eqnarray}
T(a_1, c_1) \left( T(a_1, b_1) a_2 + (1 - F(a_1, b_1)) b_2  \right) + (1 - F(a_1, c_1)) c_2
 \label{pr3} 
\end{eqnarray}
and, the following expression on the right-hand side: 
\begin{eqnarray}
T(a_1, b_1) \left( T(a_1, c_1) a_2 + (1 - F(a_1, c_1)) c_2  \right) + (1 - F(a_1, b_1)) \left( T(b_1, c_1) b_2 + (1 - F(b_1, c_1)) c_2  \right) 
 \label{pr4} 
\end{eqnarray}
Here, eq.~(\ref{pr1}) has been used within the arguments of $T$ and $F$. Matching the coefficients of $a_2, b_2, c_2$ respectively in eqs.~(\ref{pr3}) and (\ref{pr4}), we find the following three solution classes for $T$ and $F$: 

$Class \; (i)$:  When the off-diagonal entries of $T$ and $F$ are generally not identical
\begin{eqnarray}
T(a_1, c_1) &=& T(b_1, c_1)   \nonumber \\
F(a_1, c_1) &=& F(b_1, c_1)   \nonumber \\
T(a_1, b_1) &=& F(a_1, b_1)   \nonumber 
\end{eqnarray}
for any $a_1, b_1, c_1$. This implies $T = F$ as matrices, and the columns of $T$ have identical $t$-moduli
\[ T =  \begin{pmatrix}
    t_{1} & t_{2} & \cdots & t_{m} \\
    t_{1} & t_{2} & \cdots & t_{m} \\
    \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
    t_{1} & t_{2} & \cdots & t_{m}
\end{pmatrix}    \]
Here $t_i$ is the $t$-modulus associated to $\mathbb{Z}_{n_i} [t_{i+1}, t_{i+1}^{-1}]/p_i (t_{i+1})$ with $1 \leq i \leq m$.  

With this form of $T$ and $F$, the quandle multiplication can be expressed as
\begin{eqnarray}
    a_1 \bullet b_1 &=& a_1 \pmod{m}  \label{op1} \\
    a_2 \bullet b_2 &=& t_{b} a_2 + (1 - t_{b}) b_2 \pmod{n}   \label{op2} 
\end{eqnarray}
where $t_{b}$ denotes the $t$-modulus associated to the element $b = (b_1, b_2)$. 


$Class \; (ii)$: The next solution case we have is when the off-diagonal entries of $T$ and $F$ are identical. Then, 
\begin{eqnarray}
T(a_1, b_1) &=& 1   \nonumber \\
F(a_1, b_1) &=& 1   \nonumber \\
T(a_1, a_1) &=& F(a_1, a_1)   \nonumber 
\end{eqnarray}
for any $a_1, b_1$, which also implies $T = F$ as matrices, and $T$ takes the form
\[ T =  \begin{pmatrix}
    t_{1} & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\
    1 & t_{2} & \cdots & 1 \\
    \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
    1 & 1 & \cdots & t_{m}
\end{pmatrix}    \]

Then, the quandle multiplication takes the form 
\begin{eqnarray}
    a_1 \bullet b_1 &=& a_1 \pmod{m}    \\
    a_2 \bullet b_2 &=& \left( \delta_{ a_1, b_1 } \, t_a + (1 - \delta_{ a_1, b_1 }) \right) a_2 + (1 - \left( \delta_{ a_1, b_1 } \, t_a + (1 - \delta_{ a_1, b_1 }) \right) ) b_2 \pmod{n}     
\end{eqnarray}



$Class \; (iii)$:  Finally, the equality of eqs.~(\ref{pr3}) and (\ref{pr4}) is also satisfied when $T(a_1, b_1) = t_b$ and $F(a_1, b_1) = t_a$. That is, as functions of $a_1$ and $b_1$, the matrix elements $T(a_1, b_1)$ and $F(a_1, b_1)$ map to the $t$-moduli corresponding to their second and first arguments respectively. Here, $t_{b}$ is associated to the element $b = (b_1, b_2)$, and $t_{a}$ is associated to the element $a = (a_1, a_2)$.  This gives the following matrix forms for $T$ and $F$: 
\[ T =  \begin{pmatrix}
    t_{1} & t_{2} & \cdots & t_{m} \\
    t_{1} & t_{2} & \cdots & t_{m} \\
    \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
    t_{1} & t_{2} & \cdots & t_{m}
\end{pmatrix}     \quad  \quad 
F =  \begin{pmatrix}
    t_{1} & t_{1} & \cdots & t_{1} \\
    t_{2} & t_{2} & \cdots & t_{2} \\
    \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
    t_{m} & t_{m} & \cdots & t_{m}
\end{pmatrix}    
\]

These matrices then lead to the quandle multiplication mentioned in Section~2.  
\begin{eqnarray}
    a_1 \bullet b_1 &=& a_1 \pmod{m}    \label{op5}  \\
    a_2 \bullet b_2 &=& t_{b} a_2 + (1 - t_{a}) b_2 \pmod{n}   \label{op6} 
\end{eqnarray}

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\label{remtab}
Notice that, eq.~(\ref{pr1}) is equivalent to the constraint $t_{a\,*\,b} = t_a$ (since the components $a_1$ and $b_1$ refer to the module label, eq.~(\ref{pr1}) picks the label referring to the module associated to  $a$ in the product $a\,*\,b$).     

Moreover, for each of the above three solution classes,  eq.~(\ref{pr1}) is   crucial for eqs.~(\ref{pr3}) and (\ref{pr4}) to hold. If one tries to generalize eq.~(\ref{pr1}) to an operation of the form $a_1 \bullet b_1 = t a_1 + (1 - t) b_1$ for some $t \neq 1$, then self-distributivity immediately breaks down. In other words, the above three solution classes of multivariate Alexander quandle   operations only hold when $t_{a\,*\,b} = t_a$ is satisfied.

\label{teq10}
\end{eqnarray}
Substituting this in eqs.~(\ref{pr1v2}) and (\ref{pr2v2}) for $m = 2$, we will  check that they satisfies the quandle axioms. 

Note that the quandle multiplication table will now have rectangular off-diagonal blocks of dimensions $n_0 \times n_1$ and $n_1 \times n_0$ respectively. The structure of this multiplication table is of the form:
\begin{eqnarray}
\begin{array}{r@{\mskip\thickmuskip}c}
& \begin{array}{c@{\hspace{7.0em}}c} \scriptstyle 0 & \scriptstyle 1 \end{array} \\[-0.5ex]
\begin{array}{r} \scriptstyle 0 \\ \scriptstyle 1 \end{array} & 
 \left(\begin{array}{c|c}
dim (n_0 \times n_0) & dim (n_0 \times n_1) \\ \hline
dim (n_1 \times n_0) & dim (n_1 \times n_1)
\end{array}\right) 
\end{array}
\label{tab1}
\end{eqnarray}
where the block-labels ("0" or "1" above and to the left of the table) denote the tagged coordinates, which refer to the elements of the $T$-matrix (for instance, the element $T_{0\,1}$ is the $t$-variable associated to entries in the upper right block of the quandle table above).  To keep track of the specific $\mathbb{Z}$-module $M_i$  referring to each element $a \equiv (a_1, a_2)$, we will hereon denote the $a_2$ by $a_2 (a_1)$ (for instance, $a_2 (0)$ refers to the module with $a_1 = 0$). 

For the multiplication table in eq.~(\ref{tab1}), idempotence and right-invertibility follow immediately since the diagonal blocks themselves are quandle tables, and the  entries in the rows of the off-diagonal blocks are determined by the trivial quandle using $t = 1$ (which implies every column in the table in eq.~(\ref{tab1}) is a permutation).   

Now, to check self-distributivity, we will work with triples $\left( a_2 (i), \, b_2 (j), \, c_2 (k) \right)$, where $i, j, k \in \{0, 1\}$.  We need to check the following $2^3$ combinations of these triples, which we can compute explicitly using the $T$-matrix given in eq.~(\ref{teq10}) above:       

$(i) \; \& \; (ii)$  $(a_2(0), \, b_2(0), \, c_2(0))$ and $(a_2(1), \, b_2(1), \, c_2(1))$ are both trivially self-distributive since they belong to the same module.
    
$(iii)$    $(a_2(0), \, b_2(0), \, c_2(1))$ evaluates to $( a_2(0) \bullet b_2(0) )$ on both sides of the self-distributivity identity.  
   
$(iv)$   $(a_2(0), \, b_2(1), \, c_2(0))$ gives $( a_2(0) \bullet c_2(0) )$ on both sides.
   
$(v)$    $(a_2(0), \, b_2(1), \, c_2(1))$ leads to $a_2(0)$ on both sides of the identity.

$(vi)$   $(a_2(1), \, b_2(0), \, c_2(1))$ gives $( a_2(1) \bullet c_2(1) )$.

$(vii)$   $(a_2(1), \, b_2(1), \, c_2(0))$ gives $( a_2(1) \bullet b_2(1) )$.

$(viii)$  $(a_2(1), \, b_2(0), \, c_2(0))$ gives $a_2(1)$.

Since all 8 of the above combination of triples satisfy right self-distributivity, the multiplication table in eq.~(\ref{tab1}) as a whole satisfies right self-distributivity for any disjoint union of $M_0$ and $M_1$ with cardinalities $n_0$ and $n_1$ respectively. This yields a quandle for $m = 2$.   

One can now continue the above process iteratively: we append another $\mathbb{Z}$-module $M_2$ to the above quandle, and express the resulting table once again in $2 \times 2$ block form. The quandle with $X(M) = M_0 \, \bigsqcup \,  M_1$ is now treated as a single diagonal block.  As before, the off-diagonal blocks are determined by the trivial quandle with $t = 1$; and analogous to the case above, we have 8 combinations of triples of elements, whose evaluations confirm self-distributivity in the same way. Such an iterative application can be carried out for any finite number of $\mathbb{Z}$-modules $M_i$. This concludes our proof.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
Multivariate Alexander quandles are semisimple algebras.  
\label{prop5.2v0}
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Multivariate Alexander quandles necessarily satisfy $t_{(a * b)} = t_a$, which is a necessary condition for the construction of multivariate quandles when the abelianization map is generalized to distinguish between link components. Equivalently, this condition also follows from requiring right-self-distributivity of the multivariate quandle operation in eq.~(\ref{pr2}) (see Remark \ref{remtab}).  

Now, notice that $t_{(a * b)} = t_a$ realizes a right ideal on the quandle algebra such that the right-action via quandle multiplication preserves the $t$-variable of the associated ideal. Recall that each element in the module comes associated to a specific $t$. Since right action preserves $t$-variables, it realizes an ideal of the algebra defined by the corresponding $t$. The entire algebra is a disjoint union of elements labelled by particular $t$'s. Hence, the algebra decomposes into a disjoint union of proper ideals (including possible $1$-dimensional ideals).

where * denotes the standard quandle operation.
