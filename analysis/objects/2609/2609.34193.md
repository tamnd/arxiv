<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{dga which does not cogenerate}
    Let $q \in k^\times$ be not a root of unity, and consider the graded algebra (with zero differential)
    \[
    \Lambda = k\langle x, y \rangle / (x^2, y^2, xy - qyx), \qquad |x| = |y| = 2,
    \]
    the right $\Lambda$-module $V = (\Lambda/(x+y)\Lambda)[-1]$, and the dg algebra 
    \[
    A = \operatorname{End}_\Lambda(\Lambda \oplus V).
    \]
    Then $A$ is coconnective, with $H^0(A) = k \oplus k$ and $\dim_k H^*(A) = 11$.  
    Here we use cohomological grading, and ``coconnective'' means $H^i(A) = 0$ for $i < 0$. 
    Let $e \in A$ be the idempotent projecting onto the summand $\Lambda$, and 
    \[
    M = \operatorname{cofib}\left( Ae \otimes_{eAe} eA \longrightarrow A \right) \in \mathrm{Mod}_A .
    \]
    Then $M \ne 0$ (in fact $H^*(M) = k \oplus k[-1]$), but $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, A) = 0$.  Thus $A$ does not cogenerate; it does not even cogenerate the pseudo-perfect modules (i.e. modules with finite dimensional cohomology).  Details are in Section \ref{sec: example dga}.  In Appendix \ref{app:finite-dg-model}, we show that in fact $A$ and $M$ admit (not just cohomologically) finite dimensional models.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{cogeneration versus nondegeneracy}
    Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a smooth proper dg category, and 
    $0 \to \mathcal{K} \to \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{S} \to 0$ a localization sequence (it follows immediately that $\mathcal{K}$ is proper and $\mathcal{S}$ is smooth).  If $\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{K}}$ is cogenerated by compact objects, then $\mathcal{S}^!$ is nondegenerate: if $\mathcal{S}^!(M, \cdot) = 0$ then $M = 0$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{universal ff lcy}  (Lemmas \ref{lem: kernel of IDB} and \ref{lem: key})
    Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a smooth dg category, and $\mathcal{S}^!$ be its dualizing bimodule.  Then the image of any fully faithful left Calabi-Yau morphism to $\mathcal{S}$ generates the kernel of $(-)\otimes_\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}^!$ as a localizing subcategory of $\mathrm{Mod}_\mathcal{S}$.
    A sort of converse: if $\mathcal{Y}$ is contained in the kernel of $(-)\otimes_\mathcal{S} \mathcal{S}^!$, then the relative left Calabi-Yau completion  $\Pi_{n-1}(\mathcal{Y}) \to \Pi_{n}(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{Y})$ is fully faithful.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
Let $A$ be a dga with $H^*(A)$ bounded, and $N = \max\{r : H^r(A) \ne 0\}$. 
    Let $M\in \mathrm{Mod}_A$ with $H^*(M) \ne 0$ bounded below, and $n = \min\{r : H^r(M) \ne 0\}$. 
    Assume $M$ admits a semi-free resolution with visible bottom. 
    Then there is a degree $N-n$ map $\phi \in \operatorname{Hom}_A(F, A)$ that induces a nontrivial map on $H^{*}(M) \to H^{*+N-n}(A)$. In particular, $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, A) \not\simeq 0$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Choose a cocycle $a \in A^N$ with $[a] \ne 0$.  We construct $\phi$ with $\phi(v_0) = a$ 
    by induction. 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                        

    Assume $F_{(j+1)} = F_{(j)} \oplus W_j \otimes A$ as a linear space. 
    On $F_{(1)} = W_0 \otimes A$ every generator is a cocycle; put $\phi(v_0) = a$, $\phi(v) = 0$ for the other generators of $W_0$, and extend $A$-linearly. 
    Suppose $\phi$ is defined on $F_{(j)}$ and let $v$ be a generator of $W_j$.  Then $dv \in F_{(j)}$, so $\phi(dv)$ is defined and it is a cocycle of degree $|v| + 1 + N - n \ge N + 1$.  As $H^{>N}(A) = 0$, there is $c \in A$ with $d_A c = \phi(dv)$; put $\phi(v) = (-1)^{N-n} c$.  Extending $A$-linearly defines a chain map $\phi: F_{(j+1)} \to A$.  By induction, we obtain a map $F_{(k)} \to A$ for each $k$, sending $v_0$ to $a$. Taking the colimit gives the desired map.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem: semi-free resolution}
    Let $A$ be a coconnective dg algebra with $H^0(A) = k$ and $H^*(A)$ bounded above.  Then any $A$-module $M$ with $H^*(M) \ne 0$ bounded below admits a semi-free resolution with visible bottom.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We construct semi-free modules $F_{(0)} \subset F_{(1)} \subset \cdots$ and compatible maps $f_j : F_{(j)} \to M$ such that $C_{(j)} = \operatorname{cone}(f_j)$ satisfies $H^{<n}(C_{(j)}) = 0$. 
    Moreover, the induced map $H^*(C_{(j)})\to H^*(C_{(j+1)})$ is always $0$. This implies that 
    $\operatorname{cofib}( \operatorname*{colim}_j F_{(j)} \to M) = \operatorname*{colim} (C_{(0)} \to C_{(1)} \to C_{(2)} \to \cdots ) \simeq 0$. Hence we can take $F = \operatorname*{colim}_j F_{(j)}$ with the natural map $f: F \to M$. 
    
    Start with $F_{(0)} = 0$, so that $C_{(0)} = M$.  
    Assume $F_{(j)}$ and $f_j$ are given.
    For each $r\ge n$ choose cycles $(y_i, z_i) \in \operatorname{cone}(f_j) = M \oplus F_{(j)}[1]$ representing a basis of $H^r(C_{(j)})$, and for each of them adjoin to $F_{(j)}$ a free generator $v_i$ of degree $r$ with
    \[
    dv_i = z_i, \qquad f_{j+1}(v_i) = y_i.
    \]
    Since $dz_i = 0$ and $f_{j+1}(dv_i) = f_j(z_i) = dy_i$, this defines a dg module $F_{(j+1)}$ and a chain map $f_{j+1}$ extending $f_j$.  Let $W_j$ be the span of the new generators, a graded vector space concentrated in degrees $\ge n$, so that $F_{(j+1)} / F_{(j)} \cong W_j \otimes A$.
    The inclusion $F_{(j)} \subset F_{(j+1)}$ gives a short exact sequence
    \[
    0 \to C_{(j)} \to C_{(j+1)} \to (W_j \otimes A)[1] \to 0.
    \]
    The connecting map $\partial : H^r(W_j \otimes A) \to H^r(C_{(j)})$ sends $[v_i \otimes 1]$ to $[(y_i, z_i)]$, up to sign. By our construction, it is always a surjection. Hence $H^*(C_{(j)}) \to H^*(C_{(j+1)} )$ is zero. 
                                                                                                      
                                                                                   

    Note that $H^n(W_j\otimes A)=W_j^n$, with basis the classes $[v_i\otimes1]$ of the generators of degree $n$. These were adjoined one for each element of a basis $[(y_i,z_i)]$ of $H^n(C_{(j)})$, and $\partial[v_i\otimes1]=\pm[(y_i,z_i)]$, so $\partial$ is an isomorphism in degree $n$, which implies that $H^{<n} (C_{(j+1)}) = 0$ (this is where we use $H^0(A) = k$). Therefore, $F_{(j+1)}$ satisfies all the prescribed conditions.  
    
   
    

    Finally, the generators of degree $n$ adjoined at the first stage are indexed by a basis of $H^n(C_{(0)}) = H^n(M) \ne 0$; let $v_0$ be one of them.  Then $f(v_0)$ represents a nonzero class in $H^n(M)$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The hypothesis $H^0(A) = k$ is  used in the proof of Lemma \ref{lem: semi-free resolution}  to ensure that no generators of degree $< n$ are needed.
    The  dg algebra in Example \ref{dga which does not cogenerate} is coconnective, has $H^0(A) = k \oplus k$, the module $M$ is bounded, and $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, A) = 0$, so the hypothesis of Lemma \ref{lem: semi-free resolution} cannot be weakened even to asking $H^0(A)$ semisimple.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem: general noncogeneration} We have the following
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item As a complex, $A \simeq \Lambda \oplus V \oplus V^* \oplus \operatorname{End}_\Lambda(V)$.  In particular $A$ is proper if and only if $\Lambda$, $V$, $V^*$ and $\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(V)$ have finite dimensional cohomology.
        \item $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, A) = 0$.
        \item $M \simeq \operatorname{cofib}\left( \mathrm{ev} : V \otimes_\Lambda V^* \to \operatorname{End}_\Lambda(V) \right)$, so $M \ne 0$ if and only if $V$ is not perfect.
    \end{enumerate}
    Consequently, if $\Lambda$ is proper and $V$ is a $\Lambda$-module which is not perfect, but for which $V$, $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(V, \Lambda)$ and $\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(V)$ have finite dimensional cohomology, then $A = \operatorname{End}_\Lambda(\Lambda \oplus V)$ is a proper dg algebra which does not cogenerate $\mathrm{Mod}_A$.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
(1) $\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(\Lambda \oplus V)$ is the direct sum of $\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(\Lambda) = \Lambda$, $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(\Lambda, V) = V$, $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(V, \Lambda) = V^*$ and $\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(V)$.

    (2) By the tensor-hom adjunction,
    \[
    \operatorname{Hom}_A(M, A) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_A(M, \operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(P, P)) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(M \otimes_A P, P).
    \]
    Now $eA \otimes_A P = eP = \Lambda$, so
    \[
    (Ae \otimes_{eAe} eA) \otimes_A P = Ae \otimes_{eAe} eP = P \otimes_\Lambda \Lambda = P,
    \]
    and the map to $A \otimes_A P = P$ is the identity.  So $M \otimes_A P = 0$, and $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, A) = 0$.

    (3) Since $Ae = \Lambda \oplus V$ and $eA = \Lambda \oplus V^*$ as right and left $\Lambda$-modules, respectively,
    \[
    Ae \otimes_{eAe} eA = (\Lambda \oplus V) \otimes_\Lambda (\Lambda \oplus V^*) = \Lambda \oplus V \oplus V^* \oplus (V \otimes_\Lambda V^*),
    \]
    and under (1) the map $Ae \otimes_{eAe} eA \to A$ is the identity on the first three summands and $\mathrm{ev}$ on the last.  Hence $M \simeq \operatorname{cofib}(\mathrm{ev})$.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem: key}
    Let $f:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}$ be a fully faithful functor between smooth dg categories. If $\mathcal{B}^!(f(a),-)=0$ for every $a\in\mathcal{A}$, then
    $
    \Pi_{n-1}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\Pi_n(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
    $
    is fully faithful, as is the induced functor on perfect categories. The same holds with $\mathcal{B}^!(-,f(a))=0$ in place of $\mathcal{B}^!(f(a),-)=0$.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Since representable right $\mathcal{A}$-modules generate $\mathrm{Mod}_\mathcal{A}$ and tensor products preserve colimits, the hypothesis gives
\[
(M\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B})\otimes_\mathcal{B}\mathcal{B}^!=0
\qquad(M\in\mathrm{Mod}_\mathcal{A}).
\]
Full faithfulness identifies $f^*\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ with $\mathcal{A}_\Delta$, so
\begin{align*}
(M\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B})\otimes_\mathcal{B} f_!(\mathcal{A}^![n-2])
&\simeq M\otimes_\mathcal{A}  \mathcal{B} \otimes_\mathcal{A} \mathcal{A}^![n-2] \otimes_\mathcal{A} \mathcal{B} \\
&\simeq M\otimes_\mathcal{A} f^*\mathcal{B}_\Delta \otimes_\mathcal{A} \mathcal{A}^![n-2] \otimes_\mathcal{A} \mathcal{B}\\
&\simeq (M\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^![n-2])\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}.
\end{align*}
Tensoring the triangle
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:triangle}
    \mathcal{B}^![n-2]\longrightarrow f_!(\mathcal{A}^![n-2])
    \longrightarrow\Theta_{\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}}[n-1]
\end{equation}
with $M\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}$ therefore gives an equivalence
\[
(M\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^![n-2])\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}
\xrightarrow{\sim}(M\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B})\otimes_\mathcal{B}\Theta_{\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}}[n-1].
\]
Iteratively tensoring with~\eqref{eq:triangle} gives 
\[
\bigl(M\otimes_\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{A}^![n-2])^{\otimes_\mathcal{A} m}\bigr)\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}
\xrightarrow{\sim}(M\otimes_\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B})\otimes_\mathcal{B}\bigl(\Theta_{\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}}[n-1]\bigr)^{\otimes_\mathcal{B} m}
\qquad(m\ge0).
\]
Taking $M=h_a$ and evaluating at $f(a')$ gives equivalences
\[
\bigl(\mathcal{A}^![n-2]\bigr)^{\otimes_\mathcal{A} m}(a,a')
\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigl(\Theta_{\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}}[n-1]\bigr)^{\otimes_\mathcal{B} m}(f(a),f(a'))
\qquad(m\ge0).
\]
This shows that the map $\Pi_{n-1} (\mathcal{A}) \to \Pi_n (\mathcal{B}, \mathcal{A}) $ is fully faithful.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{prop: nakayama}
    The algebra $R$ is smooth, and $R^! \simeq R^\nu[-3]$, where $R^\nu$ is $R$ with its right action twisted by $\nu$.  Consequently $\Phi_R(M_\lambda) \simeq M_{\lambda - 1}[-3]$.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
For $a\in R$, put $D_a=a\otimes1-1\otimes a^{\mathrm{op}}\in R^e$.
On a bimodule this acts by $b\mapsto ab-ba$, so these are the differences
between the left and right actions.  They satisfy
$[D_a,D_b]=D_{[a,b]}$.  In particular, 
\[
[ D_\delta, D_x] = 0, \qquad [D_\delta, D_y] = -D_y, \qquad [D_x, D_y] = 0.
\]

Let $\mathfrak g$ be the Lie algebra with basis $e_x,e_y,e_\delta$ and brackets
$[e_\delta,e_x]=0$, $[e_\delta,e_y]=-e_y$, $[e_x,e_y]=0$. Then $e_i\mapsto D_i$
defines a Lie algebra map $\mathfrak g\to R^e$.
Then $R^e$ is free as a right $U(\mathfrak g)$-module, and $R^e\otimes_{U(\mathfrak g)}k \cong R$.
Applying $R^e\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g})}-$ to the Chevalley--Eilenberg resolution
$U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^\bullet\mathfrak{g}\to k$ gives a free resolution of $R$ as an $R^e$-module:
\begin{equation} \label{eq: bimodule resolution}
0\rightarrow R^e \otimes \bigwedge^3 \mathfrak{g} \xrightarrow{d_3} R^e \otimes \bigwedge^2 \mathfrak{g} \xrightarrow{d_2} R^e \otimes \mathfrak{g} \xrightarrow{d_1} R^e \xrightarrow{\mu} R \rightarrow 0.
\end{equation}
Thus $R$ is smooth. Here $\mu(a\otimes b^{\mathrm{op}})=ab$, and explicitly,
\begin{align*}
    d_1(e_i)&=D_i \qquad (i\in\{x,y,\delta\});\\
    d_2(e_i\wedge e_j)&=D_i e_j-D_j e_i-[e_i,e_j]
    \qquad (i,j\in\{x,y,\delta\});\\
    d_3(e_x\wedge e_y\wedge e_\delta)&=D_x(e_y\wedge e_\delta)-D_y(e_x\wedge e_\delta)
    +(D_\delta+1)(e_x\wedge e_y).
\end{align*}



Dualizing \eqref{eq: bimodule resolution} computes $R^!$. An explicit computation shows that the cohomology is concentrated in degree $3$, and
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^3_{R^e}(R,R^e)
=R^e\big/(D_xR^e+D_yR^e+(D_\delta+1)R^e).
\]
The assignment
\[
a\otimes b^{\mathrm{op}}\longmapsto b\,\nu(a)
\]
therefore induces an isomorphism from this quotient to $R^\nu$, as $R^e$-modules.  Hence $R^!\simeq R^\nu[-3]$.

Finally, $M_\lambda\otimes_R R^\nu\simeq M_\lambda^\nu
\simeq M_{\lambda-1}$ by \eqref{eq: M action}, which proves the last assertion.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{prop: homs}
    $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_\lambda, M_\mu) = k$, in degree $0$, if $\lambda - \mu \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, and $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_\lambda, M_\mu) = 0$ otherwise.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,M_\mu)$ to the resolution of $M_\lambda$ gives
\[
\left[M_\mu\xrightarrow{\cdot(\delta-\lambda)}M_\mu\right]
\]
in degrees $0,1$. By \eqref{eq: M action}, the differential is
\[
f\longmapsto(\mu-\lambda)f-\partial_xf+y\partial_yf.
\]
It preserves each summand $k[x]y^n$ and acts there as $(\mu+n-\lambda)-\partial_x$.
If $\mu+n-\lambda\ne0$, this is invertible, with inverse
\[

the sum being finite for each polynomial $p$. If $\mu+n-\lambda=0$, it is
$-\partial_x$, which is surjective with kernel $k$ in characteristic zero.
Thus the complex has cohomology $k$ in degree $0$ when
$\lambda-\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$, and is acyclic otherwise.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem: idea}
Lemmas \ref{lem: general degenerate} and \ref{lem: key} reduce the construction of a fully faithful left Calabi--Yau functor with nonzero source to finding a smooth dg category $\mathcal{C}$ and an object $Y$ with $\operatorname{End}_\mathcal{C}(Y)=k$, $\Phi_\mathcal{C}(Y)$ perfect, and $\operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(\Phi_\mathcal{C}(Y),Y)=0$; as noted above, $\mathcal{C}$ cannot be proper.

A right $\mathbb P_\mathcal{A}(N)$-module $F$ is the same as a pair of right $\mathcal{A}$-modules $F_0,F_1$ together with a map $F_0\otimes_\mathcal{A} B\to F_1$, that is, maps $\alpha:F_0\to F_1$ and $\beta:F_0\otimes_\mathcal{A} N\to F_1$.
                                           
Here $\alpha$ is the action of $u$, so $F$ is right orthogonal to all $\operatorname{cofib}(u_a)$ if and only if $\alpha$ is a quasi-isomorphism.
Restriction along $\Phi$ sends a $T$-module $G$ to $\Phi^*G=(G,G;\mathrm{id},\beta_G)$, where $\beta_G$ is the action of $N$ on $G$; this lies in the right orthogonal of $\{\operatorname{cofib}(u_a) \} $.
We show that $\Phi^*$ is indeed an equivalence onto the right orthogonal.  Then $\Phi_!$ is a localization with kernel generated by $\{\operatorname{cofib}(u_a) \} $. 

Let $j_i: \mathcal{A} \to \mathbb P_\mathcal{A}(N)$, $a \mapsto a_i$, be the two inclusions.
Then $j_{0!}G=(G,G\otimes_\mathcal{A} B; \mathrm{id})$ and $j_{1!}G=(0,G; 0   )$, and $\Phi_!j_{0!}G=\Phi_!j_{1!}G=G\otimes_\mathcal{A} T$.
Every $F$ has the resolution
\[
0\to j_{1!}(F_0\otimes_\mathcal{A} B)\to j_{0!}F_0\oplus j_{1!}F_1\to F\to0.
\]
Applying $\Phi_!$ and cancelling the summand $F_0\otimes_\mathcal{A} T$, which comes from the summand $\mathcal{A}_\Delta$ of $B$, gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq: Phi shriek}
\Phi_!F\simeq\operatorname{cofib}\left(F_0\otimes_\mathcal{A} N\otimes_\mathcal{A} T\to F_1\otimes_\mathcal{A} T\right)
\end{equation}
where the map is given by
\[
\qquad f\otimes n\otimes t\mapsto\alpha(f)\otimes nt-\beta(f\otimes n)\otimes t.
\]
For $F=\Phi^*G$ this is the standard resolution
$G\otimes_\mathcal{A} N\otimes_\mathcal{A} T\to G\otimes_\mathcal{A} T\to G$ of a module over a tensor category, so $\Phi_!\Phi^*G\simeq G$, and $\Phi^*$ is fully faithful.

Now let $F$ lie in the right orthogonal of $\{\operatorname{cofib}(u_a) \} $, so $\alpha: F_0 \to F_1$ is a quasi-isomorphism. 
Filter both terms of \eqref{eq: Phi shriek} by the total number of $N$-factors, including the initial $N$ in the source.  The map preserves this filtration.  On the graded piece of degree $m\ge1$ it is
\[
F_0\otimes_\mathcal{A} N^{\otimes_\mathcal{A} m}\xrightarrow{\ \alpha\otimes1\ }F_1\otimes_\mathcal{A} N^{\otimes_\mathcal{A} m},
\]
which is a quasi-isomorphism.  In degree $0$ the source vanishes and the target is $F_1$.  Hence the inclusion $F_1\to\Phi_!F$ is a quasi-isomorphism of $\mathcal{A}$-modules. 


The two components of the unit $F\to\Phi^*\Phi_!F$ are
\[
F_0\xrightarrow{\alpha}F_1\longrightarrow\Phi_!F,
\qquad
F_1\longrightarrow\Phi_!F.
\]
Both are quasi-isomorphisms, so the unit is an equivalence.
Thus $\Phi^*$ is an equivalence onto the right orthogonal.

<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem: tensor localization}
Let $q: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}'$ be a localization with kernel $\mathcal K$. <!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Write $N' = q_!N$,  $T=T_\mathcal{A}(N)$ and $T'=T_{\mathcal{A}'}(N')$, let $\Psi:T\to T'$ be the induced dg functor, and let $\iota$ be the fully faithful right adjoint of $q$ on module categories; its image is $\mathcal K^\perp$.
For $L\in\mathcal K$ and a $T$-module $G$ we have $\operatorname{Hom}_T(L\otimes_\mathcal{A} T,G)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{A}(L,G)$.
So $G$ is right orthogonal to all $L\otimes_\mathcal{A} T$ with $L\in\mathcal K$ if and only if its underlying $\mathcal{A}$-module lies in $\mathcal K^\perp$, i.e. has the form $\iota G'$.  This holds for $G=\Psi^*G'$.

\]
For $G=\Psi^*G'$ this is the standard resolution of $G'$, so $\Psi_!\Psi^*G'\simeq G'$, and $\Psi^*$ is fully faithful.
For general $G$ in the right orthogonal, the filtration argument of Lemma \ref{lem: P1 bundle} shows that $G'\to\Psi_!G$ is a quasi-isomorphism of $\mathcal{A}'$-modules, so $\Psi_!G=0$ implies $G'=0$, hence $G=0$.

\label{prop: compactification-relative-CY}

$
\widehat J=\operatorname{cofib}\left(
\widehat\mathcal{B}^!\longrightarrow\widehat f_!(\mathcal{A}^!)
\right)[n-2]$,

$
\operatorname{Perf}(\mathbb P_{\widehat\mathcal{B}}(\widehat J))
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Perf}(\Pi_n(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}))
$.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->

Since $q$ is a localization, base change $q_!$ takes $\widehat\mathcal{B}^!$ to $\mathcal{B}^!$ and commutes with duals of perfect bimodules \cite[Lemma 2.9 and Proposition 3.10]{keller2009deformed}. $
