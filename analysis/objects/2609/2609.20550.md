<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{asm:fm}
All random objects are defined on a common probability space
$(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$.  The factor path
$F=(f^{(1)},\dots,f^{(n)})$ is a random element of $\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}$
satisfying, for each $l=1,\dots,n$,
\[
  \mathbb{E}\|f^{(l)}\|^2<\infty,\qquad \mathbb{E} f^{(l)}=0,\qquad
  \mathbb{E}\big[f^{(l)}f^{(l)\top}\big]=\Sigma_f,
\]
with $\Sigma_f\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}$ positive definite and not depending on $l$.
No further restriction is imposed on the law of $F$: the marginal laws of
the $f^{(l)}$ may differ, and the cross-moments
$\mathbb{E}[f^{(l)}f^{(m)\top}]$, $l\ne m$, are unrestricted, so the factors may be
serially dependent and conditionally heteroskedastic.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Assumption~\ref{asm:fm} constrains only the second moments of the marginals
of $F$; it is what makes $\Sigma_0=B\Sigma_fB^{\!\top}$, and hence the
targets $b_1,\dots,b_k$ of \eqref{eq:sdt}, well defined.  It is used
nowhere in the asymptotics, which are conditional on the realized path.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{asm:noise}
Conditional on the factor path $F=(f^{(1)},\dots,f^{(n)})$, the columns
$z^{(1)},\dots,z^{(n)}$ of the specific returns array
$\{Z_{il}: i\ge1,\ 1\le l\le n\}$ are mutually independent, mean zero, and
identically distributed (stationary across observations).  For each $p$,
the conditional covariance matrix
$\Delta^{(p)}:=\operatorname{Cov}\big(z^{(p,l)}\mid F\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}$
does not depend on $F$; write $\delta_i^2:=\Delta^{(p)}_{ii}$ for the
specific variances.  There is a constant $C<\infty$ such that, for all
$p$ and $l$, almost surely,
\begin{align*}
  \text{(i)}&\quad \sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^p,\ \|a\|=1}
     \mathbb{E}\big[(a^{\!\top}z^{(p,l)})^4\mid F\big]\le C,\\
  \text{(ii)}&\quad \operatorname{Var}\big(\|z^{(p,l)}\|^2\mid F\big)\le C\,p .
\end{align*}
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
By the nesting convention, $\Delta^{(p)}$ is the leading $p\times p$ block
of $\Delta^{(p')}$ whenever $p'\ge p$, so $\delta_i^2$ does not depend on
$p\ge i$.  For a unit vector $a$, Jensen's inequality and condition~(i)
give $(a^{\!\top}\Delta^{(p)}a)^2\le\mathbb{E}[(a^{\!\top}z^{(p,l)})^4\mid F]\le C$;
taking $a$ to be a unit eigenvector,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Deltabd}
  \|\Delta^{(p)}\|\le\sqrt C\quad\text{for all }p,
  \qquad\text{and in particular}\qquad
  \sup_i\delta_i^2\le\sqrt C .
\end{equation}
Thus Assumption~\ref{asm:noise} does not require independence across
assets: it imposes the approximate factor structure of
\cite{chamberlain1983}, in which the specific covariance has uniformly
bounded eigenvalues.  Condition~(ii) ensures that $\|z^{(p,l)}\|^2$ deviates from its mean
$\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}\sim\delta^2p$ by $O_P(\sqrt p)$; it holds, for example, when
$\sup_i\sum_j|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{il}^2,Z_{jl}^2\mid F)|\le C$.
Condition~(i) controls the correlation of the specific returns themselves.

Because Assumption~\ref{asm:noise} conditions on the whole factor path, it
forces $\mathbb{E}[z^{(l)}f^{(m)\top}]=0$ for every pair $l,m$, and it makes the
observation covariance $\Sigma_y^{(l)}=\Sigma_0+\Delta^{(p)}$ the same for
every $l$, so that the targets $b_j$ are common to all observations
(Lemma~\ref{lem:uncorr} of the Appendix).
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
\label{asm:delta}
$\dfrac1p\sum_{i=1}^p\delta_i^2=\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}}{p}\longrightarrow\delta^2>0$ as
$p\to\infty$.
<!-- end assumption-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The limiting average specific variance $\delta^2$ plays an important role in the error estimates that we develop below.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- assumption-4: body_md -->
\label{asm:gram}
$\dfrac{B^{\!\top}B}{p}\longrightarrow G_B$ as $p \to \infty$, for some positive definite
$G_B\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}$.
<!-- end assumption-4 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
When the entries of $B$ are uniformly bounded, as in typical financial applications, Assumption~\ref{asm:gram} 
implies that the loadings on each factor are
{\em pervasive} in the sense that a positive fraction of the loadings are bounded away from zero.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- assumption-5: body_md -->
\label{asm:sep}
The deterministic symmetric matrix $M_f := G_B^{1/2}\,\Sigma_f\,G_B^{1/2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}$ has
distinct eigenvalues $\mu_1>\cdots>\mu_k$.
<!-- end assumption-5 -->
<!-- assumption-6: body_md -->
\label{asm:reg}
The realized $k\times k$ symmetric matrix $M_F:=G_B^{1/2}\,\Sigma_F\,G_B^{1/2}$, where $\Sigma_F:=FF^{\!\top}/n$, has distinct positive
eigenvalues $\lambda_1>\cdots>\lambda_k>0$.
<!-- end assumption-6 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:fm}--\ref{asm:reg} hold.  For  $j\in\{1,\dots,k\}$:

\medskip\noindent\emph{(i) Principal direction error converges for large $p$.} Conditional on $F$ and almost
surely as $p\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thm}
  \sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,b_j)
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \underbrace{\frac{\delta^2}{n\lambda_j+\delta^2}}
            _{\text{out-of-subspace error }}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\frac{n\lambda_j}{n\lambda_j+\delta^2}\,
              \sin^2\!\angle(\omega_j,\,\beta_j)}
            _{\text{in-subspace error }}.
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\emph{(ii) Each component of principal direction error converges for large $p$.}  Conditional on
$F$ and almost surely as $p\to\infty$, one has $\Pi h_j\neq0$ for all
sufficiently large $p$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thmfloor}
  \sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B)\;\longrightarrow\;
    \frac{\delta^2}{n\lambda_j+\delta^2},
  \qquad
  \cos^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B)\;\longrightarrow\;
    \frac{n\lambda_j}{n\lambda_j+\delta^2},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thmrot}
  \sin^2\!\angle_p\big(\Pi h_j,\,b_j\big)\;\longrightarrow\;
    \sin^2\!\angle\big(\omega_j,\,\beta_j\big) .
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:noisefree}
Let $S_0^{(p)}:=BFF^{\!\top}B^{\!\top}/(np)$ be the \emph{noiseless sample covariance}, the scaled sample covariance of the systematic part $BF$ of the data.  Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, for all sufficiently large $p$ the $j$th eigenvalue $\lambda_j^{(p)}$ of $S_0^{(p)}$ is simple and positive, $\lambda_j^{(p)}\to\lambda_j$, and its unit eigenvector $h_j^0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noisefree}
  \sin^2\!\angle_p\big(h_j^0,\,b_j\big)\;\longrightarrow\;\sin^2\!\angle\big(\omega_j,\,\beta_j\big)=\operatorname{RE}_j .
\end{equation}
This convergence involves no noise and holds for each fixed path $F$.  Moreover, conditional on $F$ and almost surely, $\angle_p(\Pi h_j,h_j^0)\to0$: within the factor subspace, the sample principal component $h_j$ asymptotically points along the noise-free principal component $h_j^0$.  Consequently, conditional on $F$ and almost surely,
\[
  \sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,b_j)\;-\;\Big[\sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B)+\cos^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B)\,\sin^2\!\angle_p\big(h_j^0,b_j\big)\Big]\;\longrightarrow\;0 .
\]
The proof is given at the end of Section~\ref{sec:main-proof}.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
Dividing top and bottom by $n$ shows that the out-of-subspace error is the fraction of scaled variance $\lambda_j + \delta^2/n$ attributable to noise $\delta^2/n$, while the in-subspace error is weighted by the fraction attributable to signal $\lambda_j$.
In terms of the finite-sample realized signal-to-noise ratio $\mathrm{SNR}_j := n\lambda_j/\delta^2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thm_snr}
  \sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,b_j)
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \frac{1}{1+\mathrm{SNR}_j}
  \,+\,
  \frac{\mathrm{SNR}_j}{1+\mathrm{SNR}_j}\,
  \sin^2\!\angle(\omega_j,\beta_j).
\end{equation}
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}(c),(d) and Lemma~\ref{lem:econv}(i), there is a finite $p_0$, depending on the realization, such that for $p\ge p_0$ the top $k$ eigenvalues $\theta_1^{(p)}>\cdots>\theta_k^{(p)}>0$ of $W^{(p)}$ are simple; we consider only such $p$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gramdual}(ii), applied to $A=Y/\sqrt{np}$, together with $Y=BF+Z$ and $B=\sqrt p\,\bar b\,(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:chart}(i)), gives, up to sign,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recon}
  h_j=\frac{Y\,w_j^{(p)}}{\sqrt{np\,\theta_j^{(p)}}}
     =\bar b\,\xi_j^{(p)}+\frac{Z\,w_j^{(p)}}{\sqrt{np\,\theta_j^{(p)}}},
  \qquad
  \xi_j^{(p)}:=\frac{(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}F\,w_j^{(p)}}{\sqrt{n\,\theta_j^{(p)}}}\in\mathbb{R}^k .
\end{equation}
<!-- end head-2 -->
<!-- head-3: body_md -->
Since $\Pi^{\perp}\bar b=0$ and $\|\Pi^{\perp} v\|^2=\|v\|^2-\|b^{\!\top}v\|^2$, \eqref{eq:recon} gives
\[
  \|\Pi^{\perp} h_j\|^2=\frac{1}{n\,\theta_j^{(p)}}
  \Big(w_j^{(p)\top}\tfrac{Z^{\!\top}Z}{p}\,w_j^{(p)}-\tfrac{1}{p}\|b^{\!\top}Zw_j^{(p)}\|^2\Big).
\]
The first term in parentheses tends to $\delta^2$, because $Z^{\!\top}Z/p\to\delta^2I_n$ in spectral norm and $\|w_j^{(p)}\|=1$; the second is at most $\|b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2/p=o(1)$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floor}
  \|\Pi^{\perp} h_j\|^2\longrightarrow\frac{\delta^2}{n\lambda_j+\delta^2},
  \qquad
  \|\Pi h_j\|^2=1-\|\Pi^{\perp} h_j\|^2\longrightarrow c_j^2 .
\end{equation}
<!-- end head-3 -->
<!-- head-4: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:chart}(iii), $\bar b^{\!\top}b_j=\beta_j^{(p)}$, so \eqref{eq:recon} gives
\[
  \langle h_j,b_j\rangle=\langle\xi_j^{(p)},\beta_j^{(p)}\rangle+r_p,
  \qquad
  |r_p|=\frac{|b_j^{\!\top}Zw_j^{(p)}|}{\sqrt{np\,\theta_j^{(p)}}}
  \le\frac{\|b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}}{\sqrt{np\,\theta_j^{(p)}}}=o(1).
\]
By the inputs above and the duality link \eqref{eq:omegadual}, $G_B^{1/2}Fw_j=\sqrt{n\lambda_j}\,\omega_j$,
\[
  \xi_j^{(p)}\xi_j^{(p)\top}
  =\frac{(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}F\,w_j^{(p)}w_j^{(p)\top}F^{\!\top}(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}}{n\,\theta_j^{(p)}}
  \longrightarrow
  \frac{G_B^{1/2}F\,w_jw_j^{\!\top}F^{\!\top}G_B^{1/2}}{n\lambda_j+\delta^2}
  =c_j^2\,\omega_j\omega_j^{\!\top}.
\]
Taking the trace, $\|\xi_j^{(p)}\|^2\to c_j^2$, so $\xi_j^{(p)}$ is bounded; and, using $\beta_j^{(p)}\beta_j^{(p)\top}\to\beta_j\beta_j^{\!\top}$,
\[
  \langle\xi_j^{(p)},\beta_j^{(p)}\rangle^2
  =\operatorname{tr}\big(\xi_j^{(p)}\xi_j^{(p)\top}\beta_j^{(p)}\beta_j^{(p)\top}\big)
  \longrightarrow c_j^2\operatorname{tr}\big(\omega_j\omega_j^{\!\top}\beta_j\beta_j^{\!\top}\big)
  =c_j^2\langle\omega_j,\beta_j\rangle^2 .
\]
Since $\langle h_j,b_j\rangle^2-\langle\xi_j^{(p)},\beta_j^{(p)}\rangle^2=r_p\big(2\langle\xi_j^{(p)},\beta_j^{(p)}\rangle+r_p\big)\to0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:align}
  \langle h_j,b_j\rangle^2\longrightarrow c_j^2\cos^2\!\angle(\omega_j,\beta_j).
\end{equation}
<!-- end head-4 -->
<!-- head-6: body_md -->
We continue with the notation and the almost sure event of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
By \eqref{eq:frameforms}, $S_0^{(p)}=\bar b\,M_F^{(p)}\,\bar b^{\!\top}$ with $\bar b^{\!\top}\bar b=I_k$, so the nonzero eigenvalues of $S_0^{(p)}$ are those of $M_F^{(p)}$, with unit eigenvectors $\bar b\,\omega_j^{(p)}$, where $\omega_j^{(p)}$ is a unit eigenvector of $M_F^{(p)}$.  Since $M_F^{(p)}\to M_F$ and $M_F$ has the simple positive eigenvalues $\lambda_1>\cdots>\lambda_k>0$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:reg}), Lemma~\ref{lem:econv}(i) shows that for large $p$ the eigenvalue $\lambda_j^{(p)}$ is simple and positive, $\lambda_j^{(p)}\to\lambda_j$, and $\omega_j^{(p)}\omega_j^{(p)\top}\to\omega_j\omega_j^{\!\top}$.  Thus $h_j^0=\pm\bar b\,\omega_j^{(p)}$ and, by \eqref{eq:bjframe},
\[
  \langle h_j^0,b_j\rangle^2=\langle\omega_j^{(p)},\beta_j^{(p)}\rangle^2
  =\operatorname{tr}\big(\omega_j^{(p)}\omega_j^{(p)\top}\beta_j^{(p)}\beta_j^{(p)\top}\big)
  \longrightarrow\langle\omega_j,\beta_j\rangle^2 ,
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:noisefree}; no probability is involved.  Next, by \eqref{eq:recon},
\[
  \pm\langle h_j,h_j^0\rangle=\langle\xi_j^{(p)},\omega_j^{(p)}\rangle+r_p',
  \qquad
  |r_p'|=\frac{|\omega_j^{(p)\top}\bar b^{\!\top}Zw_j^{(p)}|}{\sqrt{np\,\theta_j^{(p)}}}
  \le\frac{\|\bar b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}}{\sqrt{np\,\theta_j^{(p)}}}=o(1),
\]
since $\|\bar b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2=\operatorname{tr}(Z^{\!\top}\Pi Z)=\|b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2$.  By Step~3, $\xi_j^{(p)}$ is bounded and $\xi_j^{(p)}\xi_j^{(p)\top}\to c_j^2\,\omega_j\omega_j^{\!\top}$, so
$\langle\xi_j^{(p)},\omega_j^{(p)}\rangle^2=\operatorname{tr}\big(\xi_j^{(p)}\xi_j^{(p)\top}\omega_j^{(p)}\omega_j^{(p)\top}\big)\to c_j^2$, and therefore $\langle h_j,h_j^0\rangle^2\to c_j^2$.  Since $h_j^0\in\mathcal B$, $\langle h_j,h_j^0\rangle=\langle\Pi h_j,h_j^0\rangle$, and by \eqref{eq:floor}
\[
  \cos^2\!\angle_p(\Pi h_j,h_j^0)=\frac{\langle h_j,h_j^0\rangle^2}{\|\Pi h_j\|^2}\longrightarrow1 .
\]
Finally, by \eqref{eq:thmfloor} and \eqref{eq:noisefree}, the bracketed expression in Remark~\ref{rem:noisefree} converges to $(1-c_j^2)+c_j^2\sin^2\!\angle(\omega_j,\beta_j)$, which is also the limit of $\sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,b_j)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i).
\hfill$\qed

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rem:invariance}
The left side of \eqref{eq:thm} depends only on the data $Y$ and the systematic covariance $\Sigma_0$, whereas the proof works in the loading frame $\bar b$ and the right side is written in terms of $G_B$, $\Sigma_f$ and $F$, which are not separately identifiable (Section~\ref{sec:fac-mod}).  The two descriptions are consistent, as the following direct computation shows.
Fix an invertible $R\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}$ not depending on $p$, and replace
\[
  (B,\;F,\;\Sigma_f)\quad\text{by}\quad(BR,\;R^{-1}F,\;R^{-1}\Sigma_fR^{-\top}).
\]
This leaves $Y$, $\Sigma_0$, $\mathcal B$, $\Pi$, $b_j$, $h_j$ and $(\theta_j^{(p)},w_j^{(p)})$ unchanged, and Assumptions~\ref{asm:fm}--\ref{asm:reg} continue to hold, with $G_B$ replaced by $R^{\!\top}G_BR$.  The matrices
\[
  Q_p:=(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}R\,\big(R^{\!\top}G_B^{(p)}R\big)^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  Q:=G_B^{1/2}R\,\big(R^{\!\top}G_BR\big)^{-1/2}
\]
are orthogonal, and
\[
  \bar b\mapsto\bar b\,Q_p,\quad
  \beta_j^{(p)}\mapsto Q_p^{\!\top}\beta_j^{(p)},\quad
  \xi_j^{(p)}\mapsto Q_p^{\!\top}\xi_j^{(p)},\qquad

  M_f\mapsto Q^{\!\top}M_fQ,\quad

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:obsfloor}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:fm}--\ref{asm:reg}, conditional on $F$ and
almost surely as $p\to\infty$, for each $j\in\{1,\dots,k\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obsfloor}
  \lim_{p\to\infty} \;  \frac{\ell^{(p)}}{\theta_j^{(p)}}
   \;=\;
   \frac{\delta^2}{\,n\lambda_j+\delta^2\,}
   \;=\;\lim_{p\to\infty}  \sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B).  
\end{equation}


 Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obsfloorineq}
\lim_{p\to\infty} \; \frac{\ell^{(p)}}{\theta_j^{(p)}} \;\le\;\lim_{p\to\infty}\sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,b_j),
\end{equation}
with equality precisely when the in-subspace rotation 
$\sin^2\!\angle(\omega_j,\beta_j)$ vanishes.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
This means $\ell^{(p)}/\theta_j^{(p)}$ is a strongly consistent, fully observable
estimator of out-of-subspace error $ \sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B)$, an asymptotic lower bound for the total error.
<!-- end remark-8 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}(c), $W^{(p)}\to W$ in spectral norm,
a.s.  The limit $W=F^{\!\top}G_BF/n+(\delta^2/n)I_n$ of
\eqref{eq:Wlim} has top $k$ eigenvalues $\lambda_j+\delta^2/n$ and
smallest eigenvalue $\delta^2/n$ with multiplicity $n-k$.
 Weyl's inequality
gives, for every $i$,
$|\theta_i -\operatorname{eig}_i(W)|\le\|W^{(p)}-W\|\to0$,
where $\operatorname{eig}_i(W)$ denotes the $i$th eigenvalue of $W$.
In
particular $\theta_i\to\delta^2/n$ for each $i\in\{k+1,\dots,n\}$, and
averaging these $n-k$ limits yields $\ell^{(p)} \to\delta^2/n$.  The
convergence $\theta_j\to\lambda_j+\delta^2/n>0$ for

strictly positive,
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{\ell^{(p)}}{\theta_j^{(p)}}
   \longrightarrow
   \frac{\delta^2/n}{\lambda_j+\delta^2/n}
   =\frac{\delta^2}{n\lambda_j+\delta^2},
\end{equation*}
which equals $\lim_{p\to\infty}\|\Pi^{\perp} h_j\|^2$ by \eqref{eq:floor}, proving
\eqref{eq:obsfloor}.  Finally, the inequality in \eqref{eq:obsfloorineq} is immediate from
\eqref{eq:thm}, whose in-subspace summand is nonnegative and vanishes if and only if
$\sin^2\!\angle(\omega_j,\beta_j)=0$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:subspace}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:fm}--\ref{asm:reg}, conditional on $F$ and
almost surely as $p\to\infty$, the distance between the subspaces $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ is equal to the sum of the out-of-subspace floors of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. That is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subspace}
  \sum_{j=1}^{k}\sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B) \;=\; \tfrac12\,\|\Pi_{\mathcal H}-\Pi_{\mathcal B}\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2
   \;\longrightarrow\;
   \sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\delta^2}{\,n\lambda_j+\delta^2\,},
\end{equation}
where the first
expression is the sum of the squared sines of the $k$ principal
angles between ${\mathcal H}$ and $\mathcal B$.

By
Theorem~\ref{thm:obsfloor}, the limit is consistently estimated by the
observable statistic $\sum_{j=1}^{k}\ell^{(p)}/\theta_j^{(p)}$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $\sin^2\!\angle_p(h_j,\mathcal B)=\|\Pi^{\perp} h_j\|^2$, the limit in
\eqref{eq:subspace} is the sum over $j=1,\dots,k$ of the limits
\eqref{eq:floor} established in Step~2 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  For the first equality, expand
\begin{eqnarray}
    \|\Pi_{\mathcal H}-\Pi_{\mathcal B}\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2&=&\operatorname{tr} \Pi_{\mathcal H}+\operatorname{tr} \Pi_{\mathcal B}-2\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{\mathcal H}\Pi_{\mathcal B}) \nonumber \\
&=&2k-2\sum_{j}h_j^{\!\top}\Pi h_j
=2\sum_{j}\big(1-\|\Pi h_j\|^2\big)=2\sum_{j}\|\Pi^{\perp} h_j\|^2,
\end{eqnarray}
using $\|A\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2 = \operatorname{tr} A^2$ for any symmetric matrix $A$, and that $H$ and $b$ each have $k$ orthonormal columns.

 For the
principal-angle identification: the cosines of the principal angles $\eta_i$ between
the $k$-dimensional subspaces $\operatorname{col}(H)$ and $\mathcal B$ are the singular
 values $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k$ of $b^{\!\top}H$ \citep[\S6.4.3]{golubvanloan2013},
 so
$$\sum_{i}\sin^2\eta_i=k-\sum_i\sigma_i^2=k-\|b^{\!\top}H\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2
=\sum_{j}\big(1-\|\Pi h_j\|^2\big) = \tfrac12\,\|\Pi_{\mathcal H}-\Pi_{\mathcal B}\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2.$$
The final claim
combines \eqref{eq:subspace} with \eqref{eq:obsfloor} summed over $j$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
Since each out-of-subspace term is strictly positive, the limit in \eqref{eq:subspace} is
strictly positive, which means the sample subspace $\operatorname{col}(H)$ is not a consistent
estimator of $\mathcal B$ in the Frobenius norm in $p$.
Instead, Corollary \ref{cor:subspace} provides us with a consistent estimator of the distance between the subspaces, as measured by the left hand side of \eqref{eq:subspace}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:gramdual}
Let $A$ be a real matrix.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $AA^{\!\top}$ and $A^{\!\top}A$ have the same positive eigenvalues,
with the same multiplicities. Moreover, for $\lambda>0$ and each $j$, $\lambda$
is the $j$th largest eigenvalue (counted with multiplicity) of $AA^{\!\top}$ if
and only if it is the $j$th largest eigenvalue of $A^{\!\top}A$.
\item[(ii)] Suppose $\lambda>0$ is the $j$th largest eigenvalue of one of
$AA^{\!\top}$, $A^{\!\top}A$ and is simple there. Then the same holds for the
other. If $u$ is a unit eigenvector of $A^{\!\top}A$ for $\lambda$, then
$Au/\sqrt\lambda$ is a unit eigenvector of $AA^{\!\top}$ for $\lambda$;
conversely, if $v$ is a unit eigenvector of $AA^{\!\top}$ for $\lambda$, then
$A^{\!\top}v/\sqrt\lambda$ is a unit eigenvector of $A^{\!\top}A$ for
$\lambda$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:econv}
Let symmetric $A^{(p)}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$ converge entrywise to $A$, and let
$\zeta$ be a simple eigenvalue of $A$ with unit eigenvector $v$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For large $p$, $A^{(p)}$ has a simple eigenvalue
$\zeta^{(p)}\to\zeta$, whose unit eigenvectors $v^{(p)}$ (regardless of sign choice) satisfy
$\angle(v^{(p)},v)\to0$; equivalently, $|\langle v^{(p)},v\rangle|\to1$.
\item[(ii)] For unit vectors $u,v\in\mathbb{R}^m$,
$\|uu^{\!\top}-vv^{\!\top}\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2=2\sin^2\!\angle(u,v)$. Consequently,
for unit vectors $u^{(p)},u$, $\angle(u^{(p)},u)\to0$ if and only if
$u^{(p)}u^{(p)\top}\to uu^{\!\top}$; both statements are independent of the
signs of $u^{(p)}$ and $u$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\emph{(i)} Weyl's inequality gives $|\operatorname{eig}_i(A^{(p)})-\operatorname{eig}_i(A)|\le\|A^{(p)}-A\|\to0$,
so the eigenvalue $\zeta^{(p)}$ nearest $\zeta$ is, for all large enough $p$,  the unique one
within half the spectral gap of $\zeta$, is simple, and $\zeta^{(p)}\to\zeta$.

To show $c_p := |\langle v^{(p)}, v\rangle| \to 1$, it suffices to establish that every subsequence $c_{p_k}$ has a further subsequence converging to 1.

Let $p_k$ define a subsequence $c_{p_k} = |\langle v^{(p_k)}, v\rangle| $. Since the $v^{(p_k)}$ live on the compact unit sphere, there is a further subsequence $\{p(j)\}$ of $\{p_k\}$ such that $v^{(p(j))}$ converges to a limiting unit vector $\bar v$.  Hence
$$
A \bar v = \lim_{j \to \infty} A^{(p(j))} v^{(p(j))} = \lim_{j \to \infty} \zeta^{(p(j))} v^{(p(j))} = \zeta \bar v.
$$
By simplicity of the eigenvalue $\zeta$, this means $\bar v = \pm v$. Hence
$$
c_{p(j)} = |\langle v^{(p(j))},  v \rangle | = |\langle v^{(p(j))},  \bar v \rangle | \to 1
$$
as desired.

\emph{(ii)} Expanding with $\operatorname{tr}(uu^{\!\top})=\operatorname{tr}(vv^{\!\top})=1$ and
$\operatorname{tr}(uu^{\!\top}vv^{\!\top})=\langle u,v\rangle^2$,
\[
\|uu^{\!\top}-vv^{\!\top}\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}^2
=\operatorname{tr}\big((uu^{\!\top}-vv^{\!\top})^2\big)
=2-2\langle u,v\rangle^2=2\sin^2\!\angle(u,v),
\]
by \eqref{eq:angledef}. The equivalence follows since all norms on
$\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$ are equivalent.

\label{lem:basis}
    Given the $p \times k$  rank $k$ matrices $B$ and $b$ and other notation defined in Section \ref{sec:fac-mod},
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $C = (B^\top B)^{-1}B^\top b$ is the unique invertible $k \times k$ matrix such that $b = BC$;
    \item $C^{-1} = b^\top B$ and hence
    \begin{equation}
        \phi := C^{-1}f = b^\top Bf ;
    \end{equation}
    and
    \item $\mathbb{E}[\phi \phi^\top] = \Delta_0$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:chart}
Let $\bar b=B(B^{\!\top}B)^{-1/2}$, $G_B^{(p)}=B^{\!\top}B/p$, $M_f^{(p)}=(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}\Sigma_f(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}$ and $M_f=G_B^{1/2}\Sigma_fG_B^{1/2}$ be as in \eqref{eq:bbar}, \eqref{eq:Mpdef} and Assumption~\ref{asm:sep}, and write $\mu_1^{(p)}\ge\cdots\ge\mu_k^{(p)}$ for the eigenvalues of $M_f^{(p)}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For every $p$, $\bar b^{\!\top}\bar b=I_k$, $\bar b\bar b^{\!\top}=\Pi$, $B=\sqrt p\,\bar b\,(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}$, and $\Sigma_0/p=\bar b\,M_f^{(p)}\,\bar b^{\!\top}$.  Consequently the nonzero eigenvalues of $\Sigma_0/p$, counted with multiplicity, are $\mu_1^{(p)},\dots,\mu_k^{(p)}$, all positive; these are the $\mu_j^{(p)}$ of Section~\ref{sec:fac-mod}.
\item[(ii)] As $p\to\infty$, $(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}\to G_B^{1/2}$, $M_f^{(p)}\to M_f$, and $\mu_j^{(p)}\to\mu_j$.  Hence, for large enough $p$, $\mu_1^{(p)}>\cdots>\mu_k^{(p)}>0$, and the spectral decomposition of $\Sigma_0=B\Sigma_fB^{\!\top}$ is $b\,\Delta_0\,b^{\!\top}$ with $\Delta_0:=p\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1^{(p)},\dots,\mu_k^{(p)})$, unique up to signs of the columns of $b$.
\item[(iii)] For $p$ as in (ii), $\beta_j^{(p)}:=\bar b^{\!\top}b_j$ is the $j$th unit eigenvector of $M_f^{(p)}$; equivalently, $b_j=\bar b\,\beta_j^{(p)}$.  Moreover $bb^{\!\top}=\bar b\bar b^{\!\top}$.
\item[(iv)] $\angle(\beta_j^{(p)},\beta_j)\to0$, where $\beta_j$ is the $j$th unit eigenvector of $M_f$ (unique up to sign by Assumption~\ref{asm:sep}).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
\emph{(i)} $\bar b^\top\bar b=(B^\top B)^{-1/2}B^\top B(B^\top B)^{-1/2}=I_k$ and $\operatorname{col}(\bar b)=\operatorname{col}(B)=\mathcal B$, so $\bar b\bar b^\top$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\mathcal B$. Since $(B^\top B)^{1/2}=\sqrt p\,(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}$, we have $B=\bar b(B^\top B)^{1/2}=\sqrt p\,\bar b\,(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}$, and substituting into $\Sigma_0=B\Sigma_fB^\top$ gives $\Sigma_0/p=\bar b\,M_f^{(p)}\,\bar b^\top$.
The matrix $M_f^{(p)}$ is positive definite, because $\Sigma_f$ is and $G_B^{(p)}$ is ($B$ has rank $k$). Write $M_f^{(p)}=\sum_{j=1}^k\mu_j^{(p)}v_jv_j^\top$ with $v_1,\dots,v_k$ orthonormal. Then
$$\frac{\Sigma_0}{p}=\sum_{j=1}^k\mu_j^{(p)}\,(\bar b v_j)(\bar b v_j)^\top ,$$
and since $\bar b$ is an isometry the vectors $\bar b v_1,\dots,\bar b v_k$ are orthonormal. This is therefore a spectral decomposition of $\Sigma_0/p$, whose nonzero eigenvalues are the positive numbers $\mu_j^{(p)}$, with unit eigenvectors $\bar b v_j$.

\emph{(ii)} Since $G_B^{(p)}\to G_B$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:gram}) with $G_B$ positive definite, and the positive definite square root is continuous on positive definite matrices (a standard fact), $(G_B^{(p)})^{1/2}\to G_B^{1/2}$ and hence $M_f^{(p)}\to M_f$. Weyl's inequality, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:econv}(i), gives $\mu_j^{(p)}\to\mu_j$ for each $j$. The $\mu_j$ are distinct and positive (Assumption~\ref{asm:sep}), so for large enough $p$ so are the $\mu_j^{(p)}$. By (i), $\Sigma_0/p$ then has the simple nonzero eigenvalues $\mu_1^{(p)}>\cdots>\mu_k^{(p)}$ with unit eigenvectors $\bar b v_j$, which gives $\Sigma_0=b\Delta_0b^\top$ with $b_j=\pm\bar b v_j$, unique up to sign.

\emph{(iii)} By (ii), $b_j=\pm\bar b v_j$, so $\beta_j^{(p)}=\bar b^\top b_j=\pm v_j$ by (i), which is a unit eigenvector of $M_f^{(p)}$ for its $j$th eigenvalue $\mu_j^{(p)}$; and $\bar b\,\beta_j^{(p)}=\pm\bar b v_j=b_j$. Finally, since $v_1,\dots,v_k$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^k$, $bb^\top=\sum_j\bar b v_jv_j^\top\bar b^\top=\bar b\bar b^\top$.

\emph{(iv)} By (ii), $M_f^{(p)}\to M_f$, whose eigenvalues are simple (Assumption~\ref{asm:sep}). By (iii), $\beta_j^{(p)}$ is a unit eigenvector of $M_f^{(p)}$ for $\mu_j^{(p)}$, the eigenvalue converging to $\mu_j$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:econv}(i) gives $\angle(\beta_j^{(p)},\beta_j)\to0$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:zconc}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:noise} and~\ref{asm:delta} hold, write
$Z_{\cdot l}=z^{(p,l)}$ for the $l$th column of $Z=Z^{(p)}$, and let
$a=a^{(p)}\in\mathbb{R}^p$ be deterministic unit vectors.  Conditional on $F$,
almost surely as $p\to\infty$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $a^{\!\top}Z_{\cdot l}/\sqrt p\to0$ for each $l$;
\item[(ii)] $\|Z_{\cdot l}\|^2/p\to\delta^2$ for each $l$;
\item[(iii)] $Z_{\cdot l}^{\!\top}Z_{\cdot m}/p\to0$ for each $l\ne m$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Fix $F$ in the probability-one set on which the bounds of
Assumption~\ref{asm:noise} hold, and write $\Pr$ and $\mathbb{E}$ for conditional
probability and expectation given $F$; all constants below are
deterministic.  Each part follows from a tail bound and the
Borel--Cantelli lemma, applied for each $\epsilon$ in a sequence
decreasing to $0$.

\emph{(i)} By Markov's inequality and condition~(i) of
Assumption~\ref{asm:noise},
\[
  \Pr\big(|a^{\!\top}Z_{\cdot l}|>\epsilon\sqrt p\big)
  \le\frac{\mathbb{E}(a^{\!\top}Z_{\cdot l})^4}{\epsilon^4p^2}
  \le\frac{C}{\epsilon^4p^2},
\]
which is summable in $p$.

\emph{(ii)} Fix $l$ and set $S_p:=\|Z_{\cdot l}\|^2=\sum_{i\le p}Z_{il}^2$,
so that $\mathbb{E} S_p=\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}$.  Chebyshev's inequality and
condition~(ii) give
\[
  \Pr\big(|S_p-\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}|>\epsilon p\big)\le\frac{C}{\epsilon^2p}.
\]
This bound is not summable over all $p$, but it is summable along
$p_k:=k^2$.  Hence $(S_{p_k}-\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p_k)})/p_k\to0$, and therefore
$S_{p_k}/p_k\to\delta^2$ by Assumption~\ref{asm:delta}, almost surely.
By the nesting convention the summands $Z_{il}^2$ do not depend on $p$, so
$S_p$ is nondecreasing in $p$.  For $p_k\le p<p_{k+1}$,
\[
  \frac{p_k}{p_{k+1}}\cdot\frac{S_{p_k}}{p_k}
  \;\le\;\frac{S_p}{p}\;\le\;
  \frac{p_{k+1}}{p_k}\cdot\frac{S_{p_{k+1}}}{p_{k+1}},
\]
and $p_{k+1}/p_k\to1$, so both bounds converge to $\delta^2$ almost surely.

\emph{(iii)} Fix $l\ne m$ and write $u:=Z_{\cdot l}$, $v:=Z_{\cdot m}$.
Given $F$, $u$ and $v$ are independent, so we may integrate first over $u$
with $v$ held fixed; applying Assumption~\ref{asm:noise}(i) in the direction $v/\|v\|$ on
$\{v\ne0\}$, and then Assumption~\ref{asm:noise}(ii) together with
$\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}\le p\|\Delta^{(p)}\|\le p\sqrt C$ from \eqref{eq:Deltabd},
\[
  \mathbb{E}\big(u^{\!\top}v\big)^4\le C\,\mathbb{E}\|v\|^4
  =C\Big(\operatorname{Var}\big(\|v\|^2\big)+\big(\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}\big)^2\Big)
  \le C\big(Cp+Cp^2\big)\le 2C^2p^2 .
\]
Markov's inequality gives
$\Pr\big(|u^{\!\top}v|>\epsilon p\big)\le 2C^2/(\epsilon^4p^2)$, which is
summable in $p$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:dual}
Under our Assumptions,
conditional on $F$ and a.s.\ as $p\to\infty$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $Z^{\!\top}Z/(np)\to(\delta^2/n)I_n$ in spectral norm;
\item[(b)] $\|\Pi Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}=\|b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}=o(\sqrt p)$;
\item[(c)] $W^{(p)}\to W$ of \eqref{eq:Wlim} in spectral norm, with top
$k$ eigenpairs $(\lambda_j+\delta^2/n,\,w_j)$; and
\item[(d)] $\theta_j^{(p)}\to\lambda_j+\delta^2/n>0$ and
$\angle\big(w_j^{(p)},w_j\big)\to0$, $j=1,\dots,k$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
\emph{(a)} As $n$ is fixed, entrywise a.s.\ convergence implies
spectral-norm convergence.  The $(l,l)$ entry of $Z^{\!\top}Z/(np)$ is
$\|Z_{\cdot l}\|^2/(np)\to\delta^2/n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:zconc}(ii), and the
$(l,m)$ entry, $l\ne m$, is $Z_{\cdot l}^{\!\top}Z_{\cdot m}/(np)\to0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:zconc}(iii).  Hence the matrix limit is
$(\delta^2/n)I_n$.

\emph{(b)} The $(j,m)$ entry of $b^{\!\top}Z$ is $b_j^{\!\top}Z_{\cdot m}$
with $b_j$ a deterministic unit vector, so $b_j^{\!\top}Z_{\cdot m}=o(\sqrt p)$
a.s.\ by Lemma~\ref{lem:zconc}(i); summing the squares of the $kn$ entries gives
$\|b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}=o(\sqrt p)$.  Since $b$ has orthonormal columns,
$\|\Pi Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}=\|bb^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}=\|b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}$.

\emph{(c)} Substituting $Y=b\Phi+Z$ and using $b^{\!\top}b=I$,
\begin{equation}
  W^{(p)}=\frac{\Phi^{\!\top}\Phi}{np}
   +\frac{\Phi^{\!\top}b^{\!\top}Z+Z^{\!\top}b\Phi}{np}
   +\frac{Z^{\!\top}Z}{np}.
\end{equation}
For the systematic block, $\Phi=b^{\!\top}BF$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:basis}, and $bb^{\!\top}B=B$ because $bb^{\!\top}$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\operatorname{col}(b)=\operatorname{col}(B)$ \eqref{eq:borth}.  Hence $\Phi^{\!\top}\Phi=F^{\!\top}B^{\!\top}bb^{\!\top}BF=F^{\!\top}B^{\!\top}BF$, and Assumption~\ref{asm:gram} gives
$$\Phi^{\!\top}\Phi/(np)=F^{\!\top}G_B^{(p)}F/n=W_0^{(p)}\to F^{\!\top}G_BF/n=W_0.$$
In particular $\|\Phi\|^2=\|\Phi^{\!\top}\Phi\|=np\,\|W_0^{(p)}\|=O(p)$, since $W_0^{(p)}$ converges; and by part (b), $\|b^\top Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}} = o(\sqrt{p})$.  Hence
the cross block is bounded in norm by
$$2\|\Phi\|\,\|b^{\!\top}Z\|_{\mathrm{Fr}}/(np)\le 2\,O(\sqrt p)\,o(\sqrt p)/(np)=o(1).$$
The noise
block converges to $(\delta^2/n)I_n$ by part (a), and hence $W^{(p)}\to W$.  The eigenstructure of

Each of the following conditions on the specific returns array
$\{Z_{il}: i\ge1,\ 1\le l\le n\}$ implies Assumption~\ref{asm:noise}.

  $Z_{il}$ are mutually independent and mean zero, and stationary across

  $\Delta^{(p)}=\operatorname{diag}(\delta_1^2,\dots,\delta_p^2)$, and
  Assumption~\ref{asm:noise} conditions~(i) and~(ii) hold with $C=4\kappa_4$.
\item[(b)] \emph{Gaussian specific returns.}  Conditional on $F$, the
  columns $(Z_{1l},Z_{2l},\dots)$, $l=1,\dots,n$, are independent and
  identically distributed mean-zero Gaussian sequences whose common
  covariance array $\Sigma$ is deterministic, with
  $\|\Sigma_{[p]}\|\le C_\Delta$ for all $p$.  In this case
  $\Delta^{(p)}=\Sigma_{[p]}$, and conditions~(i) and~(ii) hold with
  $C=3C_\Delta^2$.
\item[(c)] \emph{Linear representation.}  There are an infinite array
  $\{\varepsilon_{ml}: m\ge1,\ 1\le l\le n\}$ of random variables
  (innovations) and a deterministic infinite real array
  $A=\{A_{im}\}_{i,m\ge1}$, each row of which has finitely many nonzero

    \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{ml}^2\mid F]=1,\qquad

In each case fix a column $l$, write $z:=z^{(p,l)}$, and work
conditionally on $F$; all constants are deterministic.  Since the bounds
obtained do not depend on $l$, they hold for all $l$.

\emph{(a)} The columns are conditionally independent because all entries
are, and identically distributed by stationarity; they have mean zero and
conditional covariance $\operatorname{diag}(\delta_1^2,\dots,\delta_p^2)$,
which does not depend on $F$.  Jensen's inequality gives
$\delta_i^2\le\sqrt{\kappa_4}$.  For a unit vector $a$, expanding the
fourth power of $a^{\!\top}z=\sum_ia_iZ_{il}$, only terms in which every
index appears an even number of times survive, by independence and zero
means, so
\[
  \mathbb{E}(a^{\!\top}z)^4=\sum_ia_i^4\mathbb{E} Z_{il}^4
   +3\sum_{i\ne i'}a_i^2a_{i'}^2\delta_i^2\delta_{i'}^2
  \le\kappa_4\sum_ia_i^4+3\Big(\sum_ia_i^2\delta_i^2\Big)^2
  \le\kappa_4+3\kappa_4 ,
\]
using $\sum_ia_i^4\le(\sum_ia_i^2)^2=1$.  By independence,
$\operatorname{Var}(\|z\|^2)=\sum_{i\le p}\operatorname{Var}(Z_{il}^2)\le\sum_{i\le p}\mathbb{E} Z_{il}^4\le\kappa_4p$.

\emph{(b)} The columns are conditionally i.i.d.\ with mean zero and
conditional covariance $\Sigma_{[p]}$, which does not depend on $F$.  For
a unit vector $a$, $a^{\!\top}z$ is conditionally
$N(0,a^{\!\top}\Sigma_{[p]}a)$, so
$\mathbb{E}(a^{\!\top}z)^4=3(a^{\!\top}\Sigma_{[p]}a)^2\le3C_\Delta^2$.  Writing
$\Sigma_{[p]}=U\Lambda U^{\!\top}$ with $U$ orthogonal, $z$ has the law of
$U\Lambda^{1/2}g$ with $g\sim N(0,I_p)$, so $\|z\|^2$ has the law of
$\sum_i\Lambda_{ii}g_i^2$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(\|z\|^2)=2\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{[p]}^2\le2\|\Sigma_{[p]}\|\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{[p]}\le2C_\Delta^2p$.

\emph{(c)} Write
$\varepsilon^{(c,l)}:=(\varepsilon_{1l},\dots,\varepsilon_{cl})^{\!\top}$.
Since rows $i\le p$ of $A$ vanish beyond column $c(p)$,
$z^{(p,l)}=A_{[p]}\varepsilon^{(c(p),l)}$, and the first $p$ coordinates of
$z^{(p',l)}$ coincide with $z^{(p,l)}$ for every $p'\ge p$, consistent with
the nesting convention.  Each column is the same fixed linear map applied
to the innovation vector $(\varepsilon_{1l},\varepsilon_{2l},\dots)$, and
these vectors are conditionally independent and identically distributed
across $l$ given $F$; hence the columns are conditionally i.i.d., with
mean zero and conditional covariance $A_{[p]}A_{[p]}^{\!\top}=\Delta^{(p)}$,
which does not depend on $F$.

For the moment bounds we use two identities.  Let
$\xi_1,\dots,\xi_c$ be independent, mean-zero, unit-variance random
variables with $\max_m\mathbb{E}\xi_m^4\le\nu_4$ (so $\nu_4\ge1$).  For every
deterministic $u\in\mathbb{R}^c$ and every deterministic symmetric $c\times c$
matrix $M$,
\begin{align}
  \mathbb{E}\big(u^{\!\top}\xi\big)^4
  &=\sum_m u_m^4\,\mathbb{E}\xi_m^4+3\sum_{m\ne m'}u_m^2u_{m'}^2
   \;\le\;(\nu_4+3)\,\|u\|^4, \label{eq:M1}\\
  \mathbb{E}\big(\xi^{\!\top}M\xi-\operatorname{tr} M\big)^2
  &=\sum_m M_{mm}^2\operatorname{Var}\big(\xi_m^2\big)+2\sum_{m\ne m'}M_{mm'}^2
   \;\le\;(\nu_4+1)\operatorname{tr} M^2. \label{eq:M2}
\end{align}
Both follow by expanding and using independence, zero means and unit
variances.  In~\eqref{eq:M1} only terms in which every index appears an
even number of times survive.  For~\eqref{eq:M2}, write
$\xi^{\!\top}M\xi-\operatorname{tr} M=\sum_mM_{mm}(\xi_m^2-1)+\sum_{m\ne m'}M_{mm'}\xi_m\xi_{m'}$;
the two sums are uncorrelated, and $\mathbb{E}[\xi_m\xi_{m'}\xi_r\xi_{r'}]$ with
$m\ne m'$, $r\ne r'$ vanishes unless $\{r,r'\}=\{m,m'\}$.  The
inequality uses $\operatorname{Var}(\xi_m^2)\le\nu_4-1$.

Now apply these with $\xi:=\varepsilon^{(c(p),l)}$.  For a unit vector $a$,
$a^{\!\top}z=u^{\!\top}\xi$ with $u:=A_{[p]}^{\!\top}a$ and
$\|u\|^2=a^{\!\top}\Delta^{(p)}a\le C_\Delta$, so~\eqref{eq:M1} gives
$\mathbb{E}(a^{\!\top}z)^4\le(\nu_4+3)C_\Delta^2$.  Next,
$\|z\|^2=\xi^{\!\top}M\xi$ with $M:=A_{[p]}^{\!\top}A_{[p]}$, which has the
same nonzero eigenvalues as $\Delta^{(p)}$.  Hence
$\mathbb{E}\|z\|^2=\operatorname{tr} M=\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}$ and
$\operatorname{tr} M^2=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta^{(p)})^2\le\|\Delta^{(p)}\|\operatorname{tr}\Delta^{(p)}\le C_\Delta^2p$,
so~\eqref{eq:M2} gives
$\operatorname{Var}(\|z\|^2)\le(\nu_4+1)C_\Delta^2p\le(\nu_4+3)C_\Delta^2p$.

\label{lem:uncorr}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{asm:fm} and~\ref{asm:noise} hold, and write
$\mathcal G:=\sigma(F)$ for the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the factor
path.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}

    \begin{equation}\label{eq:allpairs}
      \mathbb{E}\big[Z_{il}f_a^{(m)}\big]=0,
      \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
      \mathbb{E}\big[z^{(l)}f^{(m)\top}\big]=0\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times k}.
    \end{equation}
  \item For each $l$, the observation $y^{(l)}=Bf^{(l)}+z^{(l)}$ has
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:Syl}
      \Sigma_y^{(l)}:=\mathbb{E}\big[y^{(l)}y^{(l)\top}\big]
        =B\Sigma_fB^{\!\top}+\Delta_z,
      \qquad
      \Delta_z=\Delta^{(p)},
    \end{equation}
    the conditional covariance of Assumption~\ref{asm:noise}.
    In particular $\Sigma_y^{(l)}$ does not depend on $l$ and equals the
    matrix $\Sigma_y$ of \eqref{eq:cov}.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
(1)  Conditional mean zero identifies the conditional second moment with
the conditional variance, so $\mathbb{E}[Z_{il}^2\mid\mathcal G]=\delta_i^2$,
and $\delta_i^2\le C^{1/2}$ by \eqref{eq:Deltabd}; taking
expectations, $\mathbb{E}[Z_{il}^2]\le C^{1/2}<\infty$.  By
Assumption~\ref{asm:fm}, $\mathbb{E}[(f_a^{(m)})^2]\le\mathbb{E}\|f^{(m)}\|^2<\infty$.
Cauchy--Schwarz then gives
\[
  \mathbb{E}\big|Z_{il}f_a^{(m)}\big|
  \le\big(\mathbb{E}[Z_{il}^2]\big)^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}[(f_a^{(m)})^2]\big)^{1/2}
  \le C^{1/4}\big(\mathbb{E}\|f^{(m)}\|^2\big)^{1/2}<\infty,
\]
so the product is integrable. Since
$f_a^{(m)}$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable, we have
$\mathbb{E}[Z_{il}f_a^{(m)}\mid\mathcal G]=f_a^{(m)}\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{il}\mid\mathcal G]=0$
almost surely.  Taking expectations gives \eqref{eq:allpairs}.

(2)  Expanding $y^{(l)}y^{(l)\top}$ and taking expectations,
\[
  \Sigma_y^{(l)}=B\,\mathbb{E}\big[f^{(l)}f^{(l)\top}\big]B^{\!\top}
    +B\,\mathbb{E}\big[f^{(l)}z^{(l)\top}\big]
    +\mathbb{E}\big[z^{(l)}f^{(l)\top}\big]B^{\!\top}
    +\mathbb{E}\big[z^{(l)}z^{(l)\top}\big].
\]
The first term is $B\Sigma_fB^{\!\top}$ by Assumption~\ref{asm:fm}, and the
two cross terms vanish by \eqref{eq:allpairs} with $m=l$.  For the last
term, Assumption~\ref{asm:noise} gives
$\mathbb{E}[z^{(l)}z^{(l)\top}\mid\mathcal G]=\Delta^{(p)}$, a constant; taking
expectations yields $\mathbb{E}[z^{(l)}z^{(l)\top}]=\Delta^{(p)}$, which does not
depend on $l$.
