<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:mls_error_bound_good_data}
  Let ${\mathcal X} \in \set{\mathbb T^{d},\mathcal{S}^{d},\mathrm{SO}(3)}{d \in \mathbb N}$ and $K \in \mathbb N$ be fixed,
  and choose the ansatz space ${\mathcal G}={\mathcal G}_K$ as in \eqref{eq:ansatz_space}. There
  exists a constant $\rho=\rho({\mathcal X},K)>0$ with the following
  property. 
  For every fixed $R \geq \rho$ and quasi-uniformity constant $c \geq 1$, there
  exist constants $h_0=h_0({\mathcal X},K,R)>0$ and
  $C=C({\mathcal X},K,\phi,c,R)>0$ such that, for every
  $f \in {\mathcal C}({\mathcal X})$ and every node set $X \subset {\mathcal X}$ with 
  \begin{equation*}
    h_{X} \leq h_{0}
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \frac{h_{X}}{q_{X}} \leq c ,
  \end{equation*}
  the MLS approximation with weight function \eqref{eq:weight_function_radial}
  and support radius $\delta \equiv R\,h_X$ satisfies 
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mls_error_bound_local}
    \abs{{\mathcal M}_{f\vert_{X}}(x) - f(x)}
    \leq C \cdot \inf\limits_{g \in {\mathcal G}} \norm{f - g}_{B_{\delta}(x),\infty} ,
    \qquad x \in {\mathcal X} .
  \end{equation}
  If additionally $f \in {\mathcal C}^{K+1}({\mathcal X})$, then there also exists a constant
  $C(f)$ such that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mls_error_bound_global}
    \norm{{\mathcal M}_{f\vert_{X}} - f}_{{\mathcal X},\infty}
    \leq C(f) \cdot C \cdot h_{X}^{K+1} . 
  \end{equation}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The proofs in~\cite{Lipman2009} are stated for MLS on $\mathbb R^{d}$. In the
  spherical setting, the final step in the proof of Theorem~3.2 in
  \cite{Lipman2009} needs a small adjustment. There, the ratio of ball volumes
  equals the ratio of their radii raised to the $d$-th power. For spherical
  caps, this equality is no longer valid, but a fixed ratio of radii still gives
  a uniformly bounded volume ratio.
  The other arguments need no adjustment or
  follow directly from known results such as \cite{Hielscher2024}, thus the
  result carries over to spheres and, via $\mathcal{S}^{3}$, to $\mathrm{SO}(3)$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
For a family of node sets, the local neighbor count   
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:number_of_local_nodes}
    n(x) \coloneqq \#X(x)
  \end{equation}
  can theoretically grow arbitrarily large for any $\varepsilon > 0$, as the 
  separation distance approaches zero. Quasi-uniformity is one possible remedy.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:chi vs cond}
  The diagonal entries of $\bm{H}$ equal one,
  thus $\lambda_{\max}(\bm{H}) \geq 1$, and therefore  
  \begin{equation*}
    \chi
    \leq \frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(\bm{H})}
    \leq \frac{\lambda_{\max}(\bm{H})}{\lambda_{\min}(\bm{H})}
    = \mathrm{cond}(\bm{H}) .
  \end{equation*}
  The converse fails: $\mathrm{cond}(\bm{H})$ can be arbitrarily large while $\chi$
  stays bounded. A condition-based instability measure can therefore introduce
  unnecessary regularization even when ${\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}(x)$ is stable.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:chi vs lebesgue}
  The MLS approximation at $x$ can be written as
  \begin{equation*}
    {\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}(x) = \sum_{i \in I} a_{i}^{\ast}\, y_{i} ,
    \qquad
    a_{i}^{\ast} = \sqrt{w_{i}}\, \overline{\psi_{i}} ,
    \qquad
    \bm{\psi} \coloneqq \bm{B} \bm{H}^{-1} \bm{e}_{1} ,
  \end{equation*}
  where $a_{i}^{\ast}$ are the values of the \emph{generating functions} of MLS
  at $x$~\cite{Wendland2001b}. The sum of their absolute values 
  $\Lambda(x) \coloneqq \sum_{i \in I} \abs{a_{i}^{\ast}}$ is the
  \emph{Lebesgue constant} of MLS at $x$. Since $\sum_{i \in I} w_i = 1$, the
  Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields  
  \begin{equation*}
    \Lambda(x)
    = \sum_{i \in I} \sqrt{w_{i}}\, \abs{\psi_{i}}
    \leq \braces[\bigg]{\sum_{i \in I} w_{i}}^{\nicefrac 12}
    \norm{\bm{\psi}}_{2}
    = \sqrt{\chi(x)} .
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore, controlling $\chi$ implicitly also controls the Lebesgue constant.
  Due to the standard error bound for unregularized MLS \cite[Theorem~2.5]{Hielscher2024} 
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lebesgue error bound}
    \abs{f(x) - {\mathcal M}_{f\vert_{X}}(x)}
    \leq \bigl(1 + \Lambda(x)\bigr)
    \inf_{g \in \Pi_K^{\mathrm{tan}}({\mathcal X};x)} \norm{f - g}_{B_{\delta}(x),\infty} ,
  \end{equation}
  this even implies error bounds for MLS.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
We can now see more precisely why regularized MLS should use polynomials
    up to degree $K$ in the local tangent space, instead of ${\mathcal G}_{K}$ as in 
    \eqref{eq:ansatz_space}. For odd $K$, the original space
    ${\mathcal G}_K$ on $\mathcal{S}^{d}$ and $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ does not contain the constant function.
    Consider $\mathcal{S}^{2} = \set{(u,v,z) \in \mathbb R^{3}}{u^{2}+v^{2}+z^{2}=1}$ with $K=1$. 
    Then ${\mathcal G}_1 = \operatorname{span}\{u,v,z\}$ and at the north pole
    $(0,0,1)^{\top}$ we have $u=v=0$, so the coefficient of $z$ determines the
    value of the MLS approximation at this point. However,
    $z = \sqrt{1-u^2-v^2} = 1 + {\mathcal O}(u^{2}+v^{2})$ agrees with the constant $1$
    only to first order, but also contains quadratic and higher-order terms. If
    we used $z$ in place of $g_1\equiv1$ in the above construction, leaving its
    coefficient unpenalized would leave these nonconstant terms unpenalized as
    well. In particular, constant data need not be reproduced at the center. The
    space $\Pi_K^{\mathrm{tan}}({\mathcal X};x)$ contains $1$ exactly and thus avoids the
    issue.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:hamls_error_bound_good_data}
  Let ${\mathcal X} \in \set{\mathbb T^{d},\mathcal{S}^{d},\mathrm{SO}(3)}{d \in \mathbb N}$, let $K \in \mathbb N$ be fixed,
  and use the local ansatz space $\Pi_K^{\mathrm{tan}}({\mathcal X};x)$ with weights $w$ as in
  \Cref{thm:mls_error_bound_good_data}, with $\delta \equiv R\,h_X$ for fixed
  $R \geq \rho$ and quasi-uniformity constant $c \geq 1$. For a fixed harmonic
  bandwidth $L \in \mathbb N$, let $(q_j,\omega_j)_{j=1}^{M_Q}$ be a quadrature
  rule with positive weights that is exact on $\Pi_{2L}({\mathcal X})$.
  Then, for every $f \in {\mathcal C}^{K+1}({\mathcal X})$ and every node set $X \subset {\mathcal X}$ with
  \begin{equation*}
    h_X \leq h_0
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \frac{h_X}{q_X} \leq c ,
  \end{equation*}
  the approximation $f_{\mathrm{HAMLS}}$ computed from the exact samples
  $\set{(x_i,f(x_i))}{}_{x_i\in X}$ using unregularized MLS satisfies
  \begin{equation*}
    \norm{f-f_{\mathrm{HAMLS}}}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathcal X})}
    \leq \norm{f - f_{Q}}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathcal X})} + C({\mathcal X}, K, \phi, c, R, f) \cdot h_{X}^{K+1} .
  \end{equation*}
  The constant $C({\mathcal X},K,\phi,c,R,f)$ is independent of $L$ and the quadrature rule.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
First of all, note that \Cref{thm:mls_error_bound_good_data} remains
    true for the locally adapted ansatz space $\Pi_{K}^{\mathrm{tan}}({\mathcal X};x)$,
    with different values for the constants involved \cite{Hielscher2024}.
  Inserting $f_Q$ and applying the triangle inequality yields 
  \begin{equation*}
    \norm{f - f_{\mathrm{HAMLS}}}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathcal X})}
    \leq \norm{f - f_Q}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathcal X})} + \norm{f_Q - f_{\mathrm{HAMLS}}}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathcal X})} .
  \end{equation*}
  It therefore remains to bound the second term. Let $\bm{y}\coloneqq f\vert_X$ 
  be exact samples and let ${\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}$ be the corresponding MLS approximation. 
  We denote by 
  \begin{equation*}
    f\big\vert_Q \coloneqq (f(q_j))_{j=1}^{M_Q} , \qquad
    {\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}\big\vert_Q \coloneqq ({\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}(q_j))_{j=1}^{M_Q}
  \end{equation*}
  the vectors of values of $f$ and ${\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}$ on $Q$, respectively. The 
  harmonic coefficient vectors of $f_Q$ and $f_{\mathrm{HAMLS}}$ are then 
  $\bm{F}^H\bm{W} f\big\vert_Q$ and $\bm{F}^H\bm{W}{\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}\big\vert_Q$. Since
  both $f_Q$ and $f_{\mathrm{HAMLS}}$ belong to $\Pi_L({\mathcal X})$ and the harmonic
  basis is orthonormal in $\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathcal X})$, Parseval's identity yields  
  \begin{equation*}
      \norm{f_Q - f_{\mathrm{HAMLS}}}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathcal X})}
      = \norm{\bm{F}^H\bm{W}\bigl(f\big\vert_Q - {\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}\big\vert_Q\bigr)}_2 
      \leq \norm{\bm{F}^H\bm{W}^{1/2}}_{2\to2} \cdot 
      \norm{\bm{W}^{1/2}\bigl(f\big\vert_Q - {\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}\big\vert_Q\bigr)}_2 . 
  \end{equation*}
  Here we also used that the quadrature rule is exact on $\Pi_{2L}({\mathcal X})$, and 
  thus $\bm{F}^H\bm{W}\bm{F}=\bm{I}$. Together with
  $\sum_{j=1}^{M_Q}\omega_j=\mathrm{vol}({\mathcal X})$, we finally obtain  
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
      \norm{\bm{W}^{1/2}\bigl(f\big\vert_Q - {\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}\big\vert_Q\bigr)}_2
      &= \left(\sum_{j=1}^{M_Q}\omega_j
      \abs{f(q_j)-{\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}(q_j)}^2\right)^{\nicefrac 12} \\
      &\leq \sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({\mathcal X})}\,\norm{f-{\mathcal M}_{\bm{y}}}_{{\mathcal X},\infty} \\
      &\leq \sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({\mathcal X})}\,C(f)\,C({\mathcal X},K,\phi,c,R)\,h_X^{K+1} ,
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation*}
  where the last inequality follows from \Cref{thm:mls_error_bound_good_data}.
  This proves the claim.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
In \Cref{fig:S2TestFunction_TimeError}, the achieved relative errors for HAMLS
  with MLS degree $K=2$ and $K=3$ are very similar.
  This is not a coincidence.
  Superconvergence one order higher than expected has already been observed and
  explained in \cite{Li2020} for MLS using even polynomial degrees and is
  currently being revisited for regular grids \cite{Durst2026}. It is usually expected
  for uniformly distributed nodes and unregularized MLS. However, the
  $\mathrm{L}^{2}$-error measured here is a form of averaging the errors of MLS over many
  points, and, depending on the degree of MLS, $44\,\%$ to $89\,\%$ of the sphere
  does not receive regularization. This allows us to see this behavior even for
  ill-distributed nodes.
