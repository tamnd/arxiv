<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:dist}
Let  $\bm x = \big\{x_i \in \mathbb{R}^D\mid i\in \mathbb{N}_n\big\}$ be a set of $n$ points in   the coordinate space, and let $\bm X_n = \big\{X_{ij} = (x_i - x_j)^2 \mid i,j \in \mathbb{N}_n,\,  i \neq j\big\}$  be the corresponding  set of squared distances.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:powers}
Let $\bm a = \{a_1, ..., a_n\} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be a vector in the parameter space that lies on the hyperplane:
\begin{equation}
\label{conf}
\sum_{i=1}^n a_i = D\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:triples}
Let $\mathrm{R}_n$ denote the set of ordered index triples:
\begin{equation}
\label{triplet}
\mathrm{R}_n = \big\{\langle ijk \rangle \mid i,j,k \in \mathbb{N}_n,\, i < j < k \big\}\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Any three vertices $\{i,j,k\}$ of the polygon $P_n$ define an inscribed triangle, denoted by $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ and  called a basis triangle.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
The straight lines supporting the edges of $\triangle_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ determine  three open regions in the plane that are adjacent to its sides and lie outside the basis triangle. These regions are called open chambers. The chamber adjacent to the edge $(j,k)$, or equivalently opposite to the vertex $i$, is denoted by ${\mathbb{C}}_n^{(i)}$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
By a slight abuse of notation, we use the same symbol ${\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}$, $a\in \{i,j,k\}$, to denote both the  open region in the plane and the subset of vertices of the conformal polygon lying therein. Accordingly, the notation $p\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}$ simply means that the corresponding vertex  lies in this  chamber.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:green&orange}
A cross-ratio diagram is a self-intersecting polygon inscribed in $P_n$ whose sides are chords connecting a subset of marked vertices. The chords forming the diagram are colored green and orange according to the following geometric configurations:

\vspace{-3mm}


\begin{itemize}

\item[\textbf{(a)}] \textbf{Quadrilateral  ($n \geqslant 4$):} two opposite edges of the quadrilateral are green, while its two diagonals are orange.

\vspace{-3mm}

\item[\textbf{(b)}] \textbf{Pentagon  ($n \geqslant 5$):} two adjacent edges of the pentagon and the edge opposite to their common vertex are  green. The orange chords are three diagonals: two issuing from this common vertex to the endpoints of the opposite green edge, and one connecting the remaining endpoints of the adjacent green edges.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def1}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a planar graph, where $V$ is the set of vertices and $E$ is the set of edges $(r,s)$ connecting unordered pairs of distinct  vertices $r,s\in V$. There are three types of graphs, corresponding to the three types of figures involved (conformal polygons, basis triangles, cross-ratio diagrams):
\begin{equation}
\label{graph_polygon}
\begin{array}{l}
\displaystyle
G_{\hexagon} = \left(V_{\hexagon}, E_{\hexagon}\right) = \big(\{1,2,...,n \}\,, \{(1,2),(2,3),...,(n-1,n),(n,1) \}\big)\,,
\vspace{4mm}
\\
\displaystyle
 G_{\triangle} =  \left(V_{\triangle}, E_{\triangle}\right) =  \big( \{i,j,k\}\,, \{ (i,j),(j,k),(k,i) \} \big)\,,
\vspace{4mm}
\\
\displaystyle
G_{\times}  = \left( V_{\times}\,, E_{\times}\right),
\quad
E_{\times} =E_{\textcolor{green}{\bm \times}} \cup  E_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}\,.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def2}
For every $l \in V_{\hexagon} \setminus V_{\triangle}$ and for every $(r,s) \in E_{\triangle}$, there are  two transianic indices:
\begin{equation}
\label{transianic}
\begin{array}{l}
b_l =
\begin{cases}
1, \quad \text{if} \quad l \in V_{\times}\,, \\
0, \quad \text{otherwise} \,,
\end{cases}
\qquad \; b_{rs} =
\begin{cases}
\phantom{-} 1, \quad \text{if} \quad (r,s) \in E_{\textcolor{green}{\bm\times}}\,, \\
-1, \quad \text{if} \quad (r,s) \in E_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}\,, \\
\phantom{-} 0, \quad \text{otherwise} \,,
\end{cases}
\end{array}
\end{equation}
which are called the first and second transianic indices, respectively.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- rem-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:adjacency}
The transianic indices admit an equivalent description in terms of the adjacency matrices of the graphs $G_{\hexagon}$, $G_{\triangle}$, and $G_{\times}$, all written on the common vertex set $V_{\hexagon}$. The first indices are recovered from the diagonal entries of the squared adjacency matrices, while the second indices are obtained by restricting the signed adjacency matrix of $G_{\times}$ to the edges of $G_{\triangle}$, with the green and orange edges assigned the values $+1$ and $-1$, respectively. The explicit formulas and their derivation are given in Appendix \textbf{\ref{app:adjacency}}.
<!-- end rem-2 -->
<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{def:all_chords}
Let $\bm C_n$ denote  the set of all chords connecting unordered pairs of distinct vertices in $V_{\hexagon}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{set_of_all_chords}
\bm C_n = \Big\{(r,s) \mid r,s \in V_{\hexagon}, \, r \neq s \Big\}\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-9 -->
<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
\label{def:chords}
For a basis triangle $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}\subset P_n$, the set of basis chords is defined as
\begin{equation}
\label{set_of_basic_chords}
\bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} = E_{\triangle} \cup \Big\{(a,l) \mid a\in V_{\triangle}\,, l \in {\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)} \Big\}\,.
\end{equation}
The complementary set of non-basis chords is given by
\begin{equation}
\label{set_of_non_basis_chords}
\overline{\bm C}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} = \bm C_n \setminus \bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-10 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
The cross-ratio set  \eqref{Y_split} is constructed  as follows.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}
For a  basis triangle $\triangle_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, every cross-ratio diagram constructed by the algorithm contains a unique non-basis chord, which is always green:
\begin{equation}
\label{cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}
\begin{array}{c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{\qquad}c}
\text{subset} & \text{cross-ratio} & \text{non-basis chord} \\[1mm]
{}^{_1}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} & U[i,j,r,k]\,, \; U[i,j,k,r] & (k,r)
\vspace{1mm}
\\
{}^{_2}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} & U[i,j,r,s] & (r,s)
\vspace{1mm}
\\
\bm {\rm W}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} & W[i,j,k,r,s] & (r,s)
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Together with the cyclic permutations of $i,j,k$, these mappings exhaust all non-basis chords associated with the basis triangle, thereby establishing  a bijection:
\begin{equation}
\label{1-1YC}
\bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\, \sim \, \overline{\bm C}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\,.
\end{equation}
Moreover, in each cross-ratio diagram, apart from the non-basis chord \eqref{cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}, only the edges of $\triangle_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ may be green. Equivalently, the remaining basis chords $(a,b) \in \bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} \setminus E_{\triangle}$ are always orange.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:arguments}
The set (\ref{Y_split}) is the set of cross-ratio arguments of the $n$-point polygonal function labelled by the index triple $\langle ijk \rangle\in\mathrm{R}_n$:
\begin{equation}
\label{DA_cr}
\bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} = \Big\{{\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\,:\, p = 1,..., \textsf{q}\,,\; \text{where}\;\; \textsf{q} = \frac{n(n-3)}{2} \Big\}\,,
\end{equation}
with each cross-ratio  ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ constructed  by the diagrammatic algorithm.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- rem-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:nota}
Each element ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle} \in \bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is assigned the set of vertices and the set of (colored) edges. The notation in \eqref{graph_polygon} is specified as follows:
\begin{equation}
G_{\times}^{(p)}  = \left( V_{\times}^{(p)}\,, E_{\times}^{(p)}\right),
\quad
E_{\times}^{(p)} =E_{\textcolor{green}{\bm \times}}^{(p)} \cup  E_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}^{(p)}\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end rem-3 -->
<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
\label{def_of_trans_mat}
The transianic matrix of the cross-ratio set $\bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is the matrix
$\mathcal{B}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}
= \big(\mathcal{B}_l^{(p)}\big)_{1\leq l \leq n,\, 1\leq p \leq \textsf{q}}
\in \operatorname{Mat}_{n\times\textsf{q}}(\mathbb{Z})$ whose entries are defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{tr_mat}
\mathcal{B}_l^{(p)}=
\begin{cases}
\phantom{-} b_l^{(p)}\,, & l \notin \{i,j,k\}\,,\\
-b_{ij}^{(p)}\,, & l=i\,,\\
-b_{jk}^{(p)}\,, & l=j\,,\\
-b_{ki}^{(p)}\,, & l=k\,.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here, the column label $(p)$ corresponds to the cross-ratio ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in\bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$.
<!-- end definition-11 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{cor1}
\label{cor3}
For every ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in\bm{\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, the transianic indices satisfy
\begin{subequations}
\label{property1}
\begin{align}
{\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle} \in {}^{m}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm{\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}:
\qquad
\sum_{l\notin\{i,j,k\}}b_l^{(p)}
&=m \,,
\qquad
b_{ij}^{(p)}+b_{jk}^{(p)}+b_{ki}^{(p)}=m-1\,,
\quad m=1,2\,;
\label{property1_U}
\\[2mm]
{\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle} \in \bm{\rm W}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}:
\qquad
\sum_{l\notin\{i,j,k\}}b_l^{(p)}
&=2 \,,
\qquad
b_{ij}^{(p)}+b_{jk}^{(p)}+b_{ki}^{(p)}=1\,.
\label{property1_W}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:FLI}
For every ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in \bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, the components of the corresponding transianic vector satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{FLR}
\sum_{l = 1}^{n} \mathcal{B}_l^{(p)}  = 1 \,,
\qquad
p = 1,..., \textsf{q}\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:local_inversion_balance}
Let $(r_p,s_p)$ be the unique non-basis chord associated with ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. Then,
\begin{equation}
\label{local_inversion_balance}
-b_{ij}^{(p)}-b_{ki}^{(p)}
+\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(i)}}b_l^{(p)}
=
\begin{cases}
1\,, & i\in\{r_p,s_p\}\,,\\
0\,, & i\notin\{r_p,s_p\}\,.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Analogous relations are obtained by cyclic permutations of the vertices $i,j,k$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:diagrammatic_cross_ratio}
Each cross-ratio ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in \bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is a weighted product of the squared distances $X_{ab}\in \bm X_n$ associated with the basis chords and the squared distance associated with the unique non-basis chord:
\begin{equation}
\label{diagrammatic_cross_ratio}
{\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}
=
X_{r_p s_p}\,
X_{ij}^{b_{ij}^{(p)}}
X_{jk}^{b_{jk}^{(p)}}
X_{ki}^{b_{ki}^{(p)}}
\prod_{a\in V_{\triangle}\vphantom{{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}}}\,
\prod_{l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}}
X_{al}^{-b_l^{(p)}}\,,
\end{equation}
where $X_{r_p s_p}$ corresponds to the non-basis chord $(r_p,s_p) \in \overline{\bm C}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Given a cross-ratio diagram, its green and orange chords determine, respectively, the numerator and denominator of the corresponding function of the squared distances:
\begin{equation}
\label{cross_general_prod}
{\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}
=
\frac{
\displaystyle\prod_{(a,b)\in E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{green}{\bm\times}}}
X_{ab}}
{
\displaystyle\prod_{(c,d)\in E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}}
X_{cd}}\,.
\end{equation}
The chords in each product can be split into basis and non-basis ones, according to the chord decomposition \eqref{chord_set_decomposition}. Proposition \textbf{\ref{rem:cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}} states that each cross-ratio diagram contains a unique non-basis chord $(r_p,s_p)$, which is always green.  In turn, the basis-chord set  \eqref{set_of_basic_chords} is decomposed into the edges of the basis triangle $E_{\triangle}$ and the remaining basis chords. For every $(a,b) \in E_{\triangle}$, the second transianic index \eqref{transianic} equals  $1$, $-1$ or $0$, depending on whether the chord is green, orange, or absent, respectively. Consequently, the cross-ratio \eqref{cross_general_prod} takes the form
\begin{equation}
{\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}
=
X_{r_p s_p}\,
X_{ij}^{b_{ij}^{(p)}}
X_{jk}^{b_{jk}^{(p)}}
X_{ki}^{b_{ki}^{(p)}}
\,
\frac{
\displaystyle\prod_{(a,b)\in\,
E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{green}{\bm\times}}
\cap\,(\bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\setminus E_{\triangle})}
X_{ab}}
{
\displaystyle\prod_{(c,d)\in\,
E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}
\cap\,(\bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\setminus E_{\triangle})}
X_{cd}}\,.
\end{equation}
Proposition \textbf{\ref{rem:cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}} further states that any basis chord outside $E_{\triangle}$ that occurs in the diagram is orange, so $E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{green}{\bm\times}}
\cap\left(\bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\setminus E_{\triangle}\right) = \varnothing $. Finally, for $a\in V_{\triangle}$ and $l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}$, the first transianic index $b_l^{(p)}$ determines whether the chord $(a,l)$ occurs in the diagram. Thus,
\begin{equation}
\prod_{(c,d)\in
E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}
\cap\left(\bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\setminus E_{\triangle}\right)}
X_{cd}^{-1}
=
\prod_{a\in V_{\triangle}\vphantom{{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}}}
\prod_{l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}}
X_{al}^{-b_l^{(p)}}\,,
\end{equation}
which completes the proof of \eqref{diagrammatic_cross_ratio}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- rem-4: body_md -->
Proposition \textbf{\ref{prop:FLI}} and Lemma \textbf{\ref{lemma:local_inversion_balance}}, which encode the properties of the transianic indices, provide an independent verification of the  $O(D+1,1)$ invariance of the representation  \eqref{diagrammatic_cross_ratio}.
<!-- end rem-4 -->
<!-- rem-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:diagrammatic_interpretation}
Recalling the chord decomposition (\ref{chord_set_decomposition}), we observe that for each
non-basis chord $(r_p,s_p) \in \overline{\bm C}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, where $p=1,..., \textsf{q}$, the diagrammatic algorithm selects a unique subset of basis
chords $\bm S_p^{\langle ijk \rangle} \subset \bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. The transianic matrix then defines a sign map on $\bm S_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ that determines which squared distances  enter the numerator and which enter the denominator, ensuring that the resulting ratio is conformally invariant.
In this way, the algorithm explicitly realizes the bijection between $\bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ and $\overline{\bm C}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ established in Proposition \textbf{\ref{rem:cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}}.

<!-- definition-12: body_md -->
\label{def:cyclic_parameter_sums}
For any pair of vertices $r,s\in V_{\hexagon}$, we use the notation
\begin{equation}
\label{aa}
|{\bm a}_{r,s}|=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{l=r}^s a_l\,, & r<s\,,
\\
\displaystyle\sum_{l=r}^n a_l+\sum_{l=1}^s a_l\,, & r>s\,;
\end{cases}
\qquad
|{\bm a}_{r,s}|'=\frac{D}{2}-|{\bm a}_{r,s}|\,.
\end{equation}

<!-- definition-13: body_md -->
\label{def:polygonal_function}
Fixing $\langle ijk \rangle\in\mathrm{R}_n$,  let $\bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$  and $\mathcal{B}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$  be the cross-ratio set and the transianic matrix, respectively. Given the parameter vector  ${\bm a}$ from  Definition \textbf{\ref{def:powers}}, the $n$-point polygonal function is defined as the following hypergeometric series \cite{Alkalaev:2025zhg}:
\begin{equation}
\label{general_polygonal}
{\rm H}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}({\bm a}|\mathbf{Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle})
=\sum_{\bm m \in \mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\textsf{q}}}{\rm A}^{\langle ijk \rangle}({\bm a}|{\bm m})
\prod_{p=1}^{\textsf{q}}\frac{\big({\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\big)^{m_p}}{m_p!}\,,
\end{equation}
where the series coefficients are given by
\begin{equation}
\label{bare_n_2}
{\rm A}^{\langle ijk \rangle}({\bm a}|{\bm m})
=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{l\notin\{i,j,k\}}(-)^{M_l}(a_l)_{M_l}}
{(1+|{\bm a}_{i,j}|')_{M_{ij}}(1+|{\bm a}_{j,k}|')_{M_{jk}}(1+|{\bm a}_{k,i}|')_{M_{ki}}}\,.
\end{equation}
Here, $(a)_M = \Gamma(a+M)/\Gamma(a)$ is the Pochhammer symbol, and $\Gamma(z)$ is the Gamma function. The summation index ${\bm m}=(m_1,...,m_\textsf{q})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\textsf{q}}$ and the transianic indices determine the following linear combinations of the summation variables:
\begin{equation}
\label{MM}
M_l=\sum_{p=1}^{\textsf{q}}b_l^{(p)}m_p\,,
\qquad
M_{st}=\sum_{p=1}^{\textsf{q}}b_{st}^{(p)}m_p\,.
\end{equation}
The superscript $(p)$ enumerates the transianic indices associated with ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in\bm{\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$.
<!-- end definition-13 -->

<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
\label{def:bas}
Given the polygonal function ${\rm H}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}({\bm a}|\mathbf{Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle})$, the basis function is defined as
\begin{equation}
\label{basisfunc}
\Phi_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}(\bm a|\bm x) = {\rm S}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}(\bm a) \, {\rm V}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}(\bm a|\bm x)\, {\rm H}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}({\bm a}|\mathbf{Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle})\,,
\end{equation}
where the $\bm x$-independent \textit{triangle-factor} and the $\bm x$-dependent \textit{leg-factor}  are given by
\begin{equation}
\label{star_n}
{\rm S}_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}({\bm a}) =
\frac{\Gamma(-|\bm a_{i,j}|') \Gamma(-|\bm a_{j,k}|')
\Gamma(-|\bm a_{k,i}|')}
{\Gamma(a_i) \Gamma(a_j) \Gamma(a_k)}\,,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{V_n}
\hspace{13mm}{\rm V}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}({\bm a}|\bm x) =
X_{ij}^{|\bm a_{i,j}|'}
X_{jk}^{|\bm a_{j,k}|'}
X_{ki}^{|\bm a_{k,i}|'}
\prod_{b\in V_{\triangle}\vphantom{{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(b)}}}\,
\prod_{l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(b)}}
X_{bl}^{-a_l}\,.
\end{equation}

<!-- definition-16: body_md -->
\label{def:par_tor}
Let $\bm \alpha = (\alpha_1,...,\alpha_n) \in \mathbb{C}^n$ be a parameter vector, and let
$\mathcal{A}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times N}(\mathbb{Z})$ be a toric matrix, which  can be viewed as the vector configuration
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}=
\left\{
{\bm \mathcal{A}}^{(1)},...,{\bm \mathcal{A}}^{(N)}
\;\middle|\;
{\bm \mathcal{A}}^{(p)}
=\big(\mathcal{A}_1^{(p)},...,\mathcal{A}_n^{(p)}\big)^{\rm T}
\in\mathbb{Z}^n\,,
\quad p=1,...,N
\right\}\,.
\end{equation}
The toric matrix is required to satisfy the conditions of maximal rank and homogeneity:
\begin{enumerate}

\item The column vectors in $\mathcal{A}$ span $\mathbb{Z}^n$, so that $\mathcal{A}$ has rank $n$.

\item There exists a linear form $h:\mathbb{Z}^n\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ such that $h({\bm \mathcal{A}}^{(p)})=1$ for all $p=1,...,N$.

\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-17: body_md -->
The lattice of (integer linear) relations among the columns of $\mathcal{A}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{lat}
\mathbb{L}
= {\rm ker}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}
=\bigg\{
\bm\ell=(\ell_1,...,\ell_N)\in\mathbb{Z}^N
\;\big|\;
\sum_{p=1}^{N}\ell_p{\bm \mathcal{A}}^{(p)}=0
\bigg\}\,.
\end{equation}

<!-- definition-18: body_md -->
\label{prop:GKZ}
The GKZ hypergeometric system defined by $\mathcal{A}$ and $\bm\alpha$
consists of the following differential equations for a function
$\Phi$ of $N$ variables $\bm v=(v_1,...,v_N)\in\mathbb{C}^N$:

\begin{itemize}

\item the toric equations, one for each lattice vector
$\bm\ell=(\ell_1,...,\ell_N)\in\mathbb{L}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{toric_eq}
\Bigg[
\prod_{\ell_p>0}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v_p}\right)^{\ell_p}
-\prod_{\ell_p<0}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v_p}\right)^{-\ell_p}
\Bigg]\Phi(\bm v)=0\,;
\end{equation}

\item the Euler equations
\begin{equation}
\label{Euler_eq}
\Bigg[
\sum_{p=1}^{N}\mathcal{A}_l^{(p)}\theta_p-\alpha_l
\Bigg]\Phi(\bm v)=0\,,
\qquad
\theta_p=v_p\frac{\partial}{\partial v_p}\,,
\qquad
l=1,...,n\,.
\end{equation}

\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-18 -->
<!-- prop-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:solution}
The following $\Gamma$-series
\begin{equation}
\label{Gamma_series}
\Phi_{\mathbb{L},\bm \gamma}(\bm v)
=\sum_{\bm\ell\in\mathbb{L}}\,
\prod_{p=1}^{N}\,
\frac{v_p^{\ell_p+\gamma_p}}
{\Gamma(\ell_p+\gamma_p+1)}
\end{equation}
is a formal solution to the GKZ system (\ref{toric_eq})--(\ref{Euler_eq}), provided that the parameter vector $\bm\gamma=(\gamma_1,...,\gamma_N)\in\mathbb{C}^N$ satisfies the linear equations
\begin{equation}
\label{alpha_A_gamma}
\bm\alpha=\mathcal{A}\bm\gamma\,.
\end{equation}

<!-- rem-9: body_md -->
\label{rem:freedom}
The matrices representing the GKZ data are not unique. The transformation $(\mathcal{A},\bm\alpha)\longmapsto(U\mathcal{A},U\bm\alpha)$, where $U\in{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$,
leaves the lattice $\mathbb{L}$ intact and reorganizes the Euler equations as particular linear combinations of the original ones. A permutation of the columns of $\mathcal{A}$ amounts to a relabeling of the GKZ variables $\bm v$. Independently, the transformation $\mathcal{G}\longmapsto\mathcal{G} T$, where
$T\in{\rm GL}(\textsf{q},\mathbb{Z})$,  corresponds to changing the basis in $\mathbb{L}$.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
A direct consequence of Proposition \textbf{\ref{prop:diagrammatic_cross_ratio}} and the variable identification of Proposition \textbf{\ref{prop:mapping_new}} is

\qquad

According to \eqref{Gale_vector_configuration_polygonal}, the first $n$ components of the $p$-th column ${\bm\mathcal{G}}^{(p)}$ of the Gale dual are given by the negative transianic vector $-{\bm\mathcal{B}}^{(p)}$, while its remaining $\textsf{q}$ components form ${\bm e}^{(p)}$. (a,b)\in E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{green}{\bm\times}}}}^{\textsf{q}}}m_p
\quad-\quad

\label{prop:diagrammatic_GKZ}
\label{proposition_for_Euler_eq}
The $\Gamma$-series associated with the $n$-point polygonal function (\ref{Gamma_series_with_F_V_H}) satisfies the following $n$ Euler equations, one for each basis chord in $\bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$.

\begin{itemize}
\item For any $(a,b)\in E_{\triangle} \subset \bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$:
\end{itemize}
\begin{equation}
\label{Euler_triangle_diagrammatic}
\Bigg[
X_{ab}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{ab}}
+
\smashoperator{\sum_{\substack{p=1\\(a,b)\in E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}}}^{\textsf{q}}}
X_{r_p s_p}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{r_p s_p}}
-
\smashoperator{\sum_{\substack{p=1\\(a,b)\in E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{green}{\bm\times}}}}^{\textsf{q}}}
X_{r_p s_p}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{r_p s_p}}
-|{\bm a}_{a,b}|'
\Bigg]
\Phi_{\mathcal{B}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle},\,\bm \alpha^{\langle ijk \rangle}}(\bm X_n)=0\,.
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
\item For any $(a,b)\in\bm C_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}\setminus E_{\triangle}$ with $b\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}$:
\end{itemize}
\begin{equation}
\label{Euler_basis_diagrammatic}
\Bigg[
X_{ab}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{ab}}
+
\smashoperator{\sum_{\substack{p=1\\(a,b)\in E^{(p)}_{\textcolor{orange}{\bm\times}}}}^{\textsf{q}}}
X_{r_p s_p}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{r_p s_p}}
+a_b
\Bigg]
\Phi_{\mathcal{B}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle},\,\bm \alpha^{\langle ijk \rangle}}(\bm X_n)=0\,.
\end{equation}
In equations (\ref{Euler_triangle_diagrammatic}) and (\ref{Euler_basis_diagrammatic}), $(r_p,s_p)\in \overline{\bm C}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is the unique non-basis chord entering the cross-ratio diagram of  ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in \bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, as specified in Proposition \textbf{\ref{rem:cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}}.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Substituting the toric  matrix \eqref{toric_pol} into the Euler equations \eqref{Euler_eq} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{Euler_original_diagrammatic}
\Bigg[
\theta_l
+\sum_{p=1}^{\textsf{q}}\mathcal{B}_l^{(p)}\theta_{n+p}
-\big(\bm \alpha^{\langle ijk \rangle}\big)_l
\Bigg]
\Phi_{\mathcal{B}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle},\bm \alpha^{\langle ijk \rangle}}(\bm v)=0\,,
\qquad l=1,...,n\,.
\end{equation}
Here, $\theta_s=v_s\partial/\partial v_s$. According to  Proposition \textbf{\ref{prop:mapping_new}}, $\theta_{n+p}=X_{r_p s_p}\partial/\partial X_{r_p s_p}$, while the first $n$ variables correspond to the basis chords \eqref{set_of_basic_chords}.

For the row of \eqref{Gale_matrix_chord_entries} corresponding to the chord $(i,j)$, we have $v_i=X_{ij}$, hence $\theta_i=v_i \partial/\partial v_i = X_{ij}\partial/\partial X_{ij}$, and $(\bm\alpha^{\langle ijk \rangle})_i=|{\bm a}_{i,j}|'$ by \eqref{alpha_polyg}. Since $\mathcal{B}_i^{(p)}=-\mathcal{G}_i^{(p)}$, the relation \eqref{Gale_matrix_chord_entries} makes the sum in \eqref{Euler_original_diagrammatic} equal to the orange-chord contribution minus the green-chord contribution. For the chord $(a,b)=(i,j)$, this yields  \eqref{Euler_triangle_diagrammatic}. The rows associated with the chords $(j,k)$ and $(k,i)$ follow by cyclic permutation. The ordered representatives $(i,j),(j,k),(k,i)$ also specify the cyclic parameter sums $|{\bm a}_{a,b}|'$ in these three equations.

For the remaining basis chords $(a,l)$ with $l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(a)}$, the variable and parameter identifications are $v_l=X_{al}$ and $(\bm\alpha^{\langle ijk \rangle})_l=-a_l$, respectively. By \eqref{diagrammatic_cross_ratio}, $(a,l)$ can occur only in the denominator of a cross-ratio. Thus, \eqref{Gale_matrix_chord_entries} and $\mathcal{B}_l^{(p)}=-\mathcal{G}_l^{(p)}$ select only the orange-chord terms in the sum in \eqref{Euler_original_diagrammatic}. The substitution of $\theta_l= v_l \partial/\partial v_l = X_{al}\partial/\partial X_{al}$, then yields \eqref{Euler_basis_diagrammatic}.

\label{remark_tells_the_order}
For the  finite subsystem in Corollary \textbf{\ref{cor:finite_toric_subsystem}}, the differential order coincides with the order of the corresponding cross-ratio. Specifically, the quadratic cross-ratios in ${}^{_1}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm{\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle} \cup{}^{_2}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm{\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ correspond to   second-order equations, while the cubic cross-ratios in $\bm{\rm W}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ correspond to   third-order equations. The full family (\ref{general_toric_equation_compact}) also contains equations of higher order.

By construction, a quadratic cross-ratio ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in {}^{_1}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is represented by a quadrilateral whose four vertices consist of the three vertices of the basis triangle $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ and one additional vertex $r$  (see fig.~\textbf{\ref{Conf.Pol.11}} ({\bf a})). According to Definition \textbf{\ref{def2}}, the first transianic index $b_l^{(p)}$ equals $1$  if  vertex $l$ belongs to the cross-ratio graph, and $0$ otherwise. Since exactly one vertex of the cross-ratio lies outside $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, namely $r$, the first sum in \eqref{property1_U} equals $1$.

To evaluate the sum of the second transianic indices, we note that two vertices of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ are connected by a green edge; therefore, one of the indices $(b_{ij}^{(p)},b_{jk}^{(p)},b_{ki}^{(p)})$  equals $1$. The third vertex of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is connected by a green edge to the additional vertex $r$, while the denominator is formed by the diagonals of the quadrilateral; as a consequence, exactly one edge of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ appears as an orange edge and contributes $-1$, whereas the remaining edge of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ does not belong to the cross-ratio graph and contributes $0$. Hence, the second sum in \eqref{property1_U} is  $1-1+0=0$, which proves the claim for the subset ${}^{_1}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$.

By construction, a quadratic cross-ratio ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle} \in {}^{_2}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is represented by a quadrilateral whose four vertices consist of two vertices of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ and two additional vertices $r$ and $s$ (see fig.~ \textbf{\ref{Conf.Pol.11}} ({\bf b})). Since exactly  two vertices of the cross-ratio lie outside $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, namely $r$ and $s$, the first sum in \eqref{property1_U} equals 2.

To evaluate the sum of the second transianic indices, we observe that the vertices $r$ and $s$ are chosen from the open chambers; therefore, they do not coincide with the vertices of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. Consequently, the diagonals associated with the denominator of the cross-ratio (colored orange in fig.~\textbf{\ref{Conf.Pol.11}}) never coincide with any edge of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. In contrast, exactly one edge of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ coincides with a green edge of the cross-ratio graph. According to Definition \textbf{\ref{def2}}, this edge contributes $1$ to the second sum in \eqref{property1_U}, thereby completing the proof of the first line in Lemma \textbf{\ref{cor1}}.

By construction, a cubic cross-ratio ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in \bm {\rm W}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ is represented by a pentagon whose five vertices consist of the three vertices of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ and two additional vertices $r$ and $s$ (see fig.~\textbf{\ref{Conf.Pol.11}} ({\bf c})). Since exactly two vertices of the cross-ratio lie outside $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, namely, $r$ and $s$, the first sum in \eqref{property1_W} equals  $2$.

To evaluate the sum of the second transianic indices, we note that, by construction, two edges of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ are always green, while the remaining edge is orange. Therefore, among the three indices $(b_{ij}^{(p)},b_{jk}^{(p)},b_{ki}^{(p)})$, two are equal to $1$ and the third is equal to $-1$. Hence, the second sum in \eqref{property1_W} is $1+1-1=1$, which proves the claim for the subset $\bm {\rm W}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$.

Consider a cross-ratio ${\rm Y}_p^{\langle ijk \rangle}\in \bm {\rm Y}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. Let $\textsf{Or}_i^{\triangle}$ and $\textsf{Gr}_i^{\triangle}$ denote the numbers of orange and green chords of its diagram that are incident to the vertex $i$ and belong to the basis triangle $\triangle_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. By Definition \textbf{\ref{def2}}, the second transianic index equals $1$ for a green edge, $-1$ for an orange edge, and $0$ for an absent edge. Since the two edges of the basis triangle incident to $i$ are $(i,j)$ and $(k,i)$, it follows that
\begin{equation}
\label{lemma_helping_prop_1}
-b_{ij}^{(p)}-b_{ki}^{(p)}
=\textsf{Or}_i^{\triangle}-\textsf{Gr}_i^{\triangle}\,.
\end{equation}

For $l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(i)}$, the diagrammatic algorithm ensures that the basis chord $(i,l)$ is orange precisely when $l$ belongs to the cross-ratio diagram, and is absent otherwise. By Definition \textbf{\ref{def2}}, these two cases correspond to $b_l^{(p)}=1$ and $b_l^{(p)}=0$, respectively. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{lemma_helping_prop_2}
\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(i)}}b_l^{(p)}=\textsf{Or}_i^{\vee}\,,
\end{equation}
where $\textsf{Or}_i^{\vee}$ denotes the number of orange basis chords $(i,l)$ with $l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(i)}$ appearing in the cross-ratio diagram.

By Proposition \textbf{\ref{rem:cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}}, the unique non-basis chord is green. Hence, every orange chord is a basis chord. In particular, the orange chords incident to $i$ consist of the $\textsf{Or}_i^{\triangle}$ basis-triangle edges and the $\textsf{Or}_i^{\vee}$ remaining basis chords. Combining \eqref{lemma_helping_prop_1} and
\eqref{lemma_helping_prop_2}, we thus obtain
\begin{equation}
-b_{ij}^{(p)}-b_{ki}^{(p)}
+\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(i)}}b_l^{(p)}
=\textsf{Or}_i^{\vee}+\textsf{Or}_i^{\triangle}-\textsf{Gr}_i^{\triangle}
=\textsf{Or}_i-\textsf{Gr}_i^{\triangle}\,,
\end{equation}
where $\textsf{Or}_i$ is the total number of orange chords incident to the vertex $i$.

For both the quadratic and cubic cross-ratios \eqref{U_inv} and \eqref{W_inv}, each vertex label occurs equally many times among the endpoints of the squared distances in the numerator and denominator. Indeed, for the quadratic cross-ratio \eqref{U_inv} each label occurs once in each, while for the cubic cross-ratio \eqref{W_inv} the label $j_2$ occurs twice in each and every other label occurs once in each. Consequently, the total number $\textsf{Gr}_i$ of green chords incident to the vertex $i$ equals $\textsf{Or}_i$. This also holds when $i$ is absent from the diagram, in which case both numbers vanish. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{lemma_helping_prop_3}
-b_{ij}^{(p)}-b_{ki}^{(p)}
+\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{C}}_n^{(i)}}b_l^{(p)}
=\textsf{Gr}_i-\textsf{Gr}_i^{\triangle}\,.
\end{equation}

It remains to specify the right-hand side. According to  the diagrammatic algorithm, every green chord is either an edge of the basis triangle or the unique non-basis chord $(r_p,s_p)$ identified in Proposition \textbf{\ref{rem:cross_ratio_non_basis_chord}}. Consequently, $\textsf{Gr}_i-\textsf{Gr}_i^{\triangle}$ counts precisely whether this non-basis chord is incident to the vertex  $i$:
\begin{equation}
\textsf{Gr}_i-\textsf{Gr}_i^{\triangle}
=\begin{cases}
1\,, & i\in\{r_p,s_p\}\,,\\
0\,, & i\notin\{r_p,s_p\}\,.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Substituting this expression into
\eqref{lemma_helping_prop_3} proves
\eqref{local_inversion_balance}. The relations at $j$
and $k$ follow by cyclic permutations of $i,j,k$.

For any  $l \notin \{i,j,k\}$, the first transianic indices satisfy

\sum_{p=1}^{\textsf{q}} b^{(p)}_l = n-2\,.

Let $l\in \mathbb{C}_n^{(i)}$, while the two remaining chambers are $\mathbb{C}_n^{(j)}$ and $\mathbb{C}_n^{(k)}$. We now count, separately for each subset of cross-ratios, the number of elements for which the index $b_l^{(p)}$ is non-zero.

${}^{_1}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$: Each cross-ratio contains exactly one vertex that does not belong to $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. Therefore, for a given vertex $l$, there are precisely two admissible configurations, shown in fig.~\textbf{\ref{fan_lemma_pic_1}}.

${}^{_2}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$: Each cross-ratio contains two vertices that do not belong to $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$. By construction, these two vertices lie in two different open chambers. Since $l\in\mathbb{C}_n^{(i)}$, the second vertex can be chosen arbitrarily either from $\mathbb{C}_n^{(j)}$ or from $\mathbb{C}_n^{(k)}$. Therefore, for a given  $l$, the number of such cross-ratios is equal to $L_n^{(j)} + L_n^{(k)}$.

$\bm {\rm W}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$: Each cross-ratio contains exactly two vertices outside $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$, and these two vertices lie in the same open chamber. Since $l\in \mathbb{C}_n^{(i)}$, the second vertex can be chosen arbitrarily among the remaining vertices of the same chamber $\mathbb{C}_n^{(i)}$. Therefore, for a given $l$, the number of such cross-ratios is equal to  $L_n^{(i)} - 1$.  Thus, the sum is given by
\begin{equation}
\sum_{p=1}^{\textsf{q}} b_l^{(p)}= 2 + L_n^{(j)} + L_n^{(k)} + L_n^{(i)} - 1 = n-2\,,
\end{equation}
where in the last step we used the balance relation \eqref{balance_relation}.

Let us count, separately for each subset of cross-ratios in \eqref{Y_split}, the number of elements for which the index $b_{ij}^{(p)}$ is non-zero.

${}^{_1}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$: First, assume that the edge $(i,j)$ is green. Then, an additional vertex $l$ can be chosen from either of the two chambers adjacent to this edge, namely, $\mathbb{C}_n^{(i)}$ or $\mathbb{C}_n^{(j)}$. Hence, there are $L_n^{(i)}+L_n^{(j)}$ possible choices for $l$, as illustrated in fig.~\textbf{\ref{fan_lemma_pic_2}} {\bf (a)},~{\bf (b)}. These configurations have $b_{ij}^{(p)}=1$. Now, assume that the edge $(i,j)$ is orange. Then, the vertex outside $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ must lie opposite to the edge $(i,j)$, i.e., in the chamber $\mathbb{C}_n^{(k)}$. For each such vertex, there are exactly two admissible cross-ratio diagrams, shown in fig.~\textbf{\ref{fan_lemma_pic_2}} {\bf (c)}, {\bf (d)}. Hence, the total number of such diagrams equals  $2L_n^{(k)}$. These diagrams have $b_{ij}^{(p)}=-1$.

${}^{_2}\hspace{-0.5mm}\bm {\rm U}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$: The edge $(i,j)$ cannot be orange. If the edge $(i,j)$ is green, then the two external vertices must be chosen from the adjacent chambers $\mathbb{C}_n^{(i)}$ and $\mathbb{C}_n^{(j)}$. Therefore, the total number of such diagrams equals  $L_n^{(i)} L_n^{(j)}$, and each contributes $+1$ to the sum, see fig.~\textbf{\ref{fan_lemma_pic_2}} {\bf (e)}.

$\bm {\rm W}_n^{\langle ijk \rangle}$: For the diagrams in this subset, two edges of $\triangle_{n}^{\langle ijk \rangle}$ are always green, while the remaining one is orange. The edge $(i,j)$ is  green if the two external vertices of the corresponding cross-ratio are chosen either from the chamber $\mathbb{C}_n^{(i)}$ or from the chamber $\mathbb{C}_n^{(j)}$. This yields the positive contributions $\binom{L_n^{(i)}}{2}$  and $\binom{L_n^{(j)}}{2}$. On the other hand, the edge $(i,j)$ is orange if the two external vertices are chosen from the opposite chamber $\mathbb{C}_n^{(k)}$. This yields  the negative contribution $\binom{L_n^{(k)}}{2}$.
All in all, the required sum is given by
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c}
\displaystyle \sum_{p=1}^{\textsf{q}} b_{ij}^{(p)} = L_n^{(i)}+L_n^{(j)} - 2L_n^{(k)} {+L_n^{(i)}L_n^{(j)}} + \binom{L_n^{(i)}}{2} + \binom{L_n^{(j)}}{2} - \binom{L_n^{(k)}}{2}
\\
\displaystyle= \frac{(n-3)(n-2)}{2} - (n-1)L_n^{(k)}\,.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
