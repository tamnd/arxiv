{\bf\itshape (Equivalence Lemma)} \ 
\label{l:equiv}
For every graph $G$ we have $\mathrm{ex}(n,G) \leq  \mathrm{st}(n,G) = \mathrm{ex}(n, D(G) ) \leq   \min \{ \mathrm{ex}(n,H) : H \in  D(G) \}$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Lower bound: 
$\mathrm{ex}(n,D(G)$  means that there exists a graph $H$  with $n$ vertices and $\mathrm{ex}(n,D(G))$ edges with no copy of any member of $D(G)$, hence no non-induced subgraph on $k$ vertices contains $G$ in $H$. 

Upper bound: 
$\mathrm{ex}(n,D(G))+1$ means that every graph on $n$ vertices and $\mathrm{ex}(n,D(G))+1$ edges contains a (not necessarily induced) copy of a member of $D(G)$, hence contains a non-induced copy of $G$. 
The rightmost inequality is trivial.

\begin{itemize}

 \item[$(ii)$] If  $\chi(D(G))   = \chi(G) +1$,  then  $G$ is a complete $\chi(G)$-partite graph and  $\mathrm{st}(n,G) = \mathrm{ex}(n,K_{\chi(G) +1})$ for every $n$  sufficiently large.

$(i)$\quad 
By Theorem A  and Lemma \ref{l:equiv} we obtain $(1+o(1))\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{\chi (G)}) = \mathrm{ex}(n,G ) \leq  \mathrm{st}(n,G)  =  \mathrm{ex}(n,D(G))  =  (1+o(1))\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{\chi (G)})$  as $\chi (G) = \chi (D(G))$.

$(ii)$\quad 
If $\chi(G) = 1$, then $G$  is the empty graph and the result is immediate. 
Otherwise, $\chi(G)\geq 2$.
If $G$ is not a complete $\chi(G)$-partite graph with $\chi(G) \geq  2$, then embed it in a complete $\chi(G)$-partite $H$ with $|H| = |G|$  (using the $\chi(G)$ color classes of $G$ from a proper coloring as parts).
There is a missing edge in $H$ that can be added to get a graph $G^*$ with $|G^*| = |G|$, $G^*$ strictly containing $G$ and with $\chi(G^*) = \chi(G)$, contradicting the assumption $\chi(D(G))  = \chi(G) +1$.
On the other hand, if $G$ is a complete $\chi(G)$-partite graph, then adding any edge is possible only within the color classes, hence forcing an increase of the chromatic number by~1. 
Consequently, all these graphs formed  by adding one edge contain a color-critical edge, namely the deletion of the added edge reduces the chromatic number by 1 back to $\chi(G)$. 
The rest follows from Theorem B applied for a family of critical graphs instead of just one graph. 

$(iii)$\quad 
According to the assumptions,  $G$ is bipartite but not complete bipartite; hence it can be extended to a complete bipartite $H$ by adding the edges missing between the two color classes of $G$.  
Now, by definition, $\mathrm{st}(n,G)  \leq  \mathrm{ex}(n,H)$ holds.
The sub-quadratic upper bound on $\mathrm{st}(n,G)$ follows by the theorem of K\H ov\'ari, S\'os and Tur\'an \cite{KST}.

\underline{Case A:} \ $G^* \notin  \{  K_k  , K_k -e  \}$.

Add three vertices  $x ,y ,z$ forming a path $P_3$ and take one new edge connecting $H$ to this $P_3$ to get $H^*$ which is still $C_3$-free and $C_4$-free. 

Now clearly $\mathrm{ex}(n,\{C_3 ,C_4\}) \leq  \mathrm{st}(n,P_4)$ because if there is no $C_3$ and no $C_4$ then even if there is a $P_4$ it must be induced. 

Consider now any graph $H$ with $n$ vertices and $\mathrm{ex}(n,\{C_3 ,C_4 \}) +1$ edges.  
If $H$ contains $C_4$, we are done as it is on four vertices and strictly contains $P_4$.
So, we may assume that $H$ is $C_4$-free, hence contains $C_3$.  
If this $C_3$ has an edge incident with it, we are done as this forms a graph on 4 vertices strictly containing $P_4$.
Consequently every triangle in $H$ is a connected component.

Removing an isolated  $C_3$ we obtain a graph $H_1$ with $n-3$ vertices  and $(\mathrm{ex}(n-3 , \{C_3 ,C_4 \}) +1)-3\geq \mathrm{ex}(n-3 , \{C_3 ,C_4 \}) +1$  edges. 
But it cannot contain $C_4$  as neither $H$ did, so it contains $C_3$ and the argument can be repeated.
In this way the structure is shrinked down by isolated vertex-disjoint copies of $C_3$   until we reach $n'$ = 1 or 2 or 3 vertices.

The cases $n'=1$ and $n'=2$ are impossible because those $(C_3,C_4)$-free extremal graphs are complete, so no graph with more edges can exist with those small orders.
The only possibility would be $n'=3$ where $\mathrm{ex}(3,\{C_3 , C_4 \}) = 2$ and one further edge yields $K_3$, thus originally $H=\frac{n}{3}C_3$ and
 before the last reduction step we would have $2C_3$, i.e., six vertices and six edges.
However $\mathrm{ex}(6, \{C_3 ,C_4\} ) \geq 6$ as demonstrated by $C_6$, so by assumption we should have at least $7>e(2C_3)$ edges before the last step.
This final contradiction completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
Let $T$ be a tree of order $q +1$.
If a graph $G$ has minimum degree at least $q$, then every subtree $T^*$ of $T$ can be completed to $T$ in $G$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Suppose we get stuck at a subtree $T^{**}$ while trying to expand $T^*$ to $T$. Then there is a vertex $u$ in $T^{**}$ that has a neighbor $w$ in $T$ that was not added yet.  In particular, if $|T^{**}| \leq  q$,  then $u$ is adjacent to at most $q - 1$ vertices of $T^{**}$ but the degree of $u$ in $G$ is at least $q$, so we can add to it the neighbor $w$ from outside $T^{**}$, contradicting that the process got stuck at $T^{**}$.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{t:treediam}
Let $T$ be a tree  with diameter $k \geq  2$  and order $q \geq  k+1   \geq  3$.  
Then $\mathrm{ex}(n, \{C_3,\dots,C_{k+1} \}) \leq   \mathrm{st}(n,T) \leq  \mathrm{ex}(n,\{C_3,\dots,C_{k+1}\}) +(q-1)n$.
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Lower bound: Clearly if a graph contains no cycle from $\{C_3,\dots,C_{k+1}\}$, then if a copy of $T$ occurs, it must be induced. 

\medskip

\noindent
Upper bound: Let $G$ have $n$ vertices and $\mathrm{ex}(n, \{C_3,\dots,C_{k+1} \}  +  (q-1)n  +1$ edges. 
Repeatedly remove vertices of degree at most $q-1$ until no such vertex remains, and call the remainder graph $F$. 
This $F$ is not empty, since we removed at most $(q-1)n$ edges, fewer than $e(G)$.

Further, $F$ must contain a cycle from  $\{ C_3 ,\dots,C_{k+1} \}$, for otherwise we would have $e(F) \leq  \mathrm{ex}( |F| , \{ C_3,\dots,C_{k+1 } \}) \leq  \mathrm{ex}(n, \{C_3,\dots,C_{k+1} \})$  by monotonicity of Tur\'an numbers, and the contradiction $e(G) \leq  e(F) + (q-1)n  \leq  \mathrm{ex}(n,\{C_3,\dots,C_{k+1}\})   + (q-1)n  < e(G)$ would follow. 

Hence $F$ has minimum degree at least  $q$ and contains a cycle $C_ s$, where $3  \leq  s \leq  k +1$.  
Consider $T$.
As  $s \leq   k+1$ = diam$(T) +1$, there are two non-adjacent vertices $u ,v$ in $T$ at distance $s - 1$ apart, and connecting them by an edge creates a cycle $C_s$ with all vertices in $T$, forming a unicyclic graph $H\supsetneq T$. 

We will show that this cycle $C_s$ can be extended to a copy of $H$  in $F$, hence a non-induced copy of $T$. 
Let $u = v_1,\dots,v_s = v$   be the vertices of $C_s$, and delete the edge $(u,v)$ from $C_s$.  After this deletion still  all  vertices in $F$ have degree  at least $q-1$ and we can complete a copy of $T$  from the path $u = v_1,\dots,v_s  = v$  originally in $T$, 
by the above lemma. 
Having completed $T$ we add back the edge $(u,v)$  to get $H$, and the proof is done.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
If $G$ is an acyclic disconnected graph on $k$ vertices, then $\mathrm{ex}(n,G) \leq   \mathrm{st}(n,G) \leq  \mathrm{ex}(n,T) \leq  ( k-2)n/2$. 
In particular, $\mathrm{st}(n,G)$ is bounded by a linear function of $n$ if and only if $G$ is an acyclic disconnected graph.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Clearly $\chi(G) = 2$ and $D(G)$ contains a forest or a tree $T$ on $k$ vertices, and the upper bound follows by the recently announced proof of the Erd\H os--S\'os Conjecture;
 see \cite{RS26} for a simplified version.

If $G$ contains a cycle $C_k$, then already $\mathrm{st}(n,G)  \geq   \mathrm{ex}(n,C_k)$ is a non-linear lower bound.
Also, if $G$ is a tree, then Theorem \ref{t:treediam} proved that $\mathrm{st}(n,G)$ is not linear.
Hence $G$ must be an acyclic disconnected graph.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
The value of $st(n,G)$ is constant if and only if $G$ is edgeless or $G = K_2 \cup tK_1$ for some $t \geq 2$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
As we already noted, the edgeless case is trivial: $\mathrm{st}(n,E_k)=0$ holds for all $n\geq k$.

Concerning nonempty graphs, assume first $e(G) \geq 2$.
If $G = P_3$ then $\mathrm{ex}(n,P_3) = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ and if $G  =  2K_2$ then $\mathrm{ex}(n,2K_2) = n -1$ (for $n \geq 4$), and
 also for any larger $G$ at least a linear growth is valid for $\mathrm{st}(n,G)$
 by $\mathrm{ex}(n,G) \leq \mathrm{st}(n,G)$ and monotonicity of the Tur\'an function.
 
Hence it remains to consider $G = K_2 \cup tK_1$, $t \geq 1$.   
For $t = 1$ we have $D(K_2 \cup K_1)  = \{P_3\}$, therefore $\mathrm{ex}(n,K_2 \cup K_1) = \mathrm{ex}(n, P_3)  = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$.
 
For $t \geq 2$ any graph with two edges contains either $2K_2$ or $P_3 \cup K_1$ and in both cases properly contains $K_2 \cup K_1$.

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{t:cyc}
Let $\ell\geq 6$ be an even integer.
 \begin{itemize}
  \item[$(i)$] We have $\mathrm{st}(n,C_\ell) = O(n^{1+1/\lceil \ell/4 \rceil})$
   for every $\ell$.
  \item[$(ii)$] If $\ell\in\{6,8,10,12,18,20\}$ then
   $\mathrm{st}(n,C_\ell) = \Theta(n^{1+1/\lceil \ell/4 \rceil})$.
  \item[$(iii)$] If $\ell=14,16$ then

    for some positive constants $c_1,c_2$.
  \item[$(iv)$] If $\ell > 20$ with $\ell=4k-2$ or $\ell=4k$, then
    for some constant $c_k > 0$ we have
    $$
      \mathrm{st}(n,C_\ell) \geq
  \begin{cases}
    \begin{tabular}{ccl}
      $c_k n^{1+\frac{2}{6k-6}}$ && $\mathrm{if } \ \ell=4k-2$\,,\\ 
      $c_k n^{1+\frac{2}{6k-2}}$ && $\mathrm{if } \ \ell=4k$\,.
    \end{tabular}
  \end{cases}
    $$
 \end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Assume $\ell=2k$.
Consider $H:=\theta_{k,k,1}$ if $k$ is odd, or $H:=\theta_{k+1,k-1,1}$
 if $k$ is even.
Due to the parity distinction, $H$ is bipartite and its
 shortest cycle has length $k+1$ or $k$, respectively.
Observe that $\frac{k+1}{2} = \frac{\ell+2}{4} = \lceil \frac{\ell}{4} \rceil$
 if $k$ is odd, and $\frac{k}{2} = \frac{\ell}{4} = \lceil \frac{\ell}{4} \rceil$
 if $k$ is even.

This $H$ properly contains $C_\ell$ and satisfies the parity condition
 of Theorem~D, consequently
  $$
    \mathrm{st}(n,C_\ell) \leq \mathrm{ex}(n,H) = O(n^{1+1/\lceil \ell/4 \rceil})
  $$
 as claimed in $(i)$.
The upper bounds for $(ii)$ and $(iii)$ follow as particular cases.

For a lower bound we note that $C_\ell$ with any chord contains a cycle
 of length at most $\ell/2+1$.
So, every graph of girth at least $\ell/2+2$ is a feasible construction
 for $\mathrm{st}(n,C_\ell)$, that is
  $$
    \mathrm{st}(n,C_\ell) \geq \mathrm{ex}(n,\{C_3,\dots,C_{\ell/2+1}\}) \,.
  $$
It suffices to take $C_4$-free bipartite graphs for $\ell=6$ and $\ell=8$,
 yielding the lower bound $\Theta(n^{3/2})$ from the projective plane
 incidence graph \cite{KST}.
The estimates $\Theta(n^{4/3})$ and $\Theta(n^{6/5})$ for
 $\ell=10,12,18,20$ follow from the constructions of Lazebnik et al.\
 \cite{LUW1} on graphs without $C_6$ and $C_{10}$, respectively,
 which imply tight growth orders of the Tur\'an numbers of these cycles.

Since the growth rate of $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_8)$ is not known, concerning
 $\mathrm{st}(n,C_{14})$ and $\mathrm{st}(n,C_{16})$ the best we can do for $(iii)$ is take
 the construction of girth 10 or 12, as in case of $\ell=18,20$.

For $C_\ell$ with $\ell>20$ we apply the curerntly best known lower bounds \cite{LUW2}
 on $\mathrm{ex}(n,\{C_3,\dots,C_{2s+1}\})$, which are
 $\Theta(n^{1+\frac{2}{3s-3}})$ for $s$ odd, and
 $\Theta(n^{1+\frac{2}{3s-2}})$ for $s$ even.
These imply the validity of $(iv)$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{p:bip}
~

\begin{itemize}
 \item[$(i)$] If $G$ is a bipartite graph containing $K_{p,p}$, with both sides of order  $p+1$ and   $p^2 \leq  e(G)  \leq  (p+1)^2 -2$,
 then $\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{p,p}) \leq \mathrm{st}(n,G) \leq \mathrm{ex}(n,K_{p+1,p+1}-e) \leq  O(n^{2-1/p})$. 
 \item[$(ii)$] For any $p\geq q\geq 2$, if
 $G  =  K_{p,q} - e$  then  $\mathrm{ex}(n ,K_{p,q})/2  \leq  \mathrm{st}(n , G) \leq  O(n^{2-1/p})$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Since $G$ is missing at least two edges of being $K_{p+1,p+1}$, it is obvious that $G$ is strictly  contained in $K_{p+1,p+1} - e$; and on the other hand $G$ contains $K_{p,p}$.  
Hence by monotonicity of Tur\'an numbers and by Lemma~\ref{l:equiv}, $\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{p,p}) \leq  \mathrm{ex}(n,G) \leq  \mathrm{st}(n,G) \leq  \mathrm{ex}(n, K_{p+1,p+1} - e)  \leq  O(n^{2-1/p})$ by Theorem E. 
This proves $(i)$ and also the upper bound in $(ii)$.
For the lower bound in the latter, it suffices to note the well-known fact
 that every graph contains a bipartite subgraph with at least half
 of its edges; and apply this to a graph extremal for $K_{p,q}$.

When an asymptotic estimate of order $\Theta(n^{2-1/p})$ is known for $\mathrm{ex}(n, K_{p,p})$, we get the correct order of exponent of $n$ for $\mathrm{st}(n,G)$.  This is known for $p = 2$ and $p=3$  with exponents $3/2$ and $5/3$, respectively.

A further consequence of Theorem E above is as follows.

$\mathrm{st}(n,H(p,l ,l ) ) \leq \mathrm{ex}(n, D(H(p +1 ,l-1, l-1 )) )   \leq  c(p+1, l-1)\,n^{2- 1/(p+1)}$.
Hence whenever $\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{p+1,p+1})  \sim  n^{2-1/(p+1)}$,  the sharp  exponent for $\mathrm{st}(n , H(p,l.l))$ is obtained.
