<!-- thmx-1: body_md -->
\label{T:PHP-main}
Let $G$ be a countable discrete group with Ozawa's property PHP, and let $G\curvearrowright X$ be a strictly ergodic action on the Cantor set with dynamical comparison. Then $C(X)\rtimes_\lambda G$ is completely selfless. Consequently, the crossed product has stable rank one, strict comparison, a unique quasitracial state, and real rank zero.
<!-- end thmx-1 -->
<!-- corx-1: body_md -->
\label{T:main}
Let $G$ be a nonamenable free product of two nontrivial groups, and let $G\curvearrowright X$ be a strictly ergodic action on the Cantor set with dynamical comparison. Then $C(X)\rtimes_\lambda G$ is completely selfless.
<!-- end corx-1 -->
<!-- corx-2: body_md -->
\label{T:amenable}
Let $G$ be a countable discrete group with property PHP. Suppose that $G\curvearrowright X$ preserves a Borel probability measure on the Cantor set and that some infinite amenable subgroup acts strictly ergodically on $X$. Then the reduced crossed product is completely selfless.
<!-- end corx-2 -->
<!-- corx-3: body_md -->
\label{T:free}
Let $d\ge 2$, and suppose that an action $F_d\curvearrowright X$ on the Cantor set preserves a Borel probability measure. If some nonidentity element of $F_d$ acts strictly ergodically, then $C(X)\rtimes_\lambda F_d$ is completely selfless, has stable rank one, strict comparison, a unique quasitracial state, and real rank zero. In particular, these conclusions hold for every action in $\operatorname{A*}(F_d,X)$.
<!-- end corx-3 -->
<!-- corx-4: body_md -->
\label{C:BD}
	Let $G$ be a countably infinite residually finite group with property PHP, and let $\sigma$ be a separating normal chain in $G$. Then the Bunce--Deddens algebra $\mathsf{BD}(G,\sigma)$ is completely selfless, has stable rank one, strict comparison, a unique quasitracial state, and real rank zero.
<!-- end corx-4 -->
<!-- corx-5: body_md -->
\label{C:Toeplitz}
	Let $G$ be a countably infinite residually finite group with property PHP, and let $G\curvearrowright X$ be an infinite regular Toeplitz subshift. Then $C(X)\rtimes_\lambda G$ is completely selfless.
<!-- end corx-5 -->
<!-- notation-1: body_md -->
Throughout, $X$ denotes the Cantor set, $G$ a countable discrete
group with identity $e$, and $\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$.
<!-- end notation-1 -->
<!-- df-1: body_md -->
An action $G\curvearrowright X$ has \emph{dynamical
comparison} if $U\prec_G V$ whenever $U,V\subseteq X$ are nonempty clopen and
$\mu(U)<\mu(V)$ for every $\mu\in M_G(X)$.
<!-- end df-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{P:subgroup-comparison}
Suppose that $G\curvearrowright X$ preserves a probability measure
and that a subgroup $H\le G$ acts strictly ergodically with
dynamical comparison. Then the $G$-action is strictly ergodic and
has dynamical comparison.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $H \le G$, we have $Hx \subseteq Gx$ for every $x\in X$ and so since $H\curvearrowright X$ is strictly ergodic (in particular minimal) $X = \overline{Hx}\subseteq \overline{Gx} \subseteq X$, thus $G\curvearrowright X$ is minimal. Since every $G$-invariant probability measure is $H$-invariant, the measure $\mu$ preserved by the $G$-action belongs to $M_H(X)$. By unique ergodicity of $H\curvearrowright X$, we conclude $\{\mu\} \subseteq M_G(X) \subseteq M_H(X) = \{\mu\}$, which shows that $G\curvearrowright X$ is uniquely ergodic and hence strictly ergodic. Finally, let $U,V\subseteq X$ be clopen and suppose that $\mu(U) < \mu(V)$. Then $U\prec_H V$ via comparison for $H\curvearrowright X$ and in particular $U\prec_G V$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- df-2: body_md -->
\label{D:NA}
Let $E:A\to D$ be a faithful conditional expectation and let
$\tau=\sigma\circ E$ be a faithful tracial state. We say that
$(E,\tau)$ has \emph{norm averaging} if, for every finite
$F\subset A$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there is a unitary $w\in A$
such that, for all $x\in F$, 
\begin{equation}\label{E:NA}
 \|E(w^*xw)-\tau(x)1\|<\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
<!-- end df-2 -->
<!-- df-3: body_md -->
A unital C$^*$-algebra $A$ with faithful tracial state $\tau$ is
\emph{selfless} if there exist a nontrivial C$^*$-probability space
$(Q,\psi)$, a free ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$, and a state-preserving embedding
\[
 (A,\tau)*(Q,\psi)\longrightarrow (A^{\mathcal{U}},\tau^{\mathcal{U}})
\]
whose restriction to $A$ is the diagonal embedding.
<!-- end df-3 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{T:Ozawa-isometry}
Suppose that $(A,\tau)\subseteq(B,\psi)$ is a unital
state-preserving inclusion and that there is an isometry
$V\in B^{\mathcal{U}}$ satisfying, for all $x\in A$,
\[
 V^*xV=\tau(x)1,\quad
 \psi^{\mathcal{U}}(xVV^*x^*)=0,\quad
 V+V^*\in A^{\mathcal{U}}.
\]
Then $(A,\tau)$ is completely selfless.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- df-4: body_md -->
\label{D:group-PHP}
A group $G$ has \emph{property PHP} if, for every finite
$F\subseteq G$ and $\eta>0$, there is $N\in\mathbb{N}$ such that
for every $n\ge N$ there are $t_i\in G$ and subsets
$C_i\subseteq D_i\subseteq G$, $1\le i\le n$, for which
the indexed family
\[
 \{aC_i:a\in F,\ 1\le i\le n\}
 \ \cup\
 \{bt_i^{-1}(G\setminus D_i):b\in F,\ 1\le i\le n\}
\]
is pairwise disjoint, and
\begin{equation}\label{E:group-PHP-overlap}
 \Big\|\sum_{i=1}^n1_{D_i}
       +\sum_{i=1}^n1_{t_i^{-1}(G\setminus C_i)}
 \Big\|_\infty\le\eta\sqrt n.
\end{equation}

\label{D:balanced}
Let $\mu$ be an invariant probability measure. The action has
\emph{balanced towers with respect to $\mu$} if, for every finite
$\mathcal F\subset C(X)$, $\delta>0$, and $N\in\mathbb{N}$, there is a
clopen tower partition $\{(B_i,S_i)\}_i$ with $|S_i|\ge N$ and for all $f\in \mathcal{F}$ and $y\in B_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:balanced}
 \Big|\frac1{|S_i|}\sum_{s\in S_i}f(sy)-\mu(f)\Big|<\delta. 
\end{equation}

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{P:comparison}
A strictly ergodic action $G\curvearrowright X$ of a countable discrete group on the Cantor set has dynamical comparison if and only if it has balanced towers with respect to its unique invariant measure.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
$[\Rightarrow]$. We first prove the following. 

\textbf{Claim.} Given a partition $X=P_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_k$ into nonempty clopen sets,
$\eta>0$, and $N\in\mathbb{N}$, there is a tower partition with heights
at least $N$ such that every level is contained in one of the
$P_i$ and, in every tower, the proportion of levels in $P_i$
differs from $p_i:=\mu(P_i)$ by less than $\eta$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Step 1.}
Choose a nonempty clopen $B$ of measure $b=\mu(B)>0$, and set
\[
 n_i=\Big\lfloor\frac{p_i}{b}\Big\rfloor-1,
 \qquad n=\sum_{i=1}^k n_i.
\]
The measure $b$ can be arbitrarily small since $\mu$ is regular and nonatomic. Since all $p_i$ are positive and $n>1/b-2k$, we can choose $B$ so that all $n_i$
are positive and
\begin{equation}\label{E:height-choice}
 n\ge\max\{N,2k\},\qquad \frac{4k+2}{n}<\eta.
\end{equation}
We also have, for all $1\le i\le k$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:remainder-measure}
 b\le p_i-n_i b<2b.
\end{equation}

For each $i$, we successively place $n_i$ disjoint copies of
$B$ in $P_i$ using comparison. Indeed, after
$\ell<n_i$ copies have been placed, the unused part of $P_i$
is clopen and has measure
\[
 p_i-\ell b=(p_i-n_i b)+(n_i-\ell)b\ge2b>b.
\]
Comparison therefore supplies the next copy. Each image has
measure $b$, since subequivalences preserve $\mu$.
Enumerate the resulting maps by
$\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n:B\to X$, and put
$Y_j=\varphi_j(B)$. The $Y_j$ are pairwise disjoint clopen
sets, exactly $n_i$ of which lie in $P_i$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Step 2.}
Let $R=X\setminus\bigsqcup_{j=1}^nY_j$. Summing
\eqref{E:remainder-measure} gives
\[
 kb\le\mu(R)<2kb.
\]
Since $n\ge2k$, choose $2k$ of the ranges and write
$V=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{2k}Y_j$. Then $\mu(R)<\mu(V)$, so
comparison gives a subequivalence $\theta:R\to V$. For $1\le j\le2k$, define the clopen subset
\[
 D_j=\varphi_j^{-1}(Y_j\cap\theta(R))\subseteq B
\]
and maps 
\[
 \psi_j=\theta^{-1}\circ\varphi_j|_{D_j}:D_j\rightarrow R.
\]
These maps have pairwise disjoint images, and
\begin{equation}\label{E:remainder-partition}
 R=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{2k}\psi_j(D_j).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the sets $Y_j\cap\theta(R)$ partition $\theta(R)$,
and applying $\theta^{-1}$ gives exactly this identity.
Some $D_j$ may be empty.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Step 3.}
Take a finite partition $\mathcal B$ of $B$ into nonempty clopen
sets such that, for each $B'\in\mathcal B$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every $\varphi_j|_{B'}$ is a single group translation;
\item for each $j\le2k$, either $B'\subseteq D_j$ or
      $B'\cap D_j=\emptyset$;
\item when $B'\subseteq D_j$, the map $\psi_j|_{B'}$ is a
      single group translation and $\psi_j(B')$ lies in one
      member of $\{P_1,\ldots,P_k\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Such a partition exists by taking a common refinement of the
finitely many clopen pieces defining the $\varphi_j$ and
$\psi_j$, the sets $D_j$, and the clopen sets
$\psi_j^{-1}(P_i)$.

For $B'\in\mathcal B$, write
$\varphi_j|_{B'}=g_{j,B'}|_{B'}$, and, whenever
$B'\subseteq D_j$, write $\psi_j|_{B'}=h_{j,B'}|_{B'}$.
Define
\[
 S_{B'}=\{g_{j,B'}:1\le j\le n\}
       \cup\{h_{j,B'}:1\le j\le2k,\ B'\subseteq D_j\}.
\]
The corresponding images of $B'$ are disjoint. The original
images lie in distinct $Y_j$, the additional images lie in the
disjoint sets in \eqref{E:remainder-partition}, and no original
image meets $R$. In particular, all the displayed group elements
are distinct since equal elements would give equal nonempty images of
$B'$. Thus $(B',S_{B'})$ is a clopen tower of height
\[
 |S_{B'}|=n+t_{B'},\qquad
 t_{B'}=|\{j\le2k:B'\subseteq D_j\}|\le2k.
\]

These towers form a partition of $X$. For fixed $j$, the sets
$\varphi_j(B')$, as $B'$ runs through $\mathcal B$, partition
$Y_j$. Likewise, the sets $\psi_j(B')$ with $B'\subseteq D_j$
partition $\psi_j(D_j)$. Together with
\eqref{E:remainder-partition}, this proves both coverage and
disjointness of all levels, including levels from different towers.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Step 4.}
Fix a resulting tower and abbreviate $t=t_{B'}$. Let $r_i$ be
the number of its additional levels lying in $P_i$. Its total
number of levels in $P_i$ is $n_i+r_i$, where
$0\le r_i\le t$ and $\sum_i r_i=t$. Put
\[
 d_i= \frac{p_i}{b}-n_i\in[1,2),\qquad
 d=\sum_{i=1}^k d_i=\frac1b - n \in[k,2k).
\]
Since $n_i-p_i n=p_i d-d_i$, we have
\[
 \left|\frac{n_i}{n}-p_i\right|<\frac{2k+2}{n}.
\]
Adding the extra levels changes this proportion by at most
\[
 \left|\frac{n_i+r_i}{n+t}-\frac{n_i}{n}\right|
 =\frac{|nr_i-n_i t|}{n(n+t)}
 \le\frac{t}{n+t}\le\frac{2k}{n}.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{E:balance-bound}
 \left|\frac{n_i+r_i}{n+t}-p_i\right|
 <\frac{4k+2}{n}<\eta.
\end{equation}
Every height is at least $n\ge N$, proving the claim.

We now deduce balanced towers for continuous functions. Given
finite $\mathcal F\subset C(X)$ and $\delta>0$, choose a common
clopen partition $P_1,\ldots,P_k$ and functions
$f_0=\sum_i c_{f,i}1_{P_i}$ with
$\|f-f_0\|<\delta/3$ for $f\in\mathcal F$.
This is possible because locally constant functions are uniformly
dense in $C(X)$. Choose $\eta>0$ so that
$\eta\sum_i|c_{f,i}|<\delta/3$ for every $f\in\mathcal F$,
and apply the claim with the prescribed minimum height $N$. Let $q_i$ denote the proportion of levels in $P_i$ in a tower $(B',S)$. The tower average of $f_0$ is $\sum_i c_{f,i}q_i$, while $\mu(f_0)=\sum_i c_{f,i}p_i$. Approximating $f$ by $f_0$ in both the tower average and the integral therefore gives, for every $y\in B'$,
\begin{align*}
 \Bigg|\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}f(sy)-\mu(f)\Bigg| &\le2\|f-f_0\|+\Bigg|\sum_i c_{f,i}(q_i-p_i)\Bigg|\\
 &\le2\|f-f_0\|+\sum_i|c_{f,i}|\,|q_i-p_i|<\delta.
\end{align*}
This proves balanced towers.

\noindent $[\Leftarrow]$. Let $U,V\subseteq X$ be clopen with $\mu(U)<\mu(V)$, and choose
$\delta>0$ with $2\delta<\mu(V)-\mu(U)$. Apply balanced
towers to $\{1_U,1_V\}$, with minimum height $1$.
Refine each base $B$ by the clopen sets $B\cap s^{-1}U$
and $B\cap s^{-1}V$, for all $s$ in its shape. Keep the
same shape above each refined base. This remains a tower
partition and retains the balancing inequalities, while every
level now lies either wholly in or wholly outside each of $U,V$.

For a resulting tower $(B',S)$, let
\[
 S_U=\{s\in S:sB'\subseteq U\},\qquad
 S_V=\{s\in S:sB'\subseteq V\}.
\]
The balancing inequalities give
\[
 \frac{|S_U|}{|S|}<\mu(U)+\delta
 <\mu(V)-\delta<\frac{|S_V|}{|S|}.
\]
Choose an injection $\iota:S_U\to S_V$, and on each level
$sB'\subseteq U$ use the group element $\iota(s)s^{-1}$ to
map it onto $\iota(s)B'\subseteq V$.
The source levels, over all towers, form a finite clopen partition
of $U$. Their images are disjoint within each tower because
$\iota$ is injective, and are disjoint between towers because
the tower levels partition $X$. Thus these translations define
a subequivalence $U\prec_G V$, which proves dynamical comparison.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{L:matrix}
For every $k\in\mathbb{N}$ and $M,\eta>0$, there exists $N\in\mathbb{N}$ such
that, whenever $n\ge N$ and $X_1,\ldots,X_k\in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ have
norm at most $M$, there is $W\in\mathcal{U}(M_n(\mathbb{C}))$ with
\[
 \max_{1\le r\le k,\,0\le j<n}
 |(W^*X_rW)_{jj}-\mathrm{tr}_n(X_r)|<\eta.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Put $\delta=\eta/3$. Let $X_1,\ldots,X_k\in M_n(\mathbb C)$
have norm at most $M$, and write $\tau_r=\operatorname{tr}_n(X_r)$.
We shall construct the required unitary whenever $n$ exceeds a bound
depending only on $k,M,\eta$.

\medskip
\noindent
\textit{Step 1: make most diagonal positions good.}
Let $\Xi=\{1,-1,i,-i\}$. An element $\zeta\in\Xi^n$ is a
phase vector $\zeta=(\zeta_0,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})$. Define the
averaging operator $\operatorname{Av}$ on complex-valued functions
on $\Xi^n$ by
\[
\operatorname{Av}(h)
:=
\frac{1}{4^n}\sum_{\zeta\in\Xi^n}h(\zeta).
\]
Thus $\operatorname{Av}(h)$ is the arithmetic mean of the values
of $h$ over all $4^n$ phase vectors. For distinct indices $p,q$, define
\[
\chi_{pq}(\zeta)=\overline{\zeta_p}\zeta_q.
\]
These functions satisfy
\[
\operatorname{Av}(\chi_{pq}\overline{\chi_{ab}})
=
\begin{cases}
1, & (p,q)=(a,b),\\
0, & (p,q)\ne(a,b),
\end{cases}
\qquad p\ne q,\ a\ne b.
\]
Indeed, in the first case the product is identically $1$.
Otherwise, some coordinate of $\zeta$ occurs with a nonzero
exponent in $\{-2,-1,1,2\}$. Averaging over that coordinate
gives zero, since
\[
\frac14\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\xi^\ell=0, 
\quad \ell\in\{-2,-1,1,2\}.
\]
For each $\zeta\in\Xi^n$, put
\[
U_\zeta=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_0,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}),
\qquad
Y_r(\zeta)=F_n^*U_\zeta^*X_rU_\zeta F_n.
\]
Write $Y_{r,jj}$ for the scalar-valued function
$\zeta\mapsto Y_r(\zeta)_{jj}$, and identify scalars with
constant functions on $\Xi^n$. Writing $\omega_n=e^{2\pi i/n}$,
the Fourier-conjugation formula gives the identity of functions
\[
Y_{r,jj}-\tau_r
=
\frac1n\sum_{p\ne q}
\omega_n^{(q-p)j}(X_r)_{pq}\chi_{pq}.
\]
The orthogonality established above eliminates all cross terms
when we average the squared absolute value. Hence, for each $r,j$,
\[
\operatorname{Av}(|Y_{r,jj}-\tau_r|^2)
=
\frac1{n^2}\sum_{p\ne q}|(X_r)_{pq}|^2.
\]
By linearity of $\operatorname{Av}$,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Av}\Big(\sum_{r=1}^k\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|Y_{r,jj}-\tau_r|^2\Big)
&=\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^k\sum_{p\ne q}|(X_r)_{pq}|^2\\
&\le\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^k\operatorname{Tr}(X_r^*X_r) \le kM^2.
\end{align*}
Since this is a finite average, there is a phase vector $\zeta$
for which the expression being averaged is at most $kM^2$.
Fix such a vector, and put
\[
W_0=U_\zeta F_n,
\qquad
Y_r=W_0^*X_rW_0.
\]
Thus the matrices $Y_r$ satisfy
\[
\sum_{r=1}^k\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|Y_{r,jj}-\tau_r|^2
\le kM^2.
\]
Call a position $j$ \emph{bad} if
$|Y_{r,jj}-\tau_r|>\delta$ for some $r$, and \emph{good}
otherwise. Each bad position contributes more than $\delta^2$
to the preceding sum, so there are at most
\[
B=\Bigg\lceil\frac{kM^2}{\delta^2}\Bigg\rceil
\]
bad positions. In particular, their number is bounded
independently of $n$.

\medskip
\noindent
\textit{Step 2: spread each bad position among good ones.}
Choose an integer $m\ge2$ such that $2M/m<\delta$, and put
$\theta=\delta/m$. Since $\|Y_r\|\le M$, the row and column
norm estimates give, for each index $p$,
\[
\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}\sum_{r=1}^k
\bigl(|Y_{r,pq}|^2+|Y_{r,qp}|^2\bigr)
\le 2kM^2.
\]
Consequently, at most
\[
D=\Bigg\lceil\frac{2kM^2}{\theta^2}\Bigg\rceil
\]
indices $q\ne p$ satisfy $|Y_{r,pq}|>\theta$ or
$|Y_{r,qp}|>\theta$ for some $r$. Set
\[
N=m(B+D)+1,
\]
and suppose that $n\ge N$. Enumerate the bad positions as $b_1,\ldots,b_t$, where $t\le B$.
We claim that there are pairwise disjoint $m$-element index sets
\[
I_1,\ldots,I_t\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
\]
such that $I_j$ contains $b_j$ and $m-1$ good positions, and, for distinct $p, q\in I_j$ and $1\le r\le k$, 
\[
|Y_{r,pq}|\le\theta. 
\]
The indices in each $I_j$ need not be consecutive. Construct these sets successively, starting $I_j$ with $b_j$ and adding good indices one at a time. At each addition,
exclude all bad indices and all good indices already selected,
including those in the current set. These exclude at most
\[
B+(m-1)B=mB
\]
choices, since there are at most $B$ sets to construct, each
requiring $m-1$ good indices. Also exclude every index $q$
for which $|Y_{r,pq}|>\theta$ or $|Y_{r,qp}|>\theta$ for some
$p$ in the current set and some $r$. Each such $p$ excludes
at most $D$ choices, and the current set has fewer than $m$
elements, so these exclusions account for at most $mD$
further choices. Thus at most $m(B+D)<n$ indices are excluded.
An unused good index satisfying all the required inequalities
therefore remains available. Adding it preserves the
inequalities between every pair of distinct indices in the
current set, so this procedure completes the construction.

Let $V$ act as $F_m$ on each coordinate subspace $\operatorname{span}\{e_p:p\in I_j\}$, 
with its coordinates listed in increasing order, and as the
identity on the remaining coordinates. The disjointness of
the index sets makes $V$ unitary.

Fix $r$ and one of the sets $I_j$. Fourier conjugation makes
the diagonal part of the compression of $Y_r$ contribute
\[
\frac1m\sum_{p\in I_j}Y_{r,pp}
\]
to each new diagonal entry on this set. Since $I_j$ contains
only one bad position, and
\[
|Y_{r,pp}-\tau_r|
\le |Y_{r,pp}|+|\tau_r|
\le 2M
\]
at every position, we have
\[
\Bigg|
\frac1m\sum_{p\in I_j}Y_{r,pp}-\tau_r
\Bigg|
\le
\frac{2M+(m-1)\delta}{m}
<2\delta.
\]
Each entry of $F_m$ has modulus $m^{-1/2}$, so the
off-diagonal contribution to any new diagonal entry has
absolute value at most
\[
\frac1m
\sum_{\substack{p,q\in I_j\\p\ne q}}
|Y_{r,pq}|
\le (m-1)\theta
<\delta.
\]
Every diagonal entry of $V^*Y_rV$ on the selected index sets
is therefore within $3\delta=\eta$ of $\tau_r$. All remaining
positions were already good, and their diagonal entries
are unchanged. It follows that $W=W_0V$ satisfies the conditions of the lemma.

\label{L:tower-matrices}
The map $y\mapsto X_x(y)$ is continuous, and
$\|X_x(y)\|\le\|x\|$ for every $y\in B$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{E:matrix-trace}
\mathrm{tr}_n(X_x(y))
=
\frac1n\sum_{s\in S}E(x)(sy).
\end{equation}
If $W\in\mathcal{U}(n)$ is viewed as a constant matrix over $B$, then
for every $y\in B$ and $1\le j\le n$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:expectation-diagonal}
E\bigl(\Phi_B(W)^*x\Phi_B(W)\bigr)(s_jy)
=
(W^*X_x(y)W)_{jj}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->

Continuity follows from the formula for the matrix entries.
For $y\in B$, consider the regular representation $\pi_y$
on $\ell^2(G)$, given by
\[
\pi_y(f)\delta_s=f(sy)\delta_s,
\qquad
\pi_y(u_g)\delta_s=\delta_{gs}.
\]
With respect to the ordered basis
$\delta_{s_1},\ldots,\delta_{s_n}$, the matrix $X_x(y)$ is
the compression of $\pi_y(x)$ to $\ell^2(S)$. Hence
\[
\|X_x(y)\|\le\|\pi_y(x)\|\le\|x\|.
\]
Its diagonal entries are $f_e(s_jy)=E(x)(s_jy)$, which
gives \eqref{E:matrix-trace}. Put $w=\Phi_B(W)$. Since the tower levels are pairwise
disjoint and $y\in B$, the definition of $\Phi_B$ gives
\[
\pi_y(w)\delta_{s_j}
=
\sum_{i=1}^n W_{ij}\delta_{s_i}.
\]
Applying the same observation to $w^*=\Phi_B(W^*)$ shows
that $\ell^2(S)$ is invariant under both $\pi_y(w)$ and
$\pi_y(w^*)$, with restrictions represented by $W$ and
$W^*$, respectively. The compression of $\pi_y(w^*xw)$
to $\ell^2(S)$ therefore has matrix $W^*X_x(y)W$. For any $a\in C(X)\rtimes_\lambda G$, the diagonal matrix coefficient of $\pi_y(a)$ at $\delta_{s_j}$ is $E(a)(s_jy)$.
Taking the $j$th diagonal entry of the preceding compression
thus gives \eqref{E:expectation-diagonal}.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{P:averaging}
Let $G\curvearrowright X$ be an action of a countable discrete group on the Cantor set. Suppose that the action preserves a probability measure $\mu$ of full support and has balanced towers with respect
to $\mu$. Then the canonical expectation $E$ on $C(X)\rtimes_\lambda G$ has norm averaging with respect to $\tau=\mu\circ E$. The averaging unitaries can be chosen in the algebraic crossed product.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $A = C(X)\rtimes_\lambda G$, and let $F\subset A$ be a nonempty finite set of Fourier
polynomials, and fix $\varepsilon>0$. Put $\eta=\varepsilon/4$
and choose $M>\max_{x\in F}\|x\|$. Apply Lemma~\ref{L:matrix}
with $k=|F|$, norm bound $M$, and tolerance $\eta$, obtaining
a minimum matrix size $N$.

Choose a balanced tower partition $\{(B_i,S_i)\}_{i=1}^l$ for
the functions in the set $\{E(x):x\in F\}$, tolerance $\eta$, and minimum
height $N$. Enumerate $S_i=\{s_{i,1},\ldots,s_{i,n_i}\}$, and
write $X_{i,x}(y)$ for the matrix in
\eqref{E:compression-matrix} on this tower. Thus, for $y\in B_i$
and $x\in F$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:balanced-matrix-traces}
 \|X_{i,x}(y)\|\le\|x\|<M,
 \qquad
 |\mathrm{tr}_{n_i}(X_{i,x}(y))-\tau(x)|<\eta.
\end{equation}
The second inequality is precisely the balancing condition,
by \eqref{E:matrix-trace}. It remains to make every diagonal
entry close to this normalised trace, using unitaries that vary
locally constantly over the bases.

Fix $i$. For each $y_0\in B_i$, Lemma~\ref{L:matrix} provides
$W_{i,y_0}\in\mathcal{U}(n_i)$ such that, for all $x\in F$, $1\le j\le n_i$, 
\[
 |(W_{i,y_0}^*X_{i,x}(y_0)W_{i,y_0})_{jj}
             -\mathrm{tr}_{n_i}(X_{i,x}(y_0))|<\eta.
\]
Each matrix field $X_{i,x}$ is continuous in norm, since its finitely
many entries are continuous functions of $y$. Since $F$ is finite
and $X$ has a clopen basis, there is a clopen neighborhood
$O_{i,y_0}\subseteq B_i$ of $y_0$ such that, for $y\in O_{i,y_0}, x\in F$,
\[
 \|X_{i,x}(y)-X_{i,x}(y_0)\|<\eta/2.
\]
The normalised trace and each diagonal-entry functional have
norm one. Hence, for every $y\in O_{i,y_0}$,
\begin{align*}
 &|(W_{i,y_0}^*X_{i,x}(y)W_{i,y_0})_{jj}
                -\mathrm{tr}_{n_i}(X_{i,x}(y))|\\*
 &\quad\le
 |(W_{i,y_0}^*X_{i,x}(y_0)W_{i,y_0})_{jj}
                -\mathrm{tr}_{n_i}(X_{i,x}(y_0))|
       +2\|X_{i,x}(y)-X_{i,x}(y_0)\|<2\eta.
\end{align*}
By compactness, finitely many of the sets $O_{i,y_0}$ cover
$B_i$. Taking successive differences gives a finite clopen
partition $B_i=\bigsqcup_a C_{i,a}$ subordinate to this cover;
discard empty parts. On each $C_{i,a}$ choose the constant
unitary $W_{i,a}$ associated to one covering neighborhood.
For every $y\in C_{i,a}$, $x\in F$, and $1\le j\le n_i$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{E:local-matrix-averaging}
 |(W_{i,a}^*X_{i,x}(y)W_{i,a})_{jj}
                -\mathrm{tr}_{n_i}(X_{i,x}(y))|<2\eta.
\end{equation}
The pairs $(C_{i,a},S_i)$ form a tower partition with the same heights
and balancing estimates as before. Thus no continuous choice
of the pointwise unitaries is required.

We now assemble the locally constant choices. For each refined
tower put
\[
 p_{i,a}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}1_{s_{i,j}C_{i,a}},\qquad
 w_{i,a}=\sum_{p,q=1}^{n_i}(W_{i,a})_{pq}
               u_{s_{i,p}}1_{C_{i,a}}u_{s_{i,q}}^*.
\]
The tower-algebra embedding \eqref{E:tower-algebra} gives
\[
 w_{i,a}=p_{i,a}w_{i,a}p_{i,a},\qquad
 w_{i,a}^*w_{i,a}=w_{i,a}w_{i,a}^*=p_{i,a}.
\]
The projections $p_{i,a}$ are pairwise orthogonal and sum to
$1$, so $w=\sum_{i,a}w_{i,a}$ satisfies $w^*w=ww^*=1$.
Moreover, every summand in its defining formula is a Fourier
monomial, since
\[
 u_{s_{i,p}}1_{C_{i,a}}u_{s_{i,q}}^*
     =1_{s_{i,p}C_{i,a}}u_{s_{i,p}s_{i,q}^{-1}}.
\]
Thus $w$ belongs to the algebraic crossed product.

Although $x$ can couple distinct towers, their cross terms
vanish after applying $E$. Indeed, for $(i,a)\ne(h,b)$,
$C(X)$-bimodularity gives
\[
 E(w_{i,a}^*xw_{h,b})
   =p_{i,a}E(w_{i,a}^*xw_{h,b})p_{h,b}=0.
\]
Each remaining term is supported on its own tower. Consequently,
\eqref{E:expectation-diagonal} gives, for $y\in C_{i,a}$,
\[
 E(w^*xw)(s_{i,j}y)
       =(W_{i,a}^*X_{i,x}(y)W_{i,a})_{jj}.
\]
Combining \eqref{E:balanced-matrix-traces} and
\eqref{E:local-matrix-averaging}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 |E(w^*xw)(s_{i,j}y)-\tau(x)|
 &\le |(W_{i,a}^*X_{i,x}(y)W_{i,a})_{jj}
                       -\mathrm{tr}_{n_i}(X_{i,x}(y))|\\*
 &\quad+|\mathrm{tr}_{n_i}(X_{i,x}(y))-\tau(x)|<3\eta.
\end{align*}
The levels cover $X$, so
$\|E(w^*xw)-\tau(x)1\|\le3\eta<\varepsilon$ for every
$x\in F$.

Finally, let $F\subset A$ be any finite set. For each $x\in F$
choose a Fourier polynomial $a_x$ with
$\|x-a_x\|<\varepsilon/4$, and apply the preceding argument
to $\{a_x:x\in F\}$ with tolerance $\varepsilon/2$. The resulting
algebraic unitary $w$ satisfies
\[
 \|E(w^*xw)-\tau(x)1\|
 \le2\|x-a_x\|+\|E(w^*a_xw)-\tau(a_x)1\|<\varepsilon,
\]
using contractivity of $E$ and $\tau$. This proves norm averaging
with algebraic averaging unitaries.

<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{T:group-PHP-NA}
Let $G$ be a countable group with property PHP and $G\curvearrowright D$ an action on a separable unital C$^*$-algebra $D$ with an invariant faithful tracial state $\sigma$. If the canonical expectation $E$ on $A=D\rtimes_\lambda G$ has norm averaging with respect to $\tau=\sigma\circ E$, then $(A,\tau)$ is completely selfless.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Represent $A$ regularly on $H=\ell^2(G)\otimes L^2(D,\sigma)$ and put $\Omega=\delta_e\otimes\widehat{1}$. The vector state $\psi$ on $B(H)$ defined by $\psi(z)=\langle z\Omega,\Omega\rangle$ restricts to $\tau$ on $A$. For a finite subset $K\subseteq G$, let $R_K$ denote the projection onto $\ell^2(K)\otimes L^2(D,\sigma)$. Fix finite subsets $F,L$ of the unit ball of $A$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By norm averaging, choose $w\in\mathcal{U}(A)$
such that, for all $x\in F$,
\[
\|E(w^*xw)-\tau(x)1\|<\varepsilon.
\]
For each $x\in F$, choose a Fourier polynomial $y_x$ with $\|y_x-w^*xw\|<\varepsilon$. 
Then $\|E(y_x)-\tau(x)1\|<2\varepsilon$. Let $S\subseteq G\setminus\{e\}$ be the union of the
nonidentity Fourier supports of the $y_x$. Also choose a finite subset $K\subseteq G$ such that, for all $a\in L$,
\[
\|(1-R_K)w^*a^*\Omega\|<\varepsilon.
\]
We apply the PHP operator construction from the proofs of \cite[Theorem~14]{Oza25} and \cite[Theorem~1.4]{FloKliCobPag26b} to the finite set $\{e\}\cup S$. After choosing the PHP
tolerance sufficiently small, the estimates established there give, for arbitrarily large integers $n$, a contraction $T\in B(H)$ satisfying
\begin{enumerate}
	\item $\|T^*T-1\|<\varepsilon$;
	\item $\operatorname{dist}(T+T^*,A)<\varepsilon$;
	\item $T^*du_gT=0$ for all $d\in D, g\in S$; and 
	\item $\|R_KT\|^2\le |K|/(2n)$. 
\end{enumerate}
Only the group PHP conditions enter these estimates. The coefficient averaging condition in \cite{FloKliCobPag26b} is not needed here. Choose $n$ large enough that $\sqrt{|K|/(2n)}<\varepsilon$, and put $V=wTw^*$. Then $V$ is a contraction and
\[
\|V^*V-1\|<\varepsilon,
\qquad
\operatorname{dist}(V+V^*,A)<\varepsilon.
\]
For each $x\in F$, the annihilation of the nonidentity Fourier terms gives $T^*y_xT=T^*E(y_x)T$. Hence, using $\|T\|\le1$ and $|\tau(x)|\le1$, we have 
\begin{align*}
\|V^*xV-\tau(x)1\|
&=\|T^*w^*xwT-\tau(x)1\|\\
&\le
\|w^*xw-y_x\|
+\|T^*(E(y_x)-\tau(x)1)T\|\\
&\qquad
+|\tau(x)|\,\|T^*T-1\| <4\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
The choice of $K$ controls the state after conjugating by $w$. For every $a\in L$,
\begin{align*}
\psi(aVV^*a^*)^{1/2}
&=\|V^*a^*\Omega\|\\
&=\|T^*w^*a^*\Omega\|\\
&\le
\|T^*R_K\|\,\|w^*a^*\Omega\|
+\|(1-R_K)w^*a^*\Omega\|\\
&\le
\sqrt{|K|/(2n)}+\varepsilon < 2\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
By separability, apply this construction to increasing finite sets exhausting a countable dense subset of the unit ball of $A$, with $\varepsilon\to0$. The resulting contractions define an isometry $V\in B(H)^{\mathcal U}$ for a free ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on $\mathbb N$ satisfying the assumptions of Ozawa's criterion Theorem~\ref{T:Ozawa-isometry}. Thus $(A,\tau)$ is completely selfless.

\label{P:rr0}
Let $A$ be a simple unital C$^*$-algebra with stable rank one
and strict comparison. Suppose that $A$ has a tracial state

<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The trace $\tau$ is faithful by simplicity. Partition $X$ into

nonzero orthogonal projections summing to $1$, so one of them,
say $p_n$, satisfies $0<\tau(p_n)\le1/n$. Hence the subgroup
$\tau_*(K_0(A))\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ contains arbitrarily small positive
elements and is dense. Since $T(A)=\{\tau\}$, this says that
the range of $K_0(A)\to\operatorname{Aff}(T(A))$ is dense. Strict comparison is equivalent to almost unperforation of $W(A)$ in this setting; see \cite[Section 4]{Ror04}.
The proof of R{\o}rdam's real rank zero criterion
\cite[Proposition 7.1]{Ror04} therefore applies. Its exactness
hypothesis is used only to identify quasitraces with traces.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{L:factor}
Let $G\curvearrowright Y$ be a strictly ergodic action on the
Cantor set with dynamical comparison, and let $\nu$ be its
invariant Borel probability measure. Suppose that
$\pi:X\to Y$ is a factor map from a minimal Cantor action
$G\curvearrowright X$ and that
\[
\nu\bigl(\{y\in Y:|\pi^{-1}(y)|=1\}\bigr)=1.
\]
Then $G\curvearrowright X$ is strictly ergodic and has
dynamical comparison.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Write $Y_1=\{y\in Y:|\pi^{-1}(y)|=1\}$. This set is
$G$-invariant and Borel. Indeed, for any compatible metric on $X$,
\[
Y_1=\bigcap_{n\geq1}
\{y\in Y:\operatorname{diam}(\pi^{-1}(y))<1/n\},
\]
and the sets in this intersection are open. Let $s:Y_1\to X$
assign to each $y$ the unique point of $\pi^{-1}(y)$.
Compactness of $X$ implies that $s$ is continuous on $Y_1$,
and equivariance of $\pi$ implies that $s$ is equivariant.
Thus $\mu=s_*(\nu|_{Y_1})$ is an invariant probability measure
on $X$. Every invariant probability measure on $X$ pushes
forward to $\nu$ by unique ergodicity, and hence gives full
measure to $\pi^{-1}(Y_1)$, where $\pi$ is one-to-one. It must
therefore equal $\mu$. Since the action on $X$ is minimal by
assumption, this proves strict ergodicity.

We next prove comparison. For a clopen set $C\subseteq X$, put
\[
C^+=\pi(C)
=\{y\in Y:\pi^{-1}(y)\cap C\ne\emptyset\}
\]
and
\[
C^-=Y\setminus\pi(X\setminus C)
=\{y\in Y:\pi^{-1}(y)\subseteq C\}.
\]
The set $C^+$ is closed and $C^-$ is open, and
\begin{equation}\label{E:enclose}
\pi^{-1}(C^-)\subseteq C\subseteq\pi^{-1}(C^+).
\end{equation}
Every point of $C^+\setminus C^-$ has a fibre meeting both
$C$ and $X\setminus C$, so $C^+\setminus C^-$ is disjoint
from $Y_1$ and has $\nu$-measure zero. Since $\pi_*\mu=\nu$,
the inclusions in \eqref{E:enclose} imply
\[
\nu(C^-)=\mu(C)=\nu(C^+).
\]

Fix clopen sets $U,V\subseteq X$ with $\mu(U)<\mu(V)$, and
choose $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$2\varepsilon<\mu(V)-\mu(U)$. By regularity of $\nu$ and
zero-dimensionality of $Y$, there are clopen sets $A,B\subseteq Y$
such that $U^+\subseteq A$, $B\subseteq V^-$, and
\[
\nu(A)<\mu(U)+\varepsilon<\mu(V)-\varepsilon<\nu(B).
\]
In particular,
\[
U\subseteq\pi^{-1}(A),\qquad \pi^{-1}(B)\subseteq V.
\]
Comparison for $G\curvearrowright Y$ gives a finite clopen
partition $A=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^m A_j$ and elements $g_j\in G$
such that the sets $g_jA_j$ are pairwise disjoint subsets of $B$.
Set $U_j=U\cap\pi^{-1}(A_j)$. These sets partition $U$, and
equivariance gives
\[
g_jU_j\subseteq\pi^{-1}(g_jA_j)
\subseteq\pi^{-1}(B)\subseteq V.
\]
The images $g_jU_j$ are pairwise disjoint because the sets
$g_jA_j$ are pairwise disjoint. Discarding empty pieces gives
$U\prec_G V$.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{P:Toeplitz}
Every regular Toeplitz Cantor action is strictly ergodic and
has dynamical comparison. Moreover, every countably infinite residually finite group admits a free regular Toeplitz Cantor action.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $G\curvearrowright X$ be a regular Toeplitz Cantor action,
and let $\pi:X\to Y$ be its canonical subodometer factor.
The system $Y$ is strictly ergodic and has comparison.
It is also infinite: if $Y$ were finite, regularity would
force every fibre of $\pi$ to be a singleton, making $X$
finite. Thus $Y$ is a Cantor space, and Lemma~\ref{L:factor}
gives the first assertion.

For the second assertion, let $G$ be countably infinite and
residually finite, and choose a decreasing sequence $(G_n)$
of finite-index normal subgroups with
$\bigcap_n G_n=\{e\}$. By
\cite[Proposition~3.8]{CecCorGom24}, there exists a free
regular Toeplitz subshift $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^G$ with odometer
factor $\varprojlim G/G_n$. This subshift is infinite, hence
a Cantor system, and the first assertion gives strict
ergodicity and comparison.
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Proposition~\ref{P:Toeplitz} gives strict ergodicity and
dynamical comparison. Since $G$ has property PHP,
Theorem~\ref{T:PHP-main} applies.

<!-- rem-3: body_md -->
\label{R:new}
An infinite Toeplitz subshift is expansive and hence cannot
be equicontinuous. Moreover, the free examples in
Proposition~\ref{P:Toeplitz} can be chosen outside the scope
of Corollary~\ref{T:amenable}. To see this, let $G=F_d$,
where $d\geq2$, and choose a surjection
\[
q:F_d\twoheadrightarrow Q=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
Choose the residual normal chain $(N_n)$ used in
Proposition~\ref{P:Toeplitz} so that $N_1=\ker q$.
The resulting regular Toeplitz action factors onto its
odometer and hence onto the left translation action on $Q$.
Every amenable subgroup $H\leq F_d$ is cyclic or trivial,
so $q(H)$ has at most two elements and cannot act
transitively on $Q$. Since minimality passes to factors,
no amenable subgroup acts minimally on the Toeplitz system.
Thus these are free, strictly ergodic Cantor actions with
comparison to which Theorem~\ref{T:PHP-main} applies but
Corollary~\ref{T:amenable} does not.
