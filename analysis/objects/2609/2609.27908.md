<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
At physiological temperature the dominant decohering interactions in condensed phase are collisional, Coulombic and vibrational; blackbody photon absorption and emission on a molecular dipole is slower by more than twenty orders of magnitude once the long-wavelength suppression $(\Delta x/\lambda)^2$ is included \cite{Tegmark2000,Schlosshauer2019}. The bath here is therefore a generic thermal ancilla, not a radiation mode. The infrared enters this paper at exactly one point, in \S\ref{sec:physical}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:quadratic}
$A(p)$ and $c(p)$ are quadratic matrix polynomials in $p$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
$\Phi_p$ depends on $p$ only through the ancilla state, and depends on it linearly. Since
\[
  \tau_S(p)^{\otimes 2} \;=\; \tfrac14\Big(\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1} \;+\; p\,(S\otimes\mathbb{1} + \mathbb{1}\otimes S) \;+\; p^{2}\, S\otimes S\Big),
\]
the map $p \mapsto \Phi_p$ is a quadratic polynomial with operator coefficients, and so are its Bloch data:
\[
  A(p) = A_0 + p\,A_1 + p^2 A_2, \qquad c(p) = c_0 + p\,c_1 + p^2 c_2. \qedhere
\]

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:iso}
For channels $\Phi,\Phi':M_d\to M_d$,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\|J(\Phi)-J(\Phi')\bigr\|_F \;=\; \tfrac1d\,\|\Phi-\Phi'\|_{\mathrm{HS}} .
\end{equation}
For $d=2$ in Bloch form this reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iso2}
  \bigl\|J(\Phi)-J(\Phi')\bigr\|_F
  \;=\; \tfrac12\sqrt{\|A-A'\|_F^{2}+\|c-c'\|_2^{2}} .
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Put $\Delta=\Phi-\Phi'$. The blocks of
$J(\Phi)-J(\Phi')=\frac1d\sum_{ij}|i\rangle\langlej|\otimes\Delta(|i\rangle\langlej|)$
are mutually orthogonal, so
$\|J(\Phi)-J(\Phi')\|_F^2=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{ij}\|\Delta(|i\rangle\langlej|)\|_F^2$.
The matrix units $\{|i\rangle\langlej|\}$ form an orthonormal basis of $M_d$, so
that sum is $\|\Delta\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$. For $d=2$, in the orthonormal basis
$\{\mathbb{1}/\sqrt2,\sigma_1/\sqrt2,\sigma_2/\sqrt2,\sigma_3/\sqrt2\}$ a
trace-preserving map has superoperator matrix
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\ c&A\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$; hence $\Delta$ has
matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\ \Delta c&\Delta A\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
and $\|\Delta\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\|\Delta A\|_F^2+\|\Delta c\|_2^2$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:collapse}
Let $\Phi$ be a qubit channel with $\|A\|_2<1$. Then $\Phi^n$ has Bloch pair
$(A^n,c_n)$ with $c_n=(\mathbb{1}+A+\dots+A^{n-1})c$, and
\begin{equation}
  c_n-v^\ast \;=\; -\,A^{n}v^\ast .
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
$c_n=(\mathbb{1}-A)^{-1}(\mathbb{1}-A^n)c=(\mathbb{1}-A^n)(\mathbb{1}-A)^{-1}c
 =(\mathbb{1}-A^n)v^\ast$, the two factors commuting as functions of $A$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:decay}
Let $\Phi:M_d\to M_d$ be a channel with fixed state $\rho^\ast$ and $\eta<1$,
and let $\Phi_\infty(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)\,\rho^\ast$, so that
$J(\Phi_\infty)=\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}\otimes\rho^\ast$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decay}
  \bigl\|J(\Phi^n)-\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}\otimes\rho^\ast\bigr\|_F
  \;\le\; \frac{\eta^{n}}{d}\,
  \sqrt{\,d\,\bigl\|\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}-\rho^\ast\bigr\|_F^{2}+d^{2}-1\,}.
\end{equation}
For $d=2$ this is $\tfrac12 a^{n}\sqrt{r_\ast^{2}+3}$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Take the orthonormal basis $\{\mathbb{1}/\sqrt d\}\cup\{F_\alpha\}_{\alpha=1}^{d^2-1}$
of $M_d$ with every $F_\alpha$ traceless. Since $\Phi^n(\rho^\ast)=\rho^\ast$,
\begin{equation}
 (\Phi^n-\Phi_\infty)(\mathbb{1}/\sqrt d)=\sqrt d\,\bigl[\Phi^n(\mathbb{1}/d)-\rho^\ast\bigr]
 =\sqrt d\,\Phi^n\!\bigl(\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}-\rho^\ast\bigr),
\end{equation}
and $\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}-\rho^\ast$ is traceless, so this has norm at most
$\sqrt d\,\eta^{n}\|\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}-\rho^\ast\|_F$. On each traceless
$F_\alpha$, $(\Phi^n-\Phi_\infty)(F_\alpha)=\Phi^n(F_\alpha)$ has norm at most
$\eta^n$. Summing squares gives
$\|\Phi^n-\Phi_\infty\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le\eta^{2n}\bigl[d\|\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}-\rho^\ast\|_F^2+d^2-1\bigr]$,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:iso} supplies the factor $1/d$. For $d=2$,
$\|\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{2}-\rho^\ast\|_F=r_\ast/\sqrt2$ and $d^2-1=3$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:qubit}
Let $\Phi$ be a qubit channel with Bloch pair $(A,c)$, $a=\|A\|_2<1$, and
fixed state $\rho^\ast$ with Bloch vector $v^\ast$, $r_\ast=\|v^\ast\|<1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thm1}
  n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\Phi)\;\le\;
  \left\lceil
   \frac{\ln\!\bigl(2\sqrt{r_\ast^{2}+3}\,/\,(1-r_\ast)\bigr)}{\ln(1/a)}
  \right\rceil .
\end{equation}
More sharply,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thm1sharp}
  n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\Phi)\le\min\Bigl\{n:\
    \sqrt{\|A^{n}\|_F^{2}+\|A^{n}v^\ast\|^{2}}\le\tfrac{1-r_\ast}{2}\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
$J(\Phi_\infty)=\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{2}\otimes\rho^\ast$ is invariant under partial
transposition of the reference factor, and its spectrum is
$\{(1\pm r_\ast)/4\}$, so $\lambda_{\min}[J(\Phi_\infty)^{T_R}]=(1-r_\ast)/4>0$. Partial
transposition preserves $\|\cdot\|_F$, and $\|\cdot\|_\infty\le\|\cdot\|_F$, so
Weyl's inequality together with Lemma~\ref{lem:decay} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weylchain}
  \lambda_{\min}\bigl[J(\Phi^{n})^{T_R}\bigr]
  \;\ge\; \frac{1-r_\ast}{4}-\frac{a^{n}}{2}\sqrt{r_\ast^{2}+3}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is nonnegative as soon as
$a^{n}\le(1-r_\ast)\big/\bigl(2\sqrt{r_\ast^{2}+3}\bigr)$, which is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:thm1}. Then $\Phi^n$ is PPT, and since $d=2$ the
Peres--Horodecki criterion \cite{Peres96,Horodecki96} makes it entanglement
breaking. The sharper form follows by using $\|A^n\|_F$ and $\|A^nv^\ast\|$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:decay} instead of $a^n\sqrt{r_\ast^2+3}$, via
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:generald}
Let $\Phi:M_d\to M_d$ be a channel with $\eta<1$ and fixed state $\rho^\ast$ of
full rank, $p:=\lambda_{\min}(\rho^\ast)>0$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kd}
  K_d \;:=\; \frac{\sqrt{d^{2}-1}}{p\,d}\,
    \sqrt{\,d\bigl\|\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}-\rho^\ast\bigr\|_F^{2}+d^{2}-1\,}\;.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thm2}
  n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\Phi)\;\le\;
  \left\lceil \frac{\ln K_d}{\ln(1/\eta)} \right\rceil \;<\;\infty .
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Since $\rho^\ast\ge p\mathbb{1}$, the operator $\rho^\ast-p\mathbb{1}$ is positive
with trace $1-pd$, so $\rho^\ast=pd\,\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}+(1-pd)\,\omega$ for a
state $\omega$ (the case $pd=1$, i.e. $\rho^\ast=\mathbb{1}/d$, is immediate).
Hence, with $D=d^{2}$,
\begin{equation}
  J(\Phi_\infty)=\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}\otimes\rho^\ast
  = pd\,\frac{\mathbb{1}}{D}+(1-pd)\,\Bigl(\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}\otimes\omega\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the second term separable. Let $X$ be any state and
$\Delta=X-J(\Phi_\infty)$, so $\operatorname{Tr}\Delta=0$ and
\begin{equation}
  X = pd\Bigl[\frac{\mathbb{1}}{D}+\frac{\Delta}{pd}\Bigr]
      +(1-pd)\Bigl(\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{d}\otimes\omega\Bigr).
\end{equation}
By the Gurvits--Barnum separable-ball theorem \cite{GurvitsBarnum02}, every
state within Frobenius distance $1/\sqrt{D(D-1)}$ of $\mathbb{1}/D$ is separable;
so if $\|\Delta\|_F\le pd/\sqrt{D(D-1)}=p/\sqrt{d^{2}-1}$ then the bracket is a
separable state and $X$, a convex combination of separable states, is separable.
Applying this to $X=J(\Phi^{n})$ and inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:decay} gives
$\eta^{n}\le 1/K_d$, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:thm2}.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:unital}
A unital qubit channel is entanglement breaking if and only if
$\|A\|_1\le1$. Consequently
$n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\Phi)=\min\{n\ge1:\|A^{n}\|_1\le1\}$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Rotations of the input and output Bloch balls are unitary conjugations and
preserve EB, so $A$ may be taken diagonal, $A=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$
with $|\lambda_i|$ its singular values \cite{RSW02}. The four eigenvalues of
$J(\Phi)^{T_R}$ are $\tfrac14(1\pm\lambda_1\pm\lambda_2\pm\lambda_3)$ with an
\emph{odd} number of minus signs; an even number gives the spectrum of
$J(\Phi)$ itself, i.e. the complete-positivity condition. Each parity class
cuts out a tetrahedron in $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$, and the two
tetrahedra intersect in the octahedron $\sum_i|\lambda_i|\le1$. Complete
positivity is therefore essential: $(\lambda_i)=(0.9,0.9,-0.9)$ satisfies all
four partial-transpose inequalities while $\|A\|_1=2.7$. Imposing both,
$\Phi$ is PPT iff $\|A\|_1\le1$, and at $d=2$ PPT is EB.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:tight}

two differ, before rounding, by the constant factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tightconst}
  \frac{\ln\bigl(2\sqrt3\bigr)}{\ln 3}\;=\;1.1309\ldots
\end{equation}

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:clock}
As $a\to1^-$, $\ln(1/a)=(1-a)\bigl(1+O(1-a)\bigr)$, so
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\Phi)\;\le\;
  \frac{\ln\bigl(2\sqrt{r_\ast^2+3}/(1-r_\ast)\bigr)}{1-\|A\|_2}\,\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For a \emph{unital} channel, Proposition~\ref{prop:unital} gives
$n_{\mathrm{EB}}=\min\{n:\|A^n\|_1\le1\}$ exactly, so for normal $A$ with
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}=\#\{i:s_i=s_1\}$ the elementary bound $\|A^n\|_1\ge
k_{\mathrm{eff}}s_1^{\,n}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\Phi)\;\ge\;\frac{\ln k_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\ln(1/s_1)}
  \;=\;\frac{\ln k_{\mathrm{eff}}}{1-s_1}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),\qquad s_1\to1^- .
\end{equation}
The number of rounds a loop remains entangling and the number of rounds it
remembers its input are therefore the same quantity, up to a factor depending
only on $r_\ast$ and on how many Bloch axes contract at comparable rates.

\label{thm:floor}
For every traceless Hermitian involution $S$, the channel $\Phi_0$ is independent of $S$. Consequently $n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\Phi_0)$, and indeed every functional of $\Phi_0$, is determined by the interaction unitary $U$ alone.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:normalform}
Let $U$ be as in \eqref{eq:U}, with independent branch angles $a_0,a_1$. Then
\[
  \Phi_0 \;=\; \Lambda_{\cos^2\varphi}\;\circ\;\mathrm{Ad}_{R_y(\beta)}\;\circ\;\Delta_\delta,
  \qquad \delta \;=\; \cos\!\big(\tfrac{a_0-a_1}{2}\big),
\]
where $\Delta_\delta$ is the dephasing channel with Bloch matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\delta,\delta,1)$ and $\Lambda_a$ is the isotropic contraction $r\mapsto ar$. Equivalently, in the Bloch basis $(x,y,z)$,
\[
  A_0 \;=\; \cos^2\!\varphi\;O_y(\beta)\,\operatorname{diag}(\delta,\delta,1), \qquad c_0 = 0 .
\]
In particular $\Phi_0$ is unital, and the coupling $\kappa$ does not appear. For the supplementary construction $\delta = \sin 2\theta$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Write $c = \cos\varphi$, $s = \sin\varphi$, $S = \mathrm{SWAP}_{ML}$, $V_m = R_y(a_m)$, and $W = \exp(-i\tfrac\kappa2 Z\otimes Y)$ on $FL$, so $U_w = \mathbb{1}_M\otimes W$.

$R_y(\beta)_M$ commutes with $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}$, so $\Phi_0 = \mathrm{Ad}_{R_y(\beta)}\circ\tilde\Phi_0$ with $\tilde\Phi_0$ built from $U' = U_fU_wU_W$; on Bloch data the conjugation is $O_y(\beta)$. Put $X := U_wU_W(\rho\otimes\tfrac{\mathbb{1}_4}{4})U_W^\dagger U_w^\dagger$. Since $U_f = c\mathbb{1} - isS$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
  U'(\rho\otimes\tfrac{\mathbb{1}_4}{4})U'^\dagger \;=\; c^2X + s^2SXS + ics\,[X,S].
\end{equation}

\emph{(i) The first term is a dephasing channel, and $\kappa$ cancels.} The partial trace over $FL$ is invariant under unitaries supported on $FL$, so the $W$'s drop out. With
$U_W(\rho\otimes\tfrac{\mathbb{1}_4}{4})U_W^\dagger = \tfrac14\sum_{m,m'}\rho_{mm'}|m\rangle\!\langle m'|\otimes V_mV_{m'}^\dagger\otimes\mathbb{1}_L$,
tracing out $F$ and $L$ multiplies the $(m,m')$ entry by $\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}[V_mV_{m'}^\dagger]$, which is $1$ on the diagonal and
\[
  \tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}[V_0V_1^\dagger] \;=\; \tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\big[R_y(a_0-a_1)\big] \;=\; \cos\!\big(\tfrac{a_0-a_1}{2}\big) \;=\; \delta
\]
off it. Hence $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[X] = \Delta_\delta(\rho)$.

\emph{(ii) The swapped term is constant.} $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[SXS]$ is the $L$-marginal of $X$ transplanted onto $M$. Since $\operatorname{Tr}_M[U_W(\rho\otimes\tfrac{\mathbb{1}_4}{4})U_W^\dagger] = \tfrac14\sum_m\rho_{mm}V_mV_m^\dagger\otimes\mathbb{1}_L = \tfrac14\mathbb{1}_F\otimes\mathbb{1}_L$, conjugating by the unitary $W$ and tracing out $F$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[SXS] = \tfrac12\mathbb{1}$, independent of $\rho$: this term contributes to the affine part only.

\emph{(iii) The cross term vanishes identically.} For $B$ on $F$ put $\mathcal G(B) := W(B\otimes\mathbb{1}_L)W^\dagger$. Using $(Z\otimes Y)^2 = \mathbb{1}$,
\[
  \mathcal G(B) = \cos^2\!\tfrac\kappa2\,B\otimes\mathbb{1} + \sin^2\!\tfrac\kappa2\,ZBZ\otimes\mathbb{1} + i\cos\tfrac\kappa2\sin\tfrac\kappa2\,(BZ-ZB)\otimes Y,
\]
and since $\operatorname{Tr}[ZBZ] = \operatorname{Tr}[B]$ while $\operatorname{Tr}[BZ-ZB] = 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gtrace}
  \operatorname{Tr}_F\big[\mathcal G(B)\big] = \operatorname{Tr}[B]\,\mathbb{1}_L \qquad\text{for every }B.
\end{equation}
Writing $X = \tfrac14\sum_{m,m'}\rho_{mm'}|m\rangle\!\langle m'|\otimes\mathcal G(V_mV_{m'}^\dagger)$ and using $S|j,f,l\rangle = |l,f,j\rangle$, identity \eqref{eq:Gtrace} gives
\[
  \big(\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[XS]\big)_{ij} = \tfrac14\rho_{ij}\operatorname{Tr}[V_iV_j^\dagger] = \big(\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[SX]\big)_{ij},
\]
so $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[[X,S]] = 0$ identically.

Applying $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}$ to \eqref{eq:split} therefore gives $\tilde\Phi_0(\rho) = c^2\Delta_\delta(\rho) + s^2\tfrac{\mathbb{1}}{2}$, which is unital, so $\tilde c_0 = 0$ and $\tilde A_0 = c^2\operatorname{diag}(\delta,\delta,1)$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:gadc}
For damping $0<\gamma<1$ and polarisation $0\le p<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadcclosed}
  n_{\mathrm{EB}}(\gamma,p) \;=\; \left\lceil \frac{\ln x_-(p)}{\ln(1-\gamma)} \right\rceil,
  \qquad x_-(p) \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{2-p^2}-1}{\sqrt{2-p^2}+1},
\end{equation}
exactly, including the case in which the ratio is an integer. At $p=1$ the
index is infinite.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The parameter region in which a single GADC is entanglement breaking was
determined in \cite{KSW2020} (their noise parameter is $(1-p)/2$ in the present
notation). The $n$-fold composite is again a GADC, with damping
$1-(1-\gamma)^n$ and the same $p$, so the index follows from that region by
composition; we give the derivation for completeness. In Bloch form the
composite has $A_n=\operatorname{diag}(x^{1/2},x^{1/2},x)$ and $c_n=(0,0,(1-x)p)$ with
$x=(1-\gamma)^n$. For a Bloch-diagonal channel $\operatorname{diag}(a,a,b)$ with shift
$(0,0,c)$ the partial-transposed Choi state has spectrum
\[
  \tfrac14\big\{\,1+b+c,\;\; 1+b-c,\;\; (1-b)+\sqrt{c^2+4a^2},\;\;
  (1-b)-\sqrt{c^2+4a^2}\,\big\},
\]
and the first pair is nonnegative because $|c|=(1-b)p\le1-b\le1+b$. Here
$a^2=x=b$, so PPT, which for qubits is equivalent to entanglement breaking
\cite{HSR2003,Peres96,Horodecki96}, reduces to $(1-x)^2(1-p^2)\ge4x$. With
$q=1-p^2$ this reads $qx^2-(2q+4)x+q\ge0$, whose admissible root is
$x_-=(q+2-2\sqrt{q+1})/q=(\sqrt{q+1}-1)^2/q=(\sqrt{2-p^2}-1)/(\sqrt{2-p^2}+1)$.
Since $x$ decreases strictly in $n$ and PPT is a closed condition, the smallest
qualifying $n$ is the ceiling in \eqref{eq:gadcclosed}. At $p=1$ the condition
reads $0\ge4x$, which fails at every finite $n$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:structuralzero}
For every choice of the remaining parameters and every bath polarisation $p$,
\[
  A_{yz} \;=\; 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \cos a_0 + \cos a_1 = 0,
\]
in particular whenever $a_0 + a_1 = \pi$. The transposed element $A_{zy}$ does not vanish.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Write $c = \cos(\kappa/2)$, $s = \sin(\kappa/2)$, and let $S = \mathrm{SWAP}_{ML}$, $R = R_y(\beta)\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}$, $V = U_wU_W$.

\emph{(1) Reality.} Since $-iY$ is real, $R_y(\cdot)$ is real orthogonal; and since $-i(Z\otimes Y)$ is real, so is $U_w$. Hence $R$, $U_W$, $V$ are real. The sole non-real factor is $U_f = \cos\varphi\,\mathbb{1} - i\sin\varphi\,S$, giving $U = P_1 - iP_2$ with $P_1 = \cos\varphi\,RV$ and $P_2 = \sin\varphi\,RSV$ both real.

\emph{(2) Isolating the $y$-component.} For $2\times2$ Hermitian $G$ one has $\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma_yG] = -2\,\mathrm{Im}\,G_{01}$, so $A_{yz}$ is carried entirely by the imaginary part of the reduced output. With $\rho = \sigma_z$ and ancilla $\alpha = \tau_S(p)^{\otimes2}$ both real symmetric,
\[
  U(\rho\otimes\alpha)U^{\dagger} = \big[P_1(\rho\otimes\alpha)P_1^{\mathsf T} + P_2(\rho\otimes\alpha)P_2^{\mathsf T}\big] + i\,\big[M - M^{\mathsf T}\big],
  \quad M = P_1(\rho\otimes\alpha)P_2^{\mathsf T},
\]
and $M - M^{\mathsf T} = \cos\varphi\sin\varphi\;R\,[W,S]\,R^{\mathsf T}$ with $W = V(\sigma_z\otimes\alpha)V^{\mathsf T}$.

\emph{(3) $R$ is harmless.} $R$ acts only on $M$, so $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[RXR^{\mathsf T}] = R_y(\beta)\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[X]R_y(\beta)^{\mathsf T}$, and $R_y(\beta)$ is a rotation about $y$, preserving the $y$-component. The claim reduces to $\operatorname{Tr}_{FL}[[W,S]] = 0$.

\emph{(4) $W$ is block diagonal in $M$.} $\sigma_z$ is diagonal in the control basis; $U_W$ acts on $M$ only through that control; $U_w$ acts trivially on $M$. Hence, with $D_m = C\,(R_y(a_m)\otimes\mathbb{1})$ and $C = \exp(-i\tfrac\kappa2 Z\otimes Y)$,
\[
  W \;=\; \sum_{m} (-1)^m\, |m\rangle\!\langle m| \otimes G_m, \qquad G_m = D_m\,\alpha\,D_m^{\mathsf T} \ \text{ real symmetric}.
\]
(This is the step that fails for $\rho = \sigma_y$, which is not diagonal in the control basis, hence $A_{zy} \neq 0$.)

\emph{(5) Tracing the commutator.} Using $S|m,f,l\rangle = |l,f,m\rangle$ and block diagonality, the resulting $2\times2$ matrix is antisymmetric with single entry $a = [\operatorname{Tr}_F(G_0 + G_1)]_{01}$.

\emph{(6) Evaluating the $L$-marginal.} With $\tau_m = R_y(a_m)\,\tau_S(p)\,R_y(a_m)^{\mathsf T}$ and $z_m = \operatorname{Tr}[\tau_m Z]$, conjugation by $C$ gives
\[
  \operatorname{Tr}_F[G_m] \;=\; \tfrac12\mathbb{1} \;+\; \tfrac{p}{2}\,(c^2-s^2)\,Z \;+\; cs\,p\,z_m\,X,
\]
so $a \propto cs\,p\,(z_0+z_1)$. Since $\tau_S(p)$ has Bloch vector $(0,0,p)$ and $R_y(a)$ rotates it by $a$ about $y$, $z_m = p\cos a_m$. Therefore $a \propto cs\,p^2\,(\cos a_0 + \cos a_1)$, which vanishes precisely when $\cos a_0 + \cos a_1 = 0$. For the supplementary construction $\cos(\pi-2\theta) + \cos 2\theta = 0$ identically.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:boundary}
It is natural to try to certify the identity over a \emph{box} and so obtain a continuum statement. This does not work, for a structural reason worth recording. Evaluating $A_0$ and its predicted form over a box in $(\theta,\varphi,\beta)$ of half-width $10^{-3}$ gives a difference enclosing zero with radius $5\times10^{-3}$: consistent with the identity, but equally consistent with the identity failing by anything smaller. Taking $\kappa$ over the whole interval $[0,\pi]$ inflates the enclosure to radius $1.57$ and certifies nothing at all, even though $\kappa$ provably does not occur in $A_0$: ball arithmetic evaluates $\kappa$ independently at each of its several occurrences and cannot see that their contributions cancel. \textbf{Interval arithmetic certifies signs and values, not identities.} The continuum content of Theorem~\ref{thm:normalform} is carried by its proof; the ball-arithmetic confirmations are pointwise, at $10^{-76}$.
