<!-- introques-1: body_md -->
Which of these properties are intrinsic to the large-scale geometry of the interior of a hyperbolic space, and which depend on the particular exponential scaling of a visual metric of its visual boundary?
<!-- end introques-1 -->
<!-- introthm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:A}
Let $X$ be a proper geodesic hyperbolic space with basepoint ${\mathfrak o}$. There
exists a constant $C\ge  1$ such that, for all distinct
$\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\partial_\infty X\cong\partial_\kappa X$,
\[
    (\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}
    \le 
    r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
    \le 
    (\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}
    +
    C\kappa\bigl((\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\bigr)
    +
    C.
\]
Consequently, $1+r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})\asymp 1+(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}$
and every logarithmic metric with admissible exponent $\theta>0$ satisfies
\[
    d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
    \asymp
    \frac{1}
    {\bigl(1+(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\bigr)^\theta}.
\]
<!-- end introthm-1 -->
<!-- introthm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:B}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be proper geodesic hyperbolic spaces, and let
$\Phi:X\to Y$ be a quasi-isometry. Fix ${\mathfrak o}\in X$ and let ${\mathfrak o}' \in Y$. If the same admissible exponent $\theta>0$ is used on both
logarithmic boundaries, then the induced map
\[
\Phi_*:
(\partial_\kappa X,d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta})
\longrightarrow
(\partial_\kappa Y,d^Y_{{\mathfrak o}',\theta})
\]
is bi-Lipschitz.
<!-- end introthm-2 -->
<!-- introcor-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:D}
Let $G$ be a word-hyperbolic group, and let $H\le  G$ be a finitely generated
word-hyperbolic subgroup. Suppose that the inclusion $i:H\hookrightarrow G$
admits a Cannon-Thurston map
$\partial i:\partial H\longrightarrow \partial G.$
Let $\theta>0$ be a common admissible exponent for the log metrics on
$\partial H$ and $\partial G$. Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $H$ is quasiconvex in $G$.
    \item The Cannon-Thurston map
    $
    \partial i:
    (\partial H,d^H_{e,\theta})
    \longrightarrow
    (\partial G,d^G_{e,\theta})
    $
    is bi-Lipschitz onto its image where the boundaries are equipped with the log metric.
    \item The Cannon-Thurston map $\partial i:\partial H\to\partial G$ is
    injective.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end introcor-1 -->
<!-- introthm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:E}
Let $G$ be a non-elementary word-hyperbolic group. Then the following hold. 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The logarithmic boundary has infinite Hausdorff and Assouad
    dimensions:
    \[
    \dim_H(\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}})
    =
    \dim_A(\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}})
    =
    \infty.
    \]
    In particular, it is non-doubling and is not quasisymmetrically equivalent
    to the boundary equipped with any visual metric.
    \item Every nondegenerate connected subset
    $K\subseteq\partial_\kappa G$ satisfies
    \[
    \dim_H(K,d_{\mathrm{log}})=\infty.
    \]
    Consequently, the logarithmic boundary contains no nonconstant
    rectifiable curves. In particular, every Lipschitz map from an interval into $(\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is constant.

    \item Its Nagata dimension remains equal to the topological dimension:
    \[
    \dim_N(\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}})
    = \dim_N(\partial_\infty G,d_{\mathrm{vis}})
    = 
    \dim(\partial G).
    \]
    As a consequence,
    \[
    \operatorname{asdim}G
    =
    \dim_N(\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}})+1.
    \]

    \item The logarithmic boundary is uniformly perfect. 
    \item $
    (\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}})
    \text{ is linearly connected}
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    G\text{ is one-ended}.
    $
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end introthm-3 -->
<!-- introcor-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:F}
Let $G$ be a non-elementary hyperbolic group and let
$n=\dim \partial G$. For every sufficiently small $p\in(0,1)$, there
exist metric trees $T_0,\ldots,T_n$ and a bi-Lipschitz embedding
$
(\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}}^p)
\longrightarrow
T_0\times\cdots\times T_n.
$

On the other hand, for every $p\in(0,1]$, the space $(\partial_\kappa G,d_{\mathrm{log}}^p)$ admits no bi-Lipschitz embedding
into any finite-dimensional Euclidean space.
<!-- end introcor-2 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$ be metric spaces. For constants ${\sf k}\ge  1$ and
${\sf K}\ge  0$, a map $f\colon\thinspace X\to Y$ is called a
$({\sf k},{\sf K})$-\emph{quasi-isometric embedding} if, for all $x_1,x_2\in X$,
$$
\frac{1}{{\sf k}}d_X(x_1,x_2)-{\sf K}
\le 
d_Y(f(x_1),f(x_2))
\le 
{\sf k} d_X(x_1,x_2)+{\sf K}.
$$
If, in addition, every point of $Y$ lies in the ${\sf K}$-neighbourhood of $f(X)$,
then $f$ is called a $({\sf k},{\sf K})$-\emph{quasi-isometry}.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{Def:Quasi-Geodesic}
Let ${\sf q}\geq 1$ and ${\sf Q}\geq 0$. A $({\sf q},{\sf Q})$-\emph{quasi-geodesic} in $X$ is a continuous map $\beta:I\to X$, where $I\subseteq\mathbb R$ is a 
interval, such that
\[
\frac{1}{{\sf q}}|s-t|-{\sf Q}
\leq d\bigl(\beta(s),\beta(t)\bigr)
\leq {\sf q}|s-t|+{\sf Q}
\]
for all $s,t\in I$.
A quasi-geodesic is a map that is a $(q,Q)$-quasi-geodesic for some
$q\geq1$ and $Q\geq0$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{Lem:QI-images-of-quasigeodesics}
Let $I\subseteq \mathbb R$ be an interval, and let
$\beta\colon\thinspace I\to X$ be a $({\sf q},{\sf Q})$-quasi-geodesic. Let
$\Phi\colon\thinspace X\to Y$ be a $({\sf k},{\sf K})$-quasi-isometry. Then
$\Phi\circ\beta\colon\thinspace I\to Y$ is a $({\sf k}{\sf q},{\sf k}{\sf Q}+{\sf K})$-quasi-isometric
embedding.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- convention-1: body_md -->
Unless otherwise stated, all quasi-geodesic rays in $X$ are assumed to start at ${\mathfrak o}$.
If $\beta\colon\thinspace I\to X$ is a path, we will often use the same symbol $\beta$ for
its image $\beta(I)\subseteq X$. Thus, for $x\in X$, we write
\[
d(x,\beta):=\inf\{d(x,\beta(t)):t\in I\}.
\]
Similarly, if $\beta_1\colon\thinspace I_1\to X$ and $\beta_2\colon\thinspace I_2\to X$ are two paths,
then $d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(\beta_1,\beta_2)$ denotes the Hausdorff distance between
their images. More precisely,
\[
d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(\beta_1,\beta_2)
:=
\max\left\{
\sup_{x\in \beta_1} d(x,\beta_2),
\sup_{y\in \beta_2} d(y,\beta_1)
\right\}.
\]
<!-- end convention-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $\delta\ge  0$. A metric space $X$ is called
$\delta$-\emph{hyperbolic} if for all $x,y,z,p\in X$,
\[
(x\mid z)_p
\ge 
\min\{(x\mid y)_p,(y\mid z)_p\}-\delta.
\]
We say that $X$ is \emph{Gromov hyperbolic} if it is
$\delta$-hyperbolic for some $\delta\ge  0$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Two quasi-geodesic rays $\beta_1,\beta_2\in\mathcal{Q}_{{\mathfrak o}}(X)$ are equivalent
if their images have finite Hausdorff distance, that is, $d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(\beta_1,\beta_2)<\infty$.
The \emph{visual boundary} of $X$, denoted $\partial_\infty X$, is the set of equivalence
classes of quasi-geodesic rays based at ${\mathfrak o}$:
\[
\partial_\infty X:=\mathcal{Q}_{{\mathfrak o}}(X)/{\sim}.
\]
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{Def:boundary-gromov-product}
Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial_\infty X$. The \emph{Gromov product} of $\textbf{a}$ and $\textbf{b}$ with
respect to ${\mathfrak o}$ is defined by
\[
    (\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}
    :=
    \sup
    \,
    \liminf_{i,j\to\infty} (x_i\mid y_j)_{{\mathfrak o}}
\]
where the supremum is taken over all sequences $(x_i)$ and $(y_j)$ in $X$
converging to $\textbf{a}$ and $\textbf{b}$, respectively. We use the convention
$
(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{a})_{{\mathfrak o}}=\infty
$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:QI-Gromov-product}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic spaces, and let
$\Phi\colon\thinspace X\to Y$ be a $({\sf k},{\sf K})$-quasi-isometric embedding.
Fix ${\mathfrak o}\in X$ and let ${\mathfrak o}'=\Phi({\mathfrak o})$. Then $\Phi$ induces a topological embedding $
\Phi_*\colon\thinspace\partial_\infty X\longrightarrow\partial_\infty Y$.

Moreover, there exist constants $A\ge 1$ and $B\ge 0$, depending only on
${\sf k},{\sf K}$ and the hyperbolicity constants of $X$ and $Y$, such that for
all distinct $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\partial_\infty X$,
\[
\frac1A(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}-B
\le 
\bigl(\Phi_*(\textbf{a})\mid\Phi_*(\textbf{b})\bigr)_{{\mathfrak o}'}
\le 
A(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}+B.
\]
If $\Phi$ is a quasi-isometry, then $\Phi_*$ is surjective and hence is
a homeomorphism.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:kappa}
A function $\kappa\colon\thinspace [0,\infty)\to [1,\infty)$ is called \emph{sublinear} if
it is increasing, concave, and
\[
\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\kappa(r)}{r}=0.
\]
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{Lem:kappa_basic}
Let $\kappa$ be a sublinear function. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For every $\lambda\ge  1$ and $t\ge  0$,
    $\kappa(\lambda t)\le  \lambda \kappa(t)$.
    \item For all $s,t\ge  0$, $\kappa(s+t)\le  \kappa(s)+\kappa(t)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We use concavity in the form $\kappa(\theta u+(1-\theta)0)
    \ge 
    \theta\kappa(u)+(1-\theta)\kappa(0)$ for $u\ge  0$ and $\theta\in[0,1]$.
    
    For the first claim, take $u=\lambda t$ and $\theta=1/\lambda$. For the second claim, the case $s=t=0$ is immediate. Otherwise, let $u=s+t$. Applying the same concavity inequality with $\theta=s/(s+t)$ and then
    with $\theta=t/(s+t)$ gives
    $$
    \kappa(s)
    \ge 
    \frac{s}{s+t}\kappa(s+t)
    +
    \frac{t}{s+t}\kappa(0), \,\,\text{ and }\,\, \kappa(t)
    \ge 
    \frac{t}{s+t}\kappa(s+t)
    +
    \frac{s}{s+t}\kappa(0).
    $$
    Adding the two inequalities yields the claim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{Def:Neighborhood} 
For a closed set $Z$ and a constant ${\sf n}$ define the $(\kappa, {\sf n})$-neighbourhood 
of $Z$ to be 
\[
\mathcal{N}_\kappa(Z, {\sf n}) = \Big\{ x \in X \mathbin{\Big|} 
  d_X(x, Z) \le   {\sf n} \cdot \kappa(x)  \Big\}.
\]
Equivalently, a subset $A\subseteq X$ is contained in an $({\sf n}\kappa)$-neighborhood of $Z$ if $d_X(x,Z)\le  {\sf n}\kappa(\|x\|)$ for every $x\in A$.
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{Def:Morse}
    Let $Z\subseteq X$ be a closed subset, and let $\kappa$ be a concave sublinear
    function. We say that $Z$ is \emph{$\kappa$-Morse} if there exists a proper
    function
    $
    m_Z\colon\thinspace [1,\infty)\times [0,\infty)\to [0,\infty)
    $
    such that the following holds.
    
    For every $({\sf q},{\sf Q})$-quasi-geodesic segment
    $\beta\colon\thinspace [s,t]\to X$ with endpoints on $Z$, we have
    $$
    \beta([s,t])
    \subseteq
    \mathcal{N}_{\kappa}\bigl(Z,m_Z({\sf q},{\sf Q})\bigr).
    $$
    The function $m_Z$ is called a \emph{$\kappa$-Morse gauge} for $Z$.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{Lem:uniform-kappa-morse}
    Every geodesic ray
    in $X$ is $\kappa$-Morse. Moreover, the $\kappa$-Morse gauge can be chosen
    uniformly: for every ${\sf q}\ge  1$ and ${\sf Q}\ge  0$, there exists a constant
    $M=M(\delta,{\sf q},{\sf Q})$ such that if $\alpha$ is any geodesic ray and
    $\beta$ is a $({\sf q},{\sf Q})$-quasi-geodesic segment with endpoints on $\alpha$,
    then
    $$
    \beta\subseteq N_M(\alpha)\subseteq \mathcal{N}_{\kappa}(\alpha,M),
    $$ where $N_M(\alpha)$ is the ordinary $M$-neighborhood of $\alpha$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
This is the Morse lemma for quasi-geodesics in hyperbolic spaces; see
    \cite[Theorem~III.H.1.7]{book:BridsonHaefliger}. Since $\kappa\ge  1$, the
    ordinary $M$-neighborhood $N_M(\alpha)$ is contained in the
    $M\kappa$-neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_{\kappa}(\alpha,M)$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{Rem:uniform-morse-gauge}
    By the Morse lemma for quasi-geodesics in hyperbolic spaces
    \cite[Theorem~III.H.1.7]{book:BridsonHaefliger}, there is a constant
    $H=H(\delta,{\sf q},{\sf Q})$ such that every $({\sf q},{\sf Q})$-quasi-geodesic segment with
    endpoints on a geodesic $\alpha$ lies in the ordinary
    $H$-neighborhood of $\alpha$. In particular, we may fix the uniform gauge 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform_gauge}  
    {\sf M}({\sf q},{\sf Q}):=\max\{{\sf q},{\sf Q},H(\delta,3{\sf q},3{\sf Q}), 3H(\delta,{\sf q},{\sf Q})+30\delta\}
    \end{equation}
    where the choice of ${\sf q},{\sf Q}$ is due to standard normalization in \cite{QRT2},  $H(\delta,3{\sf q},3{\sf Q})$ provides the strong $\kappa$-Morse gauge via the weak-to-strong equivalence as in \cite[Proposition~3.10]{QRT2}, and $3H(\delta,{\sf q},{\sf Q})+30\delta$ provides the estimates needed later.
    
    Throughout the paper, every geodesic ray used as the center of a boundary neighborhood is equipped with this common strong gauge ${\sf M}$. In particular, this is the gauge used in the definitions of $\mathcal{U}_\kappa$ and of the fellow-traveling radius $r$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
We caution the reader that the terms \emph{sublinear Morse gauge} and \emph{metric conformal gauge} refer to distinct notions. For the definition of conformal gauge, see Definition~\ref{def:conf_gauge}.

<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{Def:kappa-boundary}
Let $\mathcal{M}^{\kappa}_{\mathfrak o}(X)$ be the collection of $\kappa$-Morse quasigeodesic rays based at ${\mathfrak o}$. Two rays $\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{M}^{\kappa}_{\mathfrak o}(X)$ are equivalent (denoted $\alpha\sim\beta$) if they $\kappa$-fellow travel, that is, 
   if \[ \lim_{r\to\infty} \frac{d_X(\alpha_r,\beta_r)}{r} =0. \] 
The \emph{$\kappa$-Morse boundary}, is
$$
\partial_\kappa X:=\mathcal{M}^{\kappa}_{\mathfrak o}(X)/{\sim}.
$$
<!-- end definition-9 -->
<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
If $\kappa\le  C\kappa'$ for some $C\ge  1$, there is a natural
inclusion $
\partial_\kappa X\longrightarrow\partial_{\kappa'}X$.
The family is directed, since $\kappa+\kappa'$ dominates both $\kappa$
and $\kappa'$. We define the \emph{full sublinear Morse boundary} by
\[
\partial_{\mathrm{sl}} X:=\bigcup_{\kappa}\partial_\kappa X,
\]
equipped with the topology of \cite{QRT2}, for which every
$\partial_\kappa X$ is a topological subspace.

<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
\label{Def:kappa-fellow-traveling-radius}
Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\partial_\kappa X$. Let $\alpha_0\in\textbf{a}$ and $\beta_0\in\textbf{b}$ be
geodesic representatives based at ${\mathfrak o}$. Define
\begin{align*}
    R_{\beta_0}(\textbf{a})
    &:=
    \sup\{r>0:\textbf{a}\in \mathcal{U}_\kappa(\beta_0,r)\},
\text{ and }\\  
R_{\alpha_0}(\textbf{b})
    &:=
    \sup\{r>0:\textbf{b}\in \mathcal{U}_\kappa(\alpha_0,r)\}.
\end{align*}
We use the convention that the supremum of the empty set is $0$. For $\textbf{a}\neq\textbf{b}$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def_r}  
r(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
:=
\min\{  R_{\beta_0}(\textbf{a}),R_{\alpha_0}(\textbf{b})\}.
\end{equation}

For $\textbf{a}=\textbf{b}$, define $r(\textbf{a},\textbf{a}):=\infty$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:visual-kappa-homeomorphism}
Let $X$ be a proper geodesic $\delta$-hyperbolic metric space with basepoint
${\mathfrak o}$, and let $\kappa \ge 1$ be a sublinear function. Then the natural map
\[
\iota\colon\thinspace \partial_\infty X\longrightarrow \partial_\kappa X
\]
defined by $\iota(\textbf{a})=[\alpha]_{\kappa}$, where $\alpha$ is any geodesic ray based at ${\mathfrak o}$ representing $\textbf{a}$, is a
well-defined homeomorphism.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:hyperbolic-boundary-stabilizes}
Let $X$ be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic space. For every
sublinear function $\kappa\ge  1$, the natural identifications give
\[
\partial_{\mathbf 1}X
=
\partial_\kappa X
=
\partial_{\mathrm{sl}} X
=
\partial_\infty X
\]
canonically as topological spaces, where $\mathbf 1(t)\equiv 1$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Every geodesic ray in $X$ is uniformly Morse, and hence is
$\mathbf 1$-Morse and $\kappa$-Morse for every $\kappa\ge  1$.
Conversely, every point of $\partial_\kappa X$ has a geodesic
representative by Lemma~\ref{lem:qrt242}. The equivalence relation on
rays is sublinear tracking and is independent of $\kappa$. Therefore
all the boundaries have the same underlying set. The assertion about
their topologies follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:visual-kappa-homeomorphism}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:identification_vis_sublinear}

We denote this common underlying boundary by $\partial X$. Thus, the same symbol $\textbf{a}$ may represent either a visual boundary point or the corresponding sublinear Morse boundary point; the metric under consideration will always be specified explicitly.

\label{prop:log-quasimetric-from-gjq}
Let $\lambda>1$. The function $\rho_{{\mathfrak o},\lambda}$ is a quasi-metric on
$\partial_\kappa X$. More precisely, there exists a constant $K=2\,\lambda\ge  1$ such that
for all $\textbf{a},\textbf{b},\textbf{c}\in\partial_\kappa X$,
\[
\rho_{{\mathfrak o},\lambda}(\textbf{a},\textbf{c})
\le 
K\max\{
\rho_{{\mathfrak o},\lambda}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}),
\rho_{{\mathfrak o},\lambda}(\textbf{b},\textbf{c})
\}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\rho_{{\mathfrak o},\lambda}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
\asymp
\frac{1}{1+r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})}
\]
for all $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\partial_\kappa X$, with constants depending only on $\lambda$.

\label{thm:log-metric-background}

Let $Z$ be a set and let $d$ be a metric on $Z$. The \emph{conformal gauge} of $d$ is the collection of metrics $d'$ on $Z$ for which the identity map

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:GP_basic}
Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial X$ be distinct boundary points, and let
$\alpha_0,\beta_0$ be geodesic representatives of $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}$, respectively. Then for all $s,t\ge  0$,
\[
(\alpha_0(s)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}
\ge 
\min\{s,t,(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\}-5\delta.
\]
Moreover, $ d\bigl(\alpha_0(s),\beta_0(t)\bigr) \le  s+t+10\delta - 2\min\{s,t,(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\}$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The second inequality follows immediately from the first, since
\[
d\bigl(\alpha_0(s),\beta_0(t)\bigr)
=
s+t-2(\alpha_0(s)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}.
\]

We prove the first inequality. Let $P=(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}$, and  \eqref{eq:BH_upper_lower_bound_P} gives,
\[
P-2\delta\,\,
\le \,\,
\liminf_{u,v\to\infty}
(\alpha_0(v)\mid\beta_0(u))_{\mathfrak o} \,\,\le  \,\, P.
\]
Fix $s,t\ge  0$. For every $v\ge  s$ and $u\ge  t$, applying
$\delta$-hyperbolicity twice gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\alpha_0(s)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}
&\ge 
\min\{
(\alpha_0(s)\mid\alpha_0(v))_{\mathfrak o},
(\alpha_0(v)\mid\beta_0(u))_{\mathfrak o},
(\beta_0(u)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}
\}
-2\delta .
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $\alpha_0$ and $\beta_0$ are geodesic rays based at ${\mathfrak o}$, we have
\[
(\alpha_0(s)\mid\alpha_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}=s,
\qquad
(\beta_0(u)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}=t.
\]
Therefore, we obtain, 
\[
(\alpha_0(s)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}
\ge 
\min\{s,t,(\alpha_0(v)\mid\beta_0(u))_{\mathfrak o}\}
-2\delta .
\]
Let $\varepsilon>0$. By the lower bound on the liminf, there exist arbitrarily
large $u$ and $v$ such that
\[
(\alpha_0(v)\mid\beta_0(u))_{\mathfrak o}
\ge 
P-2\delta-\varepsilon.
\]
For such $u$ and $v$, we further obtain
\[
    (\alpha_0(s)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}
    \ge 
    \min\{s,t,P-2\delta-\varepsilon\}
    -2\delta
    \ge 
    \min\{s,t,P\}-4\delta-\varepsilon.
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\to 0$ gives 
\(
    (\alpha_0(s)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}
    \ge 
    \min\{s,t,P\}-4\delta.
\)
Hence, the stated estimate with $5\delta$ holds.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:gp_basic_2}
Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial X$ be distinct boundary points, and let
$\alpha_0,\beta_0$ be geodesic representatives of $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}$, respectively. Suppose that for some $S>0$ and $0 \le L \le  S$, $d\bigl(\beta_0(S),\alpha_0\bigr)\le  L$. Then
$
(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}
\ge 
S-L-2\delta.
$
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $P=(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}$. Choose $t\ge  0$ such that $d\bigl(\beta_0(S),\alpha_0(t)\bigr)\le  L$. Since $\alpha_0$ and $\beta_0$ are geodesic rays based at ${\mathfrak o}$, this implies $|t-S|\le  L$. Moreover,
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    (\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(S))_{\mathfrak o}
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\bigl(t+S-d(\alpha_0(t),\beta_0(S))\bigr) \\
    &\ge 
    \frac{1}{2}(t+S-L) \\
    &\ge 
    S-L.
    \end{aligned}
\]
Now let $u\ge  t$ and $v\ge  S$. Applying $\delta$-hyperbolicity twice gives
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    (\alpha_0(u)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
    &\ge 
    \min\{
    (\alpha_0(u)\mid\alpha_0(t))_{\mathfrak o},
    (\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(S))_{\mathfrak o},
    (\beta_0(S)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
    \}
    -2\delta \\
    &\ge 
    \min\{t,S-L,S\}-2\delta.
    \end{aligned}
\]
Since $t\ge  S-L$, we get
\[
(\alpha_0(u)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
\ge 
S-L-2\delta
\]
for all $u\ge  t$ and $v\ge  S$. Hence,
\[
\liminf_{u,v\to\infty}
(\alpha_0(u)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
\ge 
S-L-2\delta.
\]
By \eqref{eq:BH_upper_lower_bound_P}, the liminf above
is bounded above by $P$, which gives the desired inequality.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:relationship_p_r}
Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial X$ be distinct boundary points. Then there
exists a constant $C=C(\delta,\kappa)\ge  1$ such that
\[
(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}
\le 
r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
\le 
(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}
+
C\kappa\bigl((\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\bigr)
+
C.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})}{(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}}
\longrightarrow 1
\qquad
\text{as }(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\to\infty.
\]
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $P=(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}$ and $r=r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})$. Let $\alpha_0,\beta_0$ be geodesic representatives of $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}$,
respectively, based at ${\mathfrak o}$.

We first prove the lower bound $P\le  r$. If $P=0$, there is nothing to prove.
Assume $P>0$, and let $S$ satisfy $0<S\le  P$. We show that $

$\textbf{b}$ interchanged, $\textbf{a}\in \mathcal{U}_\kappa(\textbf{b},S)$. Therefore
$r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})\ge  S$.

\[
r_{\mathfrak o}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})\ge  P.
\]

We now prove the upper bound. We set $M(\delta):={\sf M}(1,0)$. Since $\kappa$ is sublinear, we have $\tfrac{S}{\kappa(S)}\to\infty$ as $S\to\infty$.

\[
    M<\frac{S}{24\kappa(S)}
    \qquad
    \text{for all }S\ge  S_0.
\]

additive constant. Thus assume $r>S_0+1$. Let $S$ satisfy $S_0<S\le r$. Since $S\le r$, by the definition of $r$ there exists $T>S$ such that
$
\textbf{b}\in \mathcal{U}_\kappa(\textbf{a},T).
$
Since $T>S>S_0$, we have $M={\sf M}(1,0)<\tfrac{T}{24\kappa(T)}$.

$\textbf{b}$. Therefore the definition of $\mathcal{U}_\kappa(\textbf{a},T)$ gives
\[
\beta_0|_T\subseteq \mathcal{N}_\kappa(\alpha_0,M).
\]

\[
d(\beta_0(S),\alpha_0)\le  M\kappa(S).
\]
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:gp_basic_2} with $L=M\kappa(S)$ gives
\[
P\ge  S-M\kappa(S)-2\delta \quad\implies\quad S\le  P+M\kappa(S)+2\delta.
\]
If $r=\infty$, then this inequality holds for arbitrarily large $S$. This is
impossible, because $S-M\kappa(S)-2\delta\to\infty$ as $S\to\infty$, while
$P<\infty$. Hence $r<\infty$. Letting $S\nearrow r$, we obtain
\[
    r\le  P+M\kappa(r)+2\delta.
\]

We now remove the occurrence of $\kappa(r)$ from the right-hand side. Since
$\kappa$ is sublinear, there exists $R_0=R_0(M,\delta,\kappa)>0$ such that
\[
M\kappa(t)+2\delta\le  \frac{t}{2}
\qquad
\text{for all }t\ge  R_0.
\]
If $r\ge  R_0$, then $r
\le 
P+M\kappa(r)+2\delta
\le 
P+\frac{r}{2}$. Thus $r\le  2P$.
Since $\kappa$ is increasing, Lemma~\ref{Lem:kappa_basic} gives
\[
\kappa(r)
\le 
\kappa(2P)
\le 
2\kappa(P).
\]
Substituting this into $r\le  P+M\kappa(r)+2\delta
$ yields
\[
    r\le  P+2M\kappa(P)+2\delta.
\]
If $r<R_0$, the desired inequality follows after increasing the additive
constant. Therefore there exists $C=C(\delta,\kappa)\ge  1$ such that
\[
r
\le 
P+C\kappa(P)+C.
\]
Combining this with the lower bound $P\le  r$, we obtain
\[
P
\le 
r
\le 
P+C\kappa(P)+C.
\]
Finally, since $\kappa(P)/P\to 0$ as $P\to\infty$, the estimate above implies the desired limit.

\asymp
1+(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}.

=
\liminf_{n,m\to\infty}

\asymp

\asymp
(1+P)^{-\theta}
\asymp
d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}).

Thus $J_\theta$ is bi-Lipschitz.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:qi_implies_bl}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be proper geodesic hyperbolic spaces, and let
$\Phi\colon X\to Y$ be a $(q,Q)$-quasi-isometric embedding.
Fix ${\mathfrak o}\in X$ and let ${\mathfrak o}'=\Phi({\mathfrak o})$. 

Then the induced boundary map
\[
\Phi_*\colon
(\partial X,d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta})
\longrightarrow
(\partial Y,d^Y_{{\mathfrak o}',\theta})
\]
is a bi-Lipschitz embedding. In particular, it is bi-Lipschitz onto its
image. If $\Phi$ is a quasi-isometry, then $\Phi_*$ is surjective and
hence is a bi-Lipschitz equivalence.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
For $\textbf{a}, \textbf{b} \in \partial X$, let
$
p=(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}$, and $p'=
\bigl(\Phi_*(\textbf{a})\mid\Phi_*(\textbf{b})\bigr)_{{\mathfrak o}'}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:QI-Gromov-product}, there exist $A\ge 1$ and $B\ge 0$
such that
\[
\frac1A p-B\le  p'\le  Ap+B.
\]
Consequently, for some $M\ge 1$,
\[
M^{-1}(1+p)\le 1+p'\le  M(1+p).
\]
Indeed, one may take
$
M=\max\{A+B+1,A+AB+1\}$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:log-metric-gromov-product},
\begin{align*}
d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
&\asymp(1+p)^{-\theta},
\quad\text{ and }\\
d^Y_{{\mathfrak o}',\theta}
\bigl(\Phi_*(\textbf{a}),\Phi_*(\textbf{b})\bigr)
&\asymp(1+p')^{-\theta}.
\end{align*}

The comparison between $1+p$ and $1+p'$ therefore gives a constant
$L\ge 1$ such that
\[
L^{-1}d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
\le 
d^Y_{{\mathfrak o}',\theta}
\bigl(\Phi_*(\textbf{a}),\Phi_*(\textbf{b})\bigr)
\le 
L\,d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}).
\]
Since $\Phi_*$ is injective, it is a bi-Lipschitz embedding.

If $\Phi$ is a quasi-isometry, its induced boundary map is surjective,
so it is a bi-Lipschitz equivalence.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathbb{F}_2 = \langle a,b\rangle$ be a free group on two generators $a,b$, equipped with the word metric $d$ associated to $\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}\}$. Consider the Neilsen automorphism $$\Phi: (\mathbb{F}_2, d) \longrightarrow (\mathbb{F}_2, d), \quad  \Phi(a) = ab, \quad \Phi(b) = b.$$
    Its inverse is given by $\Phi^{-1}(a)=ab^{-1}$ and $\Phi^{-1}(b)=b$. 
    
    Since the images of the generators under both $\Phi$ and $\Phi^{-1}$ have word length at most $2$, we have
    \[
        \frac{1}{2}d(g,h)
        \le 
        d\bigl(\Phi(g),\Phi(h)\bigr)
        \le 
        2d(g,h)
    \]
    for all $g,h\in \mathbb{F}_2$. Thus $\Phi$ is a quasi-isometry. It is not an
    isometry of the standard Cayley graph, since $d(e,a)=1$ but $d\bigl(e,\Phi(a)\bigr)=d(e,ab)=2$.

    Let $\partial \mathbb{F}_2$ denote the visual boundary comprised of infinite freely reduced words. For distinct $\xi,\eta\in\partial \mathbb{F}_2$, let 
    \[
    N(\xi,\eta)
    =
    \max\{m\ge  0:\xi_1\cdots\xi_m=\eta_1\cdots\eta_m\}
    \]
    denote their common-prefix length. Then $N(\xi,\eta)=(\xi\mid\eta)_e$. Consequently, fixing $\theta >0$, we obtain  
    \[
    d_{\mathrm{log}}(\xi,\eta)
    =
    \begin{cases}
    0,&\xi=\eta,\\[1mm]
    \bigl(1+N(\xi,\eta)\bigr)^{-\theta},&\xi\neq\eta,
    \end{cases}
\]

    Let $\Phi_*: (\partial \mathbb{F}_2, d_{\mathrm{log}}) \longrightarrow (\partial \mathbb{F}_2, d_{\mathrm{log}})$ be the induced boundary bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism from Theorem~\ref{thm:qi_implies_bl}. Clearly, $\Phi_*$ is not an identity since $\Phi_*(a^\infty)=(ab)^\infty\neq a^\infty$.

    We claim that $\Phi_*$ is not an isometry.
    To see this, let $\xi = a^{\infty}$ and $\eta_n = a^n b^{\infty}$. Then $N(\xi, \eta_n) = n$. On the other hand, $\Phi_*(\xi) = (ab)^{\infty}$ and $\Phi_*(\eta_n) = (ab)^n b^{\infty}$. Then $N(\Phi_*(\xi), \Phi_*(\eta_n))= 2n$. Therefore,
    \[
        \frac{d_{\mathrm{log}}(\Phi_*(\xi), \Phi_*(\eta_n))}{d_{\mathrm{log}}(\xi, \eta_n)} = \left(\frac{n+1}{2n+1}\right)^{\theta} \longrightarrow 2^{-\theta} \quad \text{ as } n\to \infty
    \]
    Thus $\Phi_*$ shrinks arbitrarily small distances by at least a factor $2^{-\theta}$. 
    
    The same map also stretches arbitrarily small distances. 
    Let $\xi = (ab^{-1})^{\infty}$ and $\eta_n =(ab^{-1})^n (b^{-1})^\infty$
    Then $N(\xi, \eta_n)= 2n$. Since $\Phi(ab^{-1}) = abb^{-1} = a$, we obtain $\Phi_*(\xi) =a^{\infty}$ and 
    $\Phi_*(\eta_n) =a^{n}(b^{-1})^{\infty}$. It follows that $N(\Phi_*(\xi), \Phi_*(\eta_n))= n$, and hence,
    \[
        \frac{d_{\mathrm{log}}(\Phi_*(\xi), \Phi_*(\eta_n))}{d_{\mathrm{log}}(\xi, \eta_n)} = \left(\frac{2n+1}{n+1}\right)^{\theta} \longrightarrow 2^{\theta} \quad \text{ as } n\to \infty
    \]
    
    
Thus $\Phi_*$ both stretches and shrinks distances. In particular, it is neither an isometry nor a similarity, and every bi-Lipschitz constant for $\Phi_*$ is at least $2^\theta$.

<!-- corollary-10: body_md -->
\label{cor:characterization_quasiconvexity}
Let $G$ be a word-hyperbolic group, and let $H\le  G$ be a finitely generated
word-hyperbolic subgroup. Suppose that the inclusion $i:H\hookrightarrow G$
admits a Cannon-Thurston map
$\partial i:\partial H\longrightarrow \partial G.$
Let $\theta>0$ be a common admissible exponent for the log metrics on
$\partial H$ and $\partial G$. Then the following are equivalent:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item $H$ is quasiconvex in $G$.
    \item The Cannon-Thurston map
    $
    \partial i:
    (\partial H,d^H_{e,\theta})
    \longrightarrow
    (\partial G,d^G_{e,\theta})
    $
    is bi-Lipschitz onto its image.
    \item The Cannon-Thurston map $\partial i:\partial H\to\partial G$ is
    injective.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-10 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
If $H$ is finite, then $\partial H=\varnothing$ and all three assertions
hold trivially. If $H$ is infinite and virtually cyclic, then $H$ is
quasiconvex in $G$ and $\partial H$ consists of two points. The
Cannon-Thurston map sends these points to the distinct attracting and
repelling fixed points of an infinite-order element of $H$. Hence it is
injective and bi-Lipschitz onto its image.

We may therefore assume that $H$ is non-elementary. If $H$ is quasiconvex in $G$, then
the inclusion $i:H\hookrightarrow G$ is a quasi-isometric embedding and hence, $\partial i:
    (\partial H,d^H_{e, \theta})
    \longrightarrow
    (\partial G,d^G_{e,\theta})$ induced by $i$ is a bi-Lipschitz map onto its image by Theorem~\ref{thm:qi_implies_bl}. Since the Cannon-Thurston map is the continuous boundary extension of the same inclusion, this boundary map is precisely $\partial i$. Hence $(1)$ implies $(2)$. The implication $(2)\Rightarrow(3)$ is immediate, since a bi-Lipschitz map onto its image is injective.
    Finally, assume that $\partial i$ is injective. By \cite[Proposition~2.13]{conicallimit:jeonkapovichleiningeroshika}, equivalently by \cite[Lemma~2.5]{Mahan:commensurators}, $H$ is quasiconvex in $G$. Hence $(3)$ implies $(1)$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:compare-hausdorff-gauge-measure}
Let $\varphi$ and $\psi$ be gauge functions with $\psi\prec\varphi$. Then for
every subset $A$ of every metric space, the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\mathcal H^\varphi(A)<\infty$, then $\mathcal H^\psi(A)=0$.
    \item If $\mathcal H^\psi(A)>0$, then $\mathcal H^\varphi(A)=\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Since $\psi\prec\varphi$, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists
$\delta_0>0$ such that
\[
\psi(t)\le  \varepsilon\varphi(t)
\qquad
\text{for all }0<t\le  \delta_0.
\]
Let $0<\delta\le \delta_0$. For every cover
$A\subseteq\bigcup_i A_i$ with $\operatorname{diam} A_i\le \delta$, we have
\[
\sum_i \psi(\operatorname{diam} A_i)
\le 
\varepsilon
\sum_i \varphi(\operatorname{diam} A_i).
\]
Taking infima over all such covers gives $\mathcal H^\psi_\delta(A)
\le 
\varepsilon \mathcal H^\varphi_\delta(A)$. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives $\mathcal H^\psi(A)
\le 
\varepsilon \mathcal H^\varphi(A)$. If $\mathcal H^\varphi(A)<\infty$, then letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives
$\mathcal H^\psi(A)=0$. The second statement is the contrapositive of the first.

\label{lem:measure-bound-modulus}

non-decreasing function with $\omega(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$ such that $d_W(g(a),g(b))\le  \omega(d_Z(a,b))$ for all $a,b\in Z$. Then for every gauge function $\varphi$ and every

    \le 
    \mathcal H^{\varphi\circ\omega}(A).

\operatorname{diam}_W g(A_i)
\le 
\omega(\operatorname{diam}_Z A_i).

Therefore

\sum_i \varphi(\operatorname{diam}_W g(A_i))
\le 
\sum_i

\]
Taking the infimum over all such covers and then letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:log-to-visual-distortion}
There exist constants $a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2>0$ such that for all distinct $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial X$, if $t=d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})$, then
\[
    a_1\exp(-a_2t^{-1/\theta})
    \le 
    d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
    \le 
    b_1\exp(-b_2t^{-1/\theta}).
\]
Moreover, the identity map
$
\operatorname{id}:
(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{log}})
\longrightarrow
(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{vis}})
$
has modulus of continuity satisfying
\[
\omega_{\operatorname{id}}(t)
\le 
b_1\exp(-b_2t^{-1/\theta})
\]
for all sufficiently small $t>0$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
By Corollary~\ref{cor:log-metric-gromov-product}, there exist constants
$A_1,A_2>0$ such that for all distinct $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\partial X$,
\[
A_1\bigl(1+(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\bigr)^{-\theta}
\le 
d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
\le 
A_2\bigl(1+(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}\bigr)^{-\theta}.
\]
Since $d_{\mathrm{vis}}$ is a visual metric, there exist constants
$B_1,B_2>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ such that
\[
B_1e^{-\varepsilon(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}}
\le 
d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
\le 
B_2e^{-\varepsilon(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}}.
\]
Let $P=(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}$. Suppose that $d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})= t$. 
Then the lower bound for $d_{\mathrm{log}}$ gives $A_1(1+P)^{-\theta} \le  t$. Hence,
\[
    1+P
    \ge 
    \left(\frac{A_1}{t}\right)^{1/\theta}
    \quad \implies \quad
    P
    \ge 
    A_1^{1/\theta}t^{-1/\theta}-1.
\]
Using the upper visual estimate, we obtain,
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
&\le 
B_2e^{-\varepsilon P} \\
&\le 
B_2e^\varepsilon
\exp\left(-\varepsilon A_1^{1/\theta}t^{-1/\theta}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
This gives the upper estimate after setting $b_1=B_2e^\varepsilon$ and $b_2=\varepsilon A_1^{1/\theta}$.

For the lower estimate, we use the upper bound for the log metric $ t\le  A_2(1+P)^{-\theta}$. Thus,
\[
1+P
\le 
\left(\frac{A_2}{t}\right)^{1/\theta} \quad \implies \quad
P
\le 
A_2^{1/\theta}t^{-1/\theta}-1.
\]
Using the lower visual estimate, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
&\,\ge\, 
B_1e^{-\varepsilon P} \,\ge\, 
B_1e^\varepsilon
\exp\left(-\varepsilon A_2^{1/\theta}t^{-1/\theta}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
This gives the lower estimate after renaming constants.

Finally, suppose $d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})\le  t$. Then, by the upper estimate just proved,
\[
d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
\le 
b_1\exp\left(-b_2\,d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})^{-1/\theta}\right)
\le 
b_1\exp(-b_2t^{-1/\theta}).
\]
Taking the supremum over all pairs with
$d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})\le  t$ gives
$
\omega_{\operatorname{id}}(t)
\le 
b_1\exp(-b_2t^{-1/\theta})
$
for all sufficiently small $t>0$.

<!-- corollary-15: body_md -->
\label{cor:group-hausdorff-assouad-nondoubling}
Let $G$ be a word-hyperbolic group.  If $G$ is elementary, then
$
\dim_H(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})=0$. If $G$ is non-elementary, then
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\dim_H(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})=\infty$, $\dim_A(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})=\infty$,
    \item $(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is not doubling, and
    \item $(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is nowhere locally
doubling.
\end{enumerate}

In particular, $(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is not quasisymmetrically equivalent to
$(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$ for any visual metric $d_{\mathrm{vis}}$ on a non-elementary group $G$.
<!-- end corollary-15 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
If $G$ is finite, then $\partial G=\emptyset$. If $G$ is infinite virtually
cyclic, then $\partial G$ has exactly two points. Hence, we have $\dim_H(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})=0$.

Now suppose that $G$ is non-elementary. Fix a visual metric $d_{\mathrm{vis}}$ on $\partial G$ with parameter $a>1$. Let $e(G)$ denote the critical exponent of the action of $G$ on its Cayley graph, and let $e_a(G) = e(G)/\log{a}$. By \cite[Corollaries~5.5 and~7.6]{measure:Coornaert93}, $e_a(G) >0$ and $0< \mathcal H^{e_a(G)}_{d_a}(\partial G) < \infty$. It follows that $\dim_H(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{vis}})=e_a(G)>0$. Corollary~\ref{thm:positive-visual-dim-implies-infinite-log-dim}
therefore gives $\dim_H(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})=\infty$.

Moreover, let $U$ be a nonempty open set equipped with log metric. It contains a nonempty visual ball $B(\textbf{a}, r) \subseteq U$ since the log metric and the visual metric induce the same topology. Since $\dim_H (B(\textbf{a}, r), d_{\mathrm{vis}}) >0 $, hence by Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-visual-dim-implies-infinite-log-dim}, $\dim_H(B(\textbf{a}, r), d_{\mathrm{log}}) = \infty$. By monotonicity, $\dim_H(U, d_{\mathrm{log}}) = \infty$.

Since Hausdorff dimension is bounded above by Assouad dimension, we also obtain $\dim_A(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})=\infty$. A metric space is doubling if and only if it has finite Assouad dimension, hence $(\b G,d_{dy\mathrm{log}})$ is not doubling and $(U, d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is not doubling. Thus, no nonempty open subset of $(\partial X, d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is doubling.

Finally, the boundary of a hyperbolic group equipped with any visual metric is doubling, and doubling is preserved under quasisymmetric homeomorphisms. Therefore, $(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is not quasisymmetrically equivalent to
$(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$ for any visual metric $d_{\mathrm{vis}}$. In particular, the logarithmic and visual metrics belong to distinct
conformal gauges.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:nagata-log-leq-visual}
Let $X$ be a proper geodesic hyperbolic space. Then
\[
\dim_N(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{log}})
\le 
\dim_N(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{vis}}).
\]
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
We apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lang-schlichenmaier-homeo} to the identity map $\operatorname{id}:(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{log}})
\longrightarrow
(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$. Let $q =1/\theta$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:log-to-visual-distortion}, there exist constants
$a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2>0$ such that, for all distinct $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}$, if
$t=d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})$, then
\[
    a_1\exp(-a_2t^{-q})
    \le 
    d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
    \le 
    b_1\exp(-b_2t^{-q}).
\]

Since the boundary is compact, the log metric is bounded. Choose $T>0$ such that $d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})<T$
for all $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\partial X$. Define increasing homeomorphisms $\varphi,\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item define $\varphi(0)=\psi(0)=0$,
    \item for $0<t\le  T$, define 
    $$\varphi(t)=a_1\exp(-a_2t^{-q}) \quad \text{ and }\quad \psi(t)=c_1\exp(-c_2t^{-q}),$$
    \item for $t\ge T$, extend linearly $$\varphi(t)=\varphi(T)+(t-T) \quad \text{ and } \quad \psi(t)=\psi(T)+(t-T).$$
\end{itemize}

Then $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are increasing homeomorphisms of
$[0,\infty)$ onto $[0,\infty)$. Moreover, for all
$\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\partial_\kappa X$, since all realized values of $d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})$ lie in $[0,T)$,
\[
\varphi(d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}))
\le 
d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})
\le 
\psi(d^X_{{\mathfrak o},\theta}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})).
\]

It remains to verify the quantitative hypothesis in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lang-schlichenmaier-homeo}. Fix $\bar c>0$. We claim that
\[
\sup_{s>0}
\frac{1}{s}
\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\bar c\,\psi(s)\bigr)
<\infty.
\]

Since $\psi(s)\to0$ as $s\downarrow0$, we may choose $s_0>0$ sufficiently
small so that $s_0<T$ and, for every $0<s<s_0$, the number
$\bar c\,\psi(s)$ lies in the range of the first branch of $\varphi$. For
$0<s<s_0$, we compute
\[
\bar c\,\psi(s)
=
\bar c b_1\exp(-b_2s^{-q}), \,\,\,\,\text{and}
\]
\[
\varphi^{-1}(u)
=
\left(
\frac{a_2}{\log(a_1/u)}
\right)^{1/q}.
\]
Hence,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\bar c\,\psi(s)\bigr)
&=
\left(
\frac{a_2}
{\log\left(a_1/(\bar c b_1\exp(-b_2s^{-q}))\right)}
\right)^{1/q}  \\
&=
\left(
\frac{a_2}
{b_2s^{-q}+\log(a_1/(\bar c b_1))}
\right)^{1/q}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $b_2s^{-q}\to\infty$ as $s\downarrow0$, after decreasing $s_0$ if
necessary we may assume that
\[
b_2s^{-q}+\log(a_1/(\bar c b_1))
\ge 
\frac{b_2}{2}s^{-q}
\]
for every $0<s<s_0$. Thus,
\[
\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\bar c\,\psi(s)\bigr)
\le 
\left(\frac{2a_2}{b_2}\right)^{1/q}s.
\]
Therefore $\tfrac{1}{s}\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\bar c\,\psi(s)\bigr)$
is bounded for $0<s<s_0$.

On the compact interval $[s_0,T]$, the function
\[
s\mapsto
\frac{1}{s}\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\bar c\,\psi(s)\bigr)
\]
is continuous, and hence bounded.

Finally, for $s\ge  T$, the linear extension gives
\[
\varphi^{-1}(u)\le  T+u
\]
for all $u\ge 0$, while
\[
\psi(s)=\psi(T)+(s-T)\le  s+\psi(T).
\]
Therefore,
\[
\frac{1}{s}\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\bar c\,\psi(s)\bigr)
\le 
\frac{T}{s}
+
\bar c\frac{\psi(s)}{s}
\le 
1+\bar c+\frac{\bar c\,\psi(T)}{T}.
\]
This proves the desired supremum bound. All hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:lang-schlichenmaier-homeo} are now satisfied,
so
\[
\dim_N(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{log}})
\le 
\dim_N(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{vis}}).
\]

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:visual-lc-implies-log-lc}
Let $X$ be a proper geodesic hyperbolic space. If
$(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$ is linearly connected, then
$(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is linearly connected.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
Let $L\ge  1$ be a linear connectedness constant for $(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$. Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial_\kappa X$. If $\textbf{a}=\textbf{b}$, there is nothing to prove. Assume
$\textbf{a}\neq \textbf{b}$. By linear connectedness of $(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$, there
exists a connected set $E\subset \partial X$ containing $\textbf{a}$ and $\textbf{b}$ such that
\[
    \text{diam}_{\mathrm{vis}}(E)
    \le 
    L d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}).
\]
Since the two metrics induce the same topology, $E$ is also connected with
respect to $d_{\mathrm{log}}$. Now let $\alpha,\beta\in E$. Then
\[
    d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\alpha,\beta)
    \le 
    \text{diam}_{\mathrm{vis}}(E)
    \le 
    L d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}).
\]
Using the visual metric estimates in \eqref{eq:visual_estimates}, we get
\[
    A^{-1}e^{-\epsilon(\alpha|\beta)_{{\mathfrak o}}}
    \le 
    d_{\mathrm{vis}}(\alpha,\beta)
    \le 
    L A e^{-\epsilon(\textbf{a}|\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}}.
\]
Taking logarithms gives
\[
    (\alpha|\beta)_{{\mathfrak o}}
    \ge 
    (\textbf{a}|\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}
    -
    C, \quad \text{ where }C=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log(LA^2).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
    \inf_{\alpha,\beta\in E}(\alpha|\beta)_{{\mathfrak o}}
    \ge 
    (\textbf{a}|\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}-C.
\]

Since $(\alpha|\beta)_{{\mathfrak o}}\ge  0$, the previous inequality implies
\[
    1+(\textbf{a}|\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}
    \le 
    (1+C)\bigl(1+(\alpha|\beta)_{{\mathfrak o}}\bigr)
\]
for every $\alpha,\beta\in E$. 
Using the comparison with $d_{\mathrm{log}}$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
    d_{\mathrm{log}}(\alpha,\beta)
    &\le 
    B\bigl(1+(\alpha|\beta)_{{\mathfrak o}}\bigr)^{-\theta}  \\
    &\le 
    B(1+C)^\theta
    \bigl(1+(\textbf{a}|\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}}\bigr)^{-\theta}       \\
    &\le 
    B^2(1+C)^\theta d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over all $\alpha,\beta\in E$ gives
\[
    \text{diam}_{\log}(E)
    \le 
    B^2(1+C)^\theta d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}).
\]
Thus $E$ is a connected subset of $(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ containing $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}$
with
\[
    \text{diam}_{\log}(E)
    \le 
    L' d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}),  \quad \text{ where } L'=B^2(1+C)^\theta.
\]
Hence $(\partial X,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is linearly connected.

$(\partial G,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$ is linearly connected.

\label{prop:visual-up-implies-log-up}
    Let $X$ be a proper geodesic hyperbolic space. If $(\partial_\infty X,d_{\mathrm{vis}})$ is uniformly perfect, then $(\partial_\kappa X,d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is uniformly perfect. Consequently, if $G$ is non-elementary hyperbolic group, then $(\partial G, d_{\mathrm{log}})$ is uniformly perfect.

We prove that there exists $\sf{c}_{\mathrm{log}}\in(0,1)$ such that for every $\textbf{a} \in \partial X$ and for every $0<R<\text{diam}_{\log}(\partial X)$, there exists  $\textbf{b} \in \partial X$ so that

    {\sf{c}}_{\mathrm{log}} R \,\le\,  d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a}, \textbf{b}) \,\le\, R.
\]
Let $0< R \le \text{diam}_{\mathrm{log}} \partial X$. Let $P = (B/R)^{1/\theta}-1$ and $s = A^{-1}e^{-\varepsilon P}$. Choose $P_0 \ge 0$ so that $$s_0 : = A^{-1}e^{-\varepsilon P_0} < \text{diam}_{\mathrm{vis}}(\partial X).$$

Similarly, from the lower bound, we obtain $(\textbf{a} \mid \textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}} \le P+C$ where $C = \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \log\Bigl(\frac{A^2}{{\sf c}_{\mathrm{vis}}}\Bigr)$. Thus
$P \le (\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{{\mathfrak o}} \le P+C$ and 
\[
\begin{aligned}
    d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a}, \textbf{b})
    &\ge 
    B^{-1}(1+P+C)^{-\theta}  \\
    &=
    B^{-1}\left(\left(\frac BR\right)^{1/\theta}+C\right)^{-\theta} \\
    &=
    B^{-2}R
    \left(1+C\left(\frac RB\right)^{1/\theta}\right)^{-\theta}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $R<\operatorname{diam}_{\log}(\partial X)\le  B$, we have
$(R/B)^{1/\theta}\le  1$. Hence

    d_{\mathrm{log}}(\textbf{a}, \textbf{b})
    \ge

    \le

    R_0
    <
    R,

Choosing ${\sf c}_{\mathrm{log}} = \min\left\{
B^{-2}(1+C)^{-\theta}, B^{-2}(1+P_0+C)^{-\theta}\right\}$ proves the claim.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:linear-divergence}
Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial_\infty X$ be distinct boundary points, and let
$\alpha_0,\beta_0:[0,\infty)\to X$ be geodesic representatives of
$\textbf{a},\textbf{b}$, respectively, based at ${\mathfrak o}$. Then for every $t\ge  0$,
\[
d\bigl(\alpha_0(t),\beta_0(t)\bigr)
\ge 
2t-2(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}-4\delta.
\]
In particular,
\[
\liminf_{t\to\infty}
\frac{d\bigl(\alpha_0(t),\beta_0(t)\bigr)}{t}
\ge  2.
\]
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-30: body_md -->
Let $P=(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}$.
Fix $t\ge  0$. We first show that
$$
(\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}\le  P+2\delta.
$$
Let $u,v\ge  t$. Applying $\delta$-hyperbolicity twice gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\alpha_0(u)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
&\ge 
\min\{
(\alpha_0(u)\mid\alpha_0(t))_{\mathfrak o},
(\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o},
(\beta_0(t)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
\}
-2\delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $\alpha_0$ and $\beta_0$ are geodesic rays based at ${\mathfrak o}$,
\[
(\alpha_0(u)\mid\alpha_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}=t,
\qquad
(\beta_0(t)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}=t,
\qquad
(\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}\le  t.
\]
Therefore,
\[
(\alpha_0(u)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
\ge 
(\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}-2\delta.
\]
Taking the liminf as $u,v\to\infty$ and by \eqref{eq:BH_upper_lower_bound_P}, we obtain
\[
P \,\,\ge \,\, \liminf_{u,v\to\infty}
(\alpha_0(u)\mid\beta_0(v))_{\mathfrak o}
\,\,\ge \,\,
(\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}-2\delta.
\]
Now,
\[
d\bigl(\alpha_0(t),\beta_0(t)\bigr)
=
2t-2(\alpha_0(t)\mid\beta_0(t))_{\mathfrak o}.
\]
Using the estimate above, we obtain
\[
d\bigl(\alpha_0(t),\beta_0(t)\bigr)
\ge 
2t-2P-4\delta
=
2t-2(\textbf{a}\mid\textbf{b})_{\mathfrak o}-4\delta.
\]
Dividing by $t$ and letting $t\to\infty$ gives
\[
\liminf_{t\to\infty}
\frac{d\bigl(\alpha_0(t),\beta_0(t)\bigr)}{t}
\ge  2.

We first check that the map is well-defined. 
    Let $\textbf{a}\in\partial_\infty X$, and let $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ be two geodesic rays based at ${\mathfrak o}$ representing $\textbf{a}$. Their images have finite Hausdorff distance. Hence, there exists $D\ge 0$ such that, for every $t\ge 0$, one can find $s\ge 0$ satisfying
    \[
    d\bigl(\alpha(t),\alpha'(s)\bigr)\le  D.
    \]
    Since both rays are based at ${\mathfrak o}$ and parametrized by arclength, $|t-s|\le  D$. Therefore,
    \[
        d\bigl(\alpha(t),\alpha'(t)\bigr)
        \le 
        d\bigl(\alpha(t),\alpha'(s)\bigr)+|s-t|
        \le 2D,
    \]
    and consequently,
    \[
        \frac{d(\alpha(t),\alpha'(t))}{t}
        \le \frac{2D}{t}\longrightarrow0.
    \]
    Thus, $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ sublinearly track each other, so
    $[\alpha]_\kappa=[\alpha']_\kappa$. Hence $f(\textbf{a})$ is independent of
    the choice of geodesic representative.

    
    We now prove that $f$ is injective. Let $\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in \partial_\infty X$ and
    suppose that $ f(\textbf{a})=f(\textbf{b})$. Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be geodesic rays based at

    $f(\textbf{a})=[\alpha]_\kappa$ and $f(\textbf{b})=[\beta]_\kappa$. Hence 
    $[\alpha]_\kappa=[\beta]_\kappa$.
    By the definition of the equivalence relation on $\partial_\kappa X$, this
    means that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ sublinearly track each other, i.e.
    \[
            \lim_{t\to\infty}
            \frac{d_X(\alpha(t),\beta(t))}{t}=0.
    \]

            \ge

    \[
            \liminf_{t\to\infty}
            \frac{d_X(\alpha(t),\beta(t))}{t}
            \ge 2
    \]
    contradicting the sublinear tracking relation. Hence $\textbf{a} = \textbf{b}$. So $f$ is injective.

Proposition~\ref{prop:bijectivity-visual-kappa_appendix}. It remains to

\mathcal{U}_\kappa(\alpha_0,R),

R+H
