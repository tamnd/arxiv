<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:netresforce}
Defining the forces at the reference displacement
\[
f(\ell) = -\operatorname{Div}\big(\delta\mathcal{E}(u_0)\big)(\ell),
\]
we have that the sum of these forces can be expressed as
\[
    \sum_{\ell\in\Lambda}f(\ell) = -\sigma^0 n |\mathsf{b}_{12}| - \int_{\partial B_1(0)}\mathbb{C}[\nabla u^{\mathrm{lin}}]\cdot \nu \,\mathrm{d}\sigma.
    \]
In particular, the sum of the forces vanishes if the two terms on the right hand side balance; that is, if $u^{\mathrm{lin}}$ is chosen such that
\[-\sigma^0 n |\mathsf{b}_{12}| = \int_{\partial B_1(0)}\mathbb{C}[\nabla u^{\mathrm{lin}}]\cdot \nu \,\mathrm{d}\sigma.\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:zeronet}
If there is no residual stress at the reference lattice, so that $\sigma^0=0$, and there is no force line at the dislocation core, meaning $\int_{\partial B_1(0)}\mathbb{C}[\nabla u^{\mathrm{lin}}]\cdot \nu \,\mathrm{d} \sigma=0$, then
\[
    \sum_{\ell\in\Lambda}f(\ell) = 0.
\]

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:finitediffops}
    With the definitions of the operators $D$ and $\tilde{D}$ and the special strain $e$ given in \Cref{sec:operators,sec:specialstrain}, we find
    \begin{align*}
    e_\rho(\ell) &= \begin{cases}
      D_{\rho+\mathsf{b}_{12}}u_0(\ell) + \mathsf{b}\qquad\, & \ell\in\Omega_\Gamma,\ell_2<\hat{x}_2<(\ell+\rho)_2,\\
      D_{\rho-\mathsf{b}_{12}}u_0(\ell) - \mathsf{b} & \ell\in\Omega_\Gamma,(\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2<\ell_2,\\
      D_\rho u_0(\ell) & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}\quad\text{and}\\
    \tilde{D}_\rho u(\ell)-D_\rho u(\ell) &= \begin{cases}
      D_{\rho+\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell)-D_{\rho}u(\ell) & \ell\in\Omega_\Gamma, \ell_2<\hat{x}_2<(\ell+\rho)_2\\
      D_{\rho-\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell)-D_\rho u(\ell)& \ell\in\Omega_\Gamma, (\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2<\ell_2\\
       0 & \text{otherwise.}
        \end{cases}
\end{align*}

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We consider a general displacement $u$ and directly compute $D_\rho S_0 u(\ell)$ in the various cases possible based on the positions of $\ell$ and $\ell+\rho$. This results in the following expressions:
\begin{equation*}
    D_\rho S_0 u(\ell) = \begin{cases}
      D_\rho u_0(\ell) & \ell_2,(\ell+\rho)_2>\hat{x}_2\\
      D_\rho u(\ell-\mathsf{b}_{12}) & \ell_2,(\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2\\
      D_{\rho+\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell-\mathsf{b}_{12})+\mathsf{b} & \ell_2<\hat{x}_2<(\ell+\rho)_2\\
      D_{\rho-\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell)-\mathsf{b}&
      (\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2<\ell_2.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Applying the operator $R$ to this result, and noting our standing assumption that $\mathsf{b}_{12}$ is to be parallel to $\mathsf{e}_1$, so no additional cases arise, we find:
\begin{equation*}
    R D_\rho S_0 u(\ell) = \begin{cases}
      D_\rho u(\ell) & \ell_2,(\ell+\rho)_2>\hat{x}_2\\
      D_\rho u(\ell) & \ell_2,(\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2\\
      D_{\rho+\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell) +\mathsf{b} & \ell_2<\hat{x}_2<(\ell+\rho)_2\\
      D_{\rho-\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell)-\mathsf{b}&
      (\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2<\ell_2.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Setting $u=u_0$, this calculation directly proves the claim about $e_\rho$.

Performing the same calculation using the operator $S$ in place of $S_0$, the shifts involving $\mathsf{b}$ cancel, and we have
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{D}_\rho u (\ell) = R D_\rho S u(\ell) = \begin{cases}
      D_\rho u(\ell) & \ell_2,(\ell+\rho)_2>\hat{x}_2\\
      D_\rho u(\ell) & \ell_2,(\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2\\
      D_{\rho+\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell) & \ell_2<\hat{x}_2<(\ell+\rho)_2\\
      D_{\rho-\mathsf{b}_{12}}u(\ell)&
      (\ell+\rho)_2<\hat{x}_2<\ell_2.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Subtracting $D_\rho u$ from these expressions now yields the second result.
