There is a $W^{*}$-bundle $(M,{\mathbb E},K)$ over a compact metrizable space, which has each fiber a $\textrm{II}_{1}$-factor with separable predual, and which satisfies the following  negation of the generator problem: given any finite family $a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in M$ of continuous sections, there is a $p\in K$ (depending upon the family) so that $a_{1,p},\cdots,a_{n,p}$ do not generate $M_{p}$ as a von Neumann algebra.

<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm: main thm intro}
Let $Q$ be a $\textrm{II}_{1}$-factor which has an embedding into an ultraproduct of matrices with trivial relative commutant (e.g. $Q=L({\mathbb F}_{r})$ for $r>1$, or $Q=L(G)$ where $G$ is an i.c.c., residually finite, Property (T) group). Then there is a compact, metrizable space $K$, and a $W^{*}$-bundle $M$over $K$ so that:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item for each $p\in K$, we have that $M_{p}$ is a   $\textrm{II}_{1}$-factor with separable predual \footnote{$M$ is even separable in the uniform $2$-norm, which is a priori stronger than having fibers with separable predual}. Additionally, $M$ contains the trivial bundle over $K$ with fiber $Q$ as a sub-bundle, and $Q'\cap M_{p}={\mathbb C}$ for every $p\in K$. In particular, each $M_{p}$ is nonamenable. \label{item: intro rel commm}
    \item \label{item: intro classes} Suppose $N$ is any sub-bundle of $M$ and that for every $p\in K$, $N_{p}$ satisfies one of the following properties:
    \begin{itemize}
    \item $N_{p}$ is hyperfinite, 
        \item $N_{p}$ has a Cartan subalgebra,
        \item $N_{p}$ has diffuse central sequence algebra,
        \item  $N_{p}$ is generated by two commuting diffuse subalgebras,
        \item $N_{p}$ can be generated by a single sequential commutation orbit in the sense of \cite{SeqCommutation}.
    \end{itemize}
    Then $M$ cannot be fiberwise generated over $N$ by a finite family of continuous sections: i.e. given $n\in {\mathbb N}$ and $a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in M^{n}$ there is a $p\in K$ (depending upon $a$ and $N$) so that $M_{p}\ne W^{*}(\{a_{i,p}\}_{i=1}^{n}\cup N_{p})$.

\end{enumerate}

<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
\label{defn:W*bundle}
A \emph{$W^{*}$-bundle} is a triple $(M,{\mathbb E},K)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $K$ is a compact Hausdorff space,
    \item $M$ is a unital $C^{*}$-algebra containing a unital copy of $C(K)$ with $C(K)\subseteq Z(M)$
    \item ${\mathbb E}\colon M\to C(K)$ is a faithful conditional expectation,
    \item ${\mathbb E}$ is tracial: for all $x,y\in M$ we have ${\mathbb E}(xy)={\mathbb E}(yx)$. 
    \item $\operatorname{Ball}(M)$ is complete under the norm $\|x\|_{{\mathbb E}}=\|{\mathbb E}(x^{*}x)\|^{1/2}.$
\end{itemize}

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop: creating faces}
Let $V$ be a locally convex space and $C\subseteq V$ a metrizable Choquet simplex. If $K\subseteq \operatorname{Ext}(C)$ is compact, and $X=\overline{\operatorname{co}}(K)$, then $X$ is a face in $C$,  $\operatorname{Ext}(X)=K$, and $X=\left\{\int p\,d\mu(p):\mu \in \operatorname{Prob}(K)\right\}$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose $x_{1},x_{2}\in C$ and $t\in (0,1)$ satisfy that $x:=(1-t)x_{1}+tx_{2}\in C$. By \cite[Theorem 3.28]{GrandpaRudin} and compactness of $K$, we may write $x=\int p\,d\mu(x)$ for some $\mu\in \operatorname{Prob}(K)$. Since $C$ is metrizable, by the Choquet–Bishop de Leeuw theorem \cite[Theorem I.4.8 and Corollary I.4.9]{ChoquetTheory} we may write $x_{i}=\int p\,d\mu_{i}(x)$ with $\mu_{i}\in \operatorname{Prob}(\operatorname{Ext}(C))$. Since $C$ is Choquet, the representing measure for $x$ is unique. Since $K\subseteq \operatorname{Ext}(C)$, this forces $\mu=(1-t)\mu_{1}+t\mu_{2}$. Since $0<t<1$ and $\mu(K)=1$ we must have that $\mu_{i}(K)=1$ for $i=1,2$. This implies that $x_{i}\in \overline{\operatorname{co}}(K)=X$ for $i=1,2$. Thus $X$ is a face in $C$. Finally, since $K$ is compact, Milman's partial converse \cite[Theorem V.7.8]{Conway} tells us that $\operatorname{Ext}(X)=K$.

\label{thm: invariance of MD}
Let $(M,X)$ be a tracially complete $C^{*}$-algebra, and $(I_{i})_{i=1}^{3},(I_{i}')_{i=1}^{3}$ be sets. Suppose that $a\in M^{I_{1}},b\in M^{I_{2}},c\in M^{I_{3}}$, and $a'\in M^{I_{1}'},b'\in M^{I_{2}'},c'\in M^{I_{3}'}$ are given, and that 
\[C^{*}_{X}(b)\supseteq C^{*}_{X}(b'),\]
\[C^{*}_{X}(a,b)\subseteq C^{*}_{X}(a',b'),\]
\[C^{*}_{X}(a,b,c)\supseteq C^{*}_{X}(a',b',c').\]
Then
\[\delta_{MD}(a|b:c)\leq\delta_{MD}(a'|b':c').\]

To ease notation somewhat, set $I=\sqcup_{i=1}^{3}I_{i}$ and $I'=\sqcup_{i=1}^{3}I_{i}'$.
 Fix cutoff functions $R\in [0,\infty)^{I}$ and $R'\in [0,\infty)^{I'}$. Let $\varepsilon>0$  and a finite $F\subseteq I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}$ be given. 
 Since $C^{*}_{X}(a,b)\subseteq C^{*}_{X}(a',b')$, we may apply Proposition \ref{prop: better KDT} to choose a $P\in {\mathbb C}^{*}\ip{T_{i}':i\in I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}^{I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}}$ so that $\|P(a',b')-(a,b)\|_{2,F;X}<\varepsilon$ and that $\|P_{i}\|_{R'}\leq R_{i}$ for all $i\in I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}$. Let $F'\subseteq I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'$ be a finite set so that $P_{i}\in {\mathbb C}^{*}\ip{T_{i}':i\in F'}$ for all $i\in F$.  Set $L'=\|P|_{F}\|_{R',\operatorname{Lip}}$.  Set $\varepsilon'=\frac{\varepsilon}{L'+1}$. Since $C^{*}_{X}(a',b',c')\subseteq C^{*}_{X}(a,b,c)$, we may apply Proposition \ref{prop: better KDT} to find a $Q_{0}\in {\mathbb C}^{*}\ip{T_{i'}:i'\in I'}^{I}$ so that 
 \[\|Q_{0}(a,b,c)-(a',b',c')\|_{2,F'}<\varepsilon'.\]
Note that
 \[\|P(Q_{0}|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(a,b,c))-(a,b)\|_{2,F;X}<2\varepsilon.\]
 Thus 
 \[{\mathcal U}_{1}=\left\{\ell\in \Sigma_{R}:\sum_{i\in F}\|P_{i}(Q_{0}|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(T'))-T_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\ell)}^{2}<4\varepsilon^{2}\right\}\]
 is a weak$^{*}$-neighborhood of $L_{a,b,c;X}$. 

  Fix a weak$^{*}$-open neighborhood $\mathcal{O}$ of $L_{a',b',c';X}$, a finite $G'\subseteq I_{2}'$, and a $\theta'>0$. By Lemma \ref{lem: key lem for invariance} (\ref{item: small perturbation neighborhood}) we may find an $\eta>0$ and a finite $\widetilde{F}\subseteq I'$ so that if $y\in \prod_{i'\in I'}R_{i'}'\operatorname{Ball}(M)$ satisfies $\|(a',b'c')-y\|_{2,\widetilde{F};X}<\eta$, then there is a weak$^{*}$-neighborhood $\mathcal{V}$ of $L_{y;X}$ with $\Gamma^{(n)}_{R'}(\mathcal{V})\subseteq \Gamma^{(n)}_{R'}(\mathcal{O})$.
Without loss of generality we may assume that $\eta<\varepsilon'$ and $\widetilde{F}\supseteq F'\cup G'$.  Since $C^{*}_{X}(a',b',c')\subseteq C^{*}_{X}(a,b,c)$. Apply Proposition \ref{prop: better KDT} to find a $Q\in {\mathbb C}^{*}\ip{T_{i}:i\in I}^{I'}$ so that $\|Q(a,b,c)-(a',b',c')\|_{2,\widetilde{F}}<\eta$ and $\|Q_{i}\|_{R,\infty}\leq R_{i}'$ for all $i\in I'$.
Since $C^{*}_{X}(b)\supseteq C^{*}_{X}(b')$ we may, and will, assume that $Q|_{I_{2}'}\in {\mathbb C}^{*}\ip{T_{i}:i\in I_{2}}^{I_{2}'}$. \[\|Q|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(a,b,c)-Q_{0}|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(a,b,c)\|_{2,F';X}<2\varepsilon',\]
we have that 
\[{\mathcal U}_{2}=\left\{\ell\in \Sigma_{R}:\sum_{i'\in F'}\|Q_{i'}(T')-Q_{0,i'}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\ell)}^{2}<4\varepsilon'^{2}\right\}\]
is a weak$^{*}$-neighborhood of $L_{a,b,c;X}$.

\begin{align*}
\|x-y\|_{2,F}&\leq \varepsilon+\|P(Q_{0}|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2'}}(x))-P(Q_{0}|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(y))\|_{2,F}\\
&\leq 4\varepsilon+L\left[\|Q_{0}|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(x)-Q|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(x)\|_{2,F'}+\|Q_{0}|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(y)-Q|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(y)\|_{2,F'} \right]\\
&+\|P(Q|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(x))-P(Q|_{I_{1}'\sqcup I_{2}'}(y))\|_{2,F}\\
&\leq 4\varepsilon+5L\varepsilon'<9\varepsilon.    
\end{align*}
Thus $f\circ Q$ is $(9\varepsilon,F|G,\theta)$-injective. So we have shown that 
\[\operatorname{md}_{9\varepsilon,F|\theta,G}(\mathcal{U}:c)\leq \operatorname{md}_{\varepsilon',F'|\theta',G'}(\mathcal{O}:c').\]
A fortiori,
\[\operatorname{md}_{9\varepsilon,F}(a|b:c)\leq \operatorname{md}_{\varepsilon',F'|\theta',G'}(\mathcal{O}:c').\]
Observe that $\varepsilon',F'$ depend upon $\varepsilon,F$ but do not depend upon $\mathcal{O},\theta',G'$. Moreover, $\theta',G'$ also do not depend upon $\mathcal{O}$. Thus we may fix $\varepsilon',F',\varepsilon,F,\theta',G'$ and take the infimum over $\mathcal{O}$ to obtain
\[\operatorname{md}_{9\varepsilon,F}(a|b:c)\leq \operatorname{md}_{\varepsilon',F'|\theta',G'}(a'|b':c').\]
Since $G',\theta'$ do not depend upon $\varepsilon,F,\varepsilon',F'$ and $\varepsilon,F$ do not depend upon $\varepsilon',F',\theta',G'$ we can take the infimum over $G',\theta'$ to see  that
\[\operatorname{md}_{9\varepsilon,F}(a|b:c)\leq \operatorname{md}_{\varepsilon',F'}(a'|b':c').\]
A fortiori,
\[\operatorname{md}_{9\varepsilon,F}(a|b:c)\leq \delta_{MD}(a'|b':c').\]
Taking the supremum over $\varepsilon,F$ shows that 
\[\delta_{MD}(a|b:c)\leq \delta_{MD}(a'|b':c').\]

\label{lemm:convex sets have small boundary}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space, and let $C\subseteq V$ be a compact convex set with $\dim(C)=\dim(V)$, and suppose that $0\in \operatorname{int}(C)$. Then, for every linear subspace $W\leq V$ we have that $(\partial C)\cap W$ has measure zero with respect to any Lebesgue measure on $W$.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Fix a Hilbert space norm  on $V$.
Choose $r>0$ so that $r\operatorname{Ball}(V)\subseteq C$. Given $p\in \partial C$, we know that $\{tp+(1-t)y:t\in [0,1),y\in r\operatorname{Ball}(V)\}$ is inside $C$. We leave it as an exercise to verify that $\{tp+(1-t)y:t\in [0,1),y\in r\operatorname{Ball}(v)\}$ is open, and so $\{tp+(1-t)y:t\in [0,1),y\in r\operatorname{Ball}(v)\}\subseteq \operatorname{int}(C)$. In particular, $tp\in \operatorname{int}(C)$ for all $t\in [0,1)$. Since $p$ was arbitrary, we see that $t\partial C\subseteq  C$ for all $t\in [0,1)$, i.e. $\partial C\subseteq \frac{1}{t}C$ for all $t\in [0,1)$. Thus 
\[\partial C\cap W\subseteq \left[\left(\frac{1}{t}C\right)\setminus C\right]\cap W=\left[\frac{1}{t} C\cap W\right]\setminus \left[C\cap W\right], \textnormal{ for all $t\in [0,1)$.}\]
Fix some choice $m_{W}$ of Lebesgue measure on $W$. Then,
\[m_{W}\left(\partial C\cap W\right)\leq t^{-\dim(W)}m_{W}( C\cap W)-m_{W}(C\cap W),\textnormal{ for all $t\in [0,1)$.}\]
The proof is completed by letting $t\to 1$.

\label{lem: continuous sections on convex sets}
Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional real vector space, and let $C\subseteq V$ be a compact convex set with $\dim(C)=\dim(V)$. Suppose that $T\colon V\to W$ is a surjective, linear map. Then there is a continuous section $\phi\colon T(\operatorname{int}(C))\to \operatorname{int}(C)$ of $T\big|_{\operatorname{int}(C)}$.
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Since $\dim(C)=\dim(W)$, we know that $C$ is the closure of its interior, and in particular $\operatorname{int}(C)$ is not empty.
Note that 
$(\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T)$
is a nonempty open set in $\ker(T)$ for all $x\in \operatorname{int}(C)$. In particular, $(\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T)$ has positive measure with respect to any volume on $\ker(T)$.
Fix a choice $m_{\ker(T)}$ of Lebesgue measure on $\ker(T)$. 
Define $\phi\colon T(\operatorname{int}(C))\to \operatorname{int}(C)$ by
\[\phi(v)=\frac{1}{m_{\ker(T)}((\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T))}\int_{(\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap\ker(T)}x+w\,dw.\]
where $x\in \operatorname{int}(C)$ satisfies $T(x)=v$. This does not depend upon the choice of $x$, since $m_{\ker(T)}$ is invariant under translation by elements of $\ker(T)$.
Since $T$ is linear and $T(x+w)=v$ for all $w\in \ker(T)$, we see that 
\[T(\phi(v))=\frac{1}{m_{\ker(T)}((\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T))}\int_{(\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T)}v\,dw=v.\]

Let $v_{n}$ be a sequence in $T(\operatorname{int}(C))$ with $v_{n}\to v\in T(\operatorname{int}(C))$. Choose $x\in \operatorname{int}(C)$ with $T(x)=v$. Since $T$ is a quotient map in the topological sense, we may choose $x_{n}\in V$ with $T(x_{n})=v_{n}$ and $x_{n}\to x$. For all large $n$ we have that $x_{n}\in \operatorname{int}(C)$. Note that
\[\operatorname{vol}((\operatorname{int}(C)-x_{n})\cap \ker(T))=\int_{\ker(T)}1_{\operatorname{int}(C)}(x_{n}+w)\,dw.\]
We have that $1_{\operatorname{int}(C)}(x_{n}+w)\to 1_{\operatorname{int}(C)}(x+w)$ provided $w\notin \partial [(\operatorname{int}(C)-x)]\cap \ker(T)$. 
Since $(\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T)$ is a convex set of dimension equal to dimension of the $\ker(T)$ with $0$ in its interior, we know by Lemma \ref{lemm:convex sets have small boundary} that $\partial(\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T)$ has measure zero. 
Thus $1_{\operatorname{int}(C)}(x_{n}+w)\to 1_{\operatorname{int}(C)}(x+w)$ almost everywhere and the dominated convergence theorem implies that \[\operatorname{vol}((\operatorname{int}(C)-x_{n})\cap \ker(T))\to \operatorname{vol}((\operatorname{int}(C)-x)\cap \ker(T)).\]
Similarly,
\[\int_{\ker(T)}1_{\operatorname{int}(C)}(x_{n}+w)(x_{n}+w)\,dw\to \int_{\ker(T)}1_{\operatorname{int}(C)}(x+w)(x+w)\,dw.\]
Thus $\phi(v_{n})\to \phi(v)$. This shows that $\phi$ is continuous.

Fix $r\in {\mathbb N}$. Then
\[\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\dim_{\varepsilon}(\operatorname{Ball}(({\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.})^{r}),\|\cdot\|_{2})\]

Let
\[A=\sup_{n}\left(\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\operatorname{Ball}(S^{1}(n,\operatorname{tr})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}))}{\operatorname{vol}(\operatorname{Ball}(S^{2}(n,\operatorname{tr})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}))}\right)^{1/rn^{2}}.\]
By \cite[Corollaire 8]{SPVolumes} we know that $A<\infty$.
Fix $\kappa>0$. 
                                                                                                                         
Let $\varepsilon\in (0,1)$ be sufficiently small in a manner depending upon $\kappa$ which we will determine later. 
If we give $M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}$ the inner product coming from the norm on $S^{2}(n,\operatorname{tr})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}$, then this allows us to identify $M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}$  with its dual. 
Under this identification, the polar of $\operatorname{Ball}(M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.})^{r}$ is $\operatorname{Ball}(S^{1}(n,\operatorname{tr})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r})$.

Since we give $M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}/W$ the quotient norm, we know that $\pi$ maps the open unit ball of $M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}$ onto the open unit ball of $M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}/W$. By compactness, we in fact have that $\pi(\operatorname{Ball}(M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.})^{r})=\operatorname{Ball}(M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}/W)$. If $g(x)=g(y)$, then we have that $\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|_{2}<\varepsilon.$ Since $\pi$ is contractive with respect to the quotient norm, we have that 
\[\|x-y\|_{S^{2}(n,\operatorname{tr})^{\oplus r}/W}=\|\pi(\phi(x)-\phi(y))\|_{S^{2}(n,\operatorname{tr})^{\oplus r}/W}<\varepsilon.\]
By our choice of $W$,
\[\|x-y\|_{M_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}^{\oplus r}/W}\leq \left(4\pi A\right)^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}\|x-y\|_{S^{2}(n,\operatorname{tr})^{\oplus r}/W}<\left(4\pi A\right)^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}\varepsilon.\]

\[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\dim_{\varepsilon}((\operatorname{Ball}({\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.})^{r}),\|\cdot\|_{2})\geq (1-\kappa), \textnormal{ if $\varepsilon<(4\pi A)^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}}$.}\]

\[\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\dim_{\varepsilon}((\operatorname{Ball}({\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.})^{r}),\|\cdot\|_{2})\geq r(1-\kappa).\]
Since $\kappa>0$ was arbitrary, the proof is completed by letting $\kappa\to 0$.

<!-- lem-5: body_md -->
\label{lem: coercive unitary}
Suppose $A,B\in {\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}$ and $\|A\|,\|B\|\leq 1/4$. Then $\frac{1}{2}\|A-B\|_{2}\leq \|e^{iA}-e^{iB}\|_{2}\leq \|A-B\|_{2}$.
<!-- end lem-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Given $C\in {\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}$, we define $T_{C}\colon {\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}\to {\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})$ by 
\[T_{C}(B)=i\int_{0}^{1}e^{it C}Be^{i(1-t)C}\,dt.\]
Define $\gamma\colon [0,1]\to {\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a}$ by $\gamma(t)=(1-t)A+tB$. Since $T_{C}$ is the differential of $C\mapsto e^{iC}$ at $C$ (see \cite[Chapter, Section 14 on perturbation theory]{Bellman}), we have $e^{iB}-e^{iA}=\int_{0}^{1}T_{\gamma(t)}(B-A)\,dt$. So:
\[\|e^{iB}-e^{iA}\|_{2}\leq \int_{0}^{1}\|T_{\gamma(t)}(B-A)\|_{2}\,dt\leq \|B-A\|_{2},\]
the last estimate following as $T_{C}$ is $\|\cdot\|_{2}$-$\|\cdot\|_{2}$ for contractive for all $C\in {\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a.}$. 
So:
\[\|e^{iB}-e^{iA}-i(B-A)\|_{2}\leq \int_{0}^{1}\|(T_{\gamma(t)}-\operatorname{id})(B-A)\|_{2}\,dt.\]
Note that:
\[\|T_{\gamma(t)}-\operatorname{id}\|_{B(S^{2}(N,\operatorname{tr}))}\leq 2\sup_{X\in {\mathbb M}_{n}({\mathbb C})_{s.a},\|X\|\leq 1/4}\|e^{iX}-1\|\leq 2\sup_{x\in [-1/4,1/4]}|e^{ix}-1|\leq \frac{1}{2}.\]
Thus:
\[\|e^{iA}-e^{iB}\|_{2}\geq \frac{1}{2}\|A-B\|_{2}.\]

\label{lem: perturb extremality}
Let $V$ be a locally convex space, $C\subseteq V$ a compact convex set, and $p\in \operatorname{Ext}(C)$. Then given any neighborhood $\mathcal{O}$ of $p$ and a $\eta>0$, there is a neighborhood $\mathcal{V}$ of $p$ so that for every $\mu\in \operatorname{Prob}(C)$ with $\int x\,d\mu(x)\in \mathcal{V}$, we have $\mu(\mathcal{O})\geq 1-\eta$.
<!-- end lem-9 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
If the lemma were false, then we could find a neighborhood $\mathcal{O}$ of $p$, an $\eta>0$ and a net $\mu_{\alpha}\in \operatorname{Prob}(C)$ with $\int x\,d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\to p$, but $\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{O})<1-\eta$. Passing to a subsequence we may, and will, assume that there is a $\mu\in \operatorname{Prob}(C)$ with $\mu_{\alpha}\to\mu$ weak$^{*}$. Then $p=\int x\,d\mu(x)$, and extremality of $p$ forces $\mu=\delta_{p}$. Thus, by the Portmanteau theorem \cite[Theorem 11.1.1]{DudleyProb}, we have 
\[1=\delta_{p}(\mathcal{O})\leq \liminf_{\alpha}\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{O})\leq 1-\eta,\]
a contradiction.

 \[\iota\colon \operatorname{Ball}(B)\to \left(\prod_{a\in M}\{z\in {\mathbb C}:|z|\leq \|a\|\}\right)\times K\]

 \[\iota(b)=((\tau(ba))_{a\in M},\pi(b)).\]
 It is direct to see that for $b\in M$ the map $a\mapsto \tau(ab)$ is $\widetilde{{\mathcal T}}$-continuous, hence $\iota$ is a continuous function. We claim that its image is closed and that $\iota$ is a homeomorphism onto its image. First note that $\iota$ is injective, simply because its second coordinate is the projection map $\pi$, and from the fact that if $p\in K$ and $x,y\in M_{p}$ have $\tau_{p}(xa)=\tau_{p}(ya)$ for all $a\in M_{p}$, then $x=y$.

\[\iota(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(b,a,U,\varepsilon))=\iota(\operatorname{Ball}(B))\cap \left[\{z\in {\mathbb C}^{M}:|z_{a}-\tau(ba)|<\varepsilon\} \times U\right]
,\]
and this is an open subset of $\iota(\operatorname{Ball}(B))$.

\[B(a,c)=\lim_{\alpha}\tau_{\pi(x_{\alpha})}(c_{\pi(x_{\alpha})}^{*}x_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}).\]
Then
\[|B(a,c)|\leq \liminf_{\alpha}\|c_{\pi(x_{\alpha})}\|_{2}\|a_{\pi(x_{\alpha})}\|_{2}=\|c_{p}\|_{2}\|a_{p}\|_{2}.\]
This implies that $\widetilde{B}$ descends to a unique bounded sesquilinear form $B\colon M_{p}\times M_{p}\to {\mathbb C}$ which satisfies that $B(a_{p},c_{p})=\widetilde{B}(a,c)$ for all $a,c\in M$. Suppose there is such a function $\ell\mapsto f_{\ell}$. Let $a,R,I$ be as in the setup of Theorem \ref{thm: no continuous algorithm}. Consider the function $K\to \Sigma_{R}$ given by $p\mapsto f_{\ell_{a_{p}}}$. This is a continuous function since the maps $p\mapsto \ell_{a_{p}}$ and $\ell\mapsto f_{\ell}$ are continuous. 

Since 
\[\ell_{\vartheta_{\ell_{a_{p}}}}=\ell_{a_{p}},\]
there is a unique trace-preserving isomorphism $W^{*}(\ell)\cong M_{p}$ which sends $\pi_{\ell}(T_{i})$ to $a_{i,p}$ for $i\in I$. Thus the fact that $f_{\ell_{a_{p}}}(\vartheta_{\ell_{a_{p}}})$ generates $W^{*}(\ell)$ implies that $f_{\ell}(a_{p})$ generates $M_{p}$. This contradicts Theorem \ref{thm: no continuous algorithm}.
