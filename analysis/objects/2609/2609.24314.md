<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{le2-3-1:out-prod-vec}
Let $A=[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}, B=[\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{p}, \beta\in\mathbb{R}^{q}$. 
Then we have the following identities:
\begin{description}
\item[(1)]  $A\otimes \beta^{\top} = [\alpha_{1}\times \beta, \ldots, \alpha_{n}\times \beta]$. 
\item[(2)]  $[\alpha\times \beta_{1}, \ldots, \alpha\times \beta_{n}] = \alpha\otimes \left[\operatorname{vec}(B)\right]^{\top}$. 
\end{description}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We first note the fact that for any vectors $\alpha, \beta$
\begin{equation}\label{eq2-3-0}
\alpha\times \beta = \alpha\otimes \beta^{\top} = \alpha\beta^{\top}
\end{equation}
Thus we have 
\begin{eqnarray*}
A\otimes \beta^{\top} 
&=& \left[ \alpha_1\otimes\beta^{\top}, \alpha_2\otimes \beta^{\top},\ldots, \alpha_n\otimes \beta^{\top}\right] \\
&=& \left[ \alpha_1\times\beta, \alpha_2\times \beta,\ldots, \alpha_n\times \beta\right] 
\end{eqnarray*}
Thus (1) is proved.  Similarly we can prove (2).

<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm2-3-1}
Given any matrix $A=(A_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}$ and a vector $\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{p}$, then  
\begin{description}
\item[(1)] $(A\times \alpha)\beta = (\alpha^{\top}\beta) A$ if $\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{p}$. 
\item[(2)] $(A\times \alpha)\ast_{2}\beta = (A\beta)\times \alpha =A(\beta\alpha^{\top})$ if $\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}$. 
\item[(3)] $(A\times \alpha)\ast_{1}\beta = (A^{\top}\beta)\times \alpha$ if $\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{m}$. 
\item[(4)] $(A\times_{2}\alpha)\beta = (A\beta)\times \alpha$ if $\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}$. 
\item[(5)] $(A\times_{2}\alpha)\ast_{2}\beta = (\alpha^{\top}\beta) A$ if $\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{p}$. 
\item[(6)] $(A\times_{2}\alpha)\ast_{1}\beta = \alpha\times (A^{\top}\beta) =(\alpha\beta^{\top})A$ where $\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{m}$. 
\end{description}
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
To prove (1), we note that $A\times \alpha$ is a tensor of size $m\times n\times p$ and thus the left-hand side of the formula in (1) is an $m\times n$ matrix, which is conformal to that on the right-hand side. Furthermore, for any index $(i,j)\in [m]\times [n]$, we have 
\begin{equation}
\left[(A\times \alpha)\beta\right]_{ij}= \sum_{k=1}^{p} (A\times \alpha)_{ijk} \beta_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{p} A_{ij}\alpha_{k} \beta_{k}= A_{ij} \sum_{k=1}^{p} \alpha_{k} \beta_{k}= (\alpha^{\top}\beta) A_{ij}.
\end{equation}  
Thus (1) holds.  

To prove (2), we note that both sides of (2) are $m\times p$ matrices, and for any index $(i,j)\in [m]\times [p]$, we have 
\begin{equation}
\left[(A\times\alpha)\ast_{2}\beta\right]_{ij} =\sum_{k=1}^{n} (A\times \alpha)_{ikj} \beta_{k}= \sum_{k=1}^{n} A_{ik}\alpha_{j} \beta_{k} 
=(A\beta)_{i} \alpha_{j} = \left[ (A\beta)\times \alpha\right]_{ij}.  
\end{equation}  
Thus (2) is proved. 

The remaining items (3)--(6) follow from the same elementwise computations; for example, in (5) one contracts 
$(A\times_{2}\alpha)_{ijk} = A_{ik}\alpha_{j}$ over $j$ to obtain $(\alpha^{\top}\beta) A_{ik}$, and (6) follows analogously. We omit the details.

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem2-3-2}
Given any matrices $A, B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$ and a vector $\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}$, we have  
\begin{description}
\item[(1)] $(AB)\times \boldsymbol{c} = A (B\times \boldsymbol{c})$.
\item[(2)] $(AB)\times_{2}\boldsymbol{c} = A (B\times_{2}\boldsymbol{c})$. 
\item[(3)] $A\times (B\boldsymbol{c}) = (A\times B)\boldsymbol{c}$.
\item[(4)] $A\times_{2} (B\boldsymbol{c}) = (A\times_{c} B)\boldsymbol{c}$. 
\end{description}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
To prove (1), we note that the tensors on both sides of (1) are third-order tensors of size $n\times n\times n$. Furthermore, for any index
$(i,j,k)\in [n]\times [n]\times [n]$, we have 
\begin{eqnarray*}
\left[(AB)\times \boldsymbol{c}\right]_{ijk} 
&=&(AB)_{ij} c_{k} \\
&=& \sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n} A_{ii^{\prime}}B_{i^{\prime}j} c_{k} \\  
&=& \sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n} A_{ii^{\prime}}(B\times \boldsymbol{c})_{i^{\prime}jk} \\
&=& \left[ A (B\times \boldsymbol{c}) \right]_{ijk}.  
\end{eqnarray*}  
Thus (1) holds. Note that (2), (3), and (4) can also be proved using similar arguments.

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->

\item[(3)] $\mathcal{G}\ast_{i} A\ast_{j} B = \mathcal{G}\ast_{j} B\ast_{i} A$ for any distinct $i,j\in [m]$.
\end{description}

\end{eqnarray*}  
Thus (1) holds for $k=m$. Similarly we can show (1) for other $k\in [m]$.

\label{le4-1}
Let the three cameras defined in \eqref{eq4-1-1} be a non-degenerate reference. Then there does not exist an $3\times 3$ matrix $H$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-1-4}
P^{\prime\prime} = H P^{\prime}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-8 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We argue by contradiction. Suppose there exists $H \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}$ satisfying
\[
[B \mid \boldsymbol{b}_4] = H [A \mid \boldsymbol{a}_4].
\]
Then, equating the left and right blocks gives:
\[
B = HA, \qquad \boldsymbol{b}_4 = H\boldsymbol{a}_4.
\]
Since $A$ is invertible, we obtain $H = BA^{-1}$.

Now consider the camera centers. The center of $P'$ is $C' = (-A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4,\, 1)^\top$, and the center of $P''$ is $C'' = (-B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4,\, 1)^\top$. Substituting $B = HA$ and $\boldsymbol{b}_4 = H\boldsymbol{a}_4$ yields:
\[
C'' = \bigl(-(HA)^{-1}(H\boldsymbol{a}_4),\; 1\bigr)^\top = \bigl(-A^{-1}H^{-1}H\boldsymbol{a}_4,\; 1\bigr)^\top = \bigl(-A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4,\; 1\bigr)^\top = C'.
\]
Thus, $C' = C''$, meaning the second and third camera centers coincide. Consequently, the three camera centers $C_1 = (0,0,0,1)^\top$, $C'$, and $C''$ are collinear (since $C' = C''$ trivially lies on any line through $C_1$ and itself). This contradicts the non-degeneracy assumption that the three camera centers are non-collinear. Therefore, no such $H$ exists.

<!-- lem-10: body_md -->
\label{le4-2-1}
Let $(P, P', P'')$ be a non-degenerate canonical three-view configuration as defined in \eqref{eq4-1-1}. Then there exists at least one index $k\in\mathbb{T}$ such that the $3\times 3$ matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-2-3}
S_k = b_{k4}A - \boldsymbol{a}_4\,\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top
\end{equation}
has full rank; i.e., $\det(S_k)\neq 0$.
<!-- end lem-10 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $Q_k$ denote the $4\times 4$ submatrix obtained from the joint camera pair $(P',P'')$ by taking all rows of $P'$ together with the $k$-th row 
of $P''$
\[
Q_k = \begin{bmatrix} A & \boldsymbol{a}_4 \\ \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top & b_{k4} \end{bmatrix}.
\]
$Q_k$ is the $4\times 4$ submatrix of $Q$ defined in \eqref{eq4-5-4}, obtained by retaining $P^{\prime}$ and only the $k$th row of $P^{\prime\prime}$ and 
discarding the other two.  Since $A$ is invertible under non-degeneracy, the block determinant formula gives
\[
\det(Q_k) = \det(A) \cdot \bigl( b_{k4} - \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 \bigr).
\]

We consider $S_k$ as defined in \eqref{eq4-2-3}. If $b_{k4} \neq 0$, we factor
\[
S_k = b_{k4}A \bigl( I_3 - A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top b_{k4}^{-1} \bigr).
\]
Applying the matrix determinant lemma $\det(I - \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^\top) = 1 - \mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{u}$, we obtain
\begin{eqnarray*}\label{eq4-2-4}
\det(S_k) &=& b_{k4}^3 \det(A) \Bigl( 1 - \frac{1}{b_{k4}} (\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4) \Bigr) \\
          &=& b_{k4}^2 \det(A) \bigl( b_{k4} - \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 \bigr).
\end{eqnarray*}
Comparing the two determinant expressions yields the relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-2-5}
\det(S_k) = b_{k4}^2 \det(Q_k).
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\det(S_k) \neq 0$ if and only if both $b_{k4} \neq 0$ and $\det(Q_k) \neq 0$.

We now show that such an index $k$ indeed exists. The camera centers of $P'$ and $P''$ in inhomogeneous coordinates are $C' = -A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4$ and $C'' = -B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4$, respectively. Define the displacement vector between them as
\[
\boldsymbol{d} = C' - C'' = A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 - B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4.
\]
Observing that $b_{k4} = \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4$ for any $k\in\mathbb{T}$, we compute
\[
b_{k4} - \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 = \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4 - \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 = -\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d}.
\]
Hence,
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-2-6}
b_{k4} - \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 = -\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d},
\end{equation}
which implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-2-7}
\det(Q_k) = -\det(A)\, (\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d}),
\end{equation}
and consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-2-8}
\det(S_k) = -b_{k4}^2 \det(A)\, (\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d}).
\end{equation}
Thus, to establish $\det(S_k) \neq 0$ for some $k$, it suffices to find an index $k\in\mathbb{T}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-2-9}
b_{k4} \neq 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d} \neq 0.
\end{equation}

We proceed by contradiction. Suppose that whenever $b_{k4}\neq 0$, we necessarily have $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d} = 0$, implying $\det(S_k)=0$ for all $k\in\mathbb{T}$. Define the set of indices with non-zero epipolar components:
\[
\mathcal{K} = \{ k\in\mathbb{T} \mid b_{k4} \neq 0 \}.
\]
By non-degeneracy, the three camera centers $C=\mathbf{0}$, $C'$, and $C''$ are non-collinear. In particular,
\[
\boldsymbol{d} \neq \mathbf{0}, \qquad \boldsymbol{b}_4 = \boldsymbol{e}'' \neq \mathbf{0},
\]
so $\mathcal{K}\neq\emptyset$. Under our supposition, for every $k\in\mathcal{K}$ we have $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d} = 0$. We analyze the size of $\mathcal{K}$.

\textbf{Case 1:} $|\mathcal{K}| = 3$, i.e., $b_{k4} \neq 0$ for all $k\in\mathbb{T}$. Then $B\boldsymbol{d} = \mathbf{0}$, and since $B$ is invertible, we obtain $\boldsymbol{d} = \mathbf{0}$, contradicting $C' \neq C''$.

\textbf{Case 2:} $|\mathcal{K}| \leq 2$, i.e., at least one $b_{k4} = 0$. Let $m\notin\mathcal{K}$, so $b_{m4} = \boldsymbol{\beta}_m^\top (B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4) = 0$. For each $k\in\mathcal{K}$, the condition $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d} = 0$ gives
\[
\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top A^{-1}\boldsymbol{a}_4 = \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4.
\]
Geometrically, $\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^\top \mathbf{x} = 0$ defines the plane $\pi_i$ passing through $C=\mathbf{0}$ associated with the $i$-th row of $B$. For $k\in\mathcal{K}$, $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d} = 0$ forces $\boldsymbol{d} = C'' - C'$ to lie parallel to the plane $\pi_k$. For $m\notin\mathcal{K}$, we have
\[
0 = b_{m4} = \boldsymbol{\beta}_m^\top B^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}_4 = \boldsymbol{\beta}_m^\top C'',
\]
forcing $C''$ to lie directly on the plane $\pi_m$. Requiring $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d} = 0$ for all $k\in\mathcal{K}$ restricts $\boldsymbol{d}$ to the common line of intersection of these planes through the origin $C=\mathbf{0}$. This forces $C$, $C'$, and $C''$ to lie on a single common line in 3D, contradicting the non-degeneracy assumption that the three camera centers are non-collinear.

Therefore, there must exist at least one index $k\in\mathbb{T}$ satisfying \eqref{eq4-2-9}, ensuring $\det(Q_k) \neq 0$ and consequently
\[
\det(S_k) = -b_{k4}^2 \det(A)\, (\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^\top \boldsymbol{d}) \neq 0,
\]
so $\operatorname{rank}(S_k) = 3$. This completes the proof.

\label{rmk4-7:practical}
Theorem~\ref{thm4-2-1:trif-matrx} is not merely structural; it has three direct algorithmic consequences.
\emph{(i) A sufficient degeneracy alarm.} Since rank deficiency of any unfolding forces degeneracy (collinear camera
centers), monitoring the three unfolding ranks of a candidate tensor --- three SVDs of $3\times 9$ matrices, negligible
next to the estimation itself --- provides a \emph{sufficient} alarm: if it fires, epipole extraction and pose
decomposition must not be attempted. The alarm is not exhaustive (generic collinear configurations retain full rank),
so it complements, rather than replaces, the planarity and baseline tests of Algorithm~\ref{alg6-2:ransac}; its value
is that it certifies the failure mode documented on real imagery in Section~\ref{sec5-10:realdata} at zero cost.
\emph{(ii) Validation and projection of estimates.} A raw linear (SVD) estimate generally violates the rank
conditions; checking $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{T}[k])=3$ for $k\in\mathbb{T}$ serves as a quality gate, and the optional truncated-SVD
projection onto the rank-$3$ unfoldings (Step~4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:point-estimation}) restores the structure.
\emph{(iii) A graded conditioning signal.} Singular values depend continuously on the tensor, so configurations
approaching the collapse locus yield estimates whose smallest unfolding singular values vanish continuously: the
singular-value gap provides a graded early warning between the binary extremes ``full rank'' and ``rank deficient''.
All three consequences are exercised concretely in the PyTorch experiment of Section~\ref{sec5-12:autodiff},
where the unfolding ranks are tracked inside an autodiff loop at no measurable overhead.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
We prove the statement for $\mathcal{T}[1]$; the proofs for $\mathcal{T}[2]$ and $\mathcal{T}[3]$ follow identically by permuting the roles of the views.
For any triplet of corresponding lines $(\boldsymbol{l}, \boldsymbol{l}', \boldsymbol{l}'')$ across the three views, the fundamental trilinear constraint holds:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i,j,k} \ell_i \, \ell'_j \, \ell''_k \, T_i^{jk} = 0,
\label{eq:trilinear-constraint}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{l} = (\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell_3)^\top$, $\boldsymbol{l}' = (\ell'_1, \ell'_2, \ell'_3)^\top$, and $\boldsymbol{l}'' = (\ell''_1, \ell''_2, \ell''_3)^\top$. 

Now, fix a line $\boldsymbol{l}'$ in view 2. Consider the plane $\pi_{\boldsymbol{l}'}$ in 3D space that is the back-projection of $\boldsymbol{l}'$: it is the plane passing through the camera center of view 2 and projecting to $\boldsymbol{l}'$. This plane induces a homography $H_{13}(\boldsymbol{l}')$ between view 1 and view 3, since any point $\mathbf{x}_1$ in view 1 back-projects to a ray that intersects $\pi_{\boldsymbol{l}'}$ at a unique 3D point, which then projects to a point $\mathbf{x}_3 = H_{13}(\boldsymbol{l}') \mathbf{x}_1$ in view 3.

We now show that $H_{13}(\boldsymbol{l}')$ is precisely given by $\operatorname{vec}^{-1}(\mathcal{T}[1]^\top \boldsymbol{l}')$.

For a point $\mathbf{x}_1 = (x_1, x_2, x_3)^\top$ in view 1 and its corresponding point $\mathbf{x}_3 = (y_1, y_2, y_3)^\top$ in view 3, the condition that they correspond via the plane $\pi_{\boldsymbol{l}'}$ is equivalent to the existence of a line $\boldsymbol{l}''$ in view 3 passing through $\mathbf{x}_3$ such that $(\boldsymbol{l}, \boldsymbol{l}', \boldsymbol{l}'')$ is a line correspondence for some $\boldsymbol{l}$ passing through $\mathbf{x}_1$. 

More directly, the trilinear constraint \eqref{eq:trilinear-constraint} can be rewritten for a fixed $\boldsymbol{l}'$ as follows. Since the constraint must hold for all lines $\boldsymbol{l}$ through $\mathbf{x}_1$ and all lines $\boldsymbol{l}''$ through $\mathbf{x}_3$, we can contract \eqref{eq:trilinear-constraint} with $\mathbf{x}_1$ and $\mathbf{x}_3$. This yields the bilinear form:
\[
\sum_{i,j,k} x_i \, \ell'_j \, y_k \, T_i^{jk} = 0,
\]
which must hold for all corresponding pairs $(\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_3)$ under the homography $H_{13}(\boldsymbol{l}')$.

Now, define the $3\times 3$ matrix $M(\boldsymbol{l}')$ with entries:
\[
M(\boldsymbol{l}')_{ik} = \sum_{j=1}^3 \ell'_j \, T_i^{jk}.
\]
Then the bilinear form above becomes:
\[
\mathbf{x}_1^\top M(\boldsymbol{l}') \, \mathbf{x}_3 = 0.
\]
It remains to identify $M(\boldsymbol{l}')$ with the homography. Using \eqref{eq4-1-6} and \eqref{eq4-1-3}, we have the explicit form
\[
M(\boldsymbol{l}') = \operatorname{vec}^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal{T}[1]^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}'\bigr)
          = (A^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}')\,\boldsymbol{b}_{4}^{\top} - (\boldsymbol{a}_{4}^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}')\,B^{\top}.
\]
Let $\mathbf{X} = (\mathbf{u}^{\top}, 1)^{\top}$ be a 3D point lying on the plane $\pi_{\boldsymbol{l}'}$, i.e., $(\boldsymbol{l}')^{\top}(A\mathbf{u} + \boldsymbol{a}_{4}) = 0$, and let $\mathbf{x}_1 = \mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{x}_3 = B\mathbf{u} + \boldsymbol{b}_{4}$ be its images in views 1 and 3. Then
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathbf{x}_1^{\top} M(\boldsymbol{l}')
&=& (\boldsymbol{l}'^{\top}A\mathbf{u})\,\boldsymbol{b}_{4}^{\top} - (\boldsymbol{a}_{4}^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}')\,(B\mathbf{u})^{\top} \\
&=& \bigl((\boldsymbol{l}')^{\top}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime} - \boldsymbol{a}_{4})\bigr)\boldsymbol{b}_{4}^{\top} - (\boldsymbol{a}_{4}^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}')\,(\mathbf{x}_3 - \boldsymbol{b}_{4})^{\top} \\
&=& -(\boldsymbol{a}_{4}^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}')\,\mathbf{x}_3^{\top},
\end{eqnarray*}
where $\mathbf{x}^{\prime} = A\mathbf{u} + \boldsymbol{a}_{4}$ is the image of $\mathbf{X}$ in view 2 and $(\boldsymbol{l}')^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{\prime} = 0$ was used. For a generic line $\boldsymbol{l}'$ we have $\boldsymbol{a}_{4}^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}' \neq 0$, and therefore
\[
\mathbf{x}_3^{\top} \sim \mathbf{x}_1^{\top} M(\boldsymbol{l}'),
\]
i.e., $M(\boldsymbol{l}')$ maps view-1 points to view-3 points exactly as the homography induced by the plane $\pi_{\boldsymbol{l}'}$ (in the row-vector convention $\mathbf{x}_3^{\top} = \mathbf{x}_1^{\top}H_{13}$). Recalling that $M(\boldsymbol{l}') = \operatorname{vec}^{-1}(\mathcal{T}[1]^{\top}\boldsymbol{l}')$ proves the claim for $\mathcal{T}[1]$; the proofs for $\mathcal{T}[2]$ and $\mathcal{T}[3]$ follow identically by permuting the roles of the views.

\label{thm4-4}
For the general camera system $(P,P',P'')$ in \eqref{eq4-6-3:general}, the trifocal tensor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-6-5:general}
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{S}[A,B]\,\boldsymbol{p}_4+\mathcal{T}[\hat{A},\hat{B}],
\end{equation}
where $\hat{A}=[A\mid\boldsymbol{p}'_4]$ and $\hat{B}=[B\mid\boldsymbol{p}''_4]$.
<!-- end thm-8 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Transform the cameras into canonical form via the homography
\[
H=
\begin{bmatrix}
M^{-1} & -M^{-1}\boldsymbol{p}_4\\
\mathbf{0}^\top & 1
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The transformed cameras are then of the form \eqref{eq4-1-1}, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq4-6-6:general}
\boldsymbol{a}_4=M'C+\boldsymbol{p}'_4,\qquad \boldsymbol{b}_4=M''C+\boldsymbol{p}''_4.
\end{equation}
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm4-1} completes the proof.

<!-- thm-9: body_md -->
\label{thm4-7:trace}
Let $I_{3}$ denote the $3\times 3$ identity matrix. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{T}\ast_{(1,3)} I_{3} = A\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime\prime} - (\operatorname{tr} B) \boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}$.
\item $\mathcal{T}\ast_{(2,3)} I_{3} = A^{\top}\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime\prime} - B^{\top}\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}$.
\item $\mathcal{T}\ast_{(1,2)} I_{3} = (\operatorname{tr} A) \boldsymbol{e}^{\prime\prime} - B\boldsymbol{e}^{\prime}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thm-9 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf{u} = \mathcal{T}\ast_{(1,3)}I_{3} = (u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})^{\top}$. For each $j\in \mathbb{T}$:
\[
u_{j} = \sum_{i,k=1}^{3} \left(a_{ji}b_{k4}\delta_{ik} - b_{ki}a_{j4}\delta_{ik}\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \left(a_{ji}b_{i4} - b_{ii}a_{j4}\right) = (A\boldsymbol{b}_{4} - (\operatorname{tr} B)\boldsymbol{a}_{4})_{j},
\]
proving (1). Items (2) and (3) follow similarly.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop5-1:slices}
The frontal slices $T_1,T_2,T_3$ of $\mathcal{T}$, together with the epipoles, determine the three
fundamental matrices $F_{12},F_{13},F_{23}$. Conversely, three consistent fundamental matrices
do not uniquely determine the tensor: the tensor supplies the missing cross-view consistency.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop5-2:reduction}
For a fixed third view, constraining the tensor against known pairwise fundamental matrices
recovers the epipolar geometry of the remaining pair. Hence the tensor simultaneously encodes
three fundamental matrices subject to a single compatibility condition.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- rmk-2: body_md -->
\label{rmk5-1:segmentation}
Under independent motion, point correspondences consistent with a three-view motion group satisfy
a single trifocal relation. Distinct groups are separated by their tensor membership, which can be
recovered by alternating assignment and linear estimation.
<!-- end rmk-2 -->
<!-- exm-3: body_md -->
\label{exm5-1:perfect-lines}
We generate a random trifocal tensor $T_{true}$ and construct $N=30$ perfect line correspondences. The estimation achieves machine-precision accuracy.
<!-- end exm-3 -->
<!-- exm-4: body_md -->
\label{exm5-2:noisy-lines}
We add Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.1$) to $N=50$ line correspondences. Nonlinear refinement reduces estimation error by $\approx 53.5\%$ and enforces the rank-2 slice constraint.
<!-- end exm-4 -->
<!-- exm-5: body_md -->
\label{exm5-3:ndlt}
With $\sigma=0$ and $N=10$, the estimated tensor satisfies
$\|\tilde{C}\mathbf{t}\|_2=1.24\times10^{-15}$, confirming that the SVD step recovers the
ground-truth tensor (up to scale) in the absence of noise.

<!-- exm-6: body_md -->
\label{exm5-4:planar}
Sampling all 3D points on a plane reduces the effective DOF of the configuration; the smallest
singular value of $C$ no longer separates cleanly, and the Frobenius error in
Table~\ref{tab:numerical_results} rises sharply. A non-planar baseline is therefore required
for a reliable $7$-point minimal estimate.
<!-- end exm-6 -->
<!-- exm-7: body_md -->
\label{exm5-5:twoview}
Fixing the third view and recovering the first two fundamental matrices from the tensor slices
(Proposition~\ref{prop5-1:slices}) reproduces the pairwise epipolar geometry, while the same tensor
simultaneously enforces consistency with the third pair. This avoids the drift inherent in
independent two-view estimates.
