<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:population_obstruction}\leavevmode\par
For every $\kappa\geq0$, writing $(t)_+:=\max(t,0)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_\kappa(\Phi)
\geq(\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\kappa\tau_\Phi)_+
\geq(\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\kappa a_\Phi)_+
\geq(\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\kappa\sqrt{2A_{\mathrm{fid}}})_+.
\label{eq:population_obstruction}
\end{equation}
If $\kappa\geq\|B_*\|_2$, then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_\kappa(\Phi)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}+\|B_*\|_2a_\Phi.
\label{eq:population_attainability}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\kappa=\sqrt C$ admits $B_*$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For any matrix $B$, the class-measurable prediction $B^\top\mu_\Phi^{c(x)}$ obeys the conditional-mean orthogonality identity
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[\|z-B^\top\mu_\Phi^{c(x)}\|^2\right]
=\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2+
\mathbb E_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\|z_*^{c(x)}-B^\top\mu_\Phi^{c(x)}\|^2\right]
\geq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2.
\label{eq:hard_label_orthogonality}
\end{equation}
The reverse triangle inequality in $L^2$ gives
$R_{\mathbb P}(\Phi,B)^{1/2}\geq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\|B\|_2\tau_\Phi$.
Taking the infimum over the norm ball proves the first inequality. The remaining inequalities follow from $\tau_\Phi\leq a_\Phi\leq\sqrt{2A_{\mathrm{fid}}}$.
For the upper bound, use the feasible $B_*$ and the triangle inequality on $z-B_*^\top\phi=(z-B_*^\top y)+B_*^\top(y-\phi)$. The rows of $B_*$ lie in $\Delta_C$, so $\|B_*\|_2\leq\|B_*\|_F\leq\sqrt C$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:exclusion_certificate}\leavevmode\par
Fix the reference-class map $c$ and measurable $\Phi:\mathcal X\to\Delta_C$ using construction and selection data. Draw $M$ independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) realizations $(x^i,x^{+,i})$ from $\mathbb P_{x,x^+}$, independently of construction and selection, and set $\phi^i=\Phi(x^i)$, $z^i=\Phi(x^{+,i})$, and $c_i=c(x^i)$. Denote the random evaluation sample by $\mathcal V_M$; its observed entries are these pairs, and its distribution, conditional on the fixed construction and selection, is $\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}:=(\mathbb P_{x,x^+})^{\otimes M}$. Let $n_c:=|\{i:c_i=c\}|$. For $n_c>0$, let $\bar z_c:=n_c^{-1}\sum_{i:c_i=c}z^i$ be the mean successor coordinates in class $c$; for an empty class, choose any $\bar z_c\in\Delta_C$, since it contributes no summands. Define the empirical assignment loss $\widehat f$, within-class successor variance $\widehat s$, and confidence corrections by
\begin{align*}
\widehat f&:=\frac1M\sum_i(1-(\phi^i)_{c_i})^2,
&\widehat s&:=\frac1M\sum_i\|z^i-\bar z_{c_i}\|^2,\\
r_\delta&:=\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{M},
&V_\delta&:=(\widehat s-\sqrt{2r_\delta})_+,\\
F_\delta&:=\min\{1,\widehat f+r_\delta+
\sqrt{r_\delta^2+2r_\delta\widehat f}\},
&&0<\delta<1.
\end{align*}
Conditional on construction and selection, with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every $\kappa\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\inf_{\|B\|_2\leq\kappa}
\left(\mathbb{E}_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[\|\Phi(x^+)-B^\top\Phi(x)\|^2\right]\right)^{1/2}
&\geq L_{\kappa,\delta},\\
L_{\kappa,\delta}&:=\bigl(\sqrt{V_\delta}-\kappa\sqrt{2F_\delta}\bigr)_+.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exclusion_certificate}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Throughout this proof, expectations and probability statements are conditional on the data used for representation construction and model selection.

\emph{Step 1: lower-bound successor variation.} For each observed class, the empirical mean minimizes the sum of squared distances to its successor vectors. Consequently,
\[
\widehat s=\min_{a^1,\ldots,a^C\in\Delta_C}
\frac1M\sum_i\|z^i-a^{c_i}\|^2,
\qquad \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}\!\left[\widehat s\right]\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2.
\]
The empirical loss at the sample class means is no larger than the empirical loss at the population class means. Taking expectations proves the second inequality.

For any fixed set of class means, each loss lies in $[0,2]$. Replacing one evaluation pair changes the average by at most $2/M$; taking the minimum preserves this bound. McDiarmid's inequality~\citep{mcdiarmid1989} therefore gives
\[
\Pr_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}\!\left(
\widehat s>\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}\!\left[\widehat s\right]
+\sqrt{2r_\delta}\right)\leq\delta/2.
\]
Hence $\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2\geq V_\delta$ on the complementary event. The bounded-differences argument remains valid when class means are estimated from these same evaluation data and when some classes are empty.

\emph{Step 2: upper-bound assignment loss.} For $\ell(x):=(1-\phi_{c(x)})^2\in[0,1]$, let
$f:=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\ell(x)\right]=A_{\mathrm{fid}}$.
For $t\geq0$, the exponential-moment bound
\[
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[e^{-t\ell(x)}\right]
\leq1+f(e^{-t}-1)
\]
gives the multiplicative lower-tail inequality~\citep{hoeffding1963}
$\Pr_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}(\widehat f<f-\sqrt{2fr_\delta})\leq\delta/2$.
For $f=A_{\mathrm{fid}}\geq\widehat f$, squaring $f-\widehat f\leq\sqrt{2fr_\delta}$ and solving the resulting quadratic inequality gives
\[
 f\leq\widehat f+r_\delta+\sqrt{r_\delta^2+2r_\delta\widehat f}.
\]
The case $f<\widehat f$ is immediate. Since $f\leq1$, both cases yield $f\leq F_\delta$.
\emph{Step 3: combine the two bounds.} A union bound gives $\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2\geq V_\delta$ and $A_{\mathrm{fid}}\leq F_\delta$ on one event of probability at least $1-\delta$. Substitution into Proposition~\ref{prop:population_obstruction} proves~\eqref{eq:exclusion_certificate}. This confidence event involves only the two scalar population quantities. It does not depend on a particular prediction matrix or norm limit, which proves the simultaneous guarantee over $B$ and $\kappa$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:centered_certificate}\leavevmode\par
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:exclusion_certificate}, let
$\bar\phi_c:=n_c^{-1}\sum_{i:c_i=c}\phi^i$ for $n_c>0$, and let empty classes contribute no summands. Set
\begin{equation}
\widehat t:=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^M\|\phi^i-\bar\phi_{c_i}\|^2,
\qquad
T_\delta:=\min\!\left\{1,\widehat t+\sqrt{2r_\delta}+\frac{C}{M}\right\},
\label{eq:input_variance_confidence}
\end{equation}
where $r_\delta=\log(2/\delta)/M$, and use $V_\delta$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:exclusion_certificate}. Conditional on the construction and selection data, with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all $\kappa\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\quad\mathcal D_\kappa(\Phi)\geq
\widetilde L_{\kappa,\delta}
:=\left(\sqrt{V_\delta}-\kappa\sqrt{T_\delta}\right)_+.\quad}
\label{eq:centered_certificate}
\end{equation}
The cost of computing $\widehat t$ and $\widehat s$ is $O(MC)$ after the coordinates have been evaluated.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The empirical input dispersion is the minimum of $M^{-1}\sum_i\|\phi^i-h^{c_i}\|^2$ over representative coordinate vectors $h^c\in\Delta_C$, one for each reference class. Each loss lies in $[0,2]$, so replacing one pair changes this minimum by at most $2/M$. McDiarmid's inequality therefore gives
$\mathbb E_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}[\widehat t]\leq\widehat t+\sqrt{2r_\delta}$ except on an event of probability $\delta/2$.
Writing $u_c:=\mathbb E_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}[\|\phi-\mu_\Phi^c\|^2\mid c(x)=c]\leq1$, the class-occupancy calculation used in Proposition~\ref{prop:class_occupancy_bias} gives
\[
\tau_\Phi^2-\mathbb E_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}[\widehat t]
=\frac1M\sum_{c=1}^C u_c[1-(1-\pi_c)^M]\leq\frac{C}{M}.
\]
Also $\tau_\Phi^2\leq1$. Thus $\tau_\Phi^2\leq T_\delta$ on the stated event. The bounded-differences argument for successor variation yields $\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2\geq V_\delta$ with failure probability at most $\delta/2$. Apply a union bound and Proposition~\ref{prop:population_obstruction}. Neither event involves $\kappa$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:prototype_certificate}
A \emph{prototype} here means a representative coordinate vector $h^c\in\Delta_C$ selected for reference class $c$ before evaluation, not a KAHM reference state or the unknown class mean successor. Choose these prototypes independently of the evaluation sample (the one-hot choice $h^c=\mathrm e^c$ requires no estimation). Define
\[
\widehat q_h:=\frac{1}{2M}\sum_{i=1}^M\|\phi^i-h^{c_i}\|^2,
\qquad Q_{h,\delta}:=\min\{1,\widehat q_h+r_\delta+
\sqrt{r_\delta^2+2r_\delta\widehat q_h}\}.
\]
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:exclusion_certificate}, with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all $\kappa\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_\kappa(\Phi)\geq
L^{\mathrm{proto}}_{\kappa,\delta}(h)
:=\bigl(\sqrt{V_\delta}-\kappa\sqrt{2Q_{h,\delta}}\bigr)_+.
\label{eq:fixed_prototype_certificate}
\end{equation}
For $h^c=\mathrm e^c$, the bound is at least $L_{\kappa,\delta}$ on \emph{every} evaluation sample.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Projection onto label-measurable functions gives $\tau_\Phi^2\leq \mathbb E_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\|\phi-h^{c(x)}\|^2\right]$. The variable $\|\phi-h^{c(x)}\|^2/2$ lies in $[0,1]$, so Step~2 of Proposition~\ref{prop:exclusion_certificate} bounds its mean by $Q_{h,\delta}$ with failure probability $\delta/2$. Combine with the successor-variation confidence event by a union bound. For one-hot prototypes,
$\|\phi-\mathrm e^{c(x)}\|^2/2\leq(1-\phi_{c(x)})^2$ pointwise; monotonicity of the correction implies $Q_{h,\delta}\leq F_\delta$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:class_occupancy_bias}\leavevmode\par
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:exclusion_certificate}, let
\[
v_c:=\mathbb{E}_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[\|z-z_*^c\|^2\mid y_c=1\right],\qquad
u_c:=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\|\phi-\mu_\Phi^c\|^2\mid y_c=1\right].
\]
Conditional on the fixed construction,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}\!\left[\widehat s_M\right]
&=\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2-
\frac1M\sum_{c=1}^C v_c\bigl[1-(1-\pi_c)^M\bigr],\\
0\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2-
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}\!\left[\widehat s_M\right]
&\leq\frac{C}{M},\\
\tau_\Phi^2-\mathbb E_{\mathcal V_M\sim\mathbb P_{\mathcal V_M}}[\widehat t_M]
&=\frac1M\sum_{c=1}^Cu_c[1-(1-\pi_c)^M]\in[0,C/M].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:class_occupancy_bias}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Given $n_c=n>0$, the expected within-class sum of squared deviations from the sample mean is $(n-1)v_c$; for $n=0$ it is zero. The count $n_c$ has distribution $\mathbb P_{n_c}=\operatorname{Bin}(M,\pi_c)$, so
\[
\mathbb{E}_{n_c\sim\mathbb P_{n_c}}\!\left[n_c-\mathbbm1_{\{n_c>0\}}\right]
=M\pi_c-[1-(1-\pi_c)^M].
\]
Sum over classes and divide by $M$, using $\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2=\sum_c\pi_cv_c$.
The same calculation with $\phi$ in place of $z$ proves the input identity. Both conditional variances are at most $1$ because the vectors lie in $\Delta_C$.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:certificate_consistency}
Under the fixed-construction and i.i.d.-pair assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:exclusion_certificate}, for fixed finite $C$, fixed $\kappa\geq0$, and fixed $0<\delta<1$, the certificate satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\kappa,\delta,M}&\longrightarrow
\ell_\kappa(\Phi):=
\bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\kappa\sqrt{2A_{\mathrm{fid}}}\bigr)_+,\\
\widetilde L_{\kappa,\delta,M}&\longrightarrow
\widetilde\ell_\kappa(\Phi):=
\bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\kappa\tau_\Phi\bigr)_+
\geq\ell_\kappa(\Phi)
\end{aligned}
\quad\text{a.s. as }M\to\infty.
\label{eq:certificate_consistency}
\end{equation}
For either sequence, if its corresponding population limit exceeds a tolerance $\varepsilon\geq0$, it eventually exceeds that tolerance almost surely; if the limit is smaller, it eventually lies below the tolerance. Equality at the limit does not decide strict finite-sample exclusion.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The strong law gives $\widehat f_M\to A_{\mathrm{fid}}$, $n_c/M\to\pi_c$, and $\bar z_c\to z_*^c$ for each class of positive probability. Also,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat s_M
&=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^M\|z^i\|^2
-\sum_{c:n_c>0}\frac{n_c}{M}\|\bar z_c\|^2\\
&\longrightarrow
\mathbb{E}_{x^+\sim\mathbb{P}_{x^+}}\!\left[\|\Phi(x^+)\|^2\right]
-\sum_c\pi_c\|z_*^c\|^2
=\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sample_variance_consistency}
\end{equation}
The same strong-law argument with $\phi$ instead of $z$ yields $\widehat t_M\to\tau_\Phi^2$. Because $C$ is fixed and $r_{\delta,M}\to0$, we have $F_{\delta,M}\to A_{\mathrm{fid}}$, $V_{\delta,M}\to\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2$, and $T_{\delta,M}\to\tau_\Phi^2$. Continuity of the square root and positive-part functions proves both limits. A strict gap from $\varepsilon$ then proves the eventual inequalities.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{definition_200720261153}
Let
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
\label{eq_290620261445}
\mathcal S:=\{(x^i,y^i,x^{+,i})\}_{i=1}^N, \qquad x^{+,i}=F(x^i),
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
be a finite collection of observed one-step transitions, where $x^i$, $x^{+,i}$, and $y^i=y(x^i)$ are realizations of $x$, $x^+$, and $y$, respectively. The labels are supplied by the fixed reference-class map. The representation construction and the identities for a fixed number of matrix updates remain valid with dependent training transitions. Each statistical guarantee separately states the sampling assumptions it requires. For each reference class $c\in\{1,\cdots,C\}$, define
\[
\mathcal I^c:=\{i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}:y^i=\mathrm e^c\},\qquad N_c:=|\mathcal I^c|,
\]
and let $\mathrm I^c=(\mathrm I_1^c,\cdots,\mathrm I_{N_c}^c)$ denote the elements of $\mathcal I^c$ in increasing order. The class-$c$ reference matrix is
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
\label{eq_200720261205}
\mathbf X^c:=\left[x^{\mathrm I_1^c}\ \cdots\ x^{\mathrm I_{N_c}^c}\right]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{N_c\times n}.
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
Each KAHM dataset must contain at least two samples. <!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop_koopman_eigenfunction}\leavevmode\par
Assume that the soft-abstraction feature map is exactly closed under a matrix $B\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C}$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(F(x))=B^\top\Phi(x),\qquad x\in\mathcal X.
\label{eq:background_feature_closure}
\end{equation}
Let $Q_\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times r_\Phi}$ have orthonormal columns spanning $V_\Phi$, and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:background_reduced_matrices_QPhi}
\widetilde B_\Phi:=Q_\Phi^\top BQ_\Phi.
\end{equation}
For $\lambda\in\mathbb C$, the following statements hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every $w\in\mathbb{C}^C$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq_190720260749}
K_{F,\mathbb{C}}f_{w}=f_{Bw};
\end{equation}
in the unique reduced coordinates, this is
\[
K_{F,\mathbb C}f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde w}
=f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde B_\Phi\widetilde w},
\qquad \widetilde w\in\mathbb C^{r_\Phi};
\]

\item \label{prop_koopman_eigenfunction_1} the function $f_w$ is a Koopman eigenfunction with eigenvalue $\lambda$ if and only if
\[
P_\Phi^{\mathbb C}(w)\neq0,
\qquad
P_\Phi^{\mathbb C}(Bw-\lambda w)=0.
\]
In particular, $Bw=\lambda w$ is sufficient when $f_w\neq0$. In reduced coordinates, $f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde w}$ is a Koopman eigenfunction if and only if $\widetilde w\neq0$ and $\widetilde B_\Phi\widetilde w=\lambda\widetilde w$;

\item for every $\widetilde w\in\mathbb{C}^{r_\Phi}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq_190720260756}
K_{F,\mathbb{C}}^*f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde w}
=f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde B_\Phi^\top\widetilde w};
\end{equation}

\item \label{prop_koopman_eigenfunction_2} the function $f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde w}$ is an adjoint Koopman eigenfunction with eigenvalue $\lambda$ if and only if $\widetilde w\neq0$ and $\widetilde B_\Phi^\top\widetilde w=\lambda\widetilde w$;

\item \label{prop_koopman_eigenfunction_5} for a held-out set $\mathrm E_{\mathrm{spec}}=\{(x'^i,F(x'^i))\}_{i=1}^M$ and $v=(v_1,\cdots,v_M)^\top\in\mathbb{C}^M$, define
\begin{equation}
g_v:=\sum_{i=1}^M v_i\mathcal K(\cdot,x'^i)
=\Phi(\cdot)^\top\sum_{i=1}^M v_i\Phi(x'^i).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
\label{eq_210720260634}
\|K_{F,\mathbb{C}}^*g_v-\lambda g_v\|_{\mathcal H_\Phi^{\mathbb{C}}}^2
&=&\left\|\sum_{i=1}^M v_i\Phi(F(x'^i))-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^M v_i\Phi(x'^i)\right\|^2,\\
\label{eq_210720260635}
\|K_{F,\mathbb{C}}^*g_v\|_{\mathcal H_\Phi^{\mathbb{C}}}^2
&=&\left\|\sum_{i=1}^M v_i\Phi(F(x'^i))\right\|^2.
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{def_0307202260713}\leavevmode\par
For the held-out set $\mathrm E_{\mathrm{spec}}=\{(x'^i,F(x'^i))\}_{i=1}^M$, let
\[
g_v=\sum_{i=1}^M v_i\mathcal K(\cdot,x'^i),\qquad v\in\mathbb{C}^M.

\[

\]
Because $\rho$ is a squared ratio, this condition bounds the relative residual norm by $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. A tolerance $\varepsilon$ on the relative norm itself corresponds to $\rho\leq\varepsilon^2$.

The sampled states determine which kernel sections construct $g_v$. Under exact closure, their weighted sums give the RKHS norm of the residual for the entire function, as shown in Proposition~\ref{prop_koopman_eigenfunction}. Thus a zero residual establishes the adjoint eigenrelation for a nonzero $g_v$ whenever the denominator is positive. If $K_{F,\mathbb C}^*g_v=0$, this relative ratio is undefined. In particular, an exact eigenfunction with eigenvalue zero must be assessed with the unnormalized residual or with a positive normalization such as $\|g_v\|_{\mathcal H_\Phi^{\mathbb C}}^2$.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem_spectal_validation}
Suppose $(\lambda,\widetilde w)$ is an eigenpair of $\widetilde B_\Phi^\top$, with $\widetilde w\neq0$. It determines the candidate function $f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde w}$. To represent this function using kernel sections at the evaluation states $x'^1,\cdots,x'^M$, choose the minimum-norm solution of the following optimization problem:
\begin{equation}
\widehat v(\widetilde w;\Phi,\mathrm E_{\mathrm{spec}})
\in\operatorname{argmin}_{v\in\mathbb{C}^M}
\left(
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^M v_i\Phi(x'^i)-Q_\Phi\widetilde w\right\|^2
+\beta_{\mathrm{rep}}\|v\|^2
\right),\qquad \beta_{\mathrm{rep}}\ge0.
\end{equation}
For $\beta_{\mathrm{rep}}>0$, the minimizer is unique. For $\beta_{\mathrm{rep}}=0$, the minimum-norm convention selects a unique least-squares solution. The normalized squared error in approximating the candidate coefficient vector is
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}(\widehat v;\widetilde w,\Phi,\mathrm E_{\mathrm{spec}})
:=
\frac{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^M\widehat v_i\Phi(x'^i)-Q_\Phi\widetilde w\right\|^2}
{\|Q_\Phi\widetilde w\|^2}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is positive because $\|Q_\Phi\widetilde w\|=\|\widetilde w\|>0$. If $\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}=0$, the kernel sections represent the candidate exactly: $g_{\widehat v}=f_{Q_\Phi\widetilde w}$. If $\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}>0$, the residual is calculated for the approximating function $g_{\widehat v}$. A candidate with $g_{\widehat v}=0$ is excluded from the relative residual diagnostic.

Exact closure also makes the weighted successor sum independent of the weights used to represent a given function. To see this, if $\sum_i u_i\Phi(x'^i)=0$, then
\[
\sum_i u_i\Phi(F(x'^i))
=B^\top\sum_i u_i\Phi(x'^i)=0.
\]
If this implication fails, two sets of weights can represent the same current function but produce different successor sums. In that case, the finite-sum diagnostic depends on the chosen weights and sample, so it cannot be assigned to that function as the residual of the stated RKHS adjoint.

<!-- problem-1: body_md -->
\label{problem_central}
Fix the reference construction and a chosen map $\Phi\in\mathcal G_{\mathcal S}$, or its specified numerical implementation. Fit $B\in\mathbb R^{C\times C}$ to reduce the expected one-step prediction error:
\begin{equation}
\inf_{B\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C}}
R_{\mathbb{P}_x}(\Phi,B).
\label{eq:conditional_population_objective}
\end{equation}
For a fitted pair $(\Phi,\widehat B)$, we distinguish a population accuracy requirement from empirical prediction and spectral criteria:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]

\item \textbf{Population one-step prediction accuracy.}
For a prescribed tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cl}}>0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_{\mathbb{P}_x}(\Phi)
\le
R_{\mathbb{P}_x}(\Phi,\widehat B)^{1/2}
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cl}}.
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Multistep prediction and simplex checks.}
For a prescribed horizon $t$ and tolerances
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{t-step}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{simplex}}>0$,
an evaluation set $\mathrm E_t$ independent of model construction and
fitting satisfies
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
\widehat R_{\mathrm E_t}(\Phi,\widehat B)^{1/2}
&\le&
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{t-step}},\\
\widehat\nu_{\mathrm E_t}(\Phi,\widehat B)^{1/2}
&\le&
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{simplex}}.
\end{IEEEeqnarray}

\item \textbf{Spectral diagnostics.}
For a candidate reduced-matrix eigenpair
$(\widehat\lambda,\widehat w)$ and tolerances
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rep}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{spec}}>0$,
an independent spectral set $\mathrm E_{\mathrm{spec}}$ satisfies
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}
(\widehat v;\widehat w,\Phi,\mathrm E_{\mathrm{spec}})
&\le&
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rep}},\\
\rho
(\widehat\lambda,\widehat v;\Phi,\mathrm E_{\mathrm{spec}})
&\le&
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{spec}}.
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
\end{enumerate}
Criterion (R1) specifies population prediction risk, whereas (R2) measures finite-sample prediction error and simplex feasibility. Population interpretation of (R2) requires a separate statistical guarantee. Criterion (R3) evaluates the representation error and a weighted-feature residual; its tolerances apply to squared ratios and do not independently certify Koopman invariance.

Representation selection must also account for coordinate variation. In the exact normalized-affinity family, $\omega\downarrow0$ makes the coordinates uniformly approach $C^{-1}\mathbf 1_C$, for which identity-matrix prediction has vanishing error. The forecasting selection protocol therefore considers variation as well as error; Problem~\ref{problem_central} is formulated for the resulting fixed map.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:kahm_margin_fidelity}
Fix the reference-class map, the KAHM reconstruction maps, $\tau>0$, and a finite exponent $\omega>0$. For $C\geq2$ and thresholds $g_c>0$, define
\begin{align}
 \eta_c&:=\mathbb{P}_x\!\left(G_c(x)<g_c\mid y_c=1\right),
 &\vartheta_c&:=\frac{(C-1)e^{-\omega g_c}}
 {1+(C-1)e^{-\omega g_c}},
 \label{eq:kahm_margin_parameters}\\
 b_c&:=(1-\eta_c)\vartheta_c^2+\eta_c,
 &A_{\mathrm{geom}}&:=\sum_{c=1}^C\pi_cb_c.
 \label{eq:kahm_geometric_fidelity}
\end{align}
Then, for every reference class $c$,
\begin{align}
 \epsilon_c^2&\leq b_c,
 &m_c&\geq\pi_c(1-\eta_c)(1-\vartheta_c)^2,
 \label{eq:kahm_margin_fidelity_mass}\\
 A_{\mathrm{fid}}&\leq A_{\mathrm{geom}}\leq1,
 &a_\Phi^2&\leq2A_{\mathrm{geom}}.
 \label{eq:kahm_margin_aggregate}
\end{align}
The probability $\eta_c$ measures how often states labelled $c$ have margin below $g_c$. For the remaining states, $1-\Phi_c(x)\leq\vartheta_c$. Thus $b_c$ averages the squared-loss bound $\vartheta_c^2$ where the margin holds and the bound $1$ where it fails. Weighting these class bounds by $\pi_c$ gives $A_{\mathrm{geom}}$. The subscript ``geom'' labels this bound obtained from reconstruction-score margins; it does not denote a metric or imply coordinate invariance. A known upper bound $\overline\eta_c\in[\eta_c,1]$ can replace the unknown $\eta_c$ in these bounds. For $C=1$, normalization directly gives $\Phi\equiv1$ and $A_{\mathrm{fid}}=a_\Phi=0$.

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Normalization gives
\begin{equation}
\Phi_c(x)=\frac{1}{1+S_c(x)},\qquad
S_c(x)=\sum_{k\ne c}\left(\frac{a_k(x)}{a_c(x)}\right)^\omega.
\label{eq:kahm_competing_affinity_sum}
\end{equation}
On the event $\mathcal E_c:=\{G_c\geq g_c\}$, each competing affinity ratio $a_k/a_c$ is at most $e^{-g_c}$. Hence
$S_c\leq(C-1)e^{-\omega g_c}$ and $1-\Phi_c\leq\vartheta_c$.
On its complement, $0\leq1-\Phi_c\leq1$. Splitting the conditional expectation over these events gives
$\epsilon_c^2\leq(1-\eta_c)\vartheta_c^2+\eta_c=b_c$.
Also,
\[
m_c\geq\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\Phi_c(x)^2\mathbbm1_{\{y_c=1\}\cap\mathcal E_c}\right]
\geq\pi_c(1-\eta_c)(1-\vartheta_c)^2.
\]
Averaging $b_c\in[0,1]$ and applying Theorem~\ref{theorem_300320262124} proves~\eqref{eq:kahm_margin_aggregate}.
Increasing $\eta_c$ enlarges the assignment-error upper bounds and decreases the second-moment lower bound. Replacing it by $\overline\eta_c\geq\eta_c$ therefore preserves all conclusions. For $C=1$, normalization gives $\Phi\equiv1$.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:kahm_reconstruction_margin}
Let $C\geq2$. For each reference class $c$, let
$0\leq\overline\eta_c\leq1$. Suppose that, conditional on $y_c=1$,
an event of probability at least $1-\overline\eta_c$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_c(x)\leq t_c^-<t_c^+
       \leq\min_{k\ne c}\mathcal T_k(x),
 \qquad 0\leq t_c^-<t_c^+\leq1.
 \label{eq:kahm_folding_separation}
\end{equation}
Then the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:kahm_margin_fidelity} are
satisfied with
\begin{equation}
 g_c=\log\frac{1-t_c^-+\tau}{1-t_c^++\tau}>0,
 \qquad
 \eta_c\leq\overline\eta_c.
 \label{eq:kahm_folding_to_log_margin}
\end{equation}

The score separation can be checked through reconstruction distances and angles. On the same event, require a small distance and angular error for the labelled class, and a sufficiently large distance for every competing class:
\begin{equation}
 \|x-\mathcal A_c(x)\|\leq r_c^-,
 \qquad
 \mathcal T^{\mathrm{Cos}}_c(x)\leq\upsilon_c,
 \qquad
 \min_{k\ne c}\|x-\mathcal A_k(x)\|\geq r_c^+,
 \label{eq:kahm_reconstruction_separation}
\end{equation}
where $r_c^-,r_c^+\geq0$ and $\upsilon_c\in[0,1]$, provided that
\begin{equation}
 t_c^-:=
 \sqrt{\frac{(1-e^{-r_c^-})^2+\upsilon_c^2}{2}}
 <t_c^+:=
 \frac{1-e^{-r_c^+}}{\sqrt{2}}.
 \label{eq:kahm_reconstruction_to_folding}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
On the stated score-separation event, $a_c\geq1-t_c^-+\tau$ and
$a_k\leq1-t_c^++\tau$ for every $k\ne c$. Thus
$G_c\geq\log((1-t_c^-+\tau)/(1-t_c^++\tau))>0$, and margin failure has conditional probability at most $\overline\eta_c$.
For the reconstruction-level condition, monotonicity of $1-e^{-r}$ gives
\[
\mathcal T_c(x)\leq
\sqrt{\bigl((1-e^{-r_c^-})^2+\upsilon_c^2\bigr)/2}=t_c^-,
\qquad
\mathcal T_k(x)\geq(1-e^{-r_c^+})/\sqrt2=t_c^+.
\]
The second inequality discards the nonnegative squared angular contribution. These inequalities establish the required score separation.

<!-- proposition-12: body_md -->
\label{prop:attainable_closure}
For $\kappa\geq0$, define
\[
\mathcal D_\kappa(\Phi)
:=\inf_{\|B\|_2\leq\kappa}R_{\mathbb P_x}(\Phi,B)^{1/2},
\qquad (t)_+:=\max(t,0).
\]
If $\kappa\geq\|B_*\|_2$, then
\begin{equation}
(\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\kappa a_\Phi)_+
\leq\mathcal D_\kappa(\Phi)
\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}+L_{\mathrm{proto}}\sqrt{A_{\mathrm{fid}}}.
\label{eq:two_sided_closure}
\end{equation}
For the reconstruction-score construction, this implies
\begin{equation}
(\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\kappa\sqrt{2A_{\mathrm{geom}}})_+
\leq\mathcal D_\kappa(\Phi)
\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}+L_{\mathrm{proto}}\sqrt{A_{\mathrm{geom}}}.
\label{eq:two_sided_geometric_closure}
\end{equation}
The lower bounds hold for every norm limit $\kappa\geq0$. The upper bounds use $B_*$ as a feasible predictor and therefore require $\kappa\geq\|B_*\|_2$. Each row of $B_*$ belongs to $\Delta_C$, which gives $\|B_*\|_2\leq\sqrt C$. Thus $\kappa=\sqrt C$ always includes this predictor in the permitted matrix class.
<!-- end proposition-12 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Conditional centering of $\xi_{\mathrm{res}}=z-B_*^\top y$ gives, for every $B$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\|z(x)-B^\top y(x)\|^2\right]
&=\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2+
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\|(B_*-B)^\top y(x)\|^2\right]\\
&\geq\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality therefore yields
$R_{\mathbb P_x}(\Phi,B)^{1/2}\geq
\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}-\|B\|_2a_\Phi$.
Taking the infimum over $\|B\|_2\leq\kappa$ proves the lower bound.
For the upper bound, use the feasible matrix $B_*$ and~\eqref{eq:fidelity_direct_risk}, whose discrepancy term is then zero.
Theorem~\ref{thm:kahm_margin_fidelity} gives~\eqref{eq:two_sided_geometric_closure}.
Finally, $\|B_*\|_2\leq\|B_*\|_F\leq\sqrt C$.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rem:stochastic_risk}
Let $(x,x^+)$ have fixed joint evaluation distribution $\mathbb P_{x,x^+}$, with current-state marginal $\mathbb P_x$, and write $z=\Phi(x^+)$. Define
\[
m(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x^+\sim\mathbb P(\cdot\mid x)}\!\left[\Phi(x^+)\right].
\]
The vector $m(x)$ is the mean next-state coordinate vector conditional on the full current state. In this expectation, $x$ is the conditioning random variable; only the successor is averaged over its conditional distribution. The prediction error has two parts: error in predicting this mean and random variation around it. The standard variance decomposition gives \citep{colbrook2024stochastic}
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[\|z-B^\top\Phi(x)\|^2\right]
&=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\|m(x)-B^\top\Phi(x)\|^2\right]\\
&\quad+\mathbb{E}_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[\|z-m(x)\|^2\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:attainable_closure} also holds for this joint-law risk. By conditioning first on the current state and then on its label, the class mean can be written in either form:
\[
\begin{aligned}
z_*^c
&=\mathbb{E}_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[z\mid y_c=1\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[m(x)\mid y_c=1\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^2
=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathbb P_x}\!\left[\|m(x)-z_*^{c(x)}\|^2\right]+\mathbb{E}_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[\|z-m(x)\|^2\right].
\]
The same conditional-centering argument proves both bounds. Here successor variation has two sources: different states in one class may have different conditional mean successors, and the successor may vary randomly even at a fixed state. This decomposition describes the stochastic experiments' one-step prediction errors; the spectral identities retain their deterministic-transition assumptions.

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{prop:evaluable_risk}\leavevmode\par
Fix $\Phi$, $\widehat B$, and $A^\top=[a^1\ \cdots\ a^C]$ with $a^c\in\Delta_C$, using only construction, fitting, and selection data. Each column $a^c$ of $A^\top$ may be an estimate, obtained from training data, of the mean successor coordinates for class $c$. Draw $M$ independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) realizations $(x^i,x^{+,i})$ from the fixed joint evaluation distribution $\mathbb P_{x,x^+}$, independently of the fitted objects. Set $z=\Phi(x^+)$, $\phi^i=\Phi(x^i)$, $z^i=\Phi(x^{+,i})$, and $c_i=c(x^i)$. Define
\begin{align*}
L_A&:=\max_{c,k}\|a^c-a^k\|,
&d_A&:=\max_c\|a^c-\widehat B^\top\mathrm e^c\|,\\
\widehat f&:=M^{-1}\sum_i(1-(\phi^i)_{c_i})^2,
&\widehat v&:=M^{-1}\sum_i\|z^i-a^{c_i}\|^2,\\
\widehat e&:=M^{-1}\sum_i\|(A-\widehat B)^\top\phi^i\|^2,
&\alpha_\delta&:=\sqrt{\frac{\log(3/\delta)}{2M}}.
\end{align*}
For $0<\delta<1$, put
\begin{align*}
U_f&:=\min(1,\widehat f+\alpha_\delta),
&U_v&:=\min(2,\widehat v+2\alpha_\delta),\\
U_e&:=\min(d_A^2,\widehat e+d_A^2\alpha_\delta).
\end{align*}
Conditional on the fitted objects, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
\left(\mathbb{E}_{(x,x^+)\sim\mathbb P}\!\left[\|z-\widehat B^\top\Phi(x)\|^2\right]\right)^{1/2}
\leq L_A\sqrt{U_f}+\sqrt{U_v}+\sqrt{U_e}.
\label{eq:evaluable_risk}
\end{equation}
For $x^+=F(x)$, the left-hand side is $R_{\mathbb P_x}(\Phi,\widehat B)^{1/2}$.
<!-- end proposition-13 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Write $y=\mathrm e^{c(x)}$, $\phi=\Phi(x)$, and decompose
\[
z-\widehat B^\top\phi
=A^\top(y-\phi)+(z-A^\top y)+(A-\widehat B)^\top\phi.
\]
The first component has norm at most $L_A(1-\phi_{c(x)})$, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:prototype_diameter}.
The summands defining $\widehat f,\widehat v,\widehat e$ lie in $[0,1]$, $[0,2]$, and $[0,d_A^2]$, respectively; the last bound follows by convexity of the norm. Hoeffding's inequality, with failure probability $\delta/3$ for each loss, bounds their expectations by $U_f,U_v,U_e$ simultaneously with probability at least $1-\delta$. If $d_A=0$, the last loss vanishes identically. The $L^2$ triangle inequality proves~\eqref{eq:evaluable_risk}.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:direct_risk_upper}
The decomposition in Proposition~\ref{prop:evaluable_risk} helps explain error sources. A direct upper confidence bound is simpler when only the fitted predictor's total risk is needed. Fix $\Phi$ and $\widehat B$ before drawing $M$ i.i.d. transition pairs. Define
\[
H_{\widehat B}:=\max_{1\leq j,c\leq C}
\|\mathrm e^j-\widehat B^\top\mathrm e^c\|^2,
\qquad
\widehat R_M:=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^M
\|z^i-\widehat B^\top\phi^i\|^2.
\]
Every loss is at most $H_{\widehat B}$. Indeed,
$z-\widehat B^\top\phi=\sum_{j,c}z_j\phi_c(\mathrm e^j-\widehat B^\top\mathrm e^c)$ is a convex combination, and the squared norm is convex. Hoeffding's inequality therefore gives, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathbb P}(\Phi,\widehat B)
\leq\min\left\{H_{\widehat B},\,
\widehat R_M+H_{\widehat B}
\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2M}}\right\}.
\label{eq:direct_risk_upper}
\end{equation}
The square root of this bound gives an upper confidence bound on RMSE for one predictor fixed before evaluation, including under stochastic successors. It is computed directly from the fitted predictor and evaluation transitions. Joint comparison with the lower certificate requires allocating the total failure probability between the bounds. Empirical evaluation of these two fitted-risk upper bounds is outside the scope of the reported campaigns.
