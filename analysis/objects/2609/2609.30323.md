<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
An edge labeling $\lambda: \mathcal{E}(\mathrm{P}) \rightarrow \Lambda$ of $\mathrm{P}$ is called \textit{edge-lexicographical} (EL), if for all $s \leqslant t$ in $\mathrm{P}$, there exists a unique maximal chain $C$
\[
s = u_0 \lessdot u_1 \lessdot \cdots \lessdot u_{k} = t
\]
between $s$ and $t$ with weakly increasing labels as the rank increases: 
\[
\lambda(u_0, u_1) \trianglelefteqslant \lambda(u_1, u_2) \trianglelefteqslant \cdots \trianglelefteqslant \lambda(u_{k-2}, u_{k-1}) \trianglelefteqslant \lambda(u_{k-1}, u_k),
\] and the maximal chain $C$ is lexicographically minimal among all maximal chains from $s$ to $t$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
The poset $\mathrm{P}$ is \textit{rank-uniform} if for all $s, t \in \mathrm{P}$ of the same rank and for every positive integer $k$, the number of elements of rank $k + \operatorname{rk} s$ above $s$ is equal to the number of elements of rank $k + \operatorname{rk} s$ above $t$: 
\begin{equation*}
    W_k(s) = W_{k}(t).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end definition-2 -->

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $(\mathrm{P}, \lambda)$ be an EL-labeled poset. We say that $(\mathrm{P}, \lambda)$ is \textit{rank-uniform} if the following holds.
\begin{itemize}
    \item (Uniform width) For all $s, s' \in \mathrm{P}$ of same rank and for all $i \geqslant 1$, the widths are equal: $\omega_s(i) = \omega_{s'}(i).$
    \item (Uniform descent) For all $s, s' \in \mathrm{P}$ of same rank and for every covering relations $s \lessdot t$ and $s' \lessdot t'$ such that $\mathrm{Ind}(s,t) = \mathrm{Ind}(s',t')$, the descent counts are equal: $\mathrm{des}_{s}(t) = \mathrm{des}_{s'}(t').$
\end{itemize}

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $(\mathrm{P}, \lambda)$ be a rank-uniform labeled poset, and for all $0 \leqslant k \leqslant \operatorname{rk}(\mathrm{P})-1$ denote by $\ell_k$ the number of distinct labels above any element of rank $k$. The labeled poset $(\mathrm{P}, \lambda)$ is \textit{monotonic} if for all $0 \leqslant k \leqslant \operatorname{rk}(\mathrm{P})-1$ the function $\mathrm{des}_k(\mathbin{\vcenter{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bullet$}}}})$ is weakly increasing on $[\ell_k]$. A poset $\mathrm{P}$ admitting an EL-labeling $\lambda$ such that $(\mathrm{P}, \lambda)$ is a monotonic rank-uniform labeled poset is called \textit{uniformly monotonically edge-lexicographically shellable}, or \textit{UMEL-shellable} for short.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:1}
Let $(P,\lambda)$ be an EL-labeled poset, let $s\in P$, and let $T$ be the set of elements covering $s$. If $t_1,t_2\in T$ and there exists an element $u\in P$ such that $t_1$ and $t_2$ are the only elements of $T$ covered by $u$, then \[ \lambda(s,t_1) \neq \lambda(s,t_2). \]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The interval $[s,u]$ has exactly two maximal chains,
    \[s\lessdot t_1\lessdot u\qquad\text{and}\qquad s\lessdot t_2\lessdot u.\]
    Since $(P,\lambda)$ is EL-labeled, exactly one of these chains is increasing, while the other is decreasing. Suppose, for contradiction, that
    \[\lambda(s,t_1)=\lambda(s,t_2).\]
    Then the two chains have the same label on their first edge. Consequently, their lexicographic order is determined by the labels on their second edges. Since one chain is increasing and the other is decreasing, the decreasing chain has a smaller second label and is therefore lexicographically minimal. This contradicts the EL-labeling property, which requires the unique increasing maximal chain to be lexicographically minimal.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:2}
Let $(P,\lambda)$ be an EL-labeled poset, and let $T$ be the set of elements covering $s$. Then there exists a unique $t\in T$ such that \[ \operatorname{des}_s(t)=0. \]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $t\in T$ be such that $\lambda(s,t)$ is minimal among $\{\lambda(s,x):x\in T\}$. By an argument analogous to that in Lemma \ref{lem:1}, we have \[ \operatorname{des}_s(t)=0. \] Now suppose, for contradiction, that there exists another element $u\in T$ such that \[ \operatorname{des}_s(u)=0. \] By the EL-labeling property, the interval $[t,\widehat{1}]$ has a unique increasing maximal chain \[ t\lessdot t_1 \lessdot t_2\lessdot\cdots\lessdot\widehat{1}. \] Since $\operatorname{des}_s(t)=0$, the chain \[ s\lessdot t\lessdot t_1\lessdot t_2\lessdot\cdots\lessdot\widehat{1} \] is increasing. Similarly, the interval $[u,\widehat{1}]$ has an increasing maximal chain \[ u\lessdot u_1\lessdot u_2\lessdot\cdots\lessdot\widehat{1}, \] and since $\operatorname{des}_s(u)=0$, the chain \[ s\lessdot u\lessdot u_1\lessdot u_2\lessdot\cdots\lessdot\widehat{1} \] is also increasing. Thus, $[s,\widehat{1}]$ contains two distinct increasing maximal chains, contradicting the uniqueness of the increasing maximal chain in an EL-labeling.
<!-- end proof-2 -->

Let $(P,\lambda)$ be a labeled poset. Then, for every $s\in P$, \[ \omega_s(1)=1. \]

    monotonic rank-uniform labeled poset. By Corollary~\ref{cor:1}, we have
    \[
    \omega_{\widehat{0}}(1)=1.
    \]

    covering $\widehat{0}$ is either
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_1)
    \qquad\text{or}\qquad
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_2).
    \]
    
    Since $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_4\lessdot y_7$ and
    $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_4\lessdot y_8$ are forced to be increasing, if
    $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_4\lessdot y_6$ were also increasing, then
    $\mathrm{des}_{\widehat{0}}(x_4)=0$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:2}.

    $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_3\lessdot y_6$ must be increasing. Now suppose that
    $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_3\lessdot y_5$ is also increasing. By the
    EL-labeling property, this chain must be lexicographically minimal in
    $[\widehat{0},y_5]$, and hence
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_3)\trianglelefteqslant\lambda(\widehat{0},x_2).
    \]
    By Corollary~\ref{cor:1}, these two labels are distinct, so
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_3)\vartriangleleft\lambda(\widehat{0},x_2).
    \]
    It follows that $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_3\lessdot y_4$ is also increasing.
    Consequently, $\mathrm{des}_{\widehat{0}}(x_3)=0$, again contradicting
    Lemma~\ref{lem:2}.
        
    Therefore, the chains
    \[
    \widehat{0}\lessdot x_3\lessdot y_4
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \widehat{0}\lessdot x_3\lessdot y_5
    \]
    must be decreasing. Moreover, by Lemma~\ref{lem:1},
    \[
    \lambda(x_3,y_4)\neq\lambda(x_3,y_5).
    \]
    Hence,
    \[
    \operatorname{des}_{\widehat{0}}(x_3)=2.
    \]
    On the other hand,
    \[
    \operatorname{des}_{\widehat{0}}(x_4)=1.
    \]
    By monotonicity, we must therefore have
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_4)
    \trianglelefteqslant
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_3).
    \]
    Since these labels are distinct, Lemma~\ref{lem:1} gives
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_4)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_3).

    \widehat{0}\lessdot x_3\lessdot y_6

    lexicographically minimal, contradicting the EL-labeling property.

\label{AIA}
    Let $P$ be a bounded graded rank-uniform poset of rank $3$ with \(|\mathrm{At}(P)|=n\) and \( W_1(s)=k\) for every atom $s\in P$. Thus, every atom of $P$ is covered by exactly
    $k$ elements of rank $2$. We may construct $P$ by inserting its atoms one
    at a time as follows.
    
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Choose an ordering of the atoms of $P$,
        \[
        (x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n).
        \]
    
        \item Begin with the atom $x_1$. Let \(y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_k\) be the $k$ elements covering $x_1$, and define
        \[
        P_1=
        \{\widehat{0},x_1,y_1,\ldots,y_k,\widehat{1}\},
        \]
        with cover relations
        \[
        \widehat{0}\lessdot x_1\lessdot y_j\lessdot\widehat{1},
        \qquad 1\leq j\leq k.
        \]
    
        \item Suppose that $P_{i-1}$ has been constructed using the atoms
        $x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1}$. To insert $x_i$, consider the $k$ elements of
        $P$ that cover $x_i$. We partition them into two sets:
        \[
        \operatorname{icov}(x_i)
        =
        \{y_{i_1},\ldots,y_{i_t}\},
        \]
        consisting of those elements that are already present in $P_{i-1}$, and
        \[
        \operatorname{ocov}(x_i)
        =
        \{y_{i_{t+1}},\ldots,y_{i_k}\},
        \]
        consisting of those elements that have not yet appeared and are inserted
        together with $x_i$.
    
        We then obtain $P_i$ from $P_{i-1}$ by adding $x_i$, the elements in
        $\operatorname{ocov}(x_i)$, and the cover relations
        \[
        \widehat{0}\lessdot x_i\lessdot y
        \qquad
        \text{for every }
        y\in \operatorname{icov}(x_i)\cup\operatorname{ocov}(x_i),
        \]
        together with
        \[
        y\lessdot\widehat{1}
        \qquad
        \text{for every }
        y\in\operatorname{ocov}(x_i).
        \]
    \end{itemize}
    After all $n$ atoms have been inserted, we obtain $P_n=P$.

The existence of such an ordering is equivalent to the connectedness of the subgraph of the Hasse diagram induced by the elements of ranks $1$ and $2$. Indeed, since $P$ has rank $3$, this graph is precisely the order complex of

\label{theorem-1}
    Let $\mathrm{P}$ be an EL-shellable poset of rank-3. If $\mathrm{P}$ is rank-uniform, then there is an EL-labeling $\lambda$ of $\mathrm{P}$ such that $(\mathrm{P}, \lambda)$ is a rank-uniform EL-labeled poset.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We construct the labeling $\lambda$ simultaneously with $P$ using the
    Atoms Insertion Algorithm~\ref{AIA}. Let
    \[
    (x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)
    \]
    be an ordering of the atoms of $P$ such that
    \[
    \operatorname{icov}(x_i)\neq\varnothing
    \qquad\text{for every }2\leq i\leq n,
    \]
    as guaranteed by the previous claim. Label the edges between ranks $0$ and
    $1$ so that
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_1)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_2)
    \vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_n).
    \]
    
    We now construct the remaining labels inductively. Let
    $y_1,\ldots,y_k$ be the $k$ elements covering $x_1$. Choose the labels so
    that
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_1)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_1,y_1)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_1,y_2)
    \vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_1,y_k).
    \]
    We then label the edges from rank $2$ to $\widehat{1}$ so that
    \[
    \lambda(y_2,\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft
    \lambda(y_k,\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_1,y_1)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(y_1,\widehat{1}).
    \]
    If $n\geq 2$, we further choose these labels so that
    \[
    \lambda(y_j,\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_2)
    \qquad\text{for every }1\leq j\leq k.
    \]
    Thus, $x_1\lessdot y_1\lessdot\widehat{1}$ is the unique increasing maximal
    chain in $[x_1,\widehat{1}]$, while
    \[
    \widehat{0}\lessdot x_1\lessdot y_1\lessdot\widehat{1}
    \]
    is the unique increasing maximal chain in $[\widehat{0},\widehat{1}]$.
    
    Suppose now that the edges of $P_{i-1}$ have been labeled, and write
    \[
    \operatorname{icov}(x_i)
    =
    \{y_{i_1},\ldots,y_{i_t}\},
    \qquad
    \operatorname{ocov}(x_i)
    =
    \{y_{i_{t+1}},\ldots,y_{i_k}\}.
    \]
    By the induction hypothesis,
    \[
    \lambda(y,\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_i)
    \qquad
    \text{for every }y\in\operatorname{icov}(x_i).
    \]
    Since $\operatorname{icov}(x_i)\neq\varnothing$, choose
    $y_{i_1}\in\operatorname{icov}(x_i)$ and assign the labels on the edges
    from $x_i$ to $\operatorname{icov}(x_i)$ so that
    \[
    \lambda(x_i,y_{i_1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(y_{i_1},\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_i),
    \]
    whereas, for $2\leq r\leq t$,
    \[
    \lambda(y_{i_r},\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_i,y_{i_r})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_i).
    \]
    We may choose these labels so that
    \[
    \lambda(x_i,y_{i_1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_i,y_{i_2})
    \vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_i,y_{i_t}).
    \]
    Hence,
    \[
    x_i\lessdot y_{i_1}\lessdot\widehat{1}
    \]
    is the unique increasing maximal chain in $[x_i,\widehat{1}]$, and it is
    lexicographically minimal.
    
    For the elements in $\operatorname{ocov}(x_i)$, choose the labels so that
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_i)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_i,y_{i_{t+1}})
    \vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_i,y_{i_k}),
    \]
    and assign their upper labels so that
    \[
    \lambda(y_{i_{t+1}},\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft
    \lambda(y_{i_k},\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_i).
    \]
    Consequently, every chain through an element of
    $\operatorname{ocov}(x_i)$ has a descent before reaching $\widehat{1}$.
    Moreover, if $i<n$, then
    \[
    \lambda(y,\widehat{1})
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_i)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_{i+1})
    \]
    for every $y\in\operatorname{ocov}(x_i)$, so the induction hypothesis is
    preserved.
    
    It remains to verify that $\lambda$ is an EL-labeling. For an interval
    $[\widehat{0},y]$ with $y$ of rank $2$, let $x_i$ be the first atom in the
    chosen ordering that is covered by $y$. At the stage when $y$ is first
    inserted, we have
    \[
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_i)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(x_i,y),
    \]
    so $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_i\lessdot y$ is increasing. For every later atom
    $x_j$ covered by $y$, the element $y$ belongs to
    $\operatorname{icov}(x_j)$, and therefore
    \[
    \lambda(x_j,y)
    \vartriangleleft
    \lambda(\widehat{0},x_j).
    \]
    Thus, $\widehat{0}\lessdot x_i\lessdot y$ is the unique increasing maximal
    chain in $[\widehat{0},y]$. Since $x_i$ is the first atom covering $y$, this
    chain is also lexicographically minimal.
    
    Similarly, for every atom $x_i$, the construction makes
    \[
    x_i\lessdot y_{i_1}\lessdot\widehat{1}
    \]
    the unique increasing and lexicographically minimal maximal chain in
    $[x_i,\widehat{1}]$. Finally,
    \[
    \widehat{0}\lessdot x_1\lessdot y_1\lessdot\widehat{1}
    \]
    is the unique increasing maximal chain in $[\widehat{0},\widehat{1}]$, and it is
    lexicographically minimal because $\lambda(\widehat{0},x_1)$ is the smallest
    label on an edge covering $\widehat{0}$. Hence, $\lambda$ is an EL-labeling of
    $P$.
    
    It remains to show that $(P,\lambda)$ is rank-uniform as a labeled poset.
    By construction, the labels on the edges above each atom are distinct.
    Since every atom is covered by exactly $k$ elements, the width condition is
    therefore the same for every atom. Furthermore, for each atom $x_i$, there
    is exactly one element $y$ covering $x_i$ for which
    \[
    \operatorname{des}_{x_i}(y)=0,
    \]
    namely the element defining the unique increasing chain in
    $[x_i,\widehat{1}]$, while
    \[
    \operatorname{des}_{x_i}(y)=1
    \]
    for every other element $y$ covering $x_i$. Thus, the descent condition is
    also uniform over all atoms. Therefore, $(P,\lambda)$ is a rank-uniform
    EL-labeled poset.

\label{theorem-3}
Let $P$ be a bounded graded rank-uniform poset of rank $3$. Suppose that,
for every ordering $(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$ of the atoms of $P$,
\[
\operatorname{icov}(x_i)\neq\varnothing
\qquad\text{for every }2\leq i\leq n.
\]
Then $P$ is UMEL-shellable.
<!-- end maintheorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
We construct an ordering $(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$ of the atoms greedily.
Choose $x_1$ arbitrarily. Suppose that
$x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{i-1}$ have already been chosen. Among the remaining
atoms, choose $x_i$ so that
\[
|\operatorname{icov}(x_i)|
\]
is minimal.

By hypothesis,
\[
|\operatorname{icov}(x_i)|>0
\qquad\text{for every }i\geq 2.
\]
Moreover, as more atoms are inserted, the quantity
$|\operatorname{icov}(s)|$ can only increase for each remaining atom $s$.
Since $x_i$ is chosen to minimize this quantity among all remaining atoms,
it follows that
\[
0<
|\operatorname{icov}(x_2)|
\leq
|\operatorname{icov}(x_3)|
\leq\cdots\leq
|\operatorname{icov}(x_n)|.
\]
Therefore, Theorem~\ref{theorem-2} implies that $P$ is UMEL-shellable.
