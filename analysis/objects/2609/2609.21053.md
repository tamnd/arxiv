Since $\mathbf{C}_t = R_t^T W_t R_t$ with $W_t \succ 0$, the augmented matrix $\mathbf{C}_t$ is positive-semidefinite automatically. No regularization is needed. Moreover, the elements of $\mathbf{C}_t$ encoding the gradients and value are exact.
\par \textbf{The full augmented cost-to-go matrix is positive-semidefinite.} Since $\mathbf{C}_t \succeq 0$ and $\mathbf{S}_{t+1} \succeq 0$ implies $F_t^T \mathbf{S}_{t+1} F_t \succeq 0$, their sum $\mathbf{Q}_t = \mathbf{C}_t + F_t^T \mathbf{S}_{t+1} F_t \succeq 0$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qt}). In turn, $\mathbf{S}_t$ is the Schur complement of $Q_\mathbf{uu} \succ 0$ in $\mathbf{Q}_t \succeq 0$, so $\mathbf{S}_t \succeq 0$ as well. Since $\mathbf{S}_\ell = \mathbf{C}_\ell \succeq 0$, the argument applies by induction to all $t$. This property\textemdash positive-semidefiniteness of the \emph{full augmented} cost-to-go matrix, not just its Hessian block\textemdash is the key enabler of the square root formulation that follows in Section~\ref{sec:sqrt}.
\end{enumerate}
