<!-- head-1: body_md -->
For an integrable function \(h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{C}\), write \( \pi(h):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}h(x)\,\pi(dx) \). Throughout, \(L^2(\pi)\) denotes the complex Hilbert space
\(L^2(\mathcal{X},\pi;\mathbb{C})\), equipped with \( \langle g,h\rangle :=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\overline{g(x)}h(x)\,\pi(dx) \) and \( \| h \|_\pi^2 :=\sqrt{\langle h, h \rangle} \). Let \( L_0^2(\pi) := \{h\in L^2(\pi):\pi(h)=0\} \) denote its closed subspace of mean-zero functions. For a bounded operator \(A\) on \(L^2(\pi)\), let \(\| A \|\) denote its operator norm. For a bounded \( f \), let \( M_f: L^2(\pi) \to L^2(\pi) \) denote the multiplication operator, i.e., for \( h \in L^2(\pi) \), \( (M_f h)(x) = f(x)h(x) \). For \( u, v \in L^2(\pi) \), the operator \( u \otimes v\) acts as \( (u\otimes v) h = \langle v, h \rangle u \). For a bounded operator \( A \), \( \operatorname{spec}(A) \) denotes its spectrum, i.e.\ the set of \( z \in \mathbb{C} \) such that \( A - z I \) has no bounded inverse. Let \( \mathbf 1 \) denote the constant function \( x \mapsto 1 \), while \( e^f \) stands for \( x \mapsto e^{f(x)} \). The notation \( \mathbb{P}_\pi \) and \( \mathbb{E}_\pi \)  is used to highlight stationarity, i.e., \( X_1 \sim \pi \). All pointwise bounds on the observables below are understood \( \pi \)-a.e.; this suffices because \( \| M_g \| \) is the \( \pi \)-essential supremum of \( | g | \) and \( X_t \sim \pi \) for every \( t \) under \( \mathbb{P}_\pi \).
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
We view the Markov transition kernel \(P\) as the bounded operator on \(L^2(\pi)\) defined by
\begin{equation}
(Ph)(x):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}h(x')\,P(x,dx'),
\qquad h\in L^2(\pi).
\end{equation}
This operator is the complexification of its restriction to \(L^2(\mathcal{X},\pi;\mathbb{R})\), and its operator norm is unchanged under complexification. Let \( \Pi: L^2(\pi) \to L^2(\pi) \) be the orthogonal projector onto the subspace of constant functions, i.e., \( \Pi h = \pi(h) \mathbf 1 \). Note that \( \Pi = \mathbf 1 \otimes \mathbf 1 \) and \( P \Pi = \Pi P = \Pi \), by \( P \mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1 \) and \( \pi P = \pi \) respectively. Assuming \( \| P - \Pi \| =: \lambda < 1 \), we refer to \( 1 - \lambda \) as the \emph{absolute spectral gap}. Define \( Q_\lambda: L^2(\pi) \to L^2(\pi) \) by
\begin{equation}
Q_\lambda
:= \lambda I + (1-\lambda) \Pi
= \Pi + \lambda (I - \Pi),
\end{equation}
known as the Le{\'o}n--Perron operator~\citep{leon2004optimal}. Since \( (Q_\lambda h)(x) = \lambda h(x) + (1-\lambda) \pi(h) \), the operator \( Q_\lambda \) is itself the Markov operator of a chain: at each step such a chain stays at its current state with probability \( \lambda \) and draws a fresh sample from \( \pi \) with probability \( 1 - \lambda \). That chain is reversible with respect to \( \pi \) and satisfies \( \| Q_\lambda - \Pi \| = \lambda \), so it is the \emph{laziest} chain with the prescribed spectral parameter.
Note that \( Q_\lambda \) is self-adjoint and \( Q_\lambda \succeq \lambda I \succeq 0 \), since \( Q_\lambda - \lambda I = (1-\lambda) \Pi \); hence \( Q_\lambda^{1/2} \) is well defined, and \( Q_\lambda^{1/2} = \Pi + \sqrt{\lambda}\,(I - \Pi) \) because \( \Pi \) and \( I - \Pi \) are orthogonal projections.
<!-- end head-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:scalar-hoeffding}
Let \( (X_t)_{t \ge 1} \) be a stationary Markov chain taking values in a measurable space \( \mathcal{X} \) with invariant measure \( \pi \) and absolute spectral gap \( 1 - \lambda > 0 \). Then, for every sequence of bounded measurable functions \(f_t: \mathcal{X} \to [a_t,b_t]\) satisfying \(b_t>a_t\), and every \(\varepsilon>0\), the following bound holds:
\[
\mathbb{P}_\pi\left\{
\sum_{t=1}^n f_t(X_t)- \sum_{t=1}^n \pi(f_t) \geq \varepsilon
\right\}
\leq \exp\left( -\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda} \cdot \frac{\varepsilon^2}{2\sum_{t=1}^n(b_t-a_t)^2/4} \right).
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:monotone-in-lambda}
Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-hoeffding} remains valid with \( \lambda \) replaced by any \( \lambda' \in [\| P-\Pi \|, 1) \). Indeed, the factorization \( P = Q_{\lambda'}^{1/2} K' Q_{\lambda'}^{1/2} \) with \( K' := \Pi + \lambda'^{-1}(P-\Pi) \) holds by construction, and the only further property used in Lemma~\ref{lem:op-bound} is \( \| K' \| \le 1 \), which holds for every such \( \lambda' \). This is what one needs in practice, where an upper bound on \( \| P - \Pi \| \) is available rather than its exact value.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:nonstationary}
For an initial law \( \mu \ll \pi \), the path law under \( \mathbb{P}_\mu \) has density \( \tfrac{d\mu}{d\pi}(X_1) \) with respect to the path law under \( \mathbb{P}_\pi \), so \( \mathbb{P}_\mu(A) \leq \big\| \tfrac{d\mu}{d\pi} \big\|_\pi \mathbb{P}_\pi(A)^{1/2} \) by Cauchy--Schwarz, and Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-hoeffding} holds under \( \mathbb{P}_\mu \) with the prefactor \( \| d\mu/d\pi \|_\pi \) and half the exponent; if \( d\mu/d\pi \in L^\infty(\pi) \), H\"older gives the prefactor \( \| d\mu/d\pi \|_{L^\infty(\pi)} \) and leaves the exponent unchanged. We do not pursue this further; see~\citet{fan2021hoeffding} and~\citet{paulin2015concentration}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The special case of independent random variables, for which \( P(x, dx') = \pi(dx') \) and hence \( \lambda = 0 \), recovers the classical Hoeffding inequality.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By the standard Chernoff argument, it is enough to provide uniform in \( \theta \in \mathbb{R} \) bound for MGF
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\pi \left[\exp\left(\theta \sum_{t=1}^n f_t(X_t) - \theta \sum_{t=1}^n \pi(f_t)\right)\right]
\leq \exp\left(\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda} \cdot \frac{\theta^2}{2} \cdot \sum_{t=1}^n \frac{(b_t-a_t)^2}{4} \right).
\label{eq:mgf-target}
\end{equation}
We prove the bound in two steps. First, Lemma~\ref{lem:op-bound} demonstrates that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mgf-bound}
\mathbb{E}_\pi\exp\!\left(\theta\sum_{t=1}^{n}f_t(X_t)\right)
\leq \prod_{t=1}^n \big\| M_{e^{\theta f_t}}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f_t}}^{1/2} \big\|.
\end{equation}
Second, Lemma~\ref{lem:rank1-resolvent} shows that, for every measurable function \( f:\mathcal{X}\to[a,b] \) and every \( \theta\in\mathbb{R} \), we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:op-norm-bound}
\big\| M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} \big\|
\leq \exp\left( \theta \pi(f) + \frac{1 + \lambda}{1 - \lambda} \frac{\theta^2 (b -a)^2}{8} \right).
\end{equation}
Multiplying~\eqref{eq:mgf-bound} by \( \exp\left(-\theta \sum_{t} \pi(f_t)\right) \) and inserting~\eqref{eq:op-norm-bound} cancels the terms \( \theta \pi(f_t) \) and gives~\eqref{eq:mgf-target}; the Cram{\'e}r--Chernoff method~\citep{boucheron2013concentration} then recovers the claim.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:op-bound}
Under the setup of Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-hoeffding}, for every \( \theta \in \mathbb{R} \),
\[
\mathbb{E}_\pi\exp\!\left(\theta\sum_{t=1}^{n}f_t(X_t)\right)
\leq \prod_{t=1}^n \big\| M_{e^{\theta f_t}}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f_t}}^{1/2} \big\|.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For brevity, set \( M_t := M_{e^{\theta f_t}} \). Define the operator \( K: L^2(\pi) \to L^2(\pi) \) by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scalar-k-def}
K := \begin{cases}
\Pi + \lambda^{-1}(P-\Pi), & \lambda>0,\\
I, & \lambda=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Note that \( K \) is nonexpansive. Indeed, for \( \lambda > 0 \), the two terms act on the orthogonal subspaces \(\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi)\) and \(\mathrm{Ran}(I-\Pi)\), respectively, and hence
\begin{equation}
\|  K  \|
\leq \max \big\{ \| \Pi \|, \lambda^{-1} \|  P - \Pi  \| \big\}
= 1.
\end{equation}
Moreover, \( K \mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1 \); therefore \( \| K \| = 1 \). Finally, in both cases, \( P=Q_\lambda^{1/2}KQ_\lambda^{1/2} \). For \(\lambda>0\) this follows by construction, while for \(\lambda=0\), since \(P=\Pi\) and \(Q_0=\Pi\), we have \( Q_0^{1/2} K Q_0^{1/2} = \Pi =P \).

\medskip \noindent
Setting \(g_{n+1} = \mathbf 1\) and \(g_t:=M_tPg_{t+1}\), the Markov property gives, by backward induction,
\begin{equation}
g_t(x)
= \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \exp\!\left(\theta\sum_{s=t}^{n}f_s(X_s)\right) \middle| X_t=x \right].
\end{equation}
Since \(X_1 \sim \pi\), integrating \(g_1\) yields
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\pi\exp\!\left(\theta\sum_{t=1}^{n}f_t(X_t)\right)
=\langle \mathbf 1,g_1 \rangle
=\langle \mathbf 1,M_1PM_2P \cdots PM_n\mathbf 1\rangle.
\label{eq:feynman-kac}
\end{equation}
Define \( C_t := Q_\lambda^{1/2} M_t Q_\lambda^{1/2} \). Using \( Q_\lambda^{1/2} \mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1 \),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lemma6-inequality}
\langle \mathbf 1, M_1 P M_2 P \cdots P M_n \mathbf 1 \rangle
= \langle \mathbf 1, C_1 K C_2 K \cdots K C_n \mathbf 1 \rangle
\leq \prod_{t=1}^n \| C_t \|, 
\end{equation}
Using \( \| A^\ast A \|=\| AA^\ast \| \),
\begin{equation}
\| C_t \|
= \big\|  (M_t^{1/2} Q_\lambda^{1/2})^\ast M_t^{1/2} Q_\lambda^{1/2}  \big\|
= \big\|  M_t^{1/2} Q_\lambda^{1/2} (M_t^{1/2} Q_\lambda^{1/2})^\ast  \big\|
= \big\| M_t^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_t^{1/2} \big\|
\end{equation}
Substituting this identity into~\eqref{eq:lemma6-inequality} proves the claim.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:rank1-resolvent}
Under the setup of Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-hoeffding}, let \(a < b\), let \(f: \mathcal{X} \to [a,b] \) be measurable, and let
\(\lambda \in [0,1)\). Then, for every \( \theta \in \mathbb{R} \),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-theta-def}
\big\| M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} \big\|
\leq \exp\left( \theta \pi(f) + \frac{1 + \lambda}{1 - \lambda} \frac{\theta^2 (b -a)^2}{8} \right)
=: R_\theta.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Set \( u := e^{\theta f/2} \). Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ort-decomposition-mqm}
M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2}
= \lambda M_{e^{\theta f}} + (1-\lambda) \, M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} ( \mathbf 1 \otimes \mathbf 1 ) M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2}
= \lambda M_{e^{\theta f}} + (1-\lambda) \, u \otimes u.
\end{equation}
Therefore, for any \( h \in L^2(\pi) \),
\begin{align}
\langle h, M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} h \rangle
&= \lambda \langle h, M_{e^{\theta f}} h \rangle + (1-\lambda) \langle h, (u \otimes u) h \rangle \\
&= \lambda \langle h, M_{e^{\theta f}} h \rangle + (1-\lambda) | \langle u, h \rangle |^2.
\end{align}
Fix any \(r>0\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-admissible}
r - \lambda e^{\theta x}
> 0,
\qquad \forall x \in [a,b].
\end{equation}
Let \( w_\theta := r - \lambda e^{\theta f} \). Since \( f(x) \in [a,b] \), for every \( \theta \in \mathbb{R} \), \( w_\theta > 0 \). Therefore, Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
| \langle u, h \rangle |^2
\leq \left\| \frac{u}{\sqrt{w_\theta}} \right\|_\pi^2 \big\| \sqrt{w_\theta} \, h \big\|_\pi^2
= \pi\!\left( \frac{e^{\theta f}}{r - \lambda e^{\theta f}} \right) \langle h, (r I - \lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}) h \rangle.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fan-to-prove}
(1 - \lambda) \,  \pi\!\left( \frac{e^{\theta f}}{r - \lambda e^{\theta f}} \right)
\leq 1
\implies M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2}Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2}
\preceq rI.
\end{equation}
For \(r\) satisfying~\eqref{eq:r-admissible}, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:psi-r-def}
\psi_r(x)
:= \frac{e^{\theta x}} {r-\lambda e^{\theta x}},
\qquad x\in[a,b].
\end{equation}
Condition~\eqref{eq:r-admissible} ensures that \(\psi_r\) is well defined on \([a,b]\). Moreover, \(\psi_r\) is convex on \([a,b]\) since
\begin{equation}
\psi_r''(x)
= \frac{\theta^2 re^{\theta x} (r + \lambda e^{\theta x})}{(r - \lambda e^{\theta x})^3}
\ge 0.
\end{equation}
Convexity gives the pointwise bound,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:psi-r-fx-convexity}
\psi_r(f(x))
\leq \frac{b - f(x)}{b - a} \psi_r(a) + \frac{f(x) - a}{ b -a } \psi_r(b).
\end{equation}
Integrating this inequality gives,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:target-after-convexity}
\pi\left( \frac{e^{\theta f}}{r - \lambda e^{\theta f}} \right)
= \pi(\psi_r \circ f)
\leq (1 - \mu) \psi_r(a) + \mu \psi_r(b),
\qquad \text{where} \quad
\mu := \frac{\pi(f) - a}{b - a} \in [0,1].
\end{equation}
Now, it is enough to upper-bound the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:target-after-convexity}. Denoting \( A := e^{\theta a} \) and \( B := e^{\theta b} \), it is enough to prove
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:target-after-convexity-simple}
(1 - \lambda) \left[ (1-\mu) \frac{A}{r - \lambda A} + \mu \frac{B}{r - \lambda B}\right]
\leq 1.
\end{equation}
By~\eqref{eq:r-admissible} we have \( r>\lambda\max\{A,B\} \). Put \( C := (1-\mu) A + \mu B \). Then,~\eqref{eq:target-after-convexity-simple} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
(1 - \lambda) (r C - \lambda AB)
\leq (r - \lambda A) (r - \lambda B).
\end{equation}
Expanding brackets and collecting terms we get quadratic
\begin{equation}
r^2 - r\left[\lambda(A+B) + (1-\lambda) C\right]+ \lambda AB
\ge 0.
\end{equation}
Because \( r > 0 \),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-quadratic-bound}
r + \frac{\lambda A B}{r}
\ge \lambda (A + B) + (1- \lambda) C
= (1+\lambda) \left[(1-p) A + p B\right],
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prob-p}
p
:= \frac{\lambda + (1-\lambda)\mu}{1 + \lambda} 
\in [0,1].
\end{equation}
Dividing by \( (1+\lambda) \),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-to-bernoulli}
\frac{r + \lambda AB/r}{1 + \lambda}
\ge (1 - p) A + p B
\implies
(1 - \lambda) \pi\left( \frac{e^{\theta f}}{r - \lambda e^{\theta f}} \right)
\leq 1.
\end{equation}
The weighted AM--GM inequality gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-r-theta-pre}
\frac{r + \lambda AB/r}{1 + \lambda}
\ge r^{1/(1+\lambda)} \left( \frac{AB}{r} \right)^{\lambda/(1+\lambda)}
= \exp\left[ \frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda} \log r + \frac{\lambda}{1 + \lambda} \theta(a+b) \right].
\end{equation}
We now set \( r = R_\theta \) defined in~\eqref{eq:r-theta-def}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:cs-weights-positive}, the choice satisfies~\eqref{eq:r-admissible}. Substituting in~\eqref{eq:r-r-theta-pre} yields
\begin{equation}
\frac{R_\theta + \lambda AB/R_\theta}{1 + \lambda}
\ge \exp\Big[ \theta \underbrace{\frac{(1-\lambda) \pi(f) + \lambda(a+b)}{1 + \lambda }}_{(1-p) a + pb} + \frac{\theta^2 (b-a)^2}{8} \Big].
\end{equation}
On the other hand, the classical Hoeffding lemma for Bernoulli random variable \( X \) with \( \mathbb{P}(X = a) = (1-p) \) and \( \mathbb{P}(X =b) = p \) gives
\begin{equation}
\exp\Big[ \theta[(1-p) a + pb] + \frac{\theta^2 (b-a)^2}{8}\Big]
\ge (1 - p) e^{\theta a} + p e^{\theta b}
= (1 - p) A + p B.
\end{equation}
This proves~\eqref{eq:r-to-bernoulli} for \(r=R_\theta\), and hence~\eqref{eq:fan-to-prove}. Therefore, \(M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2}Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2}\preceq R_\theta I\), or equivalently, \(\big\| M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2}Q_\lambda M_{e^{\theta f}}^{1/2} \big\|\leq R_\theta\), as claimed.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:meaning-of-p}
The probability \( p \) in~\eqref{eq:prob-p} arises naturally and has a transparent interpretation. Combining the two fractions in~\eqref{eq:target-after-convexity-simple} and clearing the denominator produces a linear form \( c_a e^{\theta a} + c_b e^{\theta b} \) with \( c_a = 1 - (1-\lambda) \mu \) and \( c_b = \lambda + (1-\lambda) \mu \); recognizing it as a Bernoulli MGF requires normalizing, which gives \( p = c_b / (c_a + c_b) \), that is,~\eqref{eq:prob-p}. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p-shrinkage}
p - \frac{1}{2}
= \frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda} \left( \mu - \frac{1}{2} \right),
\end{equation}
so \( p \) is the endpoint weight \( \mu \) of the observable shrunk toward \( 1/2 \) by exactly the factor \( (1-\lambda)/(1+\lambda) \) that appears in the final bound. This is the whole effect of the Markov dependence at this step: the \( \lambda \)-term contributes symmetrically to the two endpoints and therefore pulls the effective Bernoulli parameter toward \( 1/2 \).

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:cs-weights-positive}
Under the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:rank1-resolvent}, for every \(\theta \in \mathbb{R}\) and every \(x \in [a,b]\),
\[
R_\theta-\lambda e^{\theta x}>0,
\]
where \(R_\theta\) is defined in~\eqref{eq:r-theta-def}.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The case \(\lambda=0\) is immediate, so assume \(0<\lambda<1\). Fix \(x \in [a,b] \). For \(\theta \geq 0\), since \(x \leq b\) and \(b-\pi(f)\leq b-a\),
\begin{equation}
\log R_\theta-\log\lambda-\theta x
\geq \log R_\theta-\log\lambda-\theta b
\geq \frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\frac{(b-a)^2 \theta^2}{8}-(b-a) \theta -\log\lambda.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is a quadratic in \(\theta\), whose minimum equals
\begin{equation}
-\frac{2(1-\lambda)}{1+\lambda}-\log\lambda
=: g(\lambda).
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
g'(\lambda)
= -\frac{(1-\lambda)^2}{\lambda(1+\lambda)^2}<0,
\qquad g(1)=0,
\end{equation}
we have \(g(\lambda)>0\) for \(0<\lambda<1\). For \(\theta<0\), the same argument, using \(x \geq a\) and \(\pi(f)-a \leq b-a\), gives the same lower bound.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:secular}
Let \( \lambda \in [0,1) \), let \( f \) be bounded and measurable, and write \( m := e^{\theta f} \) and \( \kappa := \lambda \| m \|_{L^\infty(\pi)} \). For \( R > \kappa \) set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:secular}
\Phi(R)
:= (1-\lambda) \, \pi\!\left( \frac{m}{R - \lambda m} \right).
\end{equation}
Then \( \Phi \) is finite, continuous and strictly decreasing on \( (\kappa, \infty) \) with \( \Phi(R) \to 0 \) as \( R \to \infty \), and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one-step-exact}
\big\| M_{m}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{m}^{1/2} \big\|
= \inf \big\{ R > \kappa : \Phi(R) \leq 1 \big\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if \( \Phi(R) > 1 \) for some \( R > \kappa \), then the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:one-step-exact} is the unique root of the equation \( \Phi(R) = 1 \); otherwise it equals \( \kappa \).
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Write \( u := m^{1/2} \) and \( B := M_m^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_m^{1/2} = \lambda M_m + (1-\lambda) \, u \otimes u \), by~\eqref{eq:ort-decomposition-mqm}. Note that \( B \) is self-adjoint and \( B \succeq 0 \), so \( \| B \| = \sup \operatorname{spec}(B) \). For \( R > \kappa \) we have
\begin{equation}
0
< \frac{m}{R - \lambda m}
\leq \frac{\| m \|_{L^\infty(\pi)}}{R - \kappa}
\quad \pi\text{-a.e.,}
\end{equation}
so \( \Phi(R) \) is finite; the integrand is strictly decreasing in \( R \) pointwise, and the stated continuity and limit follow by dominated convergence. The inequality \( \leq \) in~\eqref{eq:one-step-exact} is~\eqref{eq:fan-to-prove}: for every \( R > \kappa \) with \( \Phi(R) \leq 1 \) we have \( B \preceq R I \), hence \( \| B \| \leq R \). For the converse, first \( B \succeq \lambda M_m \) gives \( \| B \| \geq \| \lambda M_m \| = \kappa \). Suppose now that \( \Phi(R) > 1 \) for some \( R > \kappa \); by the above there is then a unique \( R_\ast \in (\kappa,\infty) \) with \( \Phi(R_\ast) = 1 \), and the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:one-step-exact} equals \( R_\ast \). Put
\begin{equation}
h := (R_\ast I - \lambda M_m)^{-1} u
= \frac{u}{R_\ast - \lambda m},
\end{equation}
which is bounded, hence in \( L^2(\pi) \), and nonzero. Since \( \langle u, h \rangle = \pi\big( m / (R_\ast - \lambda m) \big) = (1-\lambda)^{-1} \),
\begin{equation}
Bh
= \lambda M_m h + (1-\lambda) \langle u, h \rangle \, u
= \lambda M_m h + u
= \lambda M_m h + (R_\ast I - \lambda M_m) h
= R_\ast h .
\end{equation}
So \( R_\ast \in \operatorname{spec}(B) \) and \( \| B \| \geq R_\ast \), which completes the proof.

Let \( \mathbb{H}_d(\mathbb{C}) := \left\{A\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}:A=A^\ast \right\} \) denote the space of self-adjoint \(d\times d\) complex matrices, and let \(\lambda_{\max}(A)\) denote the largest eigenvalue of \(A\in\mathbb{H}_d(\mathbb{C})\). We write \(S_2^d:=\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}\) for the space of \(d\times d\) complex matrices, equipped with the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product \(\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}:=\operatorname{Tr}(A^\ast B)\) and the associated norm \(\| A \|_{\mathrm{HS}}:=\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(A^\ast A)}\). In finite dimensions, \(\| \cdot \|_{\rm HS}\) coincides with the Frobenius norm. Let \( \mathcal{L}(S_2^d) \) denote the space of bounded linear operators acting on \( S_2^d \).
<!-- end head-3 -->
<!-- head-4: body_md -->
As in the scalar case, let \((X_t)_{t\geq1}\) be a stationary Markov chain on a general measurable state space \(\mathcal{X}\), with transition kernel \(P\) and stationary distribution \(\pi\). Suppose that \(P\) admits an absolute spectral gap \(1-\lambda>0\) on \(L^2(\pi)\). Let \( (F_t:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{H}_d(\mathbb{C}))_{t\geq1} \) be a sequence of measurable matrix-valued functions. We work on the lifted Hilbert space \( \mathcal{H}_d:=L^2(\pi;S_2^d) \). Lift the Markov operator to \(\mathcal{H}_d\) by
\begin{equation}
(\widetilde P G)(x)
:= \int_{\mathcal{X}} G(x')\,P(x,dx'),
\qquad G \in \mathcal{H}_d,
\end{equation}
where the integral is understood in the Bochner sense. Let \(\Pi\) denote the orthogonal projection onto the constant \(S_2^d\)-valued functions, i.e., \( (\Pi G)(x) := \int_{\mathcal{X}}G(x')\,\pi(dx') \). Set \( Q_\lambda:=\Pi+\lambda(I-\Pi) \). Note that \( \| \widetilde P - \Pi \| = \| P - \Pi \| = \lambda \): under the identification \( \mathcal{H}_d \cong L^2(\pi) \otimes S_2^d \) one has \( \widetilde P - \Pi = (P - \Pi) \otimes \mathrm{id}_{S_2^d} \), where \( \Pi \) on the right denotes the projection of Section~\ref{sec:scalar} and \( \mathrm{id}_{S_2^d} \in \mathcal{L}(S_2^d) \) is the identity, and \( \| A \otimes \mathrm{id}_{S_2^d} \| = \| A \| \) for every bounded \( A \). Let \( J:S_2^d \to \mathcal{H}_d \) be the map acting as \( (JZ)(x):=Z \), so that \( \Pi=JJ^\ast \). All matrix-valued expectations below are understood as Bochner integrals.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:matrix-hoeffding}
Assume that \( \pi(F_t)=0 \) and
\(a_tI_d\preceq F_t(x)\preceq b_tI_d\) for every \(t\geq1\) and \(x\in\mathcal{X}\), where \(b_t>a_t\). Then, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\mathbb{P}_\pi \left\{ \lambda_{\max} \left( \sum_{t=1}^n F_t(X_t) \right) \ge \varepsilon \right\}
\leq d^{2 - \pi/4} \exp\left( - \frac{1 - \lambda}{1 + \lambda} \cdot \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8 \sum_{t=1}^n (b_t - a_t)^2 / \pi^2} \right).
\]

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:centering}
Centering can be imposed without restricting the class of observables, but it comes at a cost. Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix-hoeffding} to \( F_t - \pi(F_t) \) is legitimate, since \( a_t I_d \preceq F_t(x) \preceq b_t I_d \) forces \( a_t I_d \preceq \pi(F_t) \preceq b_t I_d \) and therefore \( -(b_t-a_t) I_d \preceq F_t(x) - \pi(F_t) \preceq (b_t-a_t) I_d \); the width doubles, so the exponent loses a factor \( 4 \). No such loss occurs in Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-hoeffding}, because Lemma~\ref{lem:rank1-resolvent} carries \( \pi(f) \) explicitly.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For \(\theta>0\) and \(\phi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\), define the noncommutative MGF functional~\citep{neeman2024concentration}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix-mgf}
\mathcal{M}_\phi(\theta)
:= \mathbb{E}_\pi \left\| \prod_{t=1}^n A_{t,\phi}(X_t) \right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
\qquad \text{where} \quad
A_{t,\phi}(x)
:= \exp\!\left(\frac{\theta e^{i\phi}}{2}F_t(x)\right).
\end{equation}
First, we use the bounded multi-matrix Golden--Thompson inequality~\citep[Theorem~3.4]{garg2018matrix} together with the Schatten estimate at exponent \(p=\pi/4\). More precisely, for every \(s>0\),
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\operatorname{Tr}\exp\!\left( \frac{\pi s}{4}\sum_{t=1}^n F_t(X_t) \right)\right]
\le d^{\,1-\pi/4} \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \mathcal{M}_\phi(s)\,\xi_n(d\phi).
\end{equation}
Here \(\xi_n\) is a fixed probability measure defined by the Golden--Thompson theorem; it may depend on \(n\), but not on \(s\), the matrices, or the realized sample path. Consequently, expectation and integration may be interchanged. Setting \(s=4\theta/\pi\) and applying Markov's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix-gt}
\mathbb{P}_\pi\left\{ \lambda_{\max}\left(\sum_{t=1}^nF_t(X_t)\right) \geq \varepsilon \right\}
\leq d^{1-\pi/4} \inf_{\theta>0} e^{-\theta \varepsilon} \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \mathcal{M}_\phi\left(\frac{4\theta}{\pi}\right) \,\xi_n(d\phi).
\end{equation}
Second, Lemma~\ref{lem:matrix-op-bound} reduces the noncommutative MGF~\eqref{eq:matrix-mgf} to a product of one-step operator norms:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix-op-product}
\mathcal{M}_\phi(\theta)
\leq d\prod_{t=1}^n \big\|  M_{t,\phi}(\theta)^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{t,\phi}(\theta)^{1/2} \big\|.
\end{equation}
Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:matrix-one-step} gives, uniformly in \(\phi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix-one-step}
\big\|  M_{t,\phi}(\theta)^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{t,\phi}(\theta)^{1/2} \big\|
\leq \exp\left(
\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}
\frac{\theta^2(b_t-a_t)^2}{8}
\right).
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:matrix-op-product} and
\eqref{eq:matrix-one-step},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix-mgf-final}
\mathcal{M}_\phi(\theta)
\leq
d\exp\left(
\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}
\frac{\theta^2}{8}
\sum_{t=1}^n(b_t-a_t)^2
\right).
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:matrix-mgf-final} into
\eqref{eq:matrix-gt} yields
\begin{align}
\mathbb{P}_\pi\left\{ \lambda_{\max}\left(\sum_{t=1}^nF_t(X_t)\right) \geq\varepsilon \right\}
&\leq d^{2-\pi/4} \inf_{\theta>0} \exp\left( -\theta\varepsilon + \frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda} \frac{2\theta^2}{\pi^2} \sum_{t=1}^n(b_t-a_t)^2 \right) \\
& \leq d^{2-\pi/4} \exp\left( -\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda} \frac{\pi^2\varepsilon^2} {8\sum_{t=1}^n(b_t-a_t)^2} \right),
\end{align}
as claimed.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:matrix-op-bound}
Under the setup of Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix-hoeffding}, for every \(\theta>0\) and \(\phi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\),
\[
\mathcal{M}_\phi(\theta)
\leq d\prod_{t=1}^n \big\| M_{t,\phi}(\theta)^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{t,\phi}(\theta)^{1/2}  \big\|.
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Fix \( \theta \) and \( \phi \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2] \). Denote \( M_t := M_{t,\phi}(\theta) \) and \( C_t := M_{t}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{t}^{1/2} \). For every \(t \geq 1\), define the two-sided multiplication operator \( E_{t}:\mathcal{H}_d \to \mathcal{H}_d \) by
\begin{equation}
(E_{t} G)(x)
:= \exp\!\left(\frac{\theta e^{i\phi}}{4}F_t(x)\right) \, G(x) \, \exp\!\left(\frac{\theta e^{i\phi}}{4}F_t(x)\right)^\ast,
\qquad G \in \mathcal{H}_d.
\end{equation}
Since \(F_t(x)\) is self-adjoint,
\(E_{t} \) is normal and \( E_{t}^\ast E_{t} = E_{t} E_{t}^\ast = M_{t} \). Since \( E_t \) is normal, this also means
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:op-norm-eqs}
\big\| E_{t} Q_\lambda E_t^\ast \big\|
= \big\| Q_\lambda^{1/2} E_t^\ast E_{t} Q_\lambda^{1/2} \big\|
= \big\| Q_\lambda^{1/2} M_{t} Q_\lambda^{1/2} \big\|
= \big\| \underbrace{M_{t}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{t}^{1/2}}_{C_t} \big\|.
\end{equation}

\medskip \noindent
As in~\eqref{eq:feynman-kac}, by the Markov property,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{M}_\phi(\theta)
&= \left\langle \mathbf{I},\, E_{1}^2
\widetilde P E_{2}^2 \cdots \widetilde P E_{n}^2 \mathbf{I} \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_d}
= \left\langle E_{1}^\ast\mathbf{I},\, \prod_{t=1}^{n-1} \left( E_{t} \widetilde P E_{t+1} \right) E_{n}\mathbf{I} \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_d} \\
&\leq \| E_{1}^\ast\mathbf{I} \|_{\mathcal{H}_d} \| E_{n}\mathbf{I} \|_{\mathcal{H}_d} \prod_{t=1}^{n-1} \big\|  E_{t} \widetilde P E_{t+1}  \big\|. \label{eq:matrix-mgf-product}
\end{align}
As in the scalar case~\eqref{eq:scalar-k-def}, define \( K: \mathcal{H}_d \to \mathcal{H}_d \) by
\begin{equation}
K := \begin{cases}
\Pi + \lambda^{-1}(\widetilde P-\Pi), & \lambda>0,\\
I, & \lambda=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
As in Lemma~\ref{lem:op-bound}, \( \| K \| = 1 \) and \( \widetilde P=Q_\lambda^{1/2}KQ_\lambda^{1/2} \). Consequently, 
\begin{align}
\big\| E_{t}\widetilde P E_{t+1} \big\|
&= \big\| E_{t} Q_\lambda^{1/2} K Q_\lambda^{1/2} E_{t+1} \big\| \\
& \leq \big\| E_{t} Q_\lambda^{1/2} \big\| \cdot \big\| Q_\lambda^{1/2} E_{t+1} \big\|
= \underbrace{\big\| E_{t} Q_\lambda E_{t}^\ast \big\|^{1/2}}_{= \| C_t \|^{1/2}}
\underbrace{\big\| E_{t+1}^\ast Q_\lambda E_{t+1} \big\|^{1/2}}_{= \| C_{t+1} \|^{1/2}}.
\label{eq:matrix-cross}
\end{align}
Finally, since \(\mathbf{I}\) is constant, \( Q_\lambda^{1/2}\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{I} \). Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\| E_{1}^\ast\mathbf{I} \|_{\mathcal{H}_d}^2
= \left\langle E_{1}^\ast Q_\lambda^{1/2}\mathbf{I}, E_{1}^\ast Q_\lambda^{1/2}\mathbf{I} \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_d}
= \left\langle \mathbf{I}, Q_\lambda^{1/2} M_1 Q_\lambda^{1/2}\mathbf{I} \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_d}
\leq \underbrace{\| \mathbf{I} \|_{\mathcal{H}_d}^2}_{= d} \underbrace{\big\|  Q_\lambda^{1/2} M_1 Q_\lambda^{1/2}  \big\|}_{= \| C_1 \|}.
\end{equation}
Similarly, \( \| E_{n}\mathbf{I} \|_{\mathcal{H}_d} \leq \sqrt d\, \|  C_n  \|^{1/2} \). Substituting these estimates into \eqref{eq:matrix-mgf-product}, each one-step operator norm appears with total exponent one, and hence
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_\phi(\theta)
\leq d\prod_{t=1}^n \|  C_t  \|
= d\prod_{t=1}^n \big\|  M_{t,\phi}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{t,\phi}^{1/2}  \big\|,
\end{equation}
as claimed.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:matrix-one-step}
Under the setup of Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix-hoeffding}, for every \(t\), \(\theta>0\), and
\(\phi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\),
\[
\big\| M_{t,\phi}^{1/2} Q_\lambda M_{t,\phi}^{1/2} \big\|
\leq \exp\left( \frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda} \frac{\theta^2(b_t-a_t)^2}{8} \right).
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Fix \(t\), \(\theta>0\), and \(\phi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\), and write
\begin{equation}
c:=\cos \phi \in [0,1],
\qquad \mathbf{T}:=T_{t,\phi},
\qquad a:=c a_t,
\qquad b:=c b_t,
\qquad \mathbf M:=M_{e^{\theta \mathbf{T}}}.
\end{equation}
If \(c=0\), equivalently \( \phi = \pm \pi/2\), then \(\mathbf{T}=0\), \(\mathbf M=I\), and
\begin{equation}
\big\| \mathbf M^{1/2}Q_\lambda \mathbf M^{1/2} \big\|
=\big\| Q_\lambda \big\|
=1,
\end{equation}
so the claimed bound is immediate. Hence, in the remainder, assume \(c>0\), and therefore \(b-a=c(b_t-a_t)>0\).

\medskip
Since \(F_t(x)\) is self-adjoint and \( a_t I_d \preceq F_t(x) \preceq b_t I_d \), the operator \( \mathbf{T}(x)\) is self-adjoint on \(S_2^d\) and satisfies \( a I\preceq \mathbf{T}(x) \preceq b I \). Moreover, \(\pi(F_t)=0\) implies \( \pi(\mathbf{T})=0 \). Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite-rank-decom}
\mathbf M^{1/2} Q_\lambda \mathbf M^{1/2}
= \lambda \mathbf M+(1-\lambda)\mathbf M^{1/2} JJ^\ast \mathbf M^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For \(r>\lambda e^{\theta b}\), set
\begin{equation}
D_r := rI - \lambda \mathbf M \succ 0.
\end{equation}
By the Schur-complement criterion,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:op-iff}
\mathbf M^{1/2} Q_\lambda \mathbf M^{1/2}\preceq rI
\iff (1-\lambda) \mathbf M^{1/2} J J^\ast \mathbf M^{1/2} \preceq D_r
\iff (1-\lambda)J^\ast \mathbf M^{1/2} D_r^{-1}\mathbf M^{1/2} J \preceq I.
\end{equation}
Since \(\mathbf M\) and \(D_r\) are multiplication operators, \( \mathbf M^{1/2}\) and \( D_r \) commute; therefore
\begin{equation}
J^\ast \mathbf M^{1/2} D_r^{-1} \mathbf M^{1/2} J
= J^\ast \mathbf M D_r^{-1} J
= \pi\left[ \frac{e^{\theta \mathbf{T}}} {rI-\lambda e^{\theta \mathbf{T}}} \right]
= \pi\bigl[\psi_r(\mathbf{T})\bigr],
\end{equation}
where \( \psi_r \) is given by~\eqref{eq:psi-r-def}, so that, via functional calculus for the self-adjoint operator \( \mathbf{T}(x) \),
\begin{equation}
\psi_r(\mathbf{T}(x))
= e^{\theta \mathbf{T}(x)} \big(r I - \lambda e^{\theta \mathbf{T}(x)}\big)^{-1}
\end{equation}
As in the scalar case, \(\psi_r\) is convex on \([a,b]\). Therefore the scalar inequality~\eqref{eq:psi-r-fx-convexity} lifts by functional calculus:
\begin{equation}
\psi_r(\mathbf{T})
\preceq \frac{bI-\mathbf{T}}{b-a}\psi_r(a) + \frac{\mathbf{T}-aI}{b-a}\psi_r(b).
\end{equation}
Taking the expectation and using \(\pi(\mathbf{T})=0\),
\begin{equation}
\pi[\psi_r(\mathbf{T})]
\preceq \left[ \frac{b}{b-a}\psi_r(a) + \frac{-a}{b-a}\psi_r(b) \right]I.
\end{equation}
The quantity in brackets is exactly the same two-endpoint expression as in the scalar proof. Set
\begin{equation}
r
:= \exp\left( \frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda} \frac{\theta^2(b-a)^2}{8} \right).
\end{equation}
Integrating \(aI\preceq \mathbf{T}(x)\preceq bI\) and using \(\pi(\mathbf{T})=0\) gives \(a \le 0 \le b\). Therefore, the scalar calculation in Proposition~\ref{prop:cs-weights-positive}, with mean zero, verifies \(r>\lambda e^{\theta b}\). The scalar argument gives \( (1-\lambda)\pi[\psi_r(\mathbf{T})]\preceq I \). Consequently, by~\eqref{eq:op-iff}, \( \mathbf M^{1/2} Q_\lambda \mathbf M^{1/2} \preceq rI \) and therefore
\begin{equation}
\big\| M_{t,\phi}^{1/2}Q_\lambda M_{t,\phi}^{1/2} \big\|
= \big\| \mathbf M^{1/2} Q_\lambda \mathbf M^{1/2} \big\|
\leq r
= \exp\left( \frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda} \frac{\theta^2\cos^2\phi\,(b_t-a_t)^2}{8} \right).
\end{equation}
Finally, \(\cos^2\phi\leq1\), which yields the claim.
