\label{thm:kahn}
For every $h$ and $\lambda_T(i)$ as above, $L_\lambda$ flows upward to $h_+^2$.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:halmos}
The $1$- and $0$-eigenspaces of $C$ are $U \cap V$ and $U \cap V^\perp$.
Let \( U_*= U\cap\bigl((U\cap V)\oplus(U\cap V^\perp)\bigr)^\perp\).
Then $U_*$ is $C$-invariant and has an orthonormal eigenbasis
$u_1,\ldots,u_r$ with \(Cu_j=\lambda_j u_j\),
where each \( \lambda_j \in (0,1) \).
Writing $c_j=\sqrt{\lambda_j}$ and $s_j=\sqrt{1-\lambda_j}$, there are
orthonormal vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_r\in U^\perp$ such that the spaces
$L_j=\operatorname{span}\{u_j,v_j\}$ are pairwise orthogonal and are
preserved by both $P$ and $Q$.  In the basis $(u_j,v_j)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:halmos-block}
 P=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1&0\\
  0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Q=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  c_j^2&c_js_j\\
  c_js_j&s_j^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Thus the corresponding principal angle $\theta_j\in(0,\pi/2)$ is
determined by $c_j=\cos\theta_j$, or equivalently
$\lambda_j=\cos^2\theta_j$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We note that $C$ is self-adjoint and preserves $U$.
If $u\in U$ is a unit eigenvector with $Cu=\lambda u$ then 
\(  0 \le \lambda
 =\langle u,Cu\rangle
 =\langle u,Qu\rangle
 =\langle Qu,Qu\rangle
 =\|Qu\|^2 \le \|u\|^2 = 1 \).
If $\lambda=0$, then $Qu=0$, and hence $u\in U\cap V^\perp$;
conversely, if $u\in U\cap V^\perp$ then $Cu = PQu=0$.
If $\lambda=1$ then as $\|u\|^2=\|Qu\|^2+\|(I-Q)u\|^2$
and $\|u\|^2=\|Qu\|^2=1$ we have $(I-Q)u=0$, so $Qu=u$ and $u\in U\cap V$. 
Conversely, $C$ is the identity on $U\cap V$.  

Since $C$ is self-adjoint, it preserves $U_*$ and the
spectral theorem  gives an orthonormal eigenbasis $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ 
with eigenvalues  $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$ in $(0,1)$. We define
\[
 v_j=\frac{Qu_j-\lambda_j u_j}{c_js_j}.
\]
Then
\(
 P(Qu_j-\lambda_j u_j)
 =Cu_j-\lambda_j u_j
 =0
\)
and
\(
 \|Qu_j-\lambda_j u_j\|^2
 =
 \|Qu_j\|^2
 -2\lambda_j\langle u_j,Qu_j\rangle
 +\lambda_j^2\|u_j\|^2
 =\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j),
\)
so $v_j$ is a unit vector in $U^\perp$.
Now
\(
 Qu_j=c_j^2u_j+c_js_jv_j
\)
by definition, and applying $Q$ gives
\(
 Qu_j =c_j^2Qu_j+c_js_jQv_j.
\)
Substituting and rearranging gives
\(
 c_js_jQv_j
 =c_j^2s_j^2u_j+c_js_j^3v_j,
\)
so
\(
 Qv_j=c_js_ju_j+s_j^2v_j.
\)
Since also $Pu_j=u_j$ and $Pv_j=0$, both projections preserve
$L_j$, and their matrices are those in \eqref{eq:halmos-block}.

It remains to check for $j \ne k$ that $L_j$ and $L_k$ are orthogonal. 
We already have shown orthogonality between all pairs in $\{u_j,v_j,u_k,v_k\}$
except $(v_j,v_k)$. Note that
\(
 \langle Qu_j,Qu_k\rangle
 =\langle u_j,Qu_k\rangle
 =\lambda_k\langle u_j,u_k\rangle
 =0.
\)
Substituting $Qu_j=\lambda_j u_j+c_js_jv_j$
and $Qu_k=\lambda_k u_k+c_ks_kv_k$
and expanding gives 
\(
 c_js_jc_ks_k\langle v_j,v_k\rangle=0,
\)
so $v_j\perp v_k$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:crossing-mass}
We have $\operatorname{Tr}(C-C^2)=\frac N2\mathcal{E}_g(f)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $P,Q$ are orthogonal projections,
\[
 \|(I-P)QP\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(PQ(I-P)QP\bigr)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(PQP)-\operatorname{Tr}(PQPQP)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(C-C^2).
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \|(I-P)QP\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=  \sum_{x\in\mathcal{F},\,y\notin\mathcal{F}}\widehat g(x\triangle y)^2
= \sum_T\widehat g(T)^2|\{x\in\mathcal{F}:x\triangle T\notin\mathcal{F}\}|.
\]
As $N\mathcal E_T^{(2)}(f)=2|\{x\in\mathcal{F}:x\triangle T\notin\mathcal{F}\}|$ the lemma follows.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:endpoints}
We have $\dim(U\cap V^\perp)=|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{G}|$ and $\dim(U\cap V)=|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{G}^\dagger|$,
where $\mathcal{G}^\dagger=\Omega\setminus\{\overline S:S\in\mathcal{G}\}$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
As $V=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_T:T\in\mathcal{G}\}$
we have $\phi \in V^\perp$ if and only if $\widehat\phi(T)=0$ for every $T\in\mathcal{G}$.
Thus $U\cap V^\perp=\ker R_{\mathcal{G}}$, where for  $\mathcal{H}\subseteq\Omega$ we define
\(R_{\mathcal{H}}:U\longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{H}}\) 
by \(R_{\mathcal{H}}(\phi)=\bigl(\widehat\phi(T)\bigr)_{T\in\mathcal{H}}\).

To calculate the dimension, we first note that $\{M_S:S\in\mathcal{F}\}$ form a basis of $U$,
as $\mathcal{F}$ is increasing and $(M_S(T))$ is triangular. In this basis $R_{\mathcal{H}}$
has the matrix with $(T,S)$ entry $\widehat{M_S}(T)$.
If $S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{G}$, then the whole column indexed by $S$ is zero,
as $\mathcal{G}$ does not contain any subsets of $S$, so
\(
 \operatorname{rank}R_{\mathcal{G}}\le |\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}|.
\)
For the reverse inequality, consider the submatrix
with rows and columns both indexed by $\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}$.

\)

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:flip-energy}
Let $f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ be increasing.  For $p=1,2$ and $\emptyset \ne T\subseteq[n]$ we have
$\mathcal E_T^{(p)}(f)\ge\max_{i\in T}\mathcal E_i^{(p)}(f)$.  Moreover
$\mathcal E_T^{(2)}(f)=4\sum_{|S\cap T|\text{ odd}}\widehat f(S)^2$ and
$\operatorname{Cov}(f,x_i)=\frac14\mathcal E_i^{(1)}(f)$.  If $f$ is Boolean then
$\mathcal E_i^{(1)}(f)=\mathcal E_i^{(2)}(f)=\operatorname{Inf}_i[f]$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix $j\notin T$ and let $Y$ be a uniform random subset of $[n]\setminus\{j\}$.  Given $Y=y$, write
\[
 a=f(y),\qquad b=f(y\cup\{j\}),\qquad
 c=f(y\triangle T),\qquad d=f((y\triangle T)\cup\{j\}).
\]
Since $f$ is increasing, $a\le b$ and $c\le d$.  Conditioning first on $Y$ gives
\[
 \mathcal E_T^{(p)}(f)
 =\mathbb{E}_Y\frac{|a-c|^p+|b-d|^p}{2},\qquad
 \mathcal E_{T\cup\{j\}}^{(p)}(f)
 =\mathbb{E}_Y\frac{|a-d|^p+|b-c|^p}{2}.
\]
Thus $\mathcal E_{T\cup\{j\}}^{(2)}(f)-\mathcal E_T^{(2)}(f) = \mathbb{E}_Y (b-a)(d-c) \ge 0$.
Similarly $\mathcal E_{T\cup\{j\}}^{(1)}(f)-\mathcal E_T^{(1)}(f) \ge0$, as the order-preserving
matching $a \leftrightarrow c$, $b \leftrightarrow d$ minimises total $L^1$ distance.
Iterating over the coordinates of $T$ gives $\mathcal E_T^{(p)}(f)\ge\max_{i\in T}\mathcal E_i^{(p)}(f)$.

The Fourier formula follows from
\(
 f(x)-f(x\triangle T)
 =2\sum_{|S\cap T|\text{ odd}}\widehat f(S)\chi_S(x)
\)
and Parseval.
Finally, writing $\mu_j=\mathbb{E}[f(X)\mid X_i=j]$ for $j=0,1$
we have  $\operatorname{Cov}(f,x_i)= \mathbb{E}[f(X)x_i]-\mathbb{E} f\mathbb{E} x_i
= \frac{\mu_1}{2} - \frac{\mu_0+\mu_1}{4}
= \frac{\mu_1-\mu_0}{4}$.
Here $\mathcal E_i^{(1)}(f)=\mu_1-\mu_0$ by monotonicity
and in the Boolean case $\mathcal E_i^{(1)}(f)=\mathcal E_i^{(2)}(f)=\operatorname{Inf}_i[f]$.

\(M_x^{\mathcal{E}}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{E}}\widehat{M_x}(S)\chi_S \) and
\(M_x^{\mathcal{O}}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{O}}\widehat{M_x}(S)\chi_S\).
Then $M_x^{\mathcal{E}}|_{\mathcal{A}} \in U$ and  $M_x^{\mathcal{O}}|_{\mathcal{A}}\in V$.
For any $x\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{F}$ and $z\in\mathcal{A}$, as $\mathcal{A}$ is intersecting we have $M_x(\overline z)=0$, 
so $M_x^{\mathcal{E}}(z)=M_x^{\mathcal{O}}(z)=M_x(z)/2$, and so  $m_x\in U\cap V$. As before,
they are linearly independent as their matrix on $\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{F}$ is triangular with diagonal $1$.
If $m_x(z)\ne0$, then $x\subseteq z$ so monotonicity of $h_+$ gives $h_+(z)\ge h_+(x)$. 
The remaining assertions follow.

Let $P_U$ denote orthogonal projection onto $U$, and set $L=P_UD|_V:V\to U$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted-matrix}, the vectors $\{V_{\mathcal{E}}e_S:S\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{F}\}$ and $\{V_{\mathcal{O}}e_T:T\in\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{F}\}$ are orthonormal bases of $U$ and $V$, and the matrix of $L$ in these bases is exactly
\(K_{\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{F},\,\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\), with $(S,T)$ entry 
\(
 \langle V_{\mathcal{E}}e_S,DV_{\mathcal{O}}e_T\rangle
 =(V_{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathsf T}DV_{\mathcal{O}})_{S,T}=K_{S,T}.
\)

Order $\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{F}$ as $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ so that
$h_+(x_1)\ge\cdots\ge h_+(x_d)$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:common-monomial-flag},
$W_{h_+(x_k)}\subseteq U\cap V$ has dimension at least $k$ and is supported where $D\ge h_+(x_k)I$.  
Hence for every nonzero $w\in W_{h_+(x_k)}$ we have
\[
 \|Lw\|\,\|w\|\ge\langle Lw,w\rangle
 =\langle P_UDw,w\rangle
 =\langle Dw,w\rangle
 \ge h_+(x_k)\|w\|^2,
\]
The max--min formula for singular values $s_1(L)\ge s_2(L)\ge\cdots$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:common-monomial-flag} give
\[
 s_k(L)=\max_{\dim E=k}\;\min_{0\ne w\in E}\frac{\|Lw\|}{\|w\|} \ge h_+(x_k), 
 \ \text{ so we deduce \eqref{eq:common-flag-bound} by}
\]
\[
 \|K_{\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{F},\,\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\|_F^2
 =\|L\|_F^2
 =\sum_k s_k(L)^2
 \ge\sum_{k=1}^d h_+(x_k)^2
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{F}}h_+(x)^2.
\]
