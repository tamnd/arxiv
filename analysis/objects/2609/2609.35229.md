<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There exists a B\'ezout domain that is not an elementary divisor domain.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:orientation-descent}
Let $q:K'\to K$ be a monotone surjection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, and let $X,Y:K\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous with $X^2+Y^2=1$. If $L_{X\circ q,Y\circ q}$ is orientable, then $L_{X,Y}$ is orientable.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $v$ be a continuous nowhere-zero section of $L_{X\circ q,Y\circ q}$, and put $u=v/\|v\|$. For $z\in K$, the line $L_{X,Y}(z)$ contains exactly two unit vectors. The continuous map $u$ therefore takes values in a two-point set on the connected fiber $q^{-1}(z)$, so it is constant there. Hence $u=\bar u\circ q$ for a map $\bar u:K\to\mathbb{R}^2$ such that $\bar u(z)$ is a unit vector in $L_{X,Y}(z)$. Because $K'$ is compact and $K$ is Hausdorff, $q$ is a closed map and hence a quotient map. Therefore $\bar u$ is continuous, and it orients $L_{X,Y}$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:monotone-composition}
A composite of monotone surjections between compact Hausdorff spaces is monotone.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We first note that if $q:K'\to K$ is monotone and $C\subset K$ is compact and connected, then $q^{-1}(C)$ is connected. Otherwise, write $q^{-1}(C)=F\amalg G$ with $F$ and $G$ nonempty and closed. Each fiber of $q$ meets at most one of $F$ and $G$, because it is connected. The compact sets $q(F)$ and $q(G)$ are therefore disjoint nonempty closed subsets whose union is $C$, a contradiction. Applying this observation to every fiber of a second monotone surjection proves the claim.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:jouanolou}
Let $B$ be a ring and $c\in B^{r+1}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every homomorphism $B\to B'$, one has $J_B(c)\otimes_B B'\cong J_{B'}(c')$, where $c'$ is the image of $c$. In particular, $J_B(c)/\mathfrak{a} J_B(c)\cong J_{B/\mathfrak{a}}(\bar c)$ for every ideal $\mathfrak{a}\subset B$, and the formation of $J_B(c)$ commutes with localization of $B$.
\item For every $j\in\{0,\ldots,r\}$, eliminating $\sigma_j$ gives $J_B(c)[1/c_j]\cong B[1/c_j][\sigma_i\mid i\ne j]$.
\item If $B$ is a domain and $c_j\ne0$ for some $j$, then $J_B(c)$ is a domain and $B\to J_B(c)$ is injective.
\item $J_B(c)$ is formally smooth over $B$. If $B$ is a smooth finitely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, then so is $J_B(c)$.
\item If $B$ is a Noetherian UFD, $c_j$ is a prime element of $B$, and $c_j\nmid c_m$ for some $m$, then $J_B(c)$ is a UFD.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Assertion (1) follows from the right exactness of the tensor product, and (2) from solving $q_c=0$ for $\sigma_j$.

For (3), the constant term of $q_c$ is $-1$. For every $b\in B$, the image of $q_c$ in $(B/bB)[\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r]$ is therefore a unit in the corresponding formal power series ring, and hence a nonzerodivisor in the polynomial ring. If $B$ is a domain and $0\ne b\in B$, it follows that multiplication by $b$ is injective on $J_B(c)$: from $bf=gq_c$ in $B[\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r]$, reduction modulo $b$ gives $g\in bB[\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r]$, and cancellation of $b$ gives $f\in(q_c)$. Applying this to the powers of $c_j$ shows that $J_B(c)\to J_B(c)[1/c_j]$ is injective. By (2), the target is a polynomial ring over the domain $B[1/c_j]$ and contains $B$.

For (4), put $P=B[\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r]$. Since $q_c$ has constant term $-1$, it is a unit in $B[[\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r]]$ and hence a nonzerodivisor in $P$. The $B$-derivation $\mathcal E=\sum_{i=0}^r\sigma_i\,\partial/\partial\sigma_i$ of $P$ satisfies $\mathcal E(q_c)=q_c+1$. It therefore induces a map $\Omega_{P/B}\otimes_P J_B(c)\to J_B(c)$ that sends $dq_c$ to $1$. Thus the conormal sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow(q_c)/(q_c^2)\longrightarrow
  \Omega_{P/B}\otimes_P J_B(c)\longrightarrow\Omega_{J_B(c)/B}
  \longrightarrow0
\]
is split exact, so $J_B(c)$ is formally smooth over $B$ by the split-conormal criterion \cite[Tag~031J]{Stacks}. If $B$ is smooth and finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, then $J_B(c)$ is formally smooth and of finite presentation over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence smooth.

For (5), the ring $J_B(c)[1/c_j]$ is a UFD by (2) and the facts recalled above. By (1), $J_B(c)/c_jJ_B(c)\cong J_{B/c_jB}(\bar c)$. This is a domain by (3), because $B/c_jB$ is a domain and $\bar c_m\ne0$. By (3) again, $c_j$ is nonzero in $J_B(c)$, so $c_j$ is a prime element of the Noetherian domain $J_B(c)$. Nagata's criterion shows that $J_B(c)$ is a UFD.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:marked-centre}
A \emph{marked center} at $\mathfrak{m}$ is a pair $J=(x,e)$ consisting of a regular system of parameters $x$ of $A_\mathfrak{m}$ and rational weights $e_1\ge e_2\ge\cdots\ge e_n\ge0$. For $t\in\mathbb{Q}$, its \emph{weighted ideal of level $t$} is
\[
  F_t(J)=\Bigl(x^\alpha:\;
  \begin{aligned}[t]
    &\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^n,\quad \alpha_i=0\text{ if }e_i=0,\\[-2pt]
    &\sum_{i=1}^n e_i\alpha_i\ge t
  \end{aligned}
  \Bigr)A_\mathfrak{m}.
\]
If $I\subseteq A$ is an ideal contained in $\mathfrak{m}$, then $J$ is \emph{admissible} for $I$ if $IA_\mathfrak{m}\subseteq F_1(J)$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:invariant}
Let $A$ be a smooth finitely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-domain and let $I\subset A$ be a nonzero ideal.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Invariant and maximal center; \cite[Theorem~5.3.1]{ATW}, \cite[Theorem~3.1.1]{Brais}.)} For every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq I$ there is an admissible marked center for $I$ at $\mathfrak{m}$ whose weight vector is lexicographically smallest among all admissible marked centers at $\mathfrak{m}$, and all admissible marked centers with this weight vector have the same weighted ideals. We call this weight vector the \emph{invariant} $\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$, and the common weighted ideals $F_t(\mathfrak{m})$ the \emph{maximal center} of $I$ at $\mathfrak{m}$. All weights of $\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$ are at most $1$, and its first weight is $1/\operatorname{ord}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$ \cite[Section~5.1]{ATW}. In particular, the first weight has the form $1/a$ for a positive integer $a$, and if $b\ge1$ and $IA_\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^bA_\mathfrak{m}$, then it is at most $1/b$.
\item \emph{(Polynomial-localization invariance; a special case of \cite[Theorem~5.1.3(3)]{ATW} and \cite[Proposition~3.5.1]{Brais}.)} Let $A\to B$ be a homomorphism to a smooth finitely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-domain, let $\mathfrak{n}\supseteq IB$ be a maximal ideal of $B$, and put $\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}\cap A$. Suppose that, for some $r\ge0$ and prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $A_\mathfrak{m}[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, there is an $A$-algebra isomorphism
\[
  B_\mathfrak{n}\cong A_\mathfrak{m}[t_1,\ldots,t_r]_\mathfrak{q}.
\]
Then $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal and $\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{n}(IB)=\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$. This is the special case of smooth invariance used below.
\item \emph{(The maximal locus; \cite[Theorems~5.1.3(2) and~6.1.1]{ATW}, \cite[Propositions~4.1.1 and~4.1.2]{Brais}.)} Assume $I\ne A$. Among the invariants $\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$, $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq I$ maximal, there is a largest one $\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$ for $\preceq$. There are prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}_1,\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_r$ with $\mathfrak{p}_i+\mathfrak{p}_j=A$ for $i\ne j$ such that $\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)=\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$ exactly when $\mathfrak{m}$ contains some $\mathfrak{p}_i$. If $k$ is the number of nonzero entries of $\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$ and $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}_i$, there is a regular system of parameters $x$ of $A_\mathfrak{m}$ with $\mathfrak{p}_iA_\mathfrak{m}=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ such that $(x,\operatorname{maxinv}(I))$ is admissible for $I$ at $\mathfrak{m}$; by (1), its weighted ideals are the $F_t(\mathfrak{m})$. We call $V(\mathfrak{p}_1),\ldots,V(\mathfrak{p}_r)$ the \emph{components} of the maximal locus.
\item \emph{(The global center; \cite[Proposition~4.1.1 and Definition~4.1.4]{Brais}, together with affine gluing.)} Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be one of the $\mathfrak{p}_i$. For the center obtained by retaining the component $V(\mathfrak{p})$ of the global associated center, put
\[
  F_t=\{f\in A:\ f\in F_t(\mathfrak{m})\text{ for every maximal ideal }\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}\}
  \qquad(t\in\mathbb{Q}).
\]
Indeed, restrict Brais's global associated center to an open neighborhood of $V(\mathfrak{p})$ disjoint from the other components of the maximal locus and, level by level, glue its ideals to the unit ideal on $\operatorname{Spec}(A)\setminus V(\mathfrak{p})$. The displayed intersection is the ideal of global sections of the resulting level-$t$ ideal sheaf. Hence $F_tA_\mathfrak{m}=F_t(\mathfrak{m})$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$, and $F_tA_\mathfrak{m}=A_\mathfrak{m}$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\not\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$.
\item \emph{(Well-ordering; \cite[Section~5.1]{ATW}, \cite[Proposition~3.3.3]{Brais}.)} There is a set $\Gamma$ of weight vectors, independent of $A$ and $I$, that contains all invariants and such that, for every $N$, its elements with at most $N$ nonzero entries are well-ordered by $\preceq$. This is the reciprocal, zero-padded reformulation of the sets $\Gamma_j$ constructed in \cite[Section~3.3, especially Proposition~3.3.3]{Brais}.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The set described in (5) may be larger than the set of actual invariants. We explain why it contains the latter and is still well-ordered in bounded length. In the differential construction of \cite[Sections~3.2--3.3]{Brais}, after weights $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ have been chosen, the next positive weight has the form
\[
  e_{r+1}=\frac{1-\sum_{i=1}^r\beta_i e_i}{q},
  \qquad \beta_i\in\mathbb{N},\quad q\in\mathbb{N}_{>0},\quad
  0<1-\sum_{i=1}^r\beta_i e_i\le1.
\]
Let $D$ be the product of the denominators, in lowest terms, of $e_1,\ldots,e_r$. The numerator is $h/D$ for an integer $1\le h\le D$, and therefore
\[
  e_{r+1}=\frac{h}{Dq}=\frac{D!}{a},
  \qquad a=\frac{D!Dq}{h}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.
\]
For the first weight, the empty product gives $D=1$ and $e_1=1/a$.

For a fixed prefix, the possible next positive weights lie in
$\{D!/a:a\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\}$, which is well-ordered by the reverse of the usual numerical order. Allowing a zero entry, after which every entry is zero, adds a largest element for this order. In a strictly descending sequence for $\preceq$, the first entries must therefore eventually stabilize; once a prefix has stabilized, the next entries must eventually stabilize as well. For sequences of length at most $N$, induction through the $N$ positions rules out an infinite strictly descending sequence. This proves the required well-ordering.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- notation-1: body_md -->
\label{not:rees}
Let $A$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}, let $I\subsetneq A$ be a nonzero ideal, let $V(\mathfrak{p})$ be a component of the maximal locus, and let $e=\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$ have $k$ nonzero entries. Choose an integer $d\ge1$ with $de_i\in\mathbb{Z}$ for all $i$, and index the global center of Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(4) by integers: $\mathcal{F}_j=F_{j/d}$ for $j\in\mathbb{Z}$. Put
\begin{gather*}
  \mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}_jT^j\subset A[T,T^{-1}],\qquad s=T^{-1},\\
  \mathcal{R}_+=\bigl(\mathcal{F}_jT^j:j\ge1\bigr)\mathcal{R},\qquad
  I_w=\bigl(fT^d:f\in I\bigr)\mathcal{R}.
\end{gather*}
Thus $\mathcal{R}$ is the extended Rees algebra of the weighted center along $V(\mathfrak{p})$, $s$ is the exceptional element, and $I_w$ is the weak transform of $I$. For $1\le j\le d$, let $G_j$ be a finite set of generators of $\mathcal{F}_j$.
<!-- end notation-1 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:rees}
In the situation of Notation~\ref{not:rees}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{F}_j=A$ for $j\le0$, $\mathcal{F}_1=\mathfrak{p}$, $\mathfrak{p}^j\subseteq\mathcal{F}_j$ for $j\ge0$, and $I\subseteq\mathcal{F}_d$. In particular, $I_w\subseteq \mathcal{R}$, $I\mathcal{R}=s^dI_w$, and $\mathfrak{p}=\mathcal{F}_1\subseteq s\mathcal{R}$.
\item $\mathcal{F}_j=\sum_{q=1}^d\mathcal{F}_q\mathcal{F}_{j-q}$ for $j>d$. Consequently, $\mathcal{R}=A[s,\ gT^j:\ g\in G_j,\ 1\le j\le d]$, and the elements $gT^j$ with $g\in G_j$ and $1\le j\le d$ generate $\mathcal{R}_+$.
\item $\mathcal{R}[1/s]=A[T,T^{-1}]$.
\item Let $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$ be a maximal ideal, let $x$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(3), and put $w_i=de_i$ for $i\le k$. Then $u_i\mapsto x_iT^{w_i}$ induces an isomorphism
\[
  A_\mathfrak{m}[s,u_1,\ldots,u_k]/(x_i-s^{w_i}u_i\mid1\le i\le k)\cong \mathcal{R}\otimes_AA_\mathfrak{m}.
\]
If $\mathfrak{m}\not\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$, then $\mathcal{R}\otimes_AA_\mathfrak{m}=A_\mathfrak{m}[T,T^{-1}]$. In particular, $\mathcal{R}$ is a smooth finitely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-domain.
\item $\mathcal{R}/s\mathcal{R}\cong\bigoplus_{j\ge0}\mathcal{F}_j/\mathcal{F}_{j+1}$ is a domain. Hence $s$ is a prime element of $\mathcal{R}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(4), every assertion about the ideals $\mathcal{F}_j$ may be checked after localization at maximal ideals. If $\mathfrak{m}\not\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$, then $\mathcal{F}_jA_\mathfrak{m}=A_\mathfrak{m}$ for every $j$. If $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$, then $\mathcal{F}_jA_\mathfrak{m}=F_{j/d}(\mathfrak{m})$ is generated by the monomials $x^\alpha$ with $\sum_{i=1}^k w_i\alpha_i\ge j$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(3). All weights are at most $1$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(1), so the weights $w_i$ are integers in $\{1,\ldots,d\}$. This gives $\mathcal{F}_1A_\mathfrak{m}=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)=\mathfrak{p} A_\mathfrak{m}$ and $\mathfrak{p}^j\subseteq\mathcal{F}_j$, while $I\subseteq\mathcal{F}_d$ is admissibility. Since $f=s^d\cdot fT^d$ for $f\in I$ and $g=s\cdot gT$ for $g\in\mathcal{F}_1$, we have $I\mathcal{R}=s^dI_w$ and $\mathcal{F}_1\subseteq s\mathcal{R}$. This proves (1).

For (2), a monomial of weight at least $j>d$ is the product of some $x_i$, which has weight $w_i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$, and a monomial of weight at least $j-w_i$. By induction on $j$, every homogeneous element of $\mathcal{R}$ of positive degree lies in the ideal generated by the elements $gT^j$ and is a polynomial in them with coefficients in $A$. Moreover, $\mathcal{F}_jT^j=As^{-j}$ for $j\le0$. Assertion (3) holds because $\mathcal{F}_j=A$ for $j\le0$.

For (4), the formation of $\mathcal{R}$ commutes with localization, so $\mathcal{R}\otimes_AA_\mathfrak{m}$ is the extended Rees algebra of the filtration $(F_{j/d}(\mathfrak{m}))_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}$. For a filtration given by monomials in part of a regular system of parameters, the displayed presentation is \cite[Proposition~5.2.2]{QR}; see also \cite[Equation~(2.3)]{Brais}. Its left-hand side is formally smooth over $\mathbb{Q}$ by the criterion \cite[Tag~031J]{Stacks} already used in Lemma~\ref{lem:jouanolou}(4): the derivations $\partial_1,\ldots,\partial_k$ of $A_\mathfrak{m}$, extended to $A_\mathfrak{m}[s,u_1,\ldots,u_k]$ by $\partial_i(s)=\partial_i(u_j)=0$, satisfy $\partial_i(x_j-s^{w_j}u_j)=\delta_{ij}$. Every localization of $\mathcal{R}$ at a prime ideal is a localization of some $\mathcal{R}\otimes_AA_\mathfrak{m}$, so it is formally smooth over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $\mathcal{R}$ is of finite type over $\mathbb{Q}$ by (2), it is smooth. It is a domain because it is contained in $A[T,T^{-1}]$.

For (5), the identification $\mathcal{R}/s\mathcal{R}\cong\bigoplus_j\mathcal{F}_j/\mathcal{F}_{j+1}$ follows from $s\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{F}_jT^j=\mathcal{F}_{j+1}T^j$. By (4), for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$ the graded ring $\bigoplus_j\mathcal{F}_j/\mathcal{F}_{j+1}$ becomes the polynomial ring $(A/\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{p}}[u_1,\ldots,u_k]$ after localization at $\mathfrak{m}$. In particular, each $\mathcal{F}_j/\mathcal{F}_{j+1}$ is a locally free $A/\mathfrak{p}$-module, hence torsion-free. Now let $f\in\mathcal{F}_a\setminus\mathcal{F}_{a+1}$ and $g\in\mathcal{F}_b\setminus\mathcal{F}_{b+1}$. Their images in $(\mathcal{F}_a/\mathcal{F}_{a+1})_\mathfrak{m}$ and $(\mathcal{F}_b/\mathcal{F}_{b+1})_\mathfrak{m}$ are nonzero at every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}$. Their product in $(A/\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{p}}[u_1,\ldots,u_k]$ is therefore nonzero, so $fg\notin\mathcal{F}_{a+b+1}$. Thus $\bigoplus_j\mathcal{F}_j/\mathcal{F}_{j+1}$ is a domain, and it is nonzero because $\mathcal{F}_1=\mathfrak{p}\ne A$.

\label{thm:drop}
In the situation of Notation~\ref{not:rees}, let $P$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathcal{R}$ that contains $I_w+s\mathcal{R}$ but not $\mathcal{R}_+$. Then $\operatorname{inv}_P(I_w)\prec\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We use the affine extended-Rees argument underlying property~(C) in \cite[Section~2.2]{Brais}. Let $\mathfrak{m}=P\cap A$. The weak Nullstellensatz makes $\mathfrak{m}$ maximal, and $\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}$ because $\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{R}\subseteq s\mathcal{R}\subseteq P$. Work near $\mathfrak{m}$ with compatible parameters as in Lemma~\ref{lem:rees}(4). Let $P_0$ be the vertex point over $\mathfrak{m}$, defined by $s=u_1=\cdots=u_k=0$ and the remaining parameters of $A_\mathfrak{m}$. Equivalently,
\[
  P_0=\left\{\sum_j f_jT^j\in\mathcal{R}:f_0\in\mathfrak{m}\right\}.
\]
The differential construction of the invariant at the vertex gives
$\operatorname{inv}_{P_0}(I_w)=e:=\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$: the positive weights are those of the original center, now on the $u_i$, with $s$ of weight zero. This follows by transporting each step of the differential construction through $x_i=s^{w_i}u_i$; admissibility and the successive least derivative candidates are preserved. In this maximal center every homogeneous element $fT^j$, $f\in\mathcal{F}_j$, $j\ge1$, belongs to the weighted ideal of level $1/d$.

Choose such an element $h=fT^j\notin P$. Spreading the vertex center and this membership to a principal neighborhood $D(G)$ of $P_0$, the local construction of the maximal center gives the following: at every maximal ideal $Q\in D(G)$ containing $I_w$, one has $\operatorname{inv}_Q(I_w)\preceq e$, and equality implies $h\in Q$. Indeed, in the equality case the spread center is the maximal center at $Q$ by uniqueness, and its positive-level weighted ideals lie in $Q$.

The grading acts by automorphisms $\sigma_\lambda(T)=\lambda T$, $\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$, and preserves $I_w$. Since $s\in P$, all negative-degree terms of $G$ vanish modulo $P$. The constant term of $\sigma_\lambda(G)$ modulo $P$ is nonzero, because $G\notin P_0$. Thus the image of $\sigma_\lambda(G)$ is a nonzero polynomial in $\lambda$ over the field $\mathcal{R}/P$, and some $\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$ makes it nonzero. Then $Q=\sigma_\lambda^{-1}(P)$ lies in $D(G)$, contains $I_w$, and has the same invariant as $P$. But $\sigma_\lambda(h)=\lambda^jh\notin P$, so $h\notin Q$. Equality with $e$ is impossible, and $\operatorname{inv}_P(I_w)\prec e$ follows.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:derivations}
Let $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq I$ be a maximal ideal of $A$, and let $\delta$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-derivation of $A$ with $\delta(I)\subseteq I$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The derivation $\delta$ preserves the maximal center: $\delta(F_t(\mathfrak{m}))\subseteq F_t(\mathfrak{m})$ for every $t$. Consequently, $\delta(\mathcal{F}_j)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_j$ in Notation~\ref{not:rees}.
\item Suppose that $\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m$ are derivations of $A$ with $\delta_j(I)\subseteq I$, and that $\det(\delta_j(z_q))_{1\le j,q\le m}\notin\mathfrak{m}$ for some $z_1,\ldots,z_m\in A$. Then $\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$ has at most $\dim A-m$ nonzero entries.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For (1), put $C=A_\mathfrak{m}$, write $e=\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$, and let $k$ be the number of its nonzero entries. Choose parameters $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ compatible with the maximal center and an integer $d\ge1$ such that every $de_i$ is integral. Choose an integer $D>d$. Put
\[
  B=A[\tau],\qquad \mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{m} B+\tau B,\qquad
  I_D=IB+\tau^DB.
\]
Let $G_t\subseteq B_\mathfrak{n}$ be the weighted ideal for the ordered parameters
\[
  (x_1,\ldots,x_k,\tau,x_{k+1},\ldots,x_n)
\]
with weights $(e_1,\ldots,e_k,1/D,0,\ldots,0)$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Claim.} The filtration $(G_t)$ is the maximal center of $I_D$ at $\mathfrak{n}$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Proof of the claim.}
Write $a_i=1/e_i$, so $a_i\le d<D$, and use the differential construction of \cite[Sections~3.2--3.3]{Brais}. After a prefix $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ has been chosen, the next exponent is the least candidate of the form
\[
  \frac{\sum_{j>r}\beta_j}
       {1-\sum_{i=1}^r\beta_i/a_i},
  \qquad 1-\sum_{i=1}^r\beta_i/a_i>0,
\]
for which the corresponding derivative ideal is the unit ideal; the variables include $\tau$. For $IB_\mathfrak{n}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(1)--(2) shows that the maximal center is the smooth pullback of the original center, with $\tau$ of weight zero, so its positive exponents are $a_1,\ldots,a_k$. At every stage, the derivative ideal for $I_DB_\mathfrak{n}=IB_\mathfrak{n}+\tau^DB_\mathfrak{n}$ is the sum of the derivative ideals for the two summands. The derivative ideal contributed by $\tau^DB_\mathfrak{n}$ is proper unless $\beta_\tau\ge D$, and in that case the displayed candidate is at least $D$. Since the sum of two proper ideals in the local ring $B_\mathfrak{n}$ is proper, adjoining $\tau^D$ does not alter the first $k$ exponents, all of which are at most $d<D$. After those steps, $IB_\mathfrak{n}$ is admissible for the pulled-back center, while $\tau^D$ contributes the next exponent $D$ and the parameter $\tau$, obtained by differentiating $D-1$ times. The enlarged center is admissible for $I_DB_\mathfrak{n}$, so the construction stops. Uniqueness of the maximal center identifies its filtration with $(G_t)$, proving the claim.
\medskip

Extend $\delta$ to $C$ and set $\delta(\tau)=0$. Under the identification
\[
  B_\mathfrak{n}/\tau^DB_\mathfrak{n}\cong C[\tau]/(\tau^D),
\]
the formulas
\[
  \Phi(\tau)=\tau,\qquad
  \Phi(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}\frac{\tau^j\delta^j(f)}{j!}\quad(f\in C)
\]
define an automorphism; its inverse is obtained by replacing $\tau$ by $-\tau$. Since $\delta(IC)\subseteq IC$, the automorphism $\Phi$ preserves the image of $I_D$. Put
\[
  x'_i=\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}\frac{\tau^j\delta^j(x_i)}{j!}.
\]
Modulo $(\mathfrak{n} B_\mathfrak{n})^2$, each $x'_i$ differs from $x_i$ by a scalar multiple of $\tau$. Hence
\[
  (x'_1,\ldots,x'_k,\tau,x'_{k+1},\ldots,x'_n)
\]
is a regular system of parameters. Let $G'_t$ be the weighted ideals defined by these parameters and the same weights as $G_t$. Modulo $\tau^D$, the automorphism $\Phi$ preserves the image of $I_D$ and carries the image of $G_1$ to that of $G'_1$. Therefore
\[
  I_DB_\mathfrak{n}\subseteq G'_1+\tau^DB_\mathfrak{n}=G'_1,
\]
where the equality follows from $\tau^D\in G'_1$. Thus the new center is admissible for $I_D$. The claim identifies its weight vector as maximal, so uniqueness gives $G'_t=G_t$ for every $t$.

In particular, $x'_i\in G_{e_i}$ for $1\le i\le k$. Under the natural map $B_\mathfrak{n}\to C[[\tau]]$, expand an element of $G_{e_i}$ in the monomial generators of that ideal. Only generators of $\tau$-degree zero or one can contribute to the coefficient of $\tau$. In the first case the $x$-weight is at least $e_i$, and in the second it is at least $e_i-1/D$. Hence the coefficient belongs to $F_{e_i-1/D}(\mathfrak{m})$, and therefore
\[
  \delta(x_i)\in F_{e_i-1/D}(\mathfrak{m})=F_{e_i}(\mathfrak{m}).
\]
The equality holds because $e_i$ and every $x$-monomial weight lie in $\frac1d\mathbb{Z}$, whereas $1/D<1/d$. The Leibniz rule now gives $\delta(F_t(\mathfrak{m}))\subseteq F_t(\mathfrak{m})$ for every $t$. Applying this at every maximal ideal containing the chosen component and using the definition in Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(4) proves $\delta(\mathcal{F}_j)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_j$.

For (2), let $k$ be the number of nonzero entries of $e=\operatorname{inv}_\mathfrak{m}(I)$. For $0<\varepsilon\le e_k$ we have $F_\varepsilon(\mathfrak{m})=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$, so by (1) every $\delta_j(x_i)$ with $i\le k$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}$. Writing $\delta_j=\sum_i\delta_j(x_i)\partial_i$, we see that the matrix $(\delta_j(z_l))$ is the product of $(\delta_j(x_i))_{j,i}$ and $(\partial_iz_l)_{i,l}$. Modulo $\mathfrak{m}$, the first factor has zero columns for $i\le k$, so the product has rank at most $n-k$ over $\kappa(\mathfrak{m})$. Hence $m\le n-k$.

\label{lem:divisorial}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
At every maximal ideal containing $\mathfrak{p}$, the ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ is generated by one parameter. Thus $\mathfrak{p}$ is a height-one prime, hence $\mathfrak{p}=\pi A$ because $A$ is factorial. Admissibility gives $I\subseteq\pi^aA$, and therefore $I=\pi^aI_1$.

Thus the chosen component $V(\mathfrak{p})$ disappears and no new maximal component is created. The conclusion follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(3).

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:torsor}
The ring $U$ is a smooth finitely generated factorial $\mathbb{Q}$-domain, the homomorphism $A\to U$ is injective, and $IU=s^dI_1$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->

\[
  U/sU\cong J_{\mathcal{R}/s\mathcal{R}}(\overline{\mathbf h}).
\]
The base ring is a domain by Lemma~\ref{lem:rees}(5), and the row $\overline{\mathbf h}$ is nonzero: after localizing at a maximal ideal over $\mathfrak{p}$ and writing $w_1=de_1$, one has $x_1\in\mathcal{F}_{w_1}A_\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{w_1+1}A_\mathfrak{m}$, so some generator in $G_{w_1}$ has nonzero initial form. Lemma~\ref{lem:jouanolou}(3) therefore shows that $U/sU$ is a domain, so $s$ is prime in $U$.

After inverting $s$,
\[
  U[1/s]\cong J_{A[T,T^{-1}]}(\mathbf h).
\]
The Laurent polynomial ring is factorial, the row contains the prime element $\pi T$, and it also contains an element $gT$ not divisible by $\pi T$: otherwise the height-$k$ prime $\mathfrak{p}$, with $k\ge2$, would be generated by $\pi$. Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:jouanolou}(5) makes $U[1/s]$ factorial, and Nagata's criterion makes $U$ factorial. The inclusion $A\hookrightarrow U$ follows from $A\subseteq\mathcal{R}\hookrightarrow U$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:torsor-invariant}
The ideal $I_1$ is nonzero. Moreover, either $I_1=U$, or $\operatorname{maxinv}(I_1)\prec\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$, or $\operatorname{maxinv}(I_1)=\operatorname{maxinv}(I)$ and $c(I_1)<c(I)$.

The contractions $P$ and $Q\cap A$ are maximal by the weak Nullstellensatz. Since the $h_i$ generate the unit ideal in $U$, some $h_i\notin P$; hence $U_Q$ is a localization of a polynomial ring over $\mathcal{R}_P$, and
\[
  \operatorname{inv}_Q(I_1)=\operatorname{inv}_P(I_w).
\]
If $s\in Q$, Theorem~\ref{thm:drop} gives a strict decrease. If $s\notin Q$, then $\mathcal{R}_P$ is a localization of $A_{Q\cap A}[T,T^{-1}]$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rees}(3), so Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(2) gives

  \operatorname{inv}_Q(I_1)=\operatorname{inv}_{Q\cap A}(I).
\]

and some generator from $G_1$ survives modulo $\mathfrak{p}'$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:jouanolou}(3) shows that $U/\mathfrak{p}'U$ is a nonzero domain. <!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:length-control}
Suppose that $(A,I,m)$ satisfies $(\star_N)$. In the divisorial step, $(A,I_1,m)$ satisfies $(\star_N)$. In the torsor step, $(U,I_1,m+\ell+1)$ satisfies $(\star_N)$.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
In the divisorial step, the dimension bound is unchanged and $V(I_1)\subseteq V(I)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:derivations}(1), a derivation preserving $I$ satisfies $\delta(\pi)=b\pi$ and therefore preserves $(I:\pi^a)$, since
\[
  \pi^a\delta(f)=\delta(\pi^af)-ab\,\pi^af\in I
  \qquad(\pi^af\in I).

Then $Q\cap A$ is maximal and contains $I$, so choose the $m$ derivations and elements supplied by $(\star_N)$ there, and choose $i_0$ with $h_{i_0}\notin Q$. <!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:sphere-bundle}
In the torsor step, let $K\subset\operatorname{Spec}(A)(\mathbb{R})$ be compact, put $E_i=2\,d!/j_i$ (an even integer), and let $K'\subset\operatorname{Spec}(U)(\mathbb{R})$ be the set of real points $z'$ such that
\[
  \begin{gathered}
    z'|_A\in K,\qquad z'(s)\ge0,\qquad
    \sum_{i=0}^\ell z'(h_i)^{E_i}=1,\\
    z'(\sigma_i)=z'(h_i)^{E_i-1}\qquad(0\le i\le\ell).
  \end{gathered}
\]
Then $K'$ is compact, and $K'\to K$ is a monotone surjection.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The last two conditions are compatible with $\sum_i h_i\sigma_i=1$. If $z'\in K'$ and $z=z'|_A$, then $s^{j_i}h_i=g_i$ and $j_iE_i=2d!$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s-norm}
  z'(s)^{2d!}=\sum_{i=0}^\ell z(g_i)^{E_i}=: \nu(z).
\end{equation}
The defining conditions are closed, $|z'(h_i)|,|z'(\sigma_i)|\le1$, and \eqref{eq:s-norm} bounds $z'(s)$ on $K'$. Since $U$ is generated over $A$ by $s$, the $h_i$, and the $\sigma_i$, while elements of $A$ are bounded on the compact set $K$, the criterion of Subsection~\ref{subsec:real-points} shows that $K'$ is compact. The restriction map $K'\to K$ is continuous.

The function $\nu(z)$ is positive exactly when $\mathfrak{p}\nsubseteq\ker z$: every $g_i$ lies in $\mathfrak{p}$, while the coefficients with $j_i=1$ generate $\mathfrak{p}$. If $\nu(z)>0$, equation \eqref{eq:s-norm} forces $z'(s)=\rho:=\nu(z)^{1/(2d!)}>0$. There is then a unique point over $z$: extend $z$ to $A[T,T^{-1}]$ by $z'(T)=\rho^{-1}$ and put $z'(\sigma_i)=z'(h_i)^{E_i-1}$.

Now suppose $\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\ker z$. Then every point over $z$ sends $s$ to zero. Choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\ker z$ and use the local presentation in Lemma~\ref{lem:rees}(4). Points of $\mathcal{R}$ over $z$ with $s=0$ are parametrized by $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in\mathbb{R}^k$, where $a_r$ is the value of $x_rT^{w_r}$. Write $H_i(a)$ for the value of $h_i$ at this point. Each $H_i$ is weighted homogeneous of degree $j_i$, so, with
\[
  \lambda\star a=(\lambda^{w_1}a_1,\ldots,\lambda^{w_k}a_k),
  \qquad \varphi(a)=\sum_i H_i(a)^{E_i},
\]
one has $\varphi(\lambda\star a)=\lambda^{2d!}\varphi(a)$. Moreover, if $\varphi(a)=0$, then the even exponents force every $H_i(a)$ to vanish. The corresponding point therefore vanishes on the ideal $\mathcal{R}_+$ generated by the $h_i$, including every $x_rT^{w_r}$, and hence $a=0$.

The fiber of $K'\to K$ over $z$ is therefore the level set $\varphi(a)=1$, with the values of the $\sigma_i$ determined by the defining equations. The normalization
\[
  a\longmapsto \varphi(a)^{-1/(2d!)}\star a
\]
is a continuous surjection from $\mathbb{R}^k\setminus\{0\}$ onto this fiber. Since $k\ge2$, the source is connected. Thus every fiber is nonempty and connected, and $K'\to K$ is a monotone surjection.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:principalization-extension}
Let $A$ be a smooth finitely generated factorial $\mathbb{Q}$-domain, let $a,b\in A$ be not both zero, and let $K\subset\operatorname{Spec}(A)(\mathbb{R})$ be compact. There are a smooth finitely generated factorial $\mathbb{Q}$-domain $A'$, an injection $A\hookrightarrow A'$, and a compact set $K'\subset\operatorname{Spec}(A')(\mathbb{R})$ such that $(a,b)A'$ is principal and the induced map $K'\to K$ is a monotone surjection.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Put $I=(a,b)$ and $N=\dim A$. We prove the following auxiliary assertion. Let $B$ be a smooth finitely generated factorial $\mathbb{Q}$-domain, let $\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B$ be a nonzero ideal, let $m\ge0$ be such that $(B,\mathfrak{b},m)$ satisfies $(\star_N)$, and let $K\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(B)(\mathbb{R})$ be compact. Then there are an injection $B\hookrightarrow B'$ into a smooth finitely generated factorial $\mathbb{Q}$-domain and a compact set $K'\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(B')(\mathbb{R})$ such that $\mathfrak{b} B'$ is principal and $K'\to K$ is a monotone surjection. The proposition is the initial case $(B,\mathfrak{b},m)=(A,I,0)$.

If $\mathfrak{b}=B$, take $B'=B$ and $K'=K$. For $\mathfrak{b}\ne B$ we argue by well-founded induction on the pair $(\operatorname{maxinv}(\mathfrak{b}),c(\mathfrak{b}))$, ordered lexicographically, with $\preceq$ on the first entry and the usual order on $\mathbb{N}$ on the second. This is well-founded by Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}(5), because all invariants of ideals in triples satisfying $(\star_N)$ have at most $N$ nonzero entries. Let $k$ be the number of nonzero entries of $\operatorname{maxinv}(\mathfrak{b})$, choose a component of the maximal locus of $\mathfrak{b}$, and apply the divisorial step if $k=1$ or the torsor step if $k\ge2$. This gives a smooth finitely generated factorial $\mathbb{Q}$-domain $B_1$ with an injection $B\hookrightarrow B_1$ (namely $B_1=B$, or $B_1=U$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:torsor}), a compact set $K_1\subset\operatorname{Spec}(B_1)(\mathbb{R})$ with $K_1\to K$ a monotone surjection (with $K_1=K$ in the divisorial case and $K_1$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:sphere-bundle} in the torsor case), an ideal $\mathfrak{b}_1\subseteq B_1$, and an element $\alpha\in B_1$ with $\mathfrak{b} B_1=\alpha\mathfrak{b}_1$ (where $\alpha=\pi^a$ or $\alpha=s^d$). Let $m_1=m$ in the divisorial step and $m_1=m+\ell+1$ in the torsor step. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:divisorial}, \ref{lem:torsor-invariant} and~\ref{lem:length-control}, the triple $(B_1,\mathfrak{b}_1,m_1)$ satisfies $(\star_N)$, and either $\mathfrak{b}_1=B_1$ or $(\operatorname{maxinv}(\mathfrak{b}_1),c(\mathfrak{b}_1))$ is strictly smaller than $(\operatorname{maxinv}(\mathfrak{b}),c(\mathfrak{b}))$ in this lexicographic order.

In either case, the assertion for $(B_1,\mathfrak{b}_1,m_1)$ and $K_1$ holds, trivially if $\mathfrak{b}_1=B_1$ and by induction otherwise. It gives $B_1\hookrightarrow B'$ and a compact set $K'\subset\operatorname{Spec}(B')(\mathbb{R})$ with $\mathfrak{b}_1B'=\alpha'B'$ and $K'\to K_1$ a monotone surjection. Then $\mathfrak{b} B'=\alpha\alpha'B'$ is principal, $B\to B'$ is injective, and $K'\to K$ is a monotone surjection by Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone-composition}.

<!-- construction-1: body_md -->
\label{construction:ring}
Fix a bijection $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ with $i\le\langle i,j\rangle$ for all $i,j$, for instance the Cantor pairing. Starting with $A_0=\mathbb{Q}[x,y]$ and the circle $K_0$, we construct injections
\[
  A_0\hookrightarrow A_1\hookrightarrow A_2\hookrightarrow\cdots
\]
of smooth finitely generated factorial $\mathbb{Q}$-domains and compact sets $K_n\subset\operatorname{Spec}(A_n)(\mathbb{R})$ such that each induced map $K_{n+1}\to K_n$ is a monotone surjection.

Every finitely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra is countable. Once $A_i$ has been constructed, choose a surjection
\[
  \eta_i:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow A_i\times A_i.
\]
To pass from $A_n$ to $A_{n+1}$, write $n=\langle i,j\rangle$. Then $i\le n$, and we let $(a,b)$ be the image of the pair $\eta_i(j)$ in $A_n$. If $a=0$ or $b=0$, the ideal $(a,b)A_n$ is principal, and we put $A_{n+1}=A_n$ and $K_{n+1}=K_n$. Otherwise, write $a=ca'$ and $b=cb'$ with $a'$ and $b'$ coprime, which is possible because $A_n$ is a UFD. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:principalization-extension} to $a'$, $b'$, and the compact set $K_n$, and let $A_{n+1}$ and $K_{n+1}$ be the resulting ring and compact set. Then $(a,b)A_{n+1}=c\,(a',b')A_{n+1}$ is principal.

Identify every $A_n$ with its image in later rings and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:union-ring}
  R=\bigcup_{n\ge0}A_n.
\end{equation}
<!-- end construction-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:bezout-domain}
The ring $R$ is a B\'ezout domain.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Since all transition maps are injective, the increasing union $R$ is a domain. Let $a,b\in R$. Choose $i$ such that $a,b\in A_i$, and choose $j\in\mathbb{N}$ with $\eta_i(j)=(a,b)$. This pair is processed while passing from $A_n$ to $A_{n+1}$, where $n=\langle i,j\rangle$. Hence
\[
  (a,b)A_{n+1}=gA_{n+1}
\]
for some $g\in A_{n+1}$. Extending to $R$ gives $(a,b)R=gR$. Thus $R$ is a B\'ezout domain.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:key-obstruction}
There are no elements $p,q,u,v,\mu\in R$ such that
\[
  \mu\,(p,q)\,M\binom uv=1.
\]
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Suppose such elements exist, and choose $N$ such that all five lie in $A_N$. The identity already holds in $A_N$, because $A_N\hookrightarrow R$ is injective. For $z\in K_N$, put
\[
  \xi(z)=M(z)\binom{u(z)}{v(z)}\in L_N(z).
\]
The assumed identity gives $\mu(z)(p(z),q(z))\,\xi(z)=1$, so $\xi(z)\ne0$. Thus $\xi$ is a continuous nowhere-zero section of $L_N$, contradicting \eqref{eq:nonorientable-stages}.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Take the ring $R$ from Construction~\ref{construction:ring}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:bezout-domain}, it is a B\'ezout domain. It remains to show that it is not an elementary divisor domain.

Suppose, to the contrary, that the matrix $M$ has a Smith normal form over $R$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:putative-smith-form}
  PMQ=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2),\qquad
  P,Q\in\operatorname{GL}_2(R),\qquad d_1\mid d_2.
\end{equation}
Multiplication by invertible matrices preserves the ideal generated by all matrix entries. By \eqref{eq:matrix-identities}, the entries of $M$ generate $R$. Hence $(d_1,d_2)=R$. Since $d_1\mid d_2$, this ideal equals $(d_1)$, so $d_1$ is a unit.

Let $(p,q)$ be the first row of $P$ and $(u,v)^{\mathsf T}$ the first column of $Q$. Comparing upper-left entries in \eqref{eq:putative-smith-form} gives
\[
  (p,q)M(u,v)^{\mathsf T}=d_1.
\]
Multiplying by $d_1^{-1}$ contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:key-obstruction}. Thus $M$ has no Smith normal form over $R$, so $R$ is not an elementary divisor domain.
