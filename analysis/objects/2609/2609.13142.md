<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm_main}
 There exists a strictly 
 positive constant $c=c(\mathbf f,\mathbf g )$ such that 
for all $X\geqslant 2 $ we have  
$$N_\alpha(X) =\sigma(\alpha)
\frac{X^n}{(\log X)^{\Delta(\alpha)}} +O\left(
\frac{X^n}{(\log X)^{\Delta(\alpha)+c}}  \right)
,$$where   $\sigma(\alpha)$ is 
an explicit constant that is described in 
Remark \ref{rem_constant2}.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def_polardiv}
 The constant $\Delta(\alpha)$ is defined as 
$\frac{1}{2}d(\alpha)$, where $d(\alpha)$ is the number of irreducible components of $\alpha$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The ramification locus of $\alpha$ 
consists of all $\mathbf t $ for which 
$\prod_{i=1}^r f_i(\mathbf t ) g_i(\mathbf t )=0$, hence, the smoothness assumption of 
Theorem \ref{thm_main} ensures that the   
irreducible components are 
$$\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^r\{\mathbf t : f_i(\mathbf t)=0\}\Big)
\bigcup \Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^r\{\mathbf t : g_i(\mathbf t)=0\} \Big).$$ 
In particular, 
 $\Delta(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}d(\alpha)=r$
 in the setting of Theorem \ref{thm_main}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The condition $\alpha(\mathbf{t}) = 0$ holds if and only if 
\begin{equation}\label{Or ch'è dal sol difesa}
\prod_{i=1}^r (f_i(\mathbf{t}), g_i(\mathbf{t}))_{\mathbb{R}} = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \prod_{i=1}^r (f_i(\mathbf{t}), g_i(\mathbf{t}))_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = 1 \ \ 
\text{for every prime} \ \  p, 
\end{equation} where 
$(\cdot, \cdot)_k$ is the local Hilbert symbol 
on $k =\mathbb{R}$ and $k=\mathbb{Q}_p$. When $r=1$
the condition in \eqref{Or ch'è dal sol difesa}
is about counting the number of certain 
Diophantine equations that are everywhere locally soluble.
Problems of this kind   have attracted 
  a lot of  attention recently
(see the introduction of \cite{LRS} for 
a  list of   developments). 
In contrast, the original 
problem of Serre,  regards statistics of Brauer symbols 
and 
does not quite fit into the framework 
of random Diophantine equations. In particular, 
asymptotics were   previously known only 
in two cases: for $r=1$ by work of 
Da Silva \cite[Theorem 4.1.2]{silvaphd}
and for $r> 1$ by work of 
Destagnol--Lyczak--Sofos \cite{11388}
when each $g_i$ is the same constant polynomial.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem_constant2}
In \S \ref{s-prf-thrm23} we will show that 
the leading constant $\sigma(\alpha)$ equals 
the sum  of $2^r$ Euler 
products
$$\sigma(\alpha)=
 \frac{1}{(d\pi)^{\Delta(\alpha)}}
  \sum_{\mathscr A \subset \{1,\ldots, r\}} 
 \sigma_\infty(\mathscr A)  
  \prod_{\substack{ p  \textrm{ prime} \\ p=2}}^\infty 
  \frac{\sigma_p(\mathscr A) }{( 1-\frac{1}{p} )^{\Delta(\alpha)}}
,$$ with 
$$\sigma_v(\mathscr A) =\int_{\substack{\mathbf{t} \in \mathscr{O}_v^n \\ \prod_{i=1}^r (f_i(\mathbf{t}), g_i(\mathbf{t}))_v = 1}} \left( \prod_{i \in \mathscr{A}} (f_i(\mathbf{t}), g_i(\mathbf{t}))_{\mathbb{Q}_v} \right) \mathrm{d}\mu_v(\mathbf{t})
$$ for $v\in \{\infty\}\cup\{p\textrm{ prime}\}$,
where $\mathscr O_\infty=[-1,1]$, $\mathscr O_p=\mathbb{Z}_p$,
 $\mathbb{Q}_\infty=\mathbb{R}$ 
 and $\mu_v$ is the normalised 
 Haar measure on $\mathbb{Q}_v^n$ 
 satisfying $\mu_v(\mathscr{O}_v^n) = 1$.
We will also show that each of the Euler products 
converges absolutely. Furthermore,  in
  \S \ref{s-prf-thrm23}
  we will show that  
$$ \sigma(\alpha)= 2^r \sum_{\substack{ \mathbf s , \mathbf s' \in \{-1,1\}^r \\ 
\prod_{i=1}^r (s_i,s'_i)_\mathbb{R}=1 }}
\gamma(\mathbf s , \mathbf s ')
\mathrm{vol}(\mathbf t \in [-1,1]^n : 
\mathrm{sign}(f_j(\mathbf t)= s_j, 
\mathrm{sign}(g_j(\mathbf t)= s'_j \forall j \in \{1,\ldots, r\}
)),
$$
where $\gamma(\mathbf s , \mathbf s ') $ is given by 
$$
 \lim_{T \to \infty}  
\left[
\prod_{\substack{p \ \mathrm{ prime} \\ p \leqslant T} }
\left(1-\frac1p\right)^{-\Delta(\alpha)} 
\hspace{-0.2cm} 
\mu_p
\right]
\hspace{-0.1cm}
\left \{\mathbf t \in   
\prod_{p \leqslant T} \mathbb{Z}_p^{n+1}
\colon \ 
\hspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{array}{l} 
\prod_{i=1}^r (f_i(\mathbf t ),  g_i(\mathbf t ) )_{\mathbb{Q}_p}=1 \ 
\forall p \leqslant T,  \\ 
\prod_{p\leqslant T} (f_i(\mathbf t ),  g_i(\mathbf t ) )_{\mathbb{Q}_p}
=(s_i,s'_i)_\mathbb{R}  \  \forall i\in \{1,\ldots,R\}\end{array}
\hspace{-0.1cm}
\right\}
.$$This expression is analogous to  
the leading constant in \cite[Theorem 1.1]{11388}, which arises 
from an underlying Brauer group in a 
fibration structure. 
Here, however, 
$N_\alpha(X)$  
lacks an obvious  fibration setting,
hence, 
it is not apparent if $\sigma(\alpha)$ 
fits in   the Loughran--Smeets framework \cite{LRS}.

\label{thm_main3}Let $r$ be a 
strictly positive integer. Then as $X\to \infty$ we have 
$$\#\left\{\mathbf n, \mathbf n' \in \mathbb Z^r: 
0<|n_i|,|n'_i|\leqslant X \ \forall i, \ \  
1=\prod_{i=1}^r (n_i,n'_i)_{\mathbb{R}}=
\prod_{i=1}^r (n_i,n'_i)_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \ 
\forall  \ \mathrm{prime }\  p
\right\}
\sim c_r \frac{X^{2r}}{(\log X)^r}
.$$

c_r(\mathbf s , \mathbf s '),\end{equation}

 \lim_{T \to \infty}  
\left[

\left(1-\frac1p\right)^{-r} 
\hspace{-0.2cm} 
\mu_p
\right]
\hspace{-0.1cm}
\left \{\mathbf n,\mathbf n' \in   
\prod_{p \leqslant T} \mathbb{Z}_p^{r}
\colon \ \hspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{array}{l} 
\prod_{i=1}^r (n_i,  n'_i )_{\mathbb{Q}_p}=1 \

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{stronghardylittlew}
 A set of integer polynomials $\{F_1,\ldots,F_R\}$ 
 in $n$ variables is said to be a strong 
Hardy--Littlewood system when the following three properties hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item there exists 
$\eta=\eta(\mathbf F)>0$   such that for all $\mathbf a $ and 
$q$ appearing in $\mathfrak S$ one has 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singseries}
\left| \sum_{\mathbf x \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{n}} 
\mathrm e\left( \frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^R a_i F_i(\mathbf x) \right)
\right| \ll q^{n-1-R-\eta},
\end{equation}  where  the  implied constant 
depends only on $\mathbf F$;
\item  for all  boxes
$\mathscr{B}\subset [-1,1]^{n}$ whose 
  side   length is at most $1$  
 and  all $\boldsymbol \gamma \in \mathbb R^R$ 
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singint} \left| 
\int_{\boldsymbol \zeta \in  \mathscr{B} } 
\mathrm e\Big( \sum_{i=1}^R  \gamma_i 
F_i^\natural(\boldsymbol \zeta) \Big)
 \mathrm d \boldsymbol \zeta  \right| \ll 
 (1+ \max_i | \gamma_i|)^{-R-\eta},
\end{equation} 
where the  implied constant   depends only on $\mathbf F $ 
and $\mathscr B$, and \item 
there is $\delta=\delta(\mathbf F )>0$  
such that for all  boxes
$\mathscr{B}\subset [-1,1]^{n}$ whose 
  side   length is at most $1$    and all 
 $P \geqslant 1$, $\boldsymbol \nu \in \mathbb{Z}^R$ we have  
\begin{equation}\label{eq_simon_asymptotic}
 \#\{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap  P \mathscr{B}: \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \boldsymbol \nu\}= \mathfrak{J} \mathfrak{S}
 P^{n-dR} + O(P^{n-dR-\delta}),
\end{equation} where  the  implied constant 
depends only on $\mathbf F$ and $\mathscr{B}$.
\end{itemize}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem_lopsided}
Assume that  $m,n,\Omega,\omega,L, Y,k$ are as above and
that $\limsup_{p\to\infty} \omega(p)$ is not $1$. 
Then for any $B_1,\ldots, B_n\geqslant 1 $ we have  
\begin{align*} 
&\#\left\{\mathbf x \in   \Omega \cap \prod_{i=1}^n 
[-B_i,B_i]:\mathbf x   \left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^m\right)
\in Y(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})\ \textrm{ for some } p> z\right\}\\
& \ll \frac{\prod_{i=1}^n B_i }
{L({\min_i B_i}^{1/(4m)})z^{k-1} \log z} 
+\frac{(\max_i B_i)^n}{(\min_i B_i)^{(k-1)/(4m)} 
\log \min_i B_i}
+ (\max_i B_i)^{n-k+1}
,\end{align*} where   the implied constant 
depends only on $m,n,Y$ and $\limsup_{p\to\infty} \omega(p)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $B=\max B_i$.  Since $m\geqslant 1 $, for 
 any $z_1>z$ we can upper-bound the cardinality in the lemma by  $$
\#\left\{\mathbf x \in \mathbb{Z}^n \cap \prod_{i=1}^n [-B,B]^n:\mathbf x   \left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)\in Y(\mathbb{F}_p) \ \textrm{ for some } p> z_1\right\}
$$ and then apply the geometric sieve
\cite[Theorem 3.3]{Bhargava}
to get 
$$\ll  \frac{B^n}{z^{k-1} \log z}+ B^{n-k+1}.$$ To deal with the contribution of the primes $p \in (z,z_1]$ 
we use the union bound to obtain \begin{align*} 
&\#\left\{\mathbf x \in \mathbb{Z}^n\cap \Omega \cap \prod_{i=1}^n 
[-B_i,B_i]: \mathbf x   \left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^m\right)
\in Y(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})\ \textrm{ for some } p\in (z,z_1]\right\} \\
\leqslant   \sum_{p\in (z,z_1]} 
& \#\left\{\mathbf x \in \mathbb{Z}^n \cap  \Omega \cap 
\prod_{i=1}^n [-B_i,B_i]: 
\mathbf x   \left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^m\right) \in Y(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})  \right\} =:\Xi
.\end{align*} We now apply the lopsided version of Serre's 
large sieve as given by Wilson \cite[Lemma 5.1]{wilson}. 
For   a fixed   $p$ 
and  an arbitrary prime $\ell$ we define  
$ S_\ell \subset (\mathbb{Z}/\ell^m\mathbb{Z})^n$ via 
\[S_\ell = \begin{cases}
 (\mathbb{Z}/\ell^m\mathbb{Z})^n \setminus \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^m\mathbb{Z}), & \text{if } \ell\neq p  \\
 (\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})^n \setminus (\Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})\cap Y(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})),  & \text{if } \ell = p.  \end{cases}\]
Then \cite[Lemma 5.1]{wilson} with $M_i=-B_i$,$N_i=2B_i$,
$s=m$ and  $B_0=\min_i B_i$ gives 
$$ \#\left\{\mathbf x \in \mathbb{Z}^n \cap  \Omega \cap 
\prod_{i=1}^n [-B_i,B_i]: 
\mathbf x   \left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^m\right) \in Y(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})  \right\} \ll 
\frac{B_1 \cdots B_n}{M_p(B_0^{1/(2m)})},$$
where for $t\geqslant 1 $ we define $$ M_p(t):= \sum_{q\leqslant t}
\mu(q)^2 \prod_{\ell\mid q}\frac{\#S_\ell }
{\ell^{nm}-\#S_\ell }.$$ This function occurs also in the 
proof of  \cite[Lemma 2.1]{MR4961246}, 
where it is proved that
$$M_p(t) \geqslant L(\sqrt t)\frac{\# \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})}
{\# (S(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z}) \cap \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z}))}.$$
Hence, $$ \Xi \ll \frac{B_1 \cdots B_n}{L( {B_0}^{1/(4m)}) }
\sum_{p\in (z,z_1]} 
\frac{\# (S(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z}) \cap \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z}))}
{\# \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})} .$$ Finally, it was shown in~\cite[\S 2.1]{MR4961246} that under the assumption $\limsup_{p \to \infty} \omega(p) \neq 1$ (which is subsumed by our present hypotheses), the bound
\[
\sum_{p \in (z, z_1]} \frac{\# \left( S(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z}) \cap \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z}) \right)}{\# \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})} \ll \frac{1}{z^{k-1} \log z}
\]
holds for all $z \geqslant 2$. This estimate is sufficient to complete the proof.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The condition on $\limsup_{p \to \infty} \omega(p)$ serves as the simplest criterion to guarantee that $Y \neq \Omega$. This non-triviality condition is necessary to achieve a saving beyond what can be obtained from the large sieve or the geometric sieve alone.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
Lemma~\ref{lem_lopsided} is useful primarily when the parameters $B_i$ are of comparable magnitudes. For instance, if $\min_i B_i > (\max_i B_i)^{1-c}$ for a sufficiently small constant $c = c(k,m) > 0$, then both $(\max_i B_i)^n (\min_i B_i)^{-(k-1)/(4m)}$ and $(\max_i B_i)^{n-k+1}$ are negligible compared to $\prod_{i=1}^n B_i$. For example, when 
$m=2$ then if $\max_i B_i \leqslant (\min B_i)^
{1+\frac{1}{8(n-1)}}$ then 
\begin{equation}\label{eq_papoutsia}
\frac{(\max_i B_i)^n}{(\min_i B_i)^{1/8} }
+(\max_i B_i)^{n-1}
\leqslant \frac{\prod_{i=1}^n B_i }
{(\min_iB_i )^{1/16}} 
.\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem_lopsided} is   sufficient for most applications; furthermore,  a more general version can be   obtained by   establishing a lopsided analog of the geometric sieve, which could then be substituted directly into the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem_lopsided}  without further modification.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem_hilbert_symbol_bound}
Let $r \geqslant 1, m\geqslant 3,
z\geqslant 8r^2$ 
be  integers, let $\mathbf s, \mathbf s' \in \{-1,1\}^r$ 
and let  $W_z$ be as
in \eqref{Porpora Torbido intorno al core}.
Let   $x_1, \dots, x_r\geqslant W_z^2$ and
$ x'_1, \dots, x'_r \geqslant W_z^2$ be such that  
$W_z \max_i \{x_i,x'_i\} \leqslant  
(\min \{x_i,x'_i\})^{1+\frac{1}{16r}}$ and let 
$\mathbf a,\mathbf a'\in (\mathbb{Z}/ {W_z}\mathbb{Z})^r$.
Denote by  $\Gamma_4$  the number of  
$(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}') $ in $\mathbb{N}^r 
\times \mathbb{N}^r $ satisfying 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $m_i \leqslant x_i$ and $m_i \equiv a_i \pmod {W_z}$ 
    for all $1 \leqslant i \leqslant r$,
    \item $m'_i \leqslant x'_i$ and $m'_i \equiv a'_i \pmod {W_z}$ 
    for all $1 \leqslant i \leqslant r$,
    \item $\prod_{i=1}^r (s_i m_i, s'_i m'_i)_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = 1$ for all primes $p>z$,  
    \item there exists a prime $p > z$ such that $p^2 \mid \prod_{i=1}^r m_i m'_i$  
\end{enumerate} and denote by 
 $\Gamma_3$  the number of  $(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}') $ 
 satisfying the first three properties. Then 
$$\Gamma_3 \ll 
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r} x_i x'_i}
{W_z^{2r} (\log  x_1)^r} \ \ \ \textrm{ and } \ \ \ 
\Gamma_4 \ll \frac{(\log z)^r}{z } 
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r} x_i x'_i}
{W_z^{2r} (\log  x_1)^r}
,$$
where the implied constants depend only on $r$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We start by making the 
  changes of variables 
$m_i=a_i+s_i W_z t_i$ and 
$m'_i=a'_i+s'_i W_z t'_i$  
so that the first two properties in $\Gamma_4$
imply   $|t_i| \leqslant 2M_i/W_z$ and 
$|t'_i| \leqslant 2M'_i/W_z.$ 
Let $n = 2r$ and identify $\mathbb{Z}^n$ with pairs 
$(\mathbf t, \mathbf t') \in  \mathbb{Z}^r \times \mathbb{Z}^r$. 
We shall use Lemma \ref{lem_lopsided} with $m=2$ and   
\[\Omega=\left\{ (\mathbf t, \mathbf t') 
\in \mathbb{Z}^r \times \mathbb{Z}^r : 
\prod_{i=1}^r (s_i a_i+W_z t_i, s'_i a'_i+W_z t'_i)_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = 1 \text{ for all primes } p>z \right\}.\] 
For any prime $p$, the local density $\omega(p)$ is defined by \[
\omega(p) = 1 - 
\frac{\# \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})}{p^{4r}}.\]
When $p\leqslant z$ then we clearly have $\omega(p)=0$. When $p>z$ we start 
estimating $\omega(p)$ by noting that $p\nmid W_z$
hence, an invertible change of variables in $\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$
shows that $$\# \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})= 
\#\left\{\mathbf m,\mathbf m'\in (\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^{2r}:
\prod_{i=1}^r( m_i, m'_i )_{\mathbb{Q}_p}=1\right\}.$$
The contribution of the cases  with 
$p^2 \mid \prod_{i=1}^r m_i m'_i$ 
is at most 
$$2r p^{4r-2} + (2r)^2 (p^{2r-1})^2 \leqslant 5r^2p^{4r-2}$$
coming, respectively from cases where
$p^2$ divides one of the variables
or  when  $p$ divides at least two.  If $p\nmid \prod_i m_i m'_i$ and $p>2$
then every $(\mathbf m,\mathbf m') \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^{2r}$
falls into $\Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})$.
Hence,  \[  
-\frac{5r^2}{p^2}\leqslant \omega(p) -1 +\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2r}+\lambda_p \leqslant 
 0
,\] where $\lambda_p=
 \#\{(\mathbf m,\mathbf m') \in \Omega(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}):
v_p(\prod_i m_i m'_i)=1\}p^{-4r}.$
For $p\neq 2$ 
and $v_p(\prod_i m_i m'_i)=1$
there exists a unique $i$ such that $p\mid m_im'_i$, in which case
\[ \prod_{i=1}^r (m_i,  m'_i)_{\mathbb{Q}_p}=\begin{cases} 
(\frac{m'_i}{p}), & \text{if } p \mid m_i,  \\
(\frac{m_i}{p}), & \text{if } p \mid m'_i.  \end{cases} \]
Since for $p>2$  there are $(p-1)/2$  squares in $\mathbb{F}_p^*$,
we obtain 
 \[\lambda_p= 
\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2r-1}
\frac{r}{p} .\] Let us now note that for any real 
number $x\in [0,1]$ and $n\in \mathbb N$ we have 
$$ (1-x)^n \leqslant \mathrm e^{-nx} \leqslant 
1-nx +\frac{n^2 x^2}{2}
,$$ hence,  \begin{align*}
\omega(p) \geqslant  &1 - \frac{5r^2}{p^2}
-\left(1-\frac{2r}{p} +\frac{2r^2 }{p^2} \right) - 
\frac{r}{p}\left(1-\frac{2r-1}{p} 
+\frac{(2r-1)^2 x^2}{2} \right) 
\\ = &\frac{r}{p}-\frac{5r^2+r}{p^2}
- \frac{r(2r-1)^2 }{2p^3}  \geqslant
