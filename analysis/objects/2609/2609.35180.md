<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:mainbelowc} For $\kappa=\omega_1$ or $\kappa=\omega_2$
and an infinite cardinal $\lambda>\kappa$ of uncountable cofinality
it is consistent that $2^\omega=\lambda$ and $\ell_1(\kappa)$ admits
a  normal\footnote{A tiling is normal if there are $0<r<R$ such that all tiles contain
a ball of radius $r$ and are contained in a ball of radius $R$.} 
disjoint tiling by star-shaped bodies of the form $X+B$ where $X$
is norm compact and $B$ is the unit ball of $\ell_1(\kappa)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
See section 3.5.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{maincofinal} Suppose that $\kappa$ is an uncountable cardinal
of countable cofinality. Then $\ell_1(\kappa)$ does not admit any normal disjoint  tiling  
by bodies of the form $X+B$, where $X$ is closed and norm-separable and $B$ is the unit ball
of $\ell_1(\kappa)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose that there is such a tiling.
Since all sets $X+B$ in the tiling are bodies, they are closed and hence
they are of the form $\mathrm{B}(X, 1)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:closedtube}. 
By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalcard} the tiling has cardinality $\kappa$,
contradicting Proposition \ref{cfomega}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- question-1: body_md -->
$ $
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is it consistent that there is a disjoint normal tiling of $\ell_1(\omega_n)$ 
by  bodies  of the form $X+B$ where $X$ is separable and $B$ the unit ball
while $\omega_n<2^\omega$ for $n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0, 1, 2\}$?
\item Is it consistent that there is a normal disjoint tiling of $\ell_1(\omega_\omega)$
by  star-shaped bodies?
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{support}
A subset $X$ of $\ell_1(\kappa)$ is separable if and only if $\operatorname{supp}(X)$ is countable.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:normalcard}
Suppose that $X$ is a Banach space of infinite density $\kappa$.
Every normal tiling of $X$ has cardinality $\kappa$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since the interiors of distinct elements of a tiling
are disjoint and nonempty the cardinality cannot be bigger than $\kappa$.
The normality yields a cover of $X$ by $\{\mathrm{B}(\{x_i\}, R): i\in I\}$, where
the cardinality of $I$ and the cardinality of the tiling are the same and infinite and $R>0$.
For every $y\in X$ and $n\in \mathbb{N}$ there is $i\in I$ such that $\|nRy-x_i\|\leq R$, so
$\{x_i/nR: i\in I, n\in \mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}\}$ forms a dense subset of $X$ of cardinality at most 
 the cardinality of $I$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:closedtube}
Suppose that $X\subseteq\ell_1(\kappa)$ is nonempty and closed and $B$ is
the unit ball of $\ell_1(\kappa)$. Then
$
\mathrm{B}(X,1)=X+B
$
if and only if $X+B$ is closed.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
It is enough to  note that
$
\overline{X+B}=\mathrm{B}(X,1).
$
Indeed, $X+B\subseteq\mathrm{B}(X,1)$, and $\mathrm{B}(X,1)$ is closed.
For the other inclusion note that for any  $y\in\mathrm{B}(X,1)$ there is a sequence
of points in $X+B$  which converges to $y$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{B} Suppose that $X\subseteq\ell_1(\kappa)$ is compact
and $\operatorname{diam}(X)\leq 1$.
Then $\mathrm{B}(X,1)=X+B(\ell_1(\kappa))$ is a bounded star-shaped body.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Since $X$ is compact, $X+B(\ell_1(\kappa))$ is closed, so
$\mathrm{B}(X,1)=X+B(\ell_1(\kappa))$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:closedtube}.
It is bounded since $X$ is bounded, and it is a body since every
$y=x+z$ with $x\in X$ and $\|z\|\leq1$ is the limit of the interior
points $x+tz$, where $0\leq t<1$.

Finally, if $y\in X+B(\ell_1(\kappa))$, choose $x_1\in X$ such that
$\|y-x_1\|\leq1$. For every $x_2\in X$ and $0\leq t\leq1$ we have
$\|tx_2+(1-t)y-x_1\|\leq t\|x_2-x_1\|+(1-t)\|y-x_1\|\leq1$,
so $[x_2,y]\subseteq X+B(\ell_1(\kappa))$.

\label{lem:IBm}

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{dfn:Scenter}
Let $p\in\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$, $\dot{x}$ be a name for an element of $\ell_1(\kappa)$, and
$S\subseteq\kappa$ be a countable set.
A set $\mathscr{Y}\subseteq \ell_1(\kappa)$ is an \emph{$S$-center for $(p,\dot{x})$} if there are 
\begin{enumerate}
\item an increasing sequence $(m_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ of positive natural numbers,
\item sets $\mathscr{X}(S, m_n, A_\sigma, \mathcal{I}_\sigma)$ 
for all $\sigma\in \operatorname{lev}(n, n, p)$ and $n\in \mathbb{N}$,
                                                                              
                                                   
\item a sequence $(E_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ of finite nonempty subsets of $S$ with
$\lim_{n\to\infty}E_n=\sup S$,
\item a sequence $(\epsilon_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ of positive rational numbers,
\end{enumerate}

such that

\begin{equation}
\mathscr{Y}=\bigcap_{n\in \mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{\sigma\in \operatorname{lev}(n, n, p)}\mathscr{X}(S, m_n, A_\sigma,
\mathcal{I}_\sigma),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
p\Vdash\dot{x}\in \mathscr{Y}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Eeps}
\forall y\in \mathscr{Y}\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists \alpha\in E_n\ |y(\alpha)|>\epsilon_n.
\end{equation}

A set is an \emph{$S$-center} if it is an $S$-center for some $(p,\dot{x})$ with
$p\in\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$ and $\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$-name $\dot{x}$.
We say that an $S$-center is \emph{witnessed} by the objects listed in items~(1)--(4).

If $\mathscr{Y}$ is an $S$-center with $\operatorname{diam}(\mathscr{Y})<1$ and $\alpha\in\kappa\setminus S$, then
$\mathscr{X}:=\mathscr{Y}+1_\alpha$ is called a \emph{translated center}. For such a representation we write
$S_{\mathscr{X}}:=S$, $\mathscr{Y}_{\mathscr{X}}:=\mathscr{Y}$, and $\alpha_{\mathscr{X}}:=\alpha$.

\item An $S$-center is a nonempty norm-compact set in $\ell_1(\kappa)$.
\item For a fixed countable set $S\subseteq\kappa$ there are at most $2^\omega$ many $S$-centers.
\end{enumerate}

$\ell_1(\kappa)$ with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathscr{X}_0\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}$, and each $\mathscr{X}\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\{\mathscr{X}_0\}$ is a translated center,
\item $\operatorname{dist}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{X}')>2$ for distinct $\mathscr{X},\mathscr{X}'\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}$,
\item for every $p\in\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$ and every $\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$-name $\dot{x}$ for an
element of $\ell_1(\kappa)$, there are $q\leq p$ and $\mathscr{X}\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}$ such that
$q\Vdash \operatorname{dist}(\dot{x},\mathscr{X})\leq 1$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Case 1:} Assume that $\kappa=\omega_1$.
In this case, the auxiliary forcing $\mathbb{X}$ is not needed; instead, we construct the
required family by recursion of length $\omega_1$.
Put $W:=V$.
By continuous reading of names and $\mathsf{CH}$, there 
is a family $\mathcal{N}$ of $\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$-names for elements of 
$\ell_1(\omega_1)$ such that $|\mathcal{N}|=2^\omega=\omega_1$ and for every
$\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$-name $\dot{x}$ for an element of $\ell_1(\omega_1)$ there is
$\dot{y}\in\mathcal{N}$ such that $
\mathbb{S}^{\omega}\Vdash\dot{x}=\dot{y}$.
Enumerate $\mathbb{S}^{\omega}\times\mathcal{N}$ as $\{(p_\alpha,
\dot{x}_\alpha):0<\alpha<\omega_1\}$.
Recursively on $0<\alpha<\omega_1$ define sets $\mathscr{X}_\alpha$ in $\ell_1(\omega_1)$
and conditions $q_\alpha\leq p_\alpha$, where each $\mathscr{X}_\alpha$ is either $\mathscr{X}_0$ or a translated center, such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{dist}(\mathscr{X}_\alpha, \mathscr{X}_\beta)>2$ for all $\beta<\alpha$ with $\mathscr{X}_\beta\neq \mathscr{X}_\alpha$,
\item $\operatorname{diam}(\mathscr{X}_\alpha)<1$,
\item there is $\beta\leq \alpha$ such that $q_\alpha\Vdash \operatorname{dist}(\dot{x}_\alpha,
\mathscr{X}_\beta)\leq 1$.
\end{enumerate}
Fix an ordinal number $0<\alpha<\omega_1$ and assume that sets $\mathscr{X}_\beta$ in
$\ell_1(\omega_1)$, each equal to $\mathscr{X}_0$ or a translated center, have been defined for all
$\beta<\alpha$, and conditions $q_\beta\leq p_\beta$ have been defined for all
$0<\beta<\alpha$.
If there are $\beta<\alpha$ and $r\leq p_\alpha$ such that $r\Vdash \operatorname{dist}(\dot{x}_\alpha,
\mathscr{X}_\beta)\leq 1$, then put $q_\alpha:=r$ and $\mathscr{X}_\alpha:=\mathscr{X}_\beta$.
If not, then
$p_\alpha\Vdash \operatorname{dist}(\dot{x}_\alpha, \mathscr{X}_\beta)>1$ for all $\beta<\alpha$.
Since $0<\alpha$, the preceding condition applies to $\beta=0$,
and as $\operatorname{dist}(0,\mathscr{X}_0)=1$, it follows that $p_\alpha\Vdash\dot{x}_\alpha\neq0$.
Strengthening $p_\alpha$ if needed, we may assume that there is a countable set
$S\subseteq\omega_1$ such that
$$
p_\alpha\Vdash\operatorname{supp}(\dot{x}_\alpha)\subseteq S\ \wedge\ \sup\operatorname{supp}(\dot{x}_\alpha)=\sup S.
$$
Since $\alpha<\omega_1$, the family $\{\mathscr{X}_\beta:\beta<\alpha\}$ is countable.
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:IBmXn} to the condition $p_\alpha$, the set $S$, and an enumeration
of the family $\{\mathscr{X}_\beta:\beta<\alpha\}$, we get a condition $q_\alpha\leq p_\alpha$ and
an $S$-center $\mathscr{Y}$ for $(q_\alpha,\dot{x}_\alpha)$ in $\ell_1(\omega_1)$ such that
$$
\operatorname{dist}(\mathscr{Y},\mathscr{X}_\beta)>1\quad\text{for all }\beta<\alpha
$$
and $\operatorname{diam}(\mathscr{Y})<1$.
Pick $\alpha'\in\omega_1$ outside
$S\cup\{0\}\cup\bigcup\{S_{\mathscr{X}_\beta}\cup\{\alpha_{\mathscr{X}_\beta}\}:\beta<\alpha,\ \mathscr{X}_\beta\neq \mathscr{X}_0\}$.
For the translated center $\mathscr{X}_\alpha:=\mathscr{Y}+1_{\alpha'}$, we have $\operatorname{diam}(\mathscr{X}_\alpha)<1$,
$$
q_\alpha\Vdash \operatorname{dist}(\dot{x}_\alpha, \mathscr{X}_\alpha)\leq 1
$$
and $\operatorname{dist}(\mathscr{X}_\alpha, \mathscr{X}_\beta)>2$ for all $\beta<\alpha$.

Define $\tilde{\mathcal{X}}:=\{\mathscr{X}_\alpha:\alpha<\omega_1\}$.
Let $p\in\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$ and $\dot{x}$ be an $\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$-name for an
element of $\ell_1(\omega_1)$.
By the property of the family $\mathcal{N}$ we may assume that $\dot{x}\in\mathcal{N}$.
There is $0<\alpha<\omega_1$ such that
$(p,\dot{x})=(p_\alpha,\dot{x}_\alpha)$.
By the construction, there is $\beta\leq\alpha$ such that $q_\alpha\leq p$ and
$q_\alpha\Vdash\operatorname{dist}(\dot{x},\mathscr{X}_\beta)\leq 1$.

\textbf{Case 2:} Assume that $\kappa=\omega_2$.
In this case, we first force with the auxiliary forcing $\mathbb{X}$.
Let $G_{\mathbb{X}}$ be an $\mathbb{X}$-generic filter over $V$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma}, the forcing $\mathbb{X}$ does not add new countable subsets of sets
from $V$, and thus $V[G_\mathbb{X}]$ satisfies $\mathsf{CH}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:kapcc}, the forcing $\mathbb{X}$ preserves cardinalities greater than or equal to
$\kappa$, in particular it preserves $\kappa$ and $\lambda$.
It follows that $\lambda^\omega=\lambda$, and the space $\ell_1(\kappa)$ and the forcing
$\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}$ do not change when we pass from $V$ to $V[G_\mathbb{X}]$.

Define $W:=V[G_\mathbb{X}]$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{X}}:=\bigcup G_\mathbb{X}$.
By the properties of conditions in $\mathbb{X}$ and the coding conventions fixed above, 
$\mathscr{X}_0\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}$, each $\mathscr{X}\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\{\mathscr{X}_0\}$ is a translated center,
and $\operatorname{dist}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{X}')>2$ for distinct $\mathscr{X},\mathscr{X}'\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}$.
Let $p\in\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$ and $\dot{x}$ be an $\mathbb{S}^{\omega}$-name for an element of
$\ell_1(\kappa)$. By continuous reading of names and the fact that
$\mathbb{X}$ adds no new countable sets, there are $p'\leq p$ and an
$\mathbb{S}^\omega$-name $\dot{y}\in V$ such that
$p'\Vdash\dot{x}=\dot{y}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:dense}, the set $\mathcal{D}_{p',\dot{y}}$ is dense in
$\mathbb{X}$. Since $G_\mathbb{X}$ is $\mathbb{X}$-generic over $V$, there is
$\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{D}_{p',\dot{y}}\cap G_\mathbb{X}$.
Consequently, there are $q\leq p'$ and $\mathscr{X}\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}$ such that
$q\Vdash\operatorname{dist}(\dot{x},\mathscr{X})\leq1$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{cfomega}
Suppose that $\kappa$ is an uncountable cardinal
of countable cofinality. Then $\ell_1(\kappa)$ does not admit a disjoint  tiling by 
$\kappa$ many sets of the form $\mathrm{B}(X, r)$, where $X$ is  nonempty and norm-separable 
and $r>0$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $(\kappa_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be an increasing sequence of cardinal numbers below $\kappa$ which
has supremum $\kappa$.
Suppose that $\{\mathrm{B}(X_\xi,r_\xi):\xi<\kappa\}$ is such a disjoint tiling.
Fix $\gamma_0<\kappa$ and $x\in X_{\gamma_0}$.
Choose $\alpha\in\kappa\setminus\operatorname{supp}(X_{\gamma_0})$ and put
$z=x+r_{\gamma_0}\cdot1_\alpha$.
Then $\operatorname{dist}(z,X_{\gamma_0})=r_{\gamma_0}$, and so 
$z\in\mathrm{B}(X_{\gamma_0},r_{\gamma_0})$ and
$z\not\in \mathrm{B}(X_{\xi},r_{\xi})$ for any $\xi\in\kappa\setminus\{\gamma_0\}$.

Replacing each $X_\xi$ by $X_\xi-z$, we may therefore assume that
$$
\operatorname{dist}(0,X_{\gamma_0})=r_{\gamma_0}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\operatorname{dist}(0,X_\xi)>r_\xi\quad\hbox{for every }\xi\in\kappa\setminus\{\gamma_0\}.
$$
This replacement preserves the assumptions of the proposition, since $\operatorname{supp}(z)$ is countable.
Define
$$
A_n:=\bigcup\{\operatorname{supp}(X_\gamma):\gamma< \kappa_n\}
$$
for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.
By induction on $k\in \mathbb{N}$ we construct  $n_k\in \mathbb{N}$ and $y_k\in
\ell_1(A_{n_k})$ with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $n_k<n_{k+1}$,
\item $\operatorname{dist}(y_k, X_\gamma)=r_\gamma$ for some $\gamma<\kappa_{n_k}$,
\item $y_{l}|A_{n_k}=y_k$ for $l\geq k$,
\item $0<\|y_{k+1}|(A_{n_{k+1}}\setminus A_{n_k})\|\leq 1/2^k$,
\item if $y|A_{n_k}=y_k$ and $y|(\kappa\setminus A_{n_k})\not=0$, then
$y\not\in\bigcup\{\mathrm{B}(X_\gamma,r_\gamma):\gamma<\kappa_{n_k}\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Fix $n_0\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $\gamma_0<\kappa_{n_0}$ and put $y_0=0$.
Then item~(2) follows from the above translation of tiles, and item~(5) follows from
the additivity of the $\ell_1$ norm on disjoint supports.

Now let $k\in\mathbb{N}$ and suppose that $n_k$ and $y_k\in\ell_1(A_{n_k})$ have been
constructed and satisfy the relevant items~(1)--(5).
By~(2), fix $\gamma<\kappa_{n_k}$ such that
$\operatorname{dist}(y_k,X_\gamma)=r_\gamma$.
Let $t:=\frac{1}{2^k}$. 
Fix $\alpha\in\kappa\setminus A_{n_k}$, which is possible by the separability
of the $X_\gamma$s, by Lemma \ref{support} and since $\kappa_{n_k}<\kappa$.
By~(5) and since the tiling covers $\ell_1(\kappa)$, there is $\eta\geq \kappa_{n_k}$ such that
$$
y_k+t\cdot1_\alpha\in \mathrm{B}(X_\eta,r_\eta).
$$
Since $\gamma\neq\eta$, we have $y_k\notin\mathrm{B}(X_\eta,r_\eta)$, and thus $\operatorname{dist}(y_k,
X_\eta)>r_\eta$.
If $\alpha\notin\operatorname{supp}(X_\eta)$, then
$$
\operatorname{dist}(y_k+t\cdot1_\alpha,X_\eta)=\operatorname{dist}(y_k,X_\eta)+t>r_\eta,
$$
which is impossible.
Thus $\alpha\in\operatorname{supp}(X_\eta)$.
Since $\mathrm{B}(X_\eta,r_\eta)$ is closed, $y_k\notin\mathrm{B}(X_\eta,r_\eta)$, and
$y_k+t\cdot1_\alpha\in\mathrm{B}(X_\eta,r_\eta)$, there is $t'$ such that $0<t'\leq t$ and
$$
\operatorname{dist}(y_k+t'\cdot1_\alpha,X_\eta)=r_\eta.
$$
Fix $n_{k+1}>n_k$ such that $\eta<\kappa_{n_{k+1}}$.
Then $\alpha\in A_{n_{k+1}}\setminus A_{n_k}$.
Let $y_{k+1}:=y_k+t'\cdot 1_\alpha$.
Since the tiling is disjoint and $y_{k+1}\in\mathrm{B}(X_\eta,r_\eta)$, we have
$\operatorname{dist}(y_{k+1},X_\xi)\geq r_\xi$ for all $\xi<\kappa_{n_{k+1}}$.
Then items (1)--(4) follow from the construction and (5) follows from the additivity
of the $\ell_1$ norm on disjoint supports.

Finally, let $y\in \mathbb{R}^\kappa$ be given by $y|A_{n_k}=y_k$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$
and $y|(\kappa\setminus\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n_k})=0$.
Then $y$ is a well defined element of $ \ell_1(\kappa)$ by~(3) and~(4). 
Item~(5) implies that $y\not\in\mathrm{B}(X_\gamma,r_\gamma)$ for all $\gamma<\kappa$, a
contradiction.

<!-- lemma-16: body_md -->
\label{lines} Suppose that $\kappa>0$ is a cardinal.
Let $D\subseteq\ell_1(\kappa)$ be nonempty, $z\in D$, $\alpha\in\kappa$.
Then $V_D(z)\cap (a+\mathbb{R}1_{\alpha})$
 is either empty or a closed interval in $a+\mathbb{R}1_{\alpha}$ for every $a\in \ell_1(\kappa)$
satisfying $a(\alpha)=0$.
<!-- end lemma-16 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $x,y\in V_D(z)\cap (a+\mathbb{R}1_{\alpha})$, and suppose that
$x(\alpha)\leq t\leq y(\alpha)$. Define  $u=a+t1_{\alpha}\in a+\mathbb{R}1_{\alpha}$.
We will show that $u\in V_D(z)\cap (a+\mathbb{R}1_{\alpha})$.
Fix $w\in D$, using the fact that $x|(\kappa\setminus\{\alpha\})=
y|(\kappa\setminus\{\alpha\})=u|(\kappa\setminus\{\alpha\})$ we have
$$
A:=\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}|x(\beta)-z(\beta)|=\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}|y(\beta)-z(\beta)|=
\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}|u(\beta)-z(\beta)|.
$$
$$B:=\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}|x(\beta)-w(\beta)|=\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}|y(\beta)-w(\beta)|
=\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}|u(\beta)-w(\beta)|.
$$
Since $x,y\in V_D(z)$, by the definition of the norm in $\ell_1(\kappa)$, we have
$$
|x(\alpha)-z(\alpha)|+A\leq |x(\alpha)-w(\alpha)|+B
\leqno (1)$$
and
$$
|y(\alpha)-z(\alpha)|+A\leq |y(\alpha)-w(\alpha)|+B.
\leqno(2)$$
Consider the continuous function $f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ given  by $f(s)=
 |s-z(\alpha)|-|s-w(\alpha)|$. By (1) and (2) we have $f(x(\alpha)), f(y(\alpha))\leq B-A$.
The function $f$ is  monotone on $\mathbb{R}$. Indeed, on the interval with endpoints
$z(\alpha), w(\alpha)$ $f$ is nondecreasing if $z(\alpha)<w(\alpha)$ and
nonincreasing if $z(\alpha)>w(\alpha)$ and it is constant 
on the halflines outside this interval  and on the entire $\mathbb{R}$ if $z(\alpha)=w(\alpha)$. Hence, since
$t\in[x(\alpha),y(\alpha)]$, we have $f(t)\leq B-A$ so
$$
|u(\alpha)-z(\alpha)|+A\leq |u(\alpha)-w(\alpha)|+B.
$$
Again by  the definition of the norm in $\ell_1(\kappa)$
we conclude that
$$
\|u-z\|\leq\|u-w\|.
$$
As $w\in D$ was arbitrary, $u\in V_D(z)$. Thus $V_D(z)\cap (a+\mathbb{R}1_{\alpha})$ is a
closed interval as needed.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:chebyshev}
Suppose that $\kappa$ is a cardinal. Every Chebyshev set in
$\ell_1(\kappa)$ containing at least two points has cardinality at least
$2^\omega$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let $D\subseteq\ell_1(\kappa)$ be a Chebyshev set containing distinct points
$z,w$. Then
$$
\{V_D(v):v\in D\}
$$
is a pairwise disjoint cover of $\ell_1(\kappa)$ by closed sets.

We first show that there is a line $L_{a, \alpha}=a+\mathbb{R}1_{\alpha}$
where $a(\alpha)=0$ and $a\in \ell_1(\kappa)$ which meets at least two distinct
Voronoi cells $V_D(x)$ for $x\in D$. Suppose otherwise. 

The set $\operatorname{supp}(w-z)$ is nonempty and
countable, so $\operatorname{supp}(w-z)=\{\alpha_n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ for some sequence
$(\alpha_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$, where repetitions are allowed.  Put
$$
x_n:=z+(w-z)|\{\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
$$
for $n\in\mathbb{N}$. Then $x_0=z\in V_D(z)$ and $x_n$ converges to $w$. 
So, since $w\not\in V_D(z)$ there is $k\in \mathbb{N}$ which is minimal such that $x_{k+1}\not\in V_D(z)$.
As $x_k$ and $x_{k+1}$ differ only at the coordinate $\alpha_k$
they are both in the line $L_{a_k, \alpha_k} $ for appropriate $a_k$ satisfying
 $a_k(\alpha_k)=0$ and $a_k\in \ell_1(\kappa)$. The minimality of $k$ implies
that $L=L_{a_k, \alpha_k}$ meets at least two distinct sets $V_D(x)$.

Identify $L$ with $\mathbb{R}$. By Lemma~\ref{lines}, the family
$\{V_D(v)\cap L:v\in D,\ V_D(v)\cap L\neq\emptyset\}$
is a nontrivial partition of $\mathbb{R}$ into closed intervals. By the Sierpi\'nski theorem
from \cite{sierpinski} it is uncountable.
There can be only countably many nondegenerate members of it,  so the set of points
which form singleton members together with the endpoints of the
nondegenerate members is an uncountable closed subset of $L$. Hence it has
cardinality $2^\omega$.
Consequently the partition has cardinality $2^\omega$
and so
$|D|\geq 2^\omega$.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:mainchebyshev} Suppose that $\kappa<2^\omega$ is infinite. Then
the Banach space $\ell_1(\kappa)$ does not admit a nontrivial discrete Chebyshev set.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Since the density of $\ell_1(\kappa)$ is $\kappa$, it cannot admit  discrete sets bigger than $\kappa$.
Now the theorem follows from  Proposition \ref{prop:chebyshev}.
