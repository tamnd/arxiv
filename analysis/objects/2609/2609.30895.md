<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:kahn-saks}
If $w(P)\to\infty$ then $\delta(P)\to1/2$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every fixed $k\ge2$, $t\ge1$, and $\varepsilon>0$, there is a
$W_{k,t,\varepsilon}$ such that every poset $P$ with $w(P)>W_{k,t,\varepsilon}$
contains at least one of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an $\varepsilon$-pseudo antichain of size $k$;
\item an $\varepsilon$-pseudo insertor of size $t+1$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
If $\pi(P)\to \infty$ then $\delta(P)\to 1/2$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:tuples}
For every fixed $k\ge2$ and $X\subseteq P$ with $|X|\ge k$,
$$S_X=\omega(|X|^{1/2})\hspace{12pt}\implies\hspace{12pt}\delta_k(X;P)\to1/k!.$$
Moreover, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $\eta=\eta(k,\varepsilon)>0$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-accuracy}
S_X= \omega(|X|^{1/2-\eta})\hspace{12pt}\implies\hspace{12pt}\delta_k(X;P)\ge 1/k!-\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:antichains}
For every $k\ge2$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there is $\eta'=\eta'(k,\varepsilon)>0$ such that, for every antichain $A\subseteq P$,
$$|A|=\omega(n^{2/3-\eta'})\hspace{12pt}\implies\hspace{12pt} S_A\ge\frac{|A|^2}{2n}=\omega(|A|^{1/2-\eta})\hspace{12pt}\implies\hspace{12pt} \delta_k(A;P)\ge 1/k!-\varepsilon.$$
In particular, $w(P)=\omega(n^{2/3})$ implies $\delta_k(P)\to1/k!$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:counterexample}
For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, there exist $\eta_\varepsilon>0$ and a sequence of posets $P$, with $n=|P|\to\infty$, such that
$$S_P=\omega(n^{1/2-\varepsilon}),\hspace{24pt}\delta_3(P)\le1/6-\eta_\varepsilon.$$
Furthermore, $w(P)=\omega(n^{1/2-\varepsilon})$ and $\delta(P)=1/2.$
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:local-width-counterexample}
For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/6$, there exist $\eta_\varepsilon>0$ and a sequence of posets $P$ with maximum antichains $A$, where $n=|P|\to\infty$, such that
$$|A|=\omega(n^{2/3-\varepsilon}),\hspace{24pt}\delta_3(A;P)\le1/6-\eta_\varepsilon.$$
Furthermore, $S_P=\omega(n^{2/3-\varepsilon})$ and
$\delta_k(P)=1/k!$ for fixed $k$ and sufficiently large $n$.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:selection}
Fix $k\ge2$ and $A,B,\varepsilon>0$. There is an integer $M_{k,A,B,\varepsilon}$ such that the following holds. Let $V_1,\dots,V_m$ be jointly distributed real random variables, with $m\ge M_{k,A,B,\varepsilon}$, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection}
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} V_i^2\le A,\qquad
 \mathbb{P}(|V_i-V_j|\le h)\le Bh\quad(i\ne j,\ h>0).
\end{equation}
Then there are distinct
$i_1,\cdots,i_k$ such that, for every $\pi\in S_k$,
$$\mathbb{P}(V_{i_{\pi(1)}}<\cdots <V_{i_{\pi(k)}})\ge 1/k!-\varepsilon.
$$
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix $k\ge 2$ and $A,B,\varepsilon>0$. Choose $T$ large and $h>0$ small enough so that $kA/T^2+\binom{k}{2}Bh\le \varepsilon/2$. Partition $[-T,T]$ into intervals of length at most $h$ and add the two tail intervals. Then, for $V_{i_1},\dots, V_{i_k}$, the probability that two $V_i$'s are in the same interval is at most
$$\sum_a \mathbb{P}(|V_{i_a}|>T)+\sum_{a<b} \mathbb{P}(|V_{i_a}-V_{i_b}|\le h)\le kA/T^2+\binom{k}{2}Bh\le \varepsilon/2.$$

Let $B_i$ be the index of the interval containing $V_i$, with the
intervals indexed from left to right. Apply \cite[Theorem 8.1]{AK}
to the finite-valued variables $B_i$, with error $\varepsilon/4$. For sufficiently
large $m$, it supplies distinct indices $i_1,\dots,i_k$ for which the
probabilities $p_\pi=\mathbb{P}(B_{i_{\pi(1)}}<\cdots<B_{i_{\pi(k)}})$ differ from one another by at most $\varepsilon/2$. The collision estimate gives $\sum_\pi p_\pi\ge1-\varepsilon/2$, so $p_\pi\ge\frac{1-\varepsilon/2}{k!}-\varepsilon/2\ge1/k!-\varepsilon$. A strict order of interval indices implies the same strict order of the
original variables. Hence $\mathbb{P}(V_{i_{\pi(1)}}<\cdots<V_{i_{\pi(k)}})\ge1/k!-\varepsilon$
for every $\pi$, as required.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{cp:lem:doubling}
If $P$ has no $\varepsilon$-pseudo antichain (in particular if $\delta_k(P)<1/k!-\varepsilon$), every $\ell$-ball of radius $r$ is covered by at most $N_{k,\varepsilon}$ balls of radius $r/2$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Take an $r/2$-separated subset of the $\ell$-ball centered at $b$. Partition the heights into a bounded number of intervals of length $r/8$. Within one interval, distinct points satisfy
$$d(s,t)=\ell(s,t)-\Delta(s,t)>r/2-r/8=3r/8.$$

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:selection} with the fixed $k$ to
$X_z=(Z_z-Z_b)/r$ for the vertices in this interval. Since they lie in the
ball,
$$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} X_z^2=\frac{\Delta(z,b)^2+d(z,b)^2}{r^2}\le1.$$

Moreover, $\operatorname{sd}(X_s-X_t)>3/8$, so log-concavity and \eqref{eq:inputs} give
$$\mathbb{P}(|X_s-X_t|\le u)\le Cu\hspace{24pt}(u>0).$$

These are precisely the two hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}. If the interval contained sufficiently many selected vertices, it would give a $k$-tuple with every order probability at least $1/k!-\varepsilon/(2k!)$, and hence an $\varepsilon$-pseudo antichain, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus the number of selected vertices in each interval, and hence in the ball, is bounded in terms of $k,\varepsilon$. A maximal $r/2$-separated subset supplies centers for the required covering.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix $k\ge2$, and put
\[
 K_X:=\sup_{\substack{0<u\le1/2\\b\in\mathbb{R}}}
 u\,|\{x\in X:\mu_x\in[b,b+u),\ u\le\sigma_x<2u\}|.
\]
We first show that, for every nonempty $X\subseteq P$ and
$0<s\le1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 |\{x\in X:\sigma_x\ge s\}|\le CK_Xs^{-2},
\end{equation}
where $C$ is an absolute constant.
At each dyadic scale $u=2^js\le1/2$, partition $[0,1]$ into
$O(1/u)$ intervals of length $u$. Each contains at most
$K_X/u$ elements with $u\le\sigma_x<2u$. Summing
$O(K_X/u^2)$ proves \eqref{eq:count}. Since $c_x\le\sqrt3\sigma_x$
and $c_x\le1/2$, integration gives
$$S_X=\int_0^{1/2}|\{x\in X:c_x>t\}|\,dt\le\int_0^\infty\min\{|X|,CK_Xt^{-2}\}\,dt
 =O(\sqrt{|X|K_X}).
$$
Hence $K_X=\Omega(S_X^2/|X|)$, so $S_X=\omega(|X|^{1/2})$ implies $K_X\to \infty$.

If $K_X\to\infty$, we now show that $\delta_k(X;P)\to1/k!$. We will find a growing subset of $X$
whose normalized coordinates satisfy the two hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}: bounded second moments and a uniform
small-ball bound for every pair.

By the definition of $K_X$, choose $u,b$ so that
$$A=\{x\in X:\mu_x\in[b,b+u),\ u\le\sigma_x<2u\},\hspace{24pt} m=|A|,\hspace{24pt} mu\ge K_X/2\longrightarrow\infty.$$
For $x\in A$, put $V_x=(F(x)-b)/u$. Then
$0\le\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} V_x<1$ and $1\le\text{Var} V_x<4$, so
$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} V_x^2\le5$. It remains to find many of these coordinates
whose pairwise differences do not concentrate near zero.

Put $Y_x=T_{[-10,10]}(V_x)$. The density bound in
\eqref{eq:inputs} gives $\|g_{V_x}\|_\infty\le\sqrt2$, and
Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(|V_x|>10)\le1/20$.
For any interval $J$ of length $1/4$, therefore,
$$\mathbb{P}(Y_x\in J)\le\mathbb{P}(V_x\in J)+\mathbb{P}(|V_x|>10)\le\frac{\sqrt2}{4}+\frac1{20}<\frac12.$$
Taking $J=[\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} Y_x-1/8,\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} Y_x+1/8]$ shows that
$\text{Var} Y_x\ge(1/8)^2/2=:v=1/128$.

For $I=[b-10u,b+10u]$, we have
$Y_x=(T_I(F(x))-b)/u$. Since every covariance in
\eqref{eq:budget} is nonnegative, restricting its sum to $A$ gives
$$
 \sum_{y\in A}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_x,Y_y)
 =u^{-2}\sum_{y\in A}\operatorname{Cov}(T_I(F(x)),T_I(F(y)))
 \le u^{-2}\frac{20u}{4}=\frac5u.
$$
Join distinct $x,y\in A$ by an edge when
$\operatorname{Cov}(Y_x,Y_y)>v/2$. Each vertex has degree at most
$10/(vu)$. Greedily keeping a vertex and deleting its neighbors
therefore produces a set $B\subseteq A$ with
$$
 |B|\ge\frac{m}{1+10/(vu)}=\Omega(mu)\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad \operatorname{Cov}(Y_x,Y_y)\le v/2\quad(x\ne y\in B).
$$
Here the implied constant is absolute, since $u\le1/2$. For distinct $x,y\in B$,
$$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}(Y_x-Y_y)^2\ge\text{Var} Y_x+\text{Var} Y_y-2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_x,Y_y)\ge v.$$
Clipping is $1$-Lipschitz and $|Y_x-Y_y|\le20$, hence
$$
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|V_x-V_y|\ge\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|Y_x-Y_y|
 \ge\frac{\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}(Y_x-Y_y)^2}{20}\ge\frac v{20}.
$$
The difference $V_x-V_y=(F(x)-F(y))/u$ has a log-concave
density. Thus \eqref{eq:inputs} gives, for every $h>0$,
$$
 \mathbb{P}(|V_x-V_y|\le h)
 \le\frac{3h}{\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|V_x-V_y|}\le\frac{60}{v}h.
$$
Together with $\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} V_x^2\le5$ and $|B|\to\infty$, this verifies
all hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}. It supplies $k$
elements of $B\subseteq X$ with every ordering probability
$1/k!+o(1)$. The common positive rescaling preserves their orders,
and the orders under $F$ have the same law as under $f$.
Therefore $\delta_k(X;P)\to1/k!$. Together with the bound on $K_X$, this proves the first assertion.

Now fix $\varepsilon>0$, and suppose $\delta_k(X;P)<1/k!-\varepsilon$; all constants below may depend on $k,\varepsilon$. The preceding argument gives $K_X=O_{k,\varepsilon}(1)$.
Thus \eqref{eq:count} implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-variance-count}
 |\{x\in X:\sigma_x>s\}|\le C s^{-2}\qquad(s>0).
\end{equation}
We refine the count by covering in the difference metric $d$ in rank units.

Choose an integer $L\ge2$ large enough that the finite selection
theorem applies to $L$ variables satisfying
$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} V_i^2\le5$ and
$\mathbb{P}(|V_i-V_j|\le h)\le4\sqrt2\,h$, with target error
$\varepsilon/2$. Put $D=\log_2 L$. We claim that, if $0<u\le s$
and $Y\subseteq X$ satisfies
$$
 \mu_x\in[b,b+u),\qquad \sigma_x\le s,\qquad c_x\ge u
 \quad(x\in Y),
$$
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-packing}
 |Y|\le C(s/u)^D.
\end{equation}
Consider a ball in $Y$, centered at $a\in Y$, of radius
$r\ge(n+1)u/2$. For any subset in this ball with pairwise distances
greater than $r/2$, set $V_x=(Z_x-Z_a)/r$. Then
$$
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} V_x^2=\frac{d(x,a)^2+(n+1)^2(\mu_x-\mu_a)^2}{r^2}\le5,
 \qquad
 \mathbb{P}(|V_x-V_y|\le h)
 \le\frac{2\sqrt2\,hr}{d(x,y)}\le4\sqrt2\,h.
$$
The second bound follows from the log-concave density bound in
\eqref{eq:inputs}. Subtracting the same random coordinate preserves
all orders. The deficit therefore forces this separated subset to
have fewer than $L$ elements. Taking a maximal separated subset
covers the ball by at most $L$ balls of radius $r/2$, with centers
in $Y$.

The diameter of $Y$ is at most $2(n+1)s$. Start with a ball of radius
$2^J(n+1)u$, where $J=\lceil\log_2(2s/u)\rceil$, and apply the
covering step $J+2$ times. The final radii are $(n+1)u/4$.
By \eqref{eq:fiber}, distinct points of $Y$ have distance at least
$(n+1)u/\sqrt3>(n+1)u/2$, so each final ball contains at most one point.
Hence $|Y|\le L^{J+2}\le C(s/u)^D$, proving
\eqref{eq:fixed-packing}.

For $0<u\le1/2$, separate the elements with $c_x\ge u$ into
those with $\sigma_x>s$ and those with $\sigma_x\le s$.
Use \eqref{eq:fixed-variance-count} for the first group and partition
the second group's coordinate means into $O(1/u)$ intervals of length $u$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-packing} gives, for any $s\ge u$,
$$
 |\{x\in X:c_x\ge u\}|
 \le C\left(s^{-2}+s^D u^{-(D+1)}\right).
$$
Choose $s=u^{(D+1)/(D+2)}$. Both terms are $u^{-p}$, where
$p=2(D+1)/(D+2)\in(1,2)$. Integrating this count, using
$c_x\le1/2$, yields
$$S_X\le\int_0^\infty\min\{|X|,Cu^{-p}\}\,du\le C' |X|^{1-1/p}=C'|X|^{1/2-\gamma},\hspace{24pt} \gamma=\frac1{2(D+1)}>0.$$
Taking $\eta=\gamma$, the contrapositive proves~\eqref{eq:fixed-accuracy}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:counter-independence}
Let $X,Y,Z$ have continuous, stochastically ordered marginal laws,
with $Z$ independent of $(X,Y)$. Suppose the conditional law of either
of $X,Y$, given the other, increases stochastically with the conditioning
value. If each of the six orders has probability at least $1/6-\varepsilon$,
where $\varepsilon\ge0$, then, for every $s,t\in\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counter-rectangles}
 0\le\mathbb{P}(X\le s,Y\le t)-\mathbb{P}(X\le s)\mathbb{P}(Y\le t)\le48\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Write $H_X,H_Y,H_Z$ for the distribution functions, and put
$U=H_X(X)$, $V=H_Y(Y)$, $W=H_Z(Z)$. These variables are uniform
on $[0,1]$, with $W$ independent of $(U,V)$. Stochastic ordering
makes $\{X<Z\}$ and $\{U<W\}$ nested almost surely, so
$$
 \mathbb{P}\bigl(\{X<Z\}\mathbin{\triangle}\{U<W\}\bigr)
 =|\mathbb{P}(X<Z)-1/2|\le3\varepsilon.
$$
The same holds for $Y,Z$. Thus
$q:=\mathbb{P}(\min(U,V)<W<\max(U,V))\ge1/3-8\varepsilon$.
Put $D(u,v)=\mathbb{P}(U\le u,V\le v)-uv$. Conditional monotonicity
makes $D$ concave in each coordinate, with zero boundary values,
so $D\ge0$. Independence of $W$ gives
$$
 \int_0^1D(t,t)\,dt=\frac{1/3-q}{2}\le4\varepsilon.
$$
Concavity in the two coordinates also gives
$D(t,t)\ge\min\{t,1-t\}^2D(u,v)$ for all $u,v,t\in[0,1]$.
Integrating yields $D(u,v)\le48\varepsilon$; take $u=H_X(s)$ and
$v=H_Y(t)$ to obtain~\eqref{eq:counter-rectangles}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The coordinate laws of $P_r$ are totally stochastically ordered:
this holds for a chain, series sums preserve it by their common
increasing affine embeddings, and parallel duplication repeats the same
list of laws. Put $n=n_r$, $p=p_r$, $b=2n+2p$. An old copy preceded
by $a\in\{p,p+n\}$ has embedding
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counter-dirichlet}
 F'_i=L+TF_i,\qquad
 (L,T,1-L-T)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(a,n+1,b-a-n),
\end{equation}
independently of its old coordinates. Total variance and Dirichlet
moments give
$$
 2b\text{Var}(F'_i)\le
 \frac{2b(n+1)(n+2)}{n(b+1)(b+2)}\,n\text{Var}(F_i)+1/2.
$$
The coefficient tends to $1/(1+\theta)<1$, and end-chain coordinates
have scaled variance at most $1/2$. For sufficiently large $N$,
iteration therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counter-variance}
 \sup_{r,x\in P_r}n_r\text{Var}(F_x)\le C_\theta.
\end{equation}

Consider an antichain triple with least ordering probability $1/6-\xi$.
Restrict it to its smallest recursive component $P_s$, preserving its
ordering law, and put $n=n_{s-1}$, $p=p_{s-1}$, $b=2n+2p$,
so $m=|P_s|=2b$. Two elements $x,y$ share an old copy in one branch,
while $z$ lies in the other and is independent of them.
In~\eqref{eq:counter-dirichlet}, put $R=L+T$. The old covariance is nonnegative by FKG, and $\text{Var} L,\text{Var} R\ge\operatorname{Cov}(L,R)$.
Since conditional means are convex combinations of $L,R$, total covariance gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counter-shared-covariance}
 m\operatorname{Cov}(F_x,F_y)\ge 2b\operatorname{Cov}(L,R)
 =\frac{2b\,p(n+p)}{(b+1)^2(b+2)}\ge c_\theta>0.
\end{equation}

Put $A=\sqrt m(F_x-\mu_x)$ and $B=\sqrt m(F_y-\mu_y)$. Then
$\text{Var} A,\text{Var} B\le C_\theta$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)\ge c_\theta$.
The tail bound~\eqref{eq:logconcave-tails} and Cauchy--Schwarz allow a fixed
$K=K_\theta\ge\sqrt{C_\theta}$ such that clipping both variables to $[-K,K]$
leaves covariance at least $c_\theta/2$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:counter-independence} to $(F_x,F_y,F_z)$.
Its rectangle bound is preserved by separate increasing affine changes
of coordinates. Integrating the indicator covariances over $[-K,K]^2$
therefore gives
$$
 c_\theta/2\le\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(T_{[-K,K]}(A),T_{[-K,K]}(B)\bigr)
 \le48\xi(2K)^2.
$$
Hence $\xi\ge\eta_\theta:=c_\theta/(384K_\theta^2)>0$, uniformly in the
depth and in sufficiently large seed sizes. Since $\eta_\theta\le1/384$,
triples containing a comparable pair satisfy the same bound trivially. Finally, choose $\theta$ so that $a_\theta>1/2-\varepsilon$, and fix such an $N$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:counter-result}, together with $\delta(P_r)=1/2$
for $r\ge1$, proves Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Take $H=P_r$ from the preceding construction, with $M=|H|$ and
$w=2^r=\Theta(M^{\beta_\theta})$. Choose one seed-chain vertex and,
at each step, its descendants in the first old copy of each branch.
They form a maximum antichain $D$ of size $w$. Symmetry gives
identical marginals on $D$, and \eqref{eq:counter-variance} gives
$$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} F_x=\mu,\qquad \text{Var}(F_x)\le C_\theta/M\quad(x\in D).$$

Fix a sufficiently large constant $L=L(\theta)$, and put
$$h=\left\lceil L\sqrt M\right\rceil,\hspace{24pt} T=\left\lfloor M/(2h)\right\rfloor.$$
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-counter-construction}
 Q_j=C_{jh}\oplus H\oplus C_{M-jh}\hspace{12pt}(1\le j\le T),\hspace{24pt} P=Q_1+\cdots+Q_T,\hspace{24pt} A=\bigcup_{j=1}^T D^{(j)}.
\end{equation}
Since the first vertices of the prefix chains are independent and identically
distributed, any $k$ of them have all $k!$ orders equally likely, so $\delta_k(P)=1/k!$ whenever $T\ge k$.


Every branch has size $2M$ and width $w$, so $A$ is a maximum antichain. Its groups have coordinate mean spacing $h/(2M+1)$, while all their
variances are $O_\theta(1/M)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-counter-marginals}
 \mu_j=\frac{jh+(M+1)\mu}{2M+1},\qquad
 \text{Var}(F_x)\le C_1/M\quad(x\in D^{(j)}).
\end{equation}
Indeed the embedding is $F'_x=U+VF_x$, with
$(U,V,1-U-V)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(jh,M+1,M-jh)$;
its added variance is at most $1/[4(2M+2)]$.
For $i<j$, independence across branches and Chebyshev give
$$
 \mathbb{P}(F_y<F_x)\le18C_1/L^2\le1/4
 \qquad(x\in D^{(i)},\ y\in D^{(j)}),
$$
once $L$ is large enough. Any triple meeting different branches
has three orders containing such a reversed pair, so one has
probability at most $1/12$. A triple within a branch retains its
ordering law in $H$. Therefore

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{cp:lem:scale}
Put $M=\max(R,G)$. For all vertices $x,y$,
\begin{equation}\label{cp:eq:diffusiveM}
 d(x,y)^2\le 4M\bigl(\Delta(x,y)+M\bigr),
\end{equation}
and for every interval $I$ of length $M$, $|\{z\in P:h(z)\in I\}|\le 8M+1$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
First, the definition of $R$ gives, for any vertices $u,v$,
\[
 d(u,v)\le\max\{R,\Delta(u,v)\}.
\]
Indeed, if $d(u,v)\ge\Delta(u,v)$, this pair enters the maximum
defining $R$, so $d(u,v)\le R$. Otherwise $d(u,v)<\Delta(u,v)$.

The case $x=y$ is immediate. For distinct $x,y$, interchange them
if necessary so that $h(x)\le h(y)$, and list the vertices between
them in nondecreasing height order, breaking ties arbitrarily.
Starting at $x_0=x$, choose the next anchor to be the first vertex
whose height is at least $M$ larger than the current anchor's height.
If no such vertex occurs before or at $y$, finish with $y$ as the
last anchor. Stop as soon as $y$ has been selected, and write the
resulting anchors as $x_0,\ldots,x_q=y$. Every step except possibly the last advances the height by at least $M$. Since the advances sum to $\Delta(x,y)$, we have
\[
 (q-1)M\le\Delta(x,y),\qquad
 q\le1+\frac{\Delta(x,y)}M.
\]
A step chosen by the threshold rule advances the height by at most
$M+G\le2M$: just before the first vertex crossing the threshold,
the advance is less than $M$, and the next gap is at most $G$.
A final step that does not reach the threshold has advance less
than $M$. Thus every anchor step satisfies
\[
 d(x_{i-1},x_i)
 \le\max\{R,\Delta(x_{i-1},x_i)\}\le2M.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:sub-add} now gives
$$d(x,y)^2\le\sum_{i=1}^q d(x_{i-1},x_i)^2\le q(2M)^2\le4M^2\left(1+\frac{\Delta(x,y)}M\right)=4M\bigl(\Delta(x,y)+M\bigr).$$

This proves~\eqref{cp:eq:diffusiveM}. For the vertex count, choose an anchor $b$ in a nonempty interval of length $M$. For each other vertex $z$ there,
$d(z,b)\le\max\{R,\Delta(z,b)\}\le M$, hence
$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}(Z_z-Z_b)^2\le2M^2$ and $\mathbb{P}(|Z_z-Z_b|\le2M)\ge1/2$.

Condition on the linear extension and write $f(b)=j$. The positive differences
from $Z_b$ have distributions $(n+1)\operatorname{Beta}(k,n+1-k)$,
$1\le k\le n-j$. Their summed density is bounded by the sum over
$1\le k\le n$, which before scaling is
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^n
 \frac{n!}{(k-1)!(n-k)!}t^{k-1}(1-t)^{n-k}=n.
\]
Thus the summed density is at most $n/(n+1)$ on the positive half-line;
the negative half-line is identical. Averaging over the extension gives,
for every interval $I\subset\mathbb R$,
\[
 \sum_{z\ne b}\mathbb{P}(Z_z-Z_b\in I)\le\frac n{n+1}|I|.
\]
Apply this to $I=[-2M,2M]$. Each vertex in the chosen interval other
than $b$ contributes at least $1/2$, so there are at most $8M$ such vertices.
Including $b$ gives the asserted bound $8M+1$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{cp:lem:localization}
Fix $k\ge2$ and $\varepsilon,\theta>0$. For all sufficiently large
$M=\max\{R(P),G(P)\}$, if $P$ has an internal height gap of size at least
$\theta M$, then $\delta_k(P)\ge1/k!-\varepsilon.$

Suppose instead that $\delta_k(P)<1/k!-\varepsilon$. Let $g\ge\theta M$ be the
size of an internal height gap, with endpoints $u,v$, so
$h_P(v)-h_P(u)=g$, and put
\[
 I=\{z:h_P(z)\le h_P(u)\},\qquad J=P\setminus I,
 \qquad L=AM\log^2M,
\]
where $A$ is a sufficiently large absolute constant.
Use the original coordinates $Z_z=(n+1)F(z)$ throughout.
By Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:scale} and~\eqref{eq:logconcave-tails}, applied to
$Z_y-Z_x-H$, for
$H=h_P(y)-h_P(x)\ge CM$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}_P(Z_y<Z_x)
 \le\exp\left(1-\frac{H}{d_P(x,y)}\right)
 \le C\exp(-c\sqrt{H/M}).
\]
Form $Q'$ by adjoining, all at once, the comparisons
\[
 z<u\quad\text{if }h_P(z)\le h_P(u)-L,
 \qquad
 v<z\quad\text{if }h_P(z)\ge h_P(v)+L.
\]
These comparisons stay within $I$ and $J$. Let $E$ be the event that all comparisons are satisfied for $F$.
Partition the values below $h_P(u)-L$ and above $h_P(v)+L$ into intervals of length $M$. Each interval contains at most $8M+1$ vertices by
Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:scale}. The union bound therefore gives
$$\mathbb{P}_P(E^c)\le CM\sum_{j\ge\lfloor L/M\rfloor}e^{-c\sqrt j}\le M^{-4},$$
provided $A$ and then $M$ are sufficiently large.
Thus $E$ has positive probability, the added comparisons are consistent,
and conditioning gives the uniform order-polytope law of $Q'$. Since the
ground set and rank scale are unchanged, $h_{Q'}(z)=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_P[Z_z\mid E]$.
No comparison is added from $J$ to $I$, so $I$ remains an ideal.

For $s\in I,t\in J$, write $H=h_P(t)-h_P(s)\ge g\ge\theta M$.
Equation~\eqref{cp:eq:diffusiveM} gives
$d_P(s,t)/H\le C_\theta$.
Putting $q=\mathbb{P}_P(E^c)\le M^{-4}$ in~\eqref{eq:weak-conditioning}, we obtain
\[
 \left|\frac{h_{Q'}(t)-h_{Q'}(s)}{h_P(t)-h_P(s)}-1\right|
 \le C_\theta\sqrt{\frac q{1-q}}\le\frac12
\]
for sufficiently large $M$ in terms of $\theta$. Hence
\[
 \min_{t\in J}h_{Q'}(t)-\max_{s\in I}h_{Q'}(s)\ge g/2.
\]

Every vertex of $I$ whose original height is at most $h_P(u)-L$ now lies
below $u$, and every vertex of $J$ whose original height is at least
$h_P(v)+L$ now lies above $v$. Thus the original heights from
$\max_{Q'}I$ lie in $(h_P(u)-L,h_P(u)]$, and those from
$\min_{Q'}J$ lie in $[h_P(v),h_P(v)+L)$. By applying~\eqref{eq:local-gap-input} in $Q'$, we have
$$g/2\le |\max_{Q'}I|+|\min_{Q'}J|-1.$$

Choosing the larger of these antichains gives $B\subseteq P$ with
\begin{equation}\label{cp:eq:localized-antichain}
 |B|\ge g/4\ge\theta M/4,\qquad H_P(B)\le L=AM\log^2M.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{cp:eq:diffusiveM} then gives $D_P(B)=O(M\log M)$.
Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:antichain-dispersion}, for a fixed $\eta=\eta(k,\varepsilon)>0$,
instead gives
\[
 |B|=O_{\theta,k,\varepsilon}\bigl((M\log^2M)^{1-\eta}\bigr)=o(M),
\]
contradicting~\eqref{cp:eq:localized-antichain} for sufficiently large $M$.

It suffices to treat $0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2(t+1)}$. Fix $k\ge2$ and $t\ge1$.

\textbf{Stage 1: Normalize coordinates and count them locally.}
Writing $m=|P_0|$, form $P=C_{m^2}\oplus P_0\oplus C_{m^2}$ and put
$n=|P|=2m^2+m$. Clearly $w(P)=w(P_0)$, $G(P)=G(P_0)$, and $P$ has no $\varepsilon$-pseudo antichain.

For distinct $u,v\in P$, put $D=f(v)-f(u)$. Conditional on the extension,
the rank-unit difference $Z_v-Z_u$ has mean $D$ and variance
$|D|(n+1-|D|)/(n+2)$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{cp:eq:rankvariance}
 d(u,v)^2=\text{Var}(D)+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\frac{|D|(n+1-|D|)}{n+2}\le\text{Var}(D)+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|D|.
\end{equation}
For $u,v \in P_0$, the law of $D$ and its mean are unchanged by padding,
while the conditional variance of $Z_u-Z_v$ at $s=|D|$ is $s-s(s+1)/(n+2)$, which increases with $n$. Thus $d_P(u,v)\ge d_{P_0}(u,v)$ and $\Delta_P(u,v) =\Delta_{P_0}(u,v)$, so $R:=R(P)\ge R(P_0)$. Hence it suffices to find an $\varepsilon$-pseudo insertor in $P$.

For $u,v \notin P_0$, $D$ is a fixed nonzero integer, so $d(u,v)^2<|D|\le D^2=\Delta^2(u,v)$ and the pair cannot enter the maximum defining $R$. For $u,v\in P_0$, $|D|\le m$ gives $d(u,v)^2\le m^2+m$. For $u\in P_0,v\notin P_0$, $D$ has constant sign and ranges over an interval of at most $m$ values, so if this pair enters the maximum, then $\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|D|=|\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} D|\le d(u,v)$, so $d(u,v)^2\le m^2+d(u,v)$ and $d(u,v)\le m+1$. Consequently $R\le m+1$, and a pair attaining $R>1$ has a vertex in $P_0$.

Choose a pair $x,y$ attaining $R$, with $x\in P_0$; then $\Delta(x,y)\le d(x,y)=R$. For each vertex $z\in P$, put
\begin{equation}\label{cp:eq:finite-coordinates}
 A_z=\frac{Z_z-Z_x}{R}=b_z+W_z,\qquad
 b_z=\frac{h(z)-h(x)}R,\qquad \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} W_z=0.
\end{equation}
We think of $b_z$ as the mean height of $z$ relative to the anchor $x$, and $W_z$ as its ``centered fluctuation,'' both in units of $R$. Write
\[
 d_0(s,t)=\|W_s-W_t\|_2=\frac{d(s,t)}R,\qquad
 \ell_0(s,t)=|b_s-b_t|+d_0(s,t).
\]
In particular $b_x=W_x=0$, $|b_y|\le1$, and $d_0(x,y)=1$. The joint law of $(W_z)_{z\in P}$ is log-concave, since log-concavity is preserved by the affine maps.

Since $G(P)<R$, Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:scale} with $M=R$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:variance}
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} W_z^2=\frac{d(z,x)^2}{R^2}
 \le 4\left(1+\frac{\Delta(z,x)}R\right)=4(1+|b_z|).
\end{equation}
Similarly,
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:distance}
 d_0(s,t)=\frac{d(s,t)}R
 \le\max\left\{1,\frac{\Delta(s,t)}R\right\}
 =\max\{1,|b_s-b_t|\}.
\end{equation}
The metric $\ell_0$ has a uniform doubling constant $N_{k,\varepsilon}$ by Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:doubling}. 

For an interval $I$ (with each endpoint either included or excluded), write $|I|$ for its length and put
$$N(w;I)=\frac1R\#\{z:b_z+w_z\in I\}.$$
By Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:scale}, every interval $I$ of length $1$ contains at most $9R$ of the mean heights $b_z$. We show that, for every fixed $T,\zeta,q>0$, for sufficiently large $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:counts}
 \mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{I\subseteq[-T,T]}|N(W;I)-|I||>\zeta\right)<q.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}{Proof of~\eqref{cp:fin:counts}} Conditional on the linear extension $f$, let $f(x)=r$. Exchangeability of the $n+1$ uniform spacings gives, for fixed $u>0$ and sufficiently large $R$,
\[
 \#\{z:0<A_z\le u\}\ \stackrel{\mathrm{law}}=\
 \min\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,uR/(n+1)),\,n-r\}.
\]
Indeed, consecutive spacings to the right of $U_{(r)}$ have the law
of an initial segment of the spacings starting at zero; their partial
sums count uniform sample points, stopped after $n-r$ points.
Since $x\in P_0$ and $R\le m+1$,
\[
 \min\{r-1,n-r\}\ge m^2,\qquad m\ge R-1\ge R/2\quad(R\ge2).
\]
The probability of truncation is at most $uR/m^2\le4u/R$ by
Markov's inequality. The binomial mean differs from $uR$ by
$uR/(n+1)$, and its variance is at most $uR$. Once $u/(n+1)\le\zeta/2$,
Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac1R\#\{z:0<A_z\le u\}-u\right|>\zeta\right)
 \le\frac{4u}{R}+\frac{4u}{\zeta^2R}.
\]
The left side of the anchor is identical, with truncation at $r-1$;
the anchor contributes only $1/R$. Subtraction gives the assertion
for each fixed interval. For uniformity, choose a finite grid in
$[-T,T]$ of mesh at most $\zeta/16$, including both endpoints.
The union bound makes every grid-interval error at most $\zeta/2$
outside an event of probability $q$. Any interval is sandwiched
between grid intervals whose lengths differ from its length by at
most four mesh widths; the inner interval may be empty. Monotonicity
of counts now gives an error at most $\zeta/2+\zeta/4<\zeta$.
Open and closed endpoint conventions are covered by the same
sandwich, including the anchor. This proves~\eqref{cp:fin:counts}.\end{proof}

will find a vertex close to $p$ in each $I_h$, $|h|\le H$.


For $g\in\mathcal G:=\{0\le g\le2,\ \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} g=1\}$, put

\end{equation}

\end{equation}

$0\le g_i\le2$ and $\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} g_i=1$. Centering $W_z-W_p$ gives

 t_i(z)=\tfrac12\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[\operatorname{sgn}(W_{v_i}-W_p)(W_z-W_p)],

 t_i(z)\ge\tfrac12\bigl(c_0d_0(v_i,p)-d_0(z,v_i)\bigr)
 \ge\tfrac12\bigl(c_0(\eta-r_0)-r_0\bigr)\ge r_0,

We prove its existence by counting crossings and then coloring vertices.

\textbf{Stage 3: Weak uniformity of interval counts.} We prove that for every fixed $T,\zeta>0$, for sufficiently large $R$, each $g\in \mathcal G$ and $I\subseteq [-T, T]$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:mean-density}
\left|N(u_g;I)-|I|\right|\le\zeta.
\end{equation}
This includes $N(0;I)$, since the constant weight $g=1$ has $u_1=0$.
The first step is to find a configuration close to $u_g$ where the
random interval counts hold.

Rescale coordinate $z$ by $1+|b_z|$ and use the norm
$$\left\lVertw\right\rVert_*^4=\frac1R\sum_z\frac{|w_z|^4}{(1+|b_z|)^4}.$$

The fourth-moment bound following~\eqref{eq:logconcave-tails}, together
with~\eqref{cp:fin:variance}, gives
$$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|W_z|^4\le24e(\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} W_z^2)^2\le384e(1+|b_z|)^2.$$

For each integer $j\ge0$, at most $18R$ vertices satisfy
$j\le|b_z|<j+1$, counting multiplicities of equal mean heights.
Summing over these bands gives the two bounds needed below:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac1R\sum_z(1+|b_z|)^{-2}
 &\le18\sum_{j\ge0}(j+1)^{-2}
 \le18\left(1+\int_1^\infty t^{-2}\,dt\right)=36,\\
 \frac1R\sum_z(1+|b_z|)^{-4}
 &\le18\sum_{j\ge0}(j+1)^{-4}
 \le18\left(1+\int_1^\infty t^{-4}\,dt\right)=24.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus linearity of expectation gives
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:fourth-moment}
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVertW\right\rVert_*^4\le\frac{384e}{R}\sum_z(1+|b_z|)^{-2}
 \le384e\cdot36=13824e.
\end{equation}
For $T\ge1$, the same band count gives
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:fourth-tail}
 \frac1R\sum_{|b_z|>T}\frac{\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|W_z|^4}{(1+|b_z|)^4}
 \le6912e\sum_{j\ge\lfloor T\rfloor}(j+1)^{-2}
 \le\frac{6912e}{\lfloor T\rfloor}\le\frac{13824e}{T}.
\end{equation}
Here the tail sum is at most $\int_{\lfloor T\rfloor}^\infty t^{-2}\,dt$
and $\lfloor T\rfloor\ge T/2$.

For each $r>0$ and $0<\alpha<1$, we claim that uniformly boundedly many
$\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_*$-balls of radius $r$ cover probability at least
$1-\alpha$; only their centers depend on the poset.

For $T\ge1$, \eqref{cp:fin:distance} places the vertices with $|b_z|\le T$
within $\ell_0$-distance $2T$ of $x$. Doubling gives an
$\ell_0$-net at resolution $\xi$ with at most
$K_1=K_1(T,\xi,k,\varepsilon)$ representatives in this height window. Choose $\rho(z)$ with
$\ell_0(z,\rho(z))\le\xi$.
Define $V_z=W_{\rho(z)}$ on this height window and $V_z=0$ outside it.
Since $W_z-W_{\rho(z)}$ is centered and log-concave,
\[
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|W_z-W_{\rho(z)}|^4
 \le24e\,d_0(z,\rho(z))^4\le24e\,\xi^4.
\]
The second band sum bounds the contribution inside the window;
\eqref{cp:fin:fourth-tail} bounds the contribution outside. Thus
\[
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVertW-V\right\rVert_*^4
 \le576e\,\xi^4+\frac{13824e}{T}.
\]
Set
\[
 \theta=\frac{\alpha r^4}{324},\qquad
 T=1+\frac{27648e}{\theta},\qquad
 \xi=\left(\frac{\theta}{1152e}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
The last expectation is less than $\theta$, so Markov's inequality
gives $\mathbb{P}(\left\lVertW-V\right\rVert_*\ge r/3)<81\theta/r^4=\alpha/4$.
Each representative has variance at most $4(1+T)$ by
\eqref{cp:fin:variance}. Take
$M=\sqrt{8K_1(1+T)/\alpha}$ and $\delta=r/8$.
Chebyshev's inequality and the union bound give probability at most
$4K_1(1+T)/M^2=\alpha/2$ that any representative lies outside $[-M,M]$.
When all representative values lie in $[-M,M]$, round each to a
multiple of $\delta$ with error at most $\delta$, and assign its
rounded value to the same vertices as before, producing $\widetilde V$.
Keep $\widetilde V_z=0$ outside the window. Then
\[
 \left\lVertV-\widetilde V\right\rVert_*^4
 \le \delta^4\,\frac1R\sum_z(1+|b_z|)^{-4}
 \le24(r/8)^4<(r/3)^4.
\]
There are at most $(2\lceil M/\delta\rceil+3)^{K_1}$ rounded vectors.
Except on an event of probability less than $\alpha/4+\alpha/2<\alpha$,
one is within $2r/3$ of $W$, proving the cover claim.

We next show that
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:mean-ball}
\text{for each }r>0\text{ there is }\tau>0\text{ such that }\mathbb{P}(\left\lVertW-u_g\right\rVert_*<r)\ge\tau\text{ for all }g\in\mathcal G.
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-ball}]
Put $B_*=(13824e)^{1/4}$ and
$\alpha=\min\{1/16,r^2/(6400B_*^2)\}$.
The cover gives at most $K=K(r,\alpha,k,\varepsilon)$ closed balls of
radius $r/4$ covering probability at least $1-\alpha$.
Discard balls of probability below $\alpha/K$. Their union
has probability at most $\alpha$, so the retained balls cover
probability at least $1-2\alpha$.

Define
\[
 B=\{w:\left\lVertw\right\rVert_*\le r/2\},\qquad
 \Phi(c)=\mathbb{P}(W\in c+B),\qquad A=\{c:\Phi(c)\ge\alpha/K\}.
\]
If $w$ belongs to a retained ball, that entire ball lies in $w+B$.
Thus $w\in A$ and $\mathbb{P}(W\in A)\ge1-2\alpha$.

Let $f_W$ be the density of $W$ on its affine support $S$. Then
\[
 \Phi(c)=\int_S f_W(w)\mathbf1_B(w-c)\,dw.
\]
The integrand is log-concave jointly in $(w,c)$, because $f_W$ is
log-concave and $\{(w,c):w-c\in B\}$ is convex. Integrating out $w$
preserves log-concavity by \cite[Theorem 5.1]{LV}. Thus $\log\Phi$
is concave wherever $\Phi>0$.

Fix $g\in\mathcal G$. Put $D=\{W\notin A\}$,
$\delta=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[g\mathbf1_D]\le2\mathbb{P}(D)\le4\alpha$, and
$m=1-\delta\ge1/2$, and define
\[
 v=\frac{\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[gW\mathbf1_{D^c}]}m.
\]
Jensen's inequality for $\log\Phi$, under the probability measure
with density $g\mathbf1_{D^c}/m$, gives
\[
 \log\Phi(v)\ge\frac1m\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[g\mathbf1_{D^c}\log\Phi(W)]
 \ge\log(\alpha/K).
\]
Thus $\Phi(v)\ge\alpha/K$.

The fourth-moment bound \eqref{cp:fin:fourth-moment} gives
$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVertW\right\rVert_*^2\le B_*^2$ and $\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVertW\right\rVert_*\le B_*$.
Consequently $\left\lVertv\right\rVert_*\le2B_*/m\le4B_*$.
Since $u_g=mv+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[gW\mathbf1_D]$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\lVertu_g-v\right\rVert_*
 &\le\delta\left\lVertv\right\rVert_*+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[g\left\lVertW\right\rVert_*\mathbf1_D]\\
 &\le16B_*\alpha+
  2(\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVertW\right\rVert_*^2)^{1/2}\mathbb{P}(D)^{1/2}\\
 &\le16B_*\alpha+2B_*\sqrt{2\alpha}
 \le20B_*\sqrt\alpha\le r/4<r/2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here we used $\alpha<1$ and $\alpha\le r^2/(6400B_*^2)$.
Hence $\left\lVertW-v\right\rVert_*\le r/2$ implies $\left\lVertW-u_g\right\rVert_*<r$,
and
\[
 \mathbb{P}(\left\lVertW-u_g\right\rVert_*<r)\ge\Phi(v)\ge\alpha/K.
\]
This proves~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-ball} with $\tau=\alpha/K$.\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-density}] Fix $T,\zeta>0$ and put $B=64+4(T+1)$. For $u>B$,
$2\sqrt{1+u}\le u/3$ and $T+1\le u/4$, so
\[
 u-2\sqrt{1+u}-(T+1)\ge5u/12\ge(1+u)/4.
\]
By~\eqref{cp:fin:weighted-growth}, a vertex with $|b_z|>B$ cannot
have $b_z+u_g(z)\in[-T-1,T+1]$.
If $b_z+w_z$ lies there, $e_z:=w_z-u_g(z)$ must satisfy
$|e_z|\ge(1+|b_z|)/4$. For $0<\delta<1$, the normalized number of
these far vertices, together with those having $|b_z|\le B$ and
$|e_z|>\delta$, is at most
\[
 \left(256+\frac{(1+B)^4}{\delta^4}\right)\left\lVerte\right\rVert_*^4.
\]
Indeed, each far vertex contributes at least $1/(256R)$ to the
fourth power of the norm, and each of the other exceptional vertices
contributes more than $\delta^4/[R(1+B)^4]$.

Set
\[
 \delta=\min\{1/2,\zeta/16\},\qquad
 r=\left(\frac{\zeta}{4(256+(1+B)^4/\delta^4)}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
The exceptional count is then at most $\zeta/4$ whenever $\left\lVerte\right\rVert_*<r$.
Obtain $\tau>0$ from~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-ball}. By~\eqref{cp:fin:counts},
the event that every interval in $[-T-1,T+1]$ has counting error at
most $\zeta/4$ has probability greater than $1-\tau/2$ for large $R$.
For each $g$, it therefore meets $\{\left\lVertW-u_g\right\rVert_*<r\}$, whose
probability is at least $\tau$. Choose $w$ in the intersection.

For $I\subseteq[-T,T]$, enlarge each endpoint by $\delta$ to obtain
$I^+$, and shrink by $\delta$ to obtain $I^-$; use closed outer and
open inner intervals, with $I^-=\varnothing$ if necessary.
Every nonexceptional vertex contributing to these counts has
$|b_z|\le B$ and displacement at most $\delta$. Hence
\[
 N(w;I^-)-\zeta/4\le N(u_g;I)\le N(w;I^+)+\zeta/4.
\]
The interval-length error is at most $2\delta$ on either side
(if $I^-$ is empty, $|I|\le2\delta$). Hence
$|N(u_g;I)-|I||\le2\delta+\zeta/2\le5\zeta/8<\zeta$, proving~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-density}
\end{proof}

\textbf{Stage 4: Uniform boundedness of the weighted means.}
Assign each $z\in P$ a (deterministic) ``starting position'' $x_z$ and
``velocity'' $v_z$, so that its position at time $t$ is $x_z+t v_z$.
Assume $|v_z|\le M$ with $M>0$, fix weights $0\le w_z\le1$,
and let $\tau>0$. Suppose, for some $c\ge0$, that the normalized
weighted counts satisfy

\begin{equation}\label{cp:cross:hypothesis}
 \left|\frac1R\sum_z w_z\mathbf1_{\{x_z+t v_z\in I\}}-c|I|\right|
 \le\varepsilon\qquad(t\in\{0,\tau\})
\end{equation}
for every interval $I$ in some fixed window. Put $E=M(4c\tau M+2\varepsilon)$. We show that, for intervals $I$, $J$ whose $\tau M$-neighborhoods lie within that window, 
\begin{equation}\label{cp:cross:comparison}
 \left|\frac1R\sum_{x_z\in I}w_zv_z
       -\frac{|I|}{|J|}\frac1R\sum_{x_z\in J}w_zv_z\right|
 \le E\left(1+\frac{|I|}{|J|}\right)
       +\frac{2\varepsilon|I|}{\tau}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}[Proof of~\eqref{cp:cross:comparison}]
For each point, count movements during $[0,\tau]$ from at or below
it to above it with weight $w_z/R$, and movements in the reverse
direction with weight $-w_z/R$.
For $a<b$ in the window, the signed crossing count at $b$ minus
that at $a$ is
\[
 \frac1R\sum_z w_z
 \bigl(\mathbf1_{\{x_z\in(a,b]\}}
       -\mathbf1_{\{x_z+\tau v_z\in(a,b]\}}\bigr).
\]
Each of these two interval counts differs from $c(b-a)$ by at most
$\varepsilon$. Thus signed crossing counts at any two points
differ by at most $2\varepsilon$.

For a positive-length interval $I$, partition it at all trajectory
endpoints $x_z,x_z+\tau v_z$ lying inside it. The signed crossing
count is constant on each open piece. Average these counts with
weights equal to the piece lengths divided by $|I|$.
Exchanging the finite sums over pieces and vertices shows that this
average equals the normalized weighted sum of signed trajectory
lengths inside $I$, divided by $|I|$.
To that sum, a trajectory wholly inside contributes exactly
$\tau w_zv_z/R$. Discrepancies from counting the full trajectory of
each vertex initially in $I$ can arise only within $\tau M$ of the
endpoints of $I$. The two endpoint strips have total normalized weight at
most $4c\tau M+2\varepsilon$, and each trajectory has length at most
$\tau M$. 

The average crossing count of $I$ therefore differs from
$\frac{\tau}{R|I|}\sum_{x_z\in I}w_zv_z$ by at most $\tau E/|I|$.
Vertices at endpoints are included in these strips, so either
endpoint convention is allowed. The averages for any two intervals
differ by at most $2\varepsilon$, since all the signed crossing counts do.
For positive-length intervals $I,J$ whose $\tau M$-neighborhoods lie
in that window, the triangle inequality gives a total error
$\tau E/|I|+2\varepsilon+\tau E/|J|$ between the corresponding
velocity sums scaled by $\tau/(R|I|)$ and $\tau/(R|J|)$.
Multiplying by $|I|/\tau$ gives \eqref{cp:cross:comparison}.\end{proof}

Now for every fixed $T>0$, we claim that, for sufficiently large $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{cp:fin:bounded}
 g\in\mathcal G,\ |b_z|\le T \hspace{12pt}\implies\hspace{12pt} |u_g(z)|\le3.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}[Proof of~\eqref{cp:fin:bounded}] Put $T_1=T+3$. We shall apply \eqref{cp:cross:comparison} to $\{z\in P:|b_z|\le 68+4T_1\}$.
By~\eqref{cp:fin:weighted-growth}, discarded vertices cannot
enter $[-T_1,T_1]$ under $b_z+\lambda u_g(z)$, $0\le\lambda\le1$.
On the retained set take 
$$x_z=b_z,\hspace{24pt} v_z=u_g(z),\hspace{24pt} w_z=1,\hspace{24pt} M=2\sqrt{69+4T_1}.$$
The weight $1+\lambda(g-1)$ belongs to
$\mathcal G$, so~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-density} supplies~\eqref{cp:cross:hypothesis} with $c=1$ and any prescribed $\varepsilon>0$.

Write $J_h=(h-1/4,h+1/4]$.
Set $\tau=1/(128M^2)$ and $\varepsilon=\tau/32$.
Since $M\ge1$, we have $\tau\le1$, $\tau M\le1$,
$4M\le1/\tau$, and $\varepsilon\le1/8$.
For $|h|\le T$, the $\tau M$-neighborhoods of $J_h$ and $J_0$ lie
in $[-T_1,T_1]$. Apply~\eqref{cp:cross:comparison} with
$I=J_h$ and $J=J_0$, both of length $1/2$, to obtain
$$\left|\frac1R\sum_{b_z\in J_h}u_g(z)
       -\frac1R\sum_{b_z\in J_0}u_g(z)\right|\le8M^2\tau+(4M+1/\tau)\varepsilon\le\frac1{16}+\frac{2\varepsilon}{\tau}=\frac18.
$$

Since $u_g(x)=0$, \eqref{cp:fin:distance} and
\eqref{cp:fin:weighted-lipschitz} give $|u_g(z)|\le1$ on $J_0$.
Thus the normalized sum over $J_0$ has absolute value at most
$N(0;J_0)\le1/2+\varepsilon$, and the sum over $J_h$ has absolute
value at most $1/2+\varepsilon+1/8$.
For a vertex $z$ with $|b_z|\le T$,
every vertex in $J_{b_z}$ has measurement within $1$ of $u_g(z)$,
again by these two inequalities. Since
$N(0;J_{b_z})\ge1/2-\varepsilon>0$, it follows that

$$|u_g(z)|\le1+\frac{\left|\tfrac{1}{R}\sum_{b_y\in J_{b_z}}u_g(y)\right|}{N(0;J_{b_z})}\le1+\frac{1/2+\varepsilon+1/8}{1/2-\varepsilon}\le1+\frac{3/4}{3/8}=3.$$
This proves~\eqref{cp:fin:bounded}.\end{proof}

\textbf{Stage 5: Use finite coin flips to detect copies.} 

Fix $B>0$ (to be defined below, and put, for $z\in P$ with $|b_z|\le B$ and $1\le i\le q$,
$$p_i(z):=\frac{u_{g_i}(z)+3}{6}$$


For fixed weights $0\le w_z\le1$ and $a\in \mathbb{R}^q$, put
\[
 x_z(a)=b_z+\sum_{i=1}^q a_i p_i(z),\qquad
 N_w(a;I)=\frac1R\sum_z w_z\mathbf1_{\{x_z(a)\in I\}}.
\]
where the second sum runs only over $z$ for which $x_z(a)$ is defined.

Suppose, for $0<A\le1$, $0\le c\le2$, $0<\varepsilon\le1$, and
$L\ge H_1\ge1$, that
\[
 |N_w(a;I)-c|I||\le\varepsilon
 \quad(\|a\|_1\le A,\ I\subseteq[-L-3,L+3]).
\]
Assume also that $N_1(0;I)\le|I|+1$ in this window; this will follow
from the initial unweighted counting estimate.
Take $0<\tau<A$, and for fixed $i$ replace $w_z$ by either
$w'_z=w_zp_i(z)$ or $w'_z=w_z(1-p_i(z))$. By~\eqref{cp:fin:bounded}, for sufficiently large $R$, $p_i(z)\in [0,1]$. 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               

For $\|a\|_1\le A-\tau$, if $e_i$ is the indicator vector for the $i$th coordinate, moving with velocity $p_i$ changes $a$ to
$a+\tau e_i$; moving with velocity $1-p_i$ changes it to
$a-\tau e_i$ and translates all positions by $\tau$.
Both movements satisfy the assumed count bound. The extra margin
of $3$ covers these translations and the endpoint strips. 

Apply~\eqref{cp:cross:comparison} with $M=1$, $J=(-L,L]$, and
$I\subseteq[-H_1,H_1]$. Here $E\le8\tau+2\varepsilon$.
Set $c'=N_{w'}(0;J)/(2L)$. Because $w'\le1$ and $L\ge1$,
$0\le c'\le(2L+1)/(2L)\le2$.
Also $|x_z(a)-b_z|\le\|a\|_1\le1$; changing $a$ to zero can
alter membership in $J$ only in its two endpoint strips of length
$2$. Since $w'\le w$, their total normalized weight is at most
$4c+2\varepsilon$. Hence
$$|N_{w'}(a;I)-c'|I||\le(8\tau+2\varepsilon)\left(1+\frac{H_1}{L}\right)+\frac{4H_1\varepsilon}{\tau}+\frac{H_1}{L}(4c+2\varepsilon)\le16\tau+4\varepsilon+\frac{4H_1\varepsilon}{\tau}+\frac{10H_1}{L}.$$

The last inequality uses $H_1/L\le1$, $c\le2$, and $\varepsilon\le1$.
Thus the new counts are uniform, with a density $c'$ independent of
the movement parameter $a$.

For any fixed number $D\ge1$ of rounds, target window $[-H_D,H_D]$
with $H_D\ge1$, and error $0<\varepsilon_D\le1$, the preceding estimate can
be iterated uniformly over every sequence of color choices.
Working backwards for $j=D,D-1,\ldots,1$, define
\[
 \tau_j=\frac{\varepsilon_j}{64D},\qquad
 L_j=\frac{40H_j}{\varepsilon_j},\qquad
 H_{j-1}=L_j+3,\qquad
 \varepsilon_{j-1}=\frac{\varepsilon_j\tau_j}{16H_j}.
\]
These definitions preserve $H_j\ge1$ and $0<\varepsilon_j\le1$,
so $\tau_j\le1/(64D)\le H_j$. The one-coloring error is at most
$\varepsilon_j$, because
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 16\tau_j=\frac{\varepsilon_j}{4D}\le\frac{\varepsilon_j}{4},\qquad
 \frac{10H_j}{L_j}=\frac{\varepsilon_j}{4},\\
 \left(4+\frac{4H_j}{\tau_j}\right)\varepsilon_{j-1}
 =\frac{\varepsilon_j}{4}\left(1+\frac{\tau_j}{H_j}\right)
 \le\frac{\varepsilon_j}{2}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Start with $w=1$, density $c=1$, and counting
error $\varepsilon_0$ on $[-H_0,H_0]$ for $\|a\|_1\le1$.
After round $j$, the allowed parameter radius is
$1-\sum_{r=1}^j\tau_r\ge1-D/(64D)=63/64$; in particular, every
step has $\tau_j<A$. Each new density lies in $[0,2]$,
as just proved; the required unweighted upper bound holds throughout
because all reference windows lie in the initial window and
$\varepsilon_0\le1$. Induction therefore gives, for every resulting
weight $w$ and some $c_w\in[0,2]$,
\begin{equation}\label{cp:cross:colors}
 |N_w(0;I)-c_w|I||\le\varepsilon_D
 \qquad(I\subseteq[-H_D,H_D]).
\end{equation}

Let $\alpha=s\alpha_0$, set
\[
 \gamma=\frac{\alpha}{8(2s+1)},\qquad
 n_*=\left\lceil\frac{144Q}{\gamma r_0^2}\right\rceil,
\]
and take $D=n_*q$, $H_D=H+1$, and
$\varepsilon_D=\alpha/(8\cdot2^{n_*Q})$ in the finite construction
above.

Let $H_0$ and $\varepsilon_0$ be the resulting initial parameters, and let $B=64+4(H_0+2)$.


For $\|a\|_1\le1$, the weight
$g_a=1+\frac16\sum_i a_i(g_i-1)$ lies in $\mathcal G$, because
$|g_a-1|\le1/6$ and $\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} g_a=1$. Moreover,
$$b_z+\sum_i a_i p_i(z)=b_z+u_{g_a}(z)+\tfrac12\sum_i a_i.$$
The last translation has magnitude at most $1/2$. The cutoff
calculation in the proof of~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-density}, with
$T=H_0+1$, excludes discarded vertices even after this translation.
Thus
\eqref{cp:fin:mean-density}, applied on $[-H_0-1,H_0+1]$ with error
$\varepsilon_0$, supplies all the initial counting hypotheses for
\eqref{cp:cross:colors}.

For each vertex, flip $n_*$ coins of success probability $p_i(z)$ for
each $i$, all $n_*q$ coins independent. Let $\widehat p_i(z)$ be the success frequency
and put $\widehat u_i(z)=6\widehat p_i(z)-3$. The expectation of
$\widehat u_i(z)$ is $u_{g_i}(z)$, and
\[
 \text{Var}(\widehat u_i(z))=36p_i(z)(1-p_i(z))/n_*\le9/n_*.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality and the union bound give
\[
 \mathbb{P}\left(\max_i|\widehat u_i(z)-u_{g_i}(z)|\ge r_0/4\right)
 \le\frac{144q}{n_*r_0^2}\le\gamma.
\]
Accept the outcomes if
$\max_i|\widehat u_i(z)-u_{g_i}(p)|<3r_0/4$.
By~\eqref{cp:fin:separation}, a vertex in $\mathcal C_*$ is accepted
with probability at least $1-\gamma$. A vertex in $|b_z|\le H+1$
with $d_0(z,p)\ge\eta$ is accepted with probability at most $\gamma$:
on the complementary event in the last display, some measured
difference is greater than $r_0-r_0/4=3r_0/4$.

Write $F(h)$ for the expected number of accepted vertices with
$b_z\in I_h$, divided by $R$. The acceptance rule specifies a fixed
subset of the $2^{n_*q}$ outcome sequences, the same for every vertex.
For each sequence, \eqref{cp:cross:colors} compares its expected
counts in $I_h$ and $I_0$ with error at most $2\varepsilon_D$, since
both intervals have length $2s$. Summing gives
\[
 |F(h)-F(0)|\le2^{n_*q}\cdot2\varepsilon_D\le\alpha/4
 \qquad(|h|\le H).
\]
Increase $R$ so that~\eqref{cp:fin:mean-density} also has error at
most $s<1$ on these intervals. Then
$N(0;I_0)\ge s$, so $|\mathcal C_*|/R\ge\alpha_0s=\alpha$,
and $N(0;I_h)\le2s+1$. Since $\gamma\le1/8<1/4$,
\[
 F(h)\ge F(0)-\alpha/4
 \ge(1-\gamma)\alpha-\alpha/4\ge\alpha/2.
\]
If every vertex in $I_h$ had $d_0(z,p)\ge\eta$, the acceptance
bound would instead give
$F(h)\le\gamma N(0;I_h)\le\gamma(2s+1)=\alpha/8$,
a contradiction. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{cp:eq:replicas}
 \text{for every }h\in[-H,H]\text{ there is }z\in P
 \text{ with }|b_z-h|<\eta,\quad\|W_z-W_p\|_2<\eta.
\end{equation}
Indeed $b_z\in I_h$ gives $|b_z-h|\le s<\eta$.
                                                                                                                                           
                                                                                                                              

\textbf{Stage 6: Construct the pseudo insertor.}
Put $H=1+2\sqrt{t+1}$. Choose $\eta>0$ sufficiently small (in terms of $t,\varepsilon$) as specified below. Apply stages 2--5 with these parameters to obtain a vertex $p$ and the copies guaranteed by~\eqref{cp:eq:replicas}. Since $d_0(x,y)=1$, one $v\in\{x,y\}$ satisfies $d_0(v,p)\ge1/2$. Put $V=A_v-W_p$. Then~\eqref{cp:fin:distance} gives
$$
 \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} V=b_v,\qquad |b_v|\le1,\qquad
 1/2\le\operatorname{sd}(V)=d_0(v,p)\le\max\{1,|b_v-b_p|\}\le1+\eta.
$$
Its nondegenerate log-concave law has a continuous distribution function.
By~\eqref{eq:inputs}, its density is bounded by
$\sqrt2/\operatorname{sd}(V)\le2\sqrt2$; put $K=2\sqrt2$.

Now for $1\le i\le t$, choose the quantile $q_i$ with
$\mathbb{P}(V<q_i)=i/(t+1)$. Chebyshev's inequality and \eqref{eq:inputs} give
$$
 |q_i|\le1+(1+\eta)\sqrt{t+1}<H,\qquad
 q_{i+1}-q_i\ge\frac{1}{K(t+1)}\quad(1\le i<t).
$$
Choose $z_i$ from~\eqref{cp:eq:replicas} at height $q_i$. Thus
$$
 A_v=W_p+V,\qquad A_{z_i}=W_p+q_i+e_i,
 \qquad e_i=b_{z_i}-q_i+W_{z_i}-W_p.
$$
$$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}} e_i^2=(b_{z_i}-q_i)^2+\|W_{z_i}-W_p\|_2^2\le2\eta^2$$
The $z_i$'s are distinct if $2\eta<\frac{1}{K(t+1)}$, since
$|b_{z_i}-q_i|<\eta$. Also $z_i\ne v$, since
$d_0(z_i,p)<\eta<1/2\le d_0(v,p)$.

Put $Z=\{v,z_1,\ldots,z_t\}$. Couple its actual order to the ideal
order obtained by inserting $V$
among $q_1<\cdots<q_t$; the latter has law $\mathcal{I}_t$.
If $h=\sqrt\eta<\frac{1}{2K(t+1)}$, the two orders agree whenever
$$
 \max_i|e_i|\le h\qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \min_i|V-q_i|>h.
$$
Indeed, the leaves remain in threshold order and each comparison with $v$ keeps its sign. The union bound, Chebyshev's inequality, and \eqref{eq:inputs} therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:insertion-tv}
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{P;Z},\mathcal{I}_t\bigr)
 \le \sum_{i=1}^t \mathbb{P}(|e_i|>h)+\sum_{i=1}^t \mathbb{P}(|V-q_i|\le h)
 \le\frac{2t\eta^2}{h^2}+2Kth
 =2t\eta+2Kt\sqrt\eta.
\end{equation}
Note that this does not require independence between $V$ and the errors. Hence, for sufficiently small $\eta>0$, we have
$$d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{P_0;Z},\mathcal{I}_t\right)<\varepsilon.$$
This proves the second alternative and completes the proof.

{Proof of~\eqref{cp:fin:counts}} Conditional on the linear extension $f$, let $f(x)=r$. Exchangeability of the $n+1$ uniform spacings gives, for fixed $u>0$ and sufficiently large $R$,
\[
 \#\{z:0<A_z\le u\}\ \stackrel{\mathrm{law}}=\
 \min\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,uR/(n+1)),\,n-r\}.
\]
Indeed, consecutive spacings to the right of $U_{(r)}$ have the law
of an initial segment of the spacings starting at zero; their partial
sums count uniform sample points, stopped after $n-r$ points.
Since $x\in P_0$ and $R\le m+1$,
\[
 \min\{r-1,n-r\}\ge m^2,\qquad m\ge R-1\ge R/2\quad(R\ge2).
\]
The probability of truncation is at most $uR/m^2\le4u/R$ by
Markov's inequality. The binomial mean differs from $uR$ by
$uR/(n+1)$, and its variance is at most $uR$. Once $u/(n+1)\le\zeta/2$,
Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac1R\#\{z:0<A_z\le u\}-u\right|>\zeta\right)
 \le\frac{4u}{R}+\frac{4u}{\zeta^2R}.
\]
The left side of the anchor is identical, with truncation at $r-1$;
the anchor contributes only $1/R$. Subtraction gives the assertion
for each fixed interval. For uniformity, choose a finite grid in
$[-T,T]$ of mesh at most $\zeta/16$, including both endpoints.
The union bound makes every grid-interval error at most $\zeta/2$
outside an event of probability $q$. Any interval is sandwiched
between grid intervals whose lengths differ from its length by at
most four mesh widths; the inner interval may be empty. Monotonicity
of counts now gives an error at most $\zeta/2+\zeta/4<\zeta$.
Open and closed endpoint conventions are covered by the same
sandwich, including the anchor. This proves~\eqref{cp:fin:counts}.

For each point, count movements during $[0,\tau]$ from at or below
