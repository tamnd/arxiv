<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There is a numerical constant $c>0$ such that, for every finite family
$T=\{A_1,\ldots,A_m\}\subset\mathbb R^{d\times n}$ and every $a>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-packing}
 \lVert A_i-A_j\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\ge a\quad(i\ne j)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 S(T)\ge c a\sqrt{\log m}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, with $N$ denoting the usual covering number,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-covering}
 \varepsilon\sqrt{\log N(T,\lVert \cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\varepsilon)}
 \le C S(T)\qquad(\varepsilon>0).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:entropy}
Under \eqref{eq:coisometries},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-entropy-theorem}
 \mathsf H(I\mid J)
 \le C_\delta r^2+
 C_\delta\sum_i\mu_i\lambda_i\log\frac1{\mu_i}.
\end{equation}
If $I$ and $J$ have the same marginal distribution, $p=\log m$, and
$\mathcal E=\sum_i\mu_i\lambda_i$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-entropy}
 \mathsf H(J\mid I)=\mathsf H(I\mid J)
 \le C_\delta r^2+C_\delta p\mathcal E.
\end{equation}
The constants depend only on $\delta$, and not on $n$, $m$, or the
distribution of $(I,J)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:sourcewise-kl}
Let $a_i\in\mathbb R^n$ have norm one, let $\nu$ be a probability distribution,
and put $\bar a=\sum_i\nu_i a_i$ and $v=1-\lVert \bar a\rVert_2^2$. For
$P_i=N(a_i,\mathrm{Id}_n)$ and $P=\sum_i\nu_iP_i$, if $v>\kappa>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourcewise-kl}
 D(P_i\Vert P)\ge\frac{\kappa^2}{16}
 \qquad\text{for every }i\text{ with }\nu_i>0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For every $z\in\mathbb R^n$, Hoeffding's inequality and the Gaussian
moment-generating function imply
\[
 \log\mathbb E_P\exp\langle z,Y-\bar a\rangle\le\lVert z\rVert_2^2.
\]
Indeed, $\langle z,a_i\rangle$ lies in an interval of length $2\lVert z\rVert_2$.
The variational inequality for relative entropy now gives
\[
 D(P_i\Vert P)
 \ge\sup_z\{\langle z,a_i-\bar a\rangle-\lVert z\rVert_2^2\}
 =\frac14\lVert a_i-\bar a\rVert_2^2.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \lVert a_i-\bar a\rVert_2\ge1-\lVert \bar a\rVert_2
 =1-\sqrt{1-v}\ge v/2>\kappa/2.
\]
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The charge in \eqref{eq:local-information-charge} is for the information
actually acquired at the current query. It is not a charge for a whole-label
decoding error. The sourcewise lower bound in
Lemma~\ref{lem:sourcewise-kl} is what permits the fixed weights $\lambda_i$
to remain inside the telescoping sum.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:coisometry-pruning}
For every fixed $\delta>0$, there are $b_\delta>0$ and $C_\delta<\infty$
such that every family satisfying \eqref{eq:coisometries} obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coisometry-pruning}
 \log\widehat P(\{U_i\},b_\delta)
 \ge\tfrac34\log m-C_\delta r^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The assertion is immediate for $m=1$, so assume $p=\log m>0$. Set
\[
 D_{ij}=\tfrac12(U_i-U_j)(U_i-U_j)^T
       =\mathrm{Id}_r-\operatorname{sym}(U_iU_j^T).
\]
For positive semidefinite matrices $W_i$ with $\operatorname{tr} W_i=1$, and a parameter
$\beta>0$, consider the strictly positive matrix
\[
 K(W)_{ij}=\exp(-\beta\operatorname{tr}(W_iD_{ij})).
\]
If $Q=(Q_{ij})$ is a stationary joint distribution, with equal row and
column marginal $\mu$, put
\[
 h(Q)=-\sum_{i,j}Q_{ij}\log(Q_{ij}/\mu_i),\qquad
 \mathcal E(Q)=\sum_i\lVert \sum_jQ_{ij}D_{ij}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
The Perron variational formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perron-variational}
 \log\rho(K(W))=\max_{Q\ \mathrm{stationary}}
 \left\{h(Q)-\beta\sum_{i,j}Q_{ij}\operatorname{tr}(W_iD_{ij})\right\}.
\end{equation}
To verify it, let $v>0$ be a right Perron vector and set
$R_{ij}=K(W)_{ij}v_j/(\rho(K(W))v_i)$. This is a stochastic matrix.
For stationary $Q$, the expression on the right equals
\[
 \log\rho(K(W))-\sum_i\mu_iD(Q_{i\cdot}/\mu_i\Vert R_{i\cdot}).
\]
It is at most $\log\rho(K(W))$, with equality for a stationary Markov
chain having transition matrix $R$.

The function in braces in \eqref{eq:perron-variational} is concave and
continuous in $Q$ and affine in $W$ on compact convex sets. The finite
dimensional minimax theorem \cite{Sion} therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perron-minimax}
 \min_W\log\rho(K(W))=\max_Q\{h(Q)-\beta\mathcal E(Q)\}.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy} supplies constants $A_\delta\ge1,B_\delta$ with
$h(Q)\le A_\delta p\mathcal E(Q)+B_\delta r^2$. Choose
$\beta=A_\delta p$ and a minimizing $W$. Then
$\rho(K(W))\le\exp(B_\delta r^2)$.

Normalize a positive right Perron vector $\pi$ to have sum one, put
$b_\delta=(4A_\delta)^{-1}$, and define
\[
 C_i=\{j:\operatorname{tr}(W_iD_{ij})<b_\delta\}.
\]
Each $C_i$ contains $i$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap-mass}
 \pi(C_i)\le e^{\beta b_\delta}(K(W)\pi)_i
 =e^{\beta b_\delta}\rho(K(W))\pi_i.
\end{equation}
Take independent exponential random variables with rates $\pi_i$.
Retain $i$ when its clock is the smallest among the clocks indexed by $C_i$.
The expected number retained is
\[
 \sum_i\frac{\pi_i}{\pi(C_i)}
 \ge m e^{-\beta b_\delta}/\rho(K(W))
 \ge\exp(3p/4-B_\delta r^2).
\]
Choose a realization with at least this many retained indices and order
them by increasing clock values. For an earlier retained $j$ and a later
retained $i$, one has $j\notin C_i$. The matrices $Z_i=W_iU_i$ satisfy
\[
 \lVert Z_i\rVert_*=\operatorname{tr} W_i=1,
 \qquad
 \langle Z_i,U_i-U_j\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(W_iD_{ij})\ge b_\delta.
\]
They certify the required convex separation.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:bounded-pruning}
Fix $L\ge1$. If $E\subset\mathbb R^{r\times n}$ is a finite $a$-separated
family and $\sup_{A\in E}\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le La$, then, for
$K=\operatorname{conv}(E\cup-E)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-pruning}
 \log|E|\le C_Lr^2+C_L\log\widehat P(K,c_La).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Divide the matrices by $La$, so that they are contractions separated by
$1/L$. For each resulting matrix $A$, form
\[
 U_A=[\,A\ \ F_A\,],\qquad F_A=(\mathrm{Id}_r-AA^T)^{1/2}.
\]
These are coisometries and retain the separation. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:coisometry-pruning}, they contain a convexly
$b_{1/L}$-separated sequence of length at least
$\exp(\tfrac34\log|E|-C_Lr^2)$.

The completion matrices $F_A$ lie in the operator unit ball of the
$d=r(r+1)/2$ dimensional space of symmetric matrices. A volumetric cover
by balls of radius $b_{1/L}/4$ has at most
$(1+8/b_{1/L})^d$ members. Retain the largest corresponding subsequence.
Its completion matrices have diameter at most $b_{1/L}/2$.
For any convex combination of preceding points, the completion-coordinate
difference consequently has norm at most $b_{1/L}/2$. Since
\[
 \lVert [\,A\ \ F\,]\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}+\lVert F\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
\]
the original-coordinate sequence is convexly $b_{1/L}/2$-separated.
The entropy lost in selecting the bin is at most $C_Lr^2$.
Rescaling proves \eqref{eq:bounded-pruning}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Only convex separation is transferred through the coisometry lift.
No comparison between the bilinear widths before and after that nonlinear
lift is used. The fixed value of $L$ is essential for the uniform constants
in Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded-pruning}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:cross}
Let $V\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ satisfy $\lVert V\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\le1$, and let $G$ be
an $r\times k$ standard Gaussian matrix. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-bound}
 \mathbb E\alpha(GV^T)\le\sqrt3 W.
\end{equation}
Similarly, if $\lVert U\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\le1$ and $H$ is an $n\times k$ standard
Gaussian matrix, then $\mathbb E\alpha(UH^T)\le\sqrt3 W$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Assume $V\ne0$, let $h\sim N(0,\mathrm{Id}_n)$ be independent of $G$, and set
$z=V^Th/\lVert V^Th\rVert_2$. Conditional on $h$, $Gz$ is a standard Gaussian
vector, so it is independent of $h$. With $D=\mathbb E(zh^T)$, Jensen's inequality
therefore gives $\mathbb E\alpha(GD)\le W$.

In singular coordinates for $V$, with nonzero singular values $\sigma_j$,
the corresponding singular values of $D$ are
\[
 d_j=\sigma_j\mathbb E\frac{h_j^2}
                    {(\sum_\ell\sigma_\ell^2h_\ell^2)^{1/2}}.
\]
Under the probability measure with density $h_j^2$, Jensen's inequality
and Cauchy--Schwarz show that
\[
 \mathbb E\frac{h_j^2}{(\sum_\ell\sigma_\ell^2h_\ell^2)^{1/2}}
 \ge\frac1{(\sum_\ell\sigma_\ell^2+2\sigma_j^2)^{1/2}}
 \ge\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\]
Thus $VV^T\preceq3D^TD$. Gaussian covariance domination, realized by
adding an independent centered Gaussian matrix, implies
$\mathbb E\alpha(GV^T)\le\sqrt3\mathbb E\alpha(GD)\le\sqrt3W$.
The second assertion follows by transposition.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:dual-cover}
For every $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-cover}
 \log N\left(B_*,CW(t^{-1/2}+rt^{-1})K^\circ\right)\le2t+\log4.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Factor any $X\in B_*$ as $X=UV^T$, padding
with zero columns to use exactly $r$ columns, so that
$\lVert U\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}},\lVert V\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\le1$. Let $G\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}$ and
$H\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}$ be independent standard Gaussian matrices.
Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} and the triangle inequality give
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E\alpha(\sigma^2GH^T+\sigma GV^T+\sigma UH^T)
 &\le \sigma^2 rW+2\sqrt3\sigma W\\
 &\le C W(\sigma+r\sigma^2).
\end{align*}
Let $u=C_0W(\sigma+r\sigma^2)$ with $C_0$ large enough. The event that
this seminorm is at most $u$ has Gaussian probability at least $1/2$.
Translate $(G,H)$ by $(U/\sigma,V/\sigma)$. Its image lies in
\[
 \{(G',H'):\alpha(\sigma^2G'H'^T-X)\le u\}.
\]
For standard Gaussian measure $\gamma$ and any measurable set $A$,
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the Gaussian likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \gamma(A+z)\ge\gamma(A)^2e^{-\lVert z\rVert_2^2}.
\]
The translation just used has squared norm at most $2/\sigma^2$.
Consequently, if $\nu_\sigma$ is the law of $\sigma^2GH^T$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-small-ball}
 \nu_\sigma(X+uK^\circ)\ge\tfrac14e^{-2/\sigma^2}
 \qquad(X\in B_*).
\end{equation}
A set of points of $B_*$ at pairwise $\alpha$-distance greater than $2u$
has disjoint $u$-balls, and so has cardinality at most $4e^{2/\sigma^2}$.
A maximal such set covers $B_*$ by $2u$-balls. Put $\sigma=t^{-1/2}$
and absorb the numerical factors into $C$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:convex-upper}
For every $a>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-upper}
 \log\widehat P(K,a)
 \le C\left[1+(W/a)^2+rW/a\right].
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Suppose $A_1,\ldots,A_N\in K$ are convexly $a$-separated. By
Hahn--Banach, there are $Y_j\in B_*$ such that
\[
 \langle Y_j,A_j-A_i\rangle\ge a\qquad(i<j).
\]
For $j=1$ choose any $Y_1\in B_*$. Use Lemma~\ref{lem:dual-cover} with
\[
 t=C_0[1+(W/a)^2+rW/a]
\]
to cover $B_*$ by at most $\exp(Ct)$ balls of $\alpha$-radius $a/8$.
If $Y_i,Y_j$ lie in the same such ball and $i<j$, then
\[
 \langle Y_j,A_j\rangle-\langle Y_i,A_i\rangle
 =\langle Y_j,A_j-A_i\rangle+\langle Y_j-Y_i,A_i\rangle\ge3a/4.
\]
Write $D=\sup_{A\in K}\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$. All the numbers
$\langle Y_i,A_i\rangle$ lie in $[-D,D]$, so each ball contains at most
$1+8D/(3a)$ of the witnesses. By \eqref{eq:one-matrix-lower},
$D\le(\pi/2)W$. It follows that
\[
 \log N\le Ct+\log(1+CW/a)\le C[1+(W/a)^2+rW/a].
\]

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:bounded-row}
Fix $L\ge1$. If $E\subset\mathbb R^{r\times n}$ is $a$-separated and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-row}
 \log|E|\le C_L\left[r^2+1+(\mathcal W(E)/a)^2+r\mathcal W(E)/a\right].
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-row-sqrt}
 \sqrt{\log|E|}\le C_L(1+r+\mathcal W(E)/a).
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:compression}
Let $A_1,\ldots,A_m\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$ be $a$-separated and satisfy
\[
 \sup_i\lVert A_i\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le La,
 \qquad \sup_i\lVert A_i\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\le H,
 \qquad L\ge1.
\]
There is a family $F\subset\mathbb R^{k\times N}$, for some $N$, such that
\begin{align}
 k&\le C(1+H^2/a^2),\label{eq:compression-dim}\\
 \log|F|&\ge\tfrac12\log(m/2),\label{eq:compression-card}\\
 \lVert B-B'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\ge ca\quad(B\ne B'\in F),\label{eq:compression-sep}\\
 \sup_{B\in F}\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\le CLa,
 \qquad \mathcal W(F)\le C\mathcal W(\{A_i\}).\label{eq:compression-radius-width}
\end{align}
In particular, the dimension $k$ is a common row dimension.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Choose a small numerical $\eta>0$, then a sufficiently small numerical
$\tau/a>0$, and finally a sufficiently large numerical constant in
\[
 k=\left\lceil C_0(1+H^2/a^2)\right\rceil.
\]
Split each matrix at singular-value threshold $\tau$ as $A_i=B_i+E_i$.
Then
\[
 \operatorname{rank} B_i\le H^2/\tau^2,
 \quad\lVert E_i\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\tau,
 \quad\lVert E_i\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\le H,
 \quad\lVert B_i\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le La.
\]
Let $P\in\mathbb R^{k\times d}$ and $Q\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ be independent,
with entries $N(0,1/k)$.

Call an index good if $P$ embeds the range of $B_i$, and $Q^T$ embeds its
row space, with the bounds \eqref{eq:gaussian-subspace}, and if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-good}
 \max\{\lVert PE_i\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\lVert E_iQ\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
                   \lVert PE_iQ\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\}\le\eta a.
\end{equation}
For each individual index this event has probability at least $0.99$.
To see the tail assertion, \eqref{eq:gaussian-product-norm} gives
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert PE_i\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\ \mathbb E\lVert E_iQ\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\tau+H/\sqrt{k}.
\]
Conditioning on $Q$ and using
$\mathbb E\lVert E_iQ\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\lVert E_i\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}$ also gives
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert PE_iQ\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\tau+2H/\sqrt{k}.
\]
Choose the constants so that Markov's inequality bounds the total tail
failure probability by $0.005$. Equation~\eqref{eq:gaussian-subspace}
controls the two remaining failures. All these choices are numerical.

Define the common linear map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
 \Phi(A)=\begin{pmatrix}PAQ&PA\\AQ&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For a good index, the embedding upper bounds and
\eqref{eq:tail-good} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-radius}
 \lVert \Phi(A_i)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le8La+3\eta a\le CLa.
\end{equation}

For good indices $i,j$, choose a left inverse $L_i$ of $P$ on the range
of $B_i$, and a right inverse $R_j$ of $Q$ on the row space of $B_j$.
They have norms at most two and satisfy $L_iPB_i=B_i$ and $B_jQR_j=B_j$.
For $D=B_i-B_j$, the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compression-identity}
 D=L_iPD+DQ R_j-L_iPDQ R_j
\end{equation}
gives
\[
 \lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\lVert PD\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}+2\lVert DQ\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
                                      +4\lVert PDQ\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
On the other hand $\lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\ge a-2\tau$. The tail bound for each
of $i,j$ shows, on choosing $\eta$ sufficiently small, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-separation}
 \lVert \Phi(A_i)-\Phi(A_j)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\ge c_0a.
\end{equation}
This is a simultaneous conclusion for every pair of good indices; it
does not require a union bound over pairs.

Let $T=\{A_i\}$. Gaussian conditioning gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\mathcal W(PT)\le\mathcal W(T),\qquad
 \mathbb E_Q\mathcal W(TQ)\le\mathcal W(T),\qquad
 \mathbb E_{P,Q}\mathcal W(PTQ)\le\mathcal W(T).
\]
For example, conditional on $g\in\mathbb R^k$, the vector $P^Tg$ has law
$(\lVert g\rVert_2/\sqrt{k})g'$ with $g'$ standard; use
$\mathbb E\lVert g\rVert_2/\sqrt{k}\le1$. The three blocks in \eqref{eq:Phi} thus give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-width}
 \mathbb E_{P,Q}\mathcal W(\Phi(T))\le3\mathcal W(T).
\end{equation}
The expected number of bad indices is at most $0.01m$. By Markov's
inequality, there is a choice of $P,Q$ with at least $m/2$ good indices
and $\mathcal W(\Phi(T))\le12\mathcal W(T)$.

For this choice write, over good indices,
\[
 X_i=[\,PA_iQ\ \ PA_i\,],\qquad Y_i=(A_iQ)^T.
\]
These both have $k$ rows, and
\[
 \lVert \Phi(A_i)-\Phi(A_j)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\lVert X_i-X_j\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}+\lVert Y_i-Y_j\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Take maximal $t$-separated sets in the two coordinate families, where
$t=c_0a/8$. Their $t$-balls cover the respective families. Two distinct
indices cannot be assigned to the same pair of balls, by
\eqref{eq:Phi-separation}. Therefore
\[
 m/2\leP(\{X_i\},t)P(\{Y_i\},t).
\]
Select a packing of cardinality at least $\sqrt{m/2}$ in one coordinate
family. Taking a block, and transposing when needed, contracts $\mathcal W$.
Equations \eqref{eq:Phi-radius} and \eqref{eq:Phi-width} prove all the
assertions.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:dyadic}
Suppose $T$ is an $a$-separated finite family with $p=\log|T|>0$.
There are $s\ge a$, $q>0$, and an $s$-separated subfamily $T_0\subset T$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-conclusion}
 s\sqrt q\ge a\sqrt p,\qquad
 \log|T_0|\ge3q/4,\qquad
 \operatorname{diam}_{\mathrm{op}}(T_0)\le4s.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Set $q_j=\logP(T,2^ja)$ for $j\ge0$ and choose $j$ maximizing
$4^jq_j$. The maximum exists since $q_j=0$ for all sufficiently large
$j$, and it is positive since $q_0=p$. Set $s=2^ja$, $q=q_j$.
Then $q_{j+1}\le q/4$ and $s^2q\ge a^2p$.

A maximal $2s$-separated subset covers $T$ by at most $e^{q/4}$ operator
balls of radius $2s$. An $s$-packing with $e^q$ points has at least
$e^{3q/4}$ points in one such ball. Their diameter is at most $4s$.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The assertion is trivial for $m=1$. Put $M=S(T)$ and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:dyadic}, obtaining $s,q,T_0$. Since $q>0$, the original
family contains two points separated by at least $s$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x-lower}
 M\ge s/\pi.
\end{equation}
If $q\le16$, \eqref{eq:dyadic-conclusion} and \eqref{eq:x-lower} already
prove the desired result. Hence assume $q>16$.

Choose $u=C_0M/q^{1/4}$, with $C_0$ large enough that
\eqref{eq:known-HS}, applied to $T_0$, supplies a Hilbert--Schmidt cover
with at most $e^{q/8}$ balls of radius $u$. One ball contains at least
$e^{5q/8}$ points. Translate those points by one of them and call the
resulting family $T_1$. Then
\begin{align}
 \log|T_1|&\ge5q/8,
 &\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\ge s\quad(A\ne B),\notag\\
 \sup_{A\in T_1}\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\le4s,
 &\sup_{A\in T_1}\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}&\le CM/q^{1/4}.
 \label{eq:localized-family}
\end{align}
Translation preserves $S$, and $0\in T_1$, so $\mathcal W(T_1)\le2M$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:compression} to $T_1$. We obtain a family
$F\subset\mathbb R^{r\times N}$ with
\begin{align}
 \log|F|&\ge q/4,\label{eq:final-family-card}\\
 \lVert B-B'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\ge cs\quad(B\ne B'),
 &\sup_{B\in F}\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\le Cs,\notag\\
 \mathcal W(F)&\le CM,
 &r&\le C\left(1+\frac{M^2}{s^2\sqrt q}\right).
 \label{eq:final-family-bounds}
\end{align}
The constants in the radius-to-separation ratio are numerical, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded-row} applies with a fixed numerical $L$.
Write $x=M/s$ and $y=\sqrt q$. Equations
\eqref{eq:final-family-card}--\eqref{eq:final-family-bounds} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closing-quadratic}
 y\le C(1+r+x)\le C(1+x+x^2/y).
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $y$ and the quadratic formula imply
$y\le C'(1+x)$. Since $x\ge1/\pi$ by \eqref{eq:x-lower}, it follows
that $s\sqrt q\le CM$. Finally,
\[
 a\sqrt{\log|T|}\le s\sqrt q\le CS(T).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:main-packing}. A maximal
$\varepsilon$-separated subset is an $\varepsilon$-cover, so applying
the packing assertion to that subset proves \eqref{eq:main-covering}.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The dyadic selection need not retain a fixed fraction of the original
entropy. It retains the quantity $s\sqrt q$, which is the numerator of
the desired Sudakov ratio. The Hilbert--Schmidt radius and the common row
dimension are then recomputed using this new $q$. This gives simultaneously
a fixed radius-to-separation ratio and
$r\lesssim1+(M/s)^2/\sqrt q$. Keeping a rank bound based on the old entropy

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:projections}
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ be orthogonal projections of the same rank, satisfying
$\lVert P_i-P_j\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\ge a$ for $i\ne j$. For a standard Gaussian vector
$z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-oscillation}
 \mathbb E\left[\max_i z^TP_i z-\min_i z^TP_i z\right]
 \ge c a\sqrt{\log m}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $g,h$ be independent standard Gaussian vectors and put
$u=(g+h)/\sqrt2$, $v=(g-h)/\sqrt2$. These are independent standard
Gaussian vectors, and symmetry of the projections gives
\[
 g^TP_i h=\tfrac12(u^TP_i u-v^TP_i v).
\]
Taking maxima and expectations shows that twice the bilinear supremum is
at most the oscillation in \eqref{eq:projection-oscillation}.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The theorem also applies to a finite family of Hilbert--Schmidt operators
between separable real Hilbert spaces. Compress onto increasing finite
dimensional subspaces. Hilbert--Schmidt convergence implies operator-norm
convergence and $L^2$ convergence of each associated chaos. For a finite
family the expected supremum therefore converges. Passing to the limit
proves the same inequality. Arbitrary index sets are treated through their
finite separated subfamilies whenever the supremum is well defined.
