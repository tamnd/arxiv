We assume $\mathfrak{g}$ is a simple Lie algebra and   $H$-admissibility of $(L_\cdot^\tau , V_\tau^G))$. Then, the isotopic component $(V_\tau^G)_{K-fin}[(V_\sigma^H)_{L-fin}][Z]$, determined by the lowest $L$-types  of the representation of $(\mathfrak{h},L)$  in the  isotypic component $(V_\tau^G)_{K-fin}[(V_\sigma^H)_{L-fin}]$   is contained in a homogeneous subspace $\mathcal V^{(k)} =L_{ S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^- )}^\tau ( W) .$

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The Lemma follows after we show that for $k \not= t$,  $S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$   and  $S^t(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ have no irreducible $L$-factor in common. This happens, owing to the subspace of $K$-finite vectors in $V_\tau^G$, $(V_\tau^G)_{K-fin}$, is $K$-isomorphic to $S(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W=\oplus_{k\geq 0} \, S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$. A particular  $K$-isomorphism is $L_D^\tau (D\otimes w)\leftarrow D\otimes W \in S(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ and in \ref{sub:vn} we have defined  $\mathcal V^{(k)}= L_{S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)}^\tau(W)$. The action of $K$ in $S(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ is 
 the one that is obtained via the Adjoint representation of $K$ in $\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-$ tensor with the action $\tau$ of $K$ in $W$. Now, since $G/K$ and $\mathfrak{g}$ is a simple Lie algebra the representation $K$ in $\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^+$ is irreducible and the center $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k}$ of ${\mathfrak k}$ is one dimensional. Shur's lemma gives  $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k}$ acts by one dimensional representation either on $\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-$ or $W$, they are respectively: $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k} \ni Y \mapsto \beta (Y), Y \mapsto \chi_\tau (Y)$. Thus, $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k} $ acts in $S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ by the representation $Y \mapsto k\beta (Y)+ \chi_\tau (Y)$. Whence, we conclude: the representations of $K$ in  $S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ and  $S^t(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ for $t\not= k$ are {\it not   equivalent} due that respective representations of $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k}$ are not equivalent. 
 
 To follow we consider a holomorphic immersion $H/L \rightarrow G/K$ and a holomorphic representation $V_\tau^G$ of $G$ so that its restriction to $H$ is admissible. We claim: {\it for each $L$-isotypic component $V_\tau^G [V_\sigma^H][Z]$, there exists $k$ so that $V_\tau^G [V_\sigma^H][Z]\subseteq S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$}. Indeed, the hypothesis holomorphic immersion, $\mathfrak{g}$ is a simple Lie algebra and $H$-admissibility of $res_H(V_\tau^G) $ implies $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k} \subset \mathfrak l $. For a proof see \cite[Table 2.1.3]{Kob9}. Since we are considering the $L$-isotypic component $V_\tau^G [V_\sigma^H][Z]$ we have that center of $\mathfrak l$, $\mathfrak z_\mathfrak l$, acts by  a {\it fixed} one dimensional representation   on $V_\tau^G [V_\sigma^H][Z]$. Since, $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k} \subseteq \mathfrak z_\mathfrak l$, the previous observations on the action of $\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k}$ on $S^\cdot(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ yields $V_\tau^G [V_\sigma^H][Z]\subseteq S^k(\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{g}^-)\otimes W$ for some $k$.

\begin{equation*} \begin{split} L_X^\tau(Y_1Y_2Y_3\otimes w)& =[XY_1Y_2Y_3 \otimes w] \\ & = [Y_1XY_2Y_3 + [X,Y_1]Y_2Y_3 \otimes w ]

+[[X,Y_1],Y_2]]Y_3 \otimes w ] 
 \\ & = [ Y_1Y_2Y_3X\otimes w + Y_1Y_2[X,Y_3]\otimes w   \\ & \quad +Y_1Y_3[X,Y_2]\otimes w+Y_1 [[X,Y_2],Y_3]] \otimes w ]

 \\ & \quad \quad \quad + Y_3 [[X,Y_1],Y_2]] \otimes w + [[[X,Y_1],Y_2],Y_3 ]\otimes w] 
 \\ & = [ Y_1Y_2Y_3\otimes \tau(X) w + Y_1Y_2 \otimes \tau([X,Y_3]) w   \\ & \quad +Y_1Y_3\otimes \tau([X,Y_2]) w+ Y_2Y_3 \otimes \tau([X,Y_1])  w ]

\end{equation*}

 \end{split} 
\end{equation*}

\end{equation*}

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The  proof closely  follows the proof of Proposition~\ref{sub:7}. To begin with, we verify $[\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{h}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}_0}^-]=\mathfrak q_{\mathbb C} \cap {\mathfrak k}_{\mathbb C}$. \\ For this,  we fix generators $(Y_j, Y_j),  (R_j, -R_j) \,  j=1,\dots $ for $\mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}}^+ (resp. \,\mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}_0}^-) $. Then, $[\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{h}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}_0}^-] =linspan_{\mathbb C} \{ ([Y_j, R_s], -[Y_j, R_s]) \} $ and $Y_j \in  \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^+, R_j \in \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^-$. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
  Now, $\mathfrak q_{\mathbb C} \cap {\mathfrak k}_{\mathbb C} =\{ (X,-X): X\in ({\mathfrak k}_0)_{\mathbb C} \}$. Obviously, $[\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{h}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}_0}^-]\subset \mathfrak q_{\mathbb C} \cap {\mathfrak k}_{\mathbb C}$. For the other inclusion, we recall the equality $({\mathfrak k}_0)_{\mathbb C} =[(\mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0})_{\mathbb C}, (\mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0})_{\mathbb C}]=[\mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^-] +[\mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^-, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^+]= [\mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{g}_0}^-] $. 
Thus, for $X\in ({\mathfrak k}_0)_{\mathbb C} $,  we have 
 
  $(X,-X)=(\sum_{j,r} c_{j,s} [Y_j,R_s], - \sum_{j,r}  c_{j,s} [Y_j,R_s] )\in [\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{h}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}_0}^-] $. 
 
  In particular, $(\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k})_{\mathbb C}  \cap \mathfrak q_{\mathbb C} \subseteq   [\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{h}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}_0}^-]$ (for $\mathfrak{g}_0=\mathfrak{su}(1,1)$ they are equal).  We notice,  the ideal in ${\mathfrak k}_{\mathbb C}$ spanned by $\mathfrak q_{\mathbb C} \cap {\mathfrak k}_{\mathbb C}$ is equal to $({\mathfrak k}_{ss})_{\mathbb C} +(\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k})_{\mathbb C}  \cap \mathfrak q_{\mathbb C} $. Next, as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{sub:7} if the equality $\mathcal V^{(1)} \cap \mathcal U(\mathfrak{h}_0)W =\mathcal V^{(1)} \cap \mathcal L_{W,H}^c$ holds, then $Ker(\tau)$ contains  $[\mathfrak p_\mathfrak{h}^+, \mathfrak p_{\mathfrak{h}_0}^-] $, hence $Ker(\tau)$  contains ${\mathfrak k}_{ss}$ and $(\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak k})_{\mathbb C}  \cap \mathfrak q_{\mathbb C}$, the irreducibility of $\tau$ forces $\tau$  is a one dimensional representation. The converse statement repeats the proof of Proposition~\ref{sub:7}.
