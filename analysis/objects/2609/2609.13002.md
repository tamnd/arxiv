<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Given $\mathcal{H}$, finding a subhypergraph $\mathcal{C}\subseteq \mathcal{H}$ that witnesses $g(r)\leq 2r-3$ for $\mathcal{H}$ corresponds to finding a vector of weights $x \in \{0, 1\}^{E(\mathcal{H})}$ such that for every vertex $v\in V(\mathcal{H})$ we have $\sum_{e\ni v} x_e\in \{k, \dots, k+2r-4\}$ (where $x_e=1$ means that the edge $e$ is included in the cover $\mathcal{C}$ and $x_e=0$ means that it is not). Note that we may assume that $d\geq k+2r-3$, as otherwise taking $\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{H}$ already satisfies the needed conditions.

We find these values using the following algorithm. We start by setting $x_e=\frac{k+r-2}{d}$ for each $e\in E(\mathcal{H})$ and we will vary these variables until we arrive at a $\{0, 1\}$-valued vector satisfying $\sum_{e\ni v} x_e\in \{k, \dots, k+2r-4\}$ for each $v\in V(\mathcal{H})$.

The algorithm maintains a partition of the edge set into \textit{active} and \textit{fixed} edges, $E(\mathcal{H})=E_a\cup E_f$, and a partition of the vertex set into \textit{safe} and \textit{unsafe} vertices, $V(\mathcal{H})=V_s\cup V_u$.

The active edges will be the ones having $0<x_e<1$, and these values will be changed throughout the rest of the algorithm. On the other hand, when a variable $x_e$ reaches value $0$ or $1$, it becomes fixed and remains fixed for the rest of the process. If a vertex $v$ has at most $r-1$ active incident edges, then we declare $v$ to be \textit{safe} until the end of the algorithm.

Throughout the algorithm, we maintain the following set of constraints.
\[\sum_{e\ni v} x_e=k+r-2\text{ for every }v\in V_u\text{ and }0\leq x_e\leq 1\text{ for every }e\in E(\mathcal{H}).\]

In a general step of the algorithm, as long as the number of active edges exceeds the number of vertex constraints, i.e. as long as $|E_a|>|V_u|$, we do the following. We find a solution to the system of equations \[\sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\in E_a}} y_e=0\text{ for every }v\in V_u.\]
If $|E_a|>|V_u|$, this system is underdetermined and so there exists a nontrivial solution $(y_e)_{e\in E_a}$ to this system. We then take this solution, define $y_e=0$ for all fixed edges and update the variables $x_e\mapsto x_e+\lambda y_e$ for some real number $\lambda$. Observe that the new values of $x_e$ still satisfy $\sum_{e\ni v} x_e=k+r-2$ for every $v\in V_u$. Moreover, to choose the value of $\lambda$, we start at $\lambda=0$ and increase it until one (or more) of the variables $x_e$ becomes $0$ or $1$. At this point, we fix $x_e$, we check if there is any vertex $v$ which becomes safe, and proceed to the next step of the algorithm. The algorithm terminates when $|E_a|=|V_u|$.

Let us make a couple of observations about this algorithm. First of all, we observe that if a vertex $v$ is declared to be safe, then no matter whether the remaining active edges incident to $v$ are included in the cover or not, $v$ will have degree between $k$ and $k+2r-4$ in the cover. To see this, we argue based on how many edges incident to $v$ have been fixed with $x_e=1$, at the moment when $v$ is declared to be safe. If all edges incident to $v$ are fixed when $v$ is declared safe, then $v$ will surely have degree $k+r-2$ in the final subhypergraph. So, suppose that this is not the case.

Since at least one and at most $r-1$ active edges are incident to $v$, we have $0<\sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\in E_a}} x_e<r-1$. Moreover, this quantity is an integer, and thus $\sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\in E_f}}x_e=k+r-2-\sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\in E_a}} x_e\in \{k, \dots, k+r-3\}$. In other words, between $k$ and $k+r-3$ fixed edges containing $v$ have $x_e=1$. Thus, no matter what happens with the remaining $r-1$ edges, $v$ will have degree between $k$ and $k+2r-4$ in the resulting subhypergraph.

Let $\mathcal{H}'$ be the hypergraph of rank at most $r$ on the vertex set $V_u$, with edges given by $e\cap V_u$ for $e\in E_a$. Let us show that when the algorithm terminates with $|E_a|=|V_u|$, the hypergraph $\mathcal{H}'$ is $r$-regular and $r$-uniform. Note that every vertex of $V_u$ is incident to at least $r$ edges of $E_a$, so the average degree is at least $r$. On the other hand, the average degree of $\mathcal{H}'$ can be calculated as follows:
\[\bar{d}(\mathcal{H}')=\frac{1}{|V_u|}\sum_{e\in E_a}|e\cap V_u|\leq \frac{r|E_a|}{|V_u|}\leq r.\]
Hence, we have the equality all the way through, meaning that each edge of $E_a$ is completely contained in $V_u$ and that $\mathcal{H}'$ is an $r$-regular, $r$-uniform hypergraph.

So, when the algorithm terminates, we have obtained a partial subhypergraph $\mathcal{C}_0$ consisting of those fixed edges having $x_e=1$. Let $f_v=\deg_{\mathcal{C}_0} v$ denote the degree already assigned to the vertex $v$. It can be computed from the equation $f_v+\sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\in E_a}}x_e=k+r-2$. 

To complete the proof, we need to decide which of the remaining active edges we add to the set $\mathcal{C}_0$ to form the final hypergraph $\mathcal{C}$ having the property that $\deg_{\mathcal{C}}v\in \{k, \dots, k+2r-4\}$. We do that as follows: if an edge has $x_e\geq 1/2$ when the above algorithm terminates, we include it in the final hypergraph $\mathcal{C}$, and otherwise we leave it out.

The number of additional edges incident to $v$ differs from $\sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\in E_a}}x_e$ by at most $r/2$, since the rounding of every edge contributes at most $1/2$ to the difference. Hence, the degree of $v$ in the final hypergraph $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies
\[|\deg_\mathcal{C} v-(k+r-2)|\leq \Big|\deg_\mathcal{C} v-f_v-\sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\in E_a}}x_e\Big|\leq r/2< r-1.\]
Since $\deg_\mathcal{C} v$ is an integer, we conclude $\deg_{\mathcal{C}} v \in \{k, \dots, k+2r-4\}$, thus completing the proof.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:low_uniformity}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be an $r$-regular hypergraph, with all edges having size between $k$ and $3k$, for some $1\leq k\leq r$. Then there exists a set $S\subseteq V(\mathcal{F})$ such that $1\leq |e\cap S|\leq 100\log r$ for every $e\in E(\mathcal{F})$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $S$ be a random set of vertices, obtained by including every $v\in V(\mathcal{F})$ independently with probability $\frac{10\log r}{k}$ (we may assume that $k\geq 10\log r$, as otherwise one may take $S=V(\mathcal{F})$). We aim to show that with positive probability one has $1\leq |e\cap S|\leq 100\log r$ for every $e\in E(\mathcal{F})$.

Our main goal will be to show that for every fixed hyperedge $e\in E(\mathcal{F})$ we have \[\mathbb{P}\big[1\leq |e\cap S|\leq 100 \log r\big]\geq 1- \frac{1}{10kr}.\] Suppose we have shown this. Then, a simple application of the local lemma suffices to complete the proof as follows. For every $e\in E(\mathcal{F})$, define the bad event $B_e$ as the complement of the event $1\leq |e\cap S|\leq 100\log r$. Since the event $B_e$ only depends on whether the vertices of $e$ are included in $S$ or not, it is independent of all $B_{e'}$ with $e\cap e'=\varnothing$. So, the maximum degree of the dependency graph is at most $3k\cdot r$, since $|e|\leq 3k$ and every vertex of $e$ is contained in at most $r$ other edges. Since ${\rm{e}}(3kr+1)\cdot \frac{1}{10kr}<1$, the local lemma implies that with positive probability no bad event $B_e$ occurs, showing that we have $1\leq |e\cap S|\leq 100\log r$ for every $e\in E(\mathcal{F})$.

So, it remains to bound $\mathbb{P}[1\leq |e\cap S|\leq 100\log r]$. To do this, note first that 
\[\mathbb{P}[e\cap S=\varnothing]\leq \Big(1-\frac{10\log r}{k}\Big)^k\leq {\rm{e}}^{-10\log r}=r^{-10}.\]
On the other hand, by the union bound, for any $t$ we have
\[\mathbb{P}\big[|e\cap S|\geq t\big]\leq \binom{3k}{t}\Big(\frac{10\log r}{k}\Big)^t\leq \Big(\frac{3{\rm{e}}k}{t}\Big)^t \Big(\frac{10\log r}{k}\Big)^t\leq  \Big(\frac{3{\rm{e}} \cdot 10\log r}{t}\Big)^t.\]
Putting $t=100\log r$ gives 
\[\mathbb{P}\big[|e\cap S|\geq 100\log r\big]\leq \Big(\frac{3{\rm{e}} \cdot 10\log r}{100\log r}\Big)^{100\log r}\leq 0.9^{100\log r}\leq r^{-10},\]
where we have used that $30{\rm{e}}/100<0.9$ and $0.9^{100}\leq e^{-10}$. By a simple union bound, we obtain that $\mathbb{P}[1\leq |e\cap S|\leq 100\log r]\geq 1-2r^{-10}\geq 1-\frac{1}{10kr}$, which completes the proof as we already explained.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be the given $d$-regular $r$-uniform hypergraph and let $\mathcal{F}$ be its dual. If we have $d\geq 3r$, then by Theorem~\ref{thm:r-uniform-general} applied with $k=r$ we find a spanning subhypergraph $\mathcal{C}\subseteq \mathcal{H}$ in which all degrees are in $\{r, \dots, 3r-4\}$. Taking its dual hypergraph $\mathcal{F}'$ corresponds to restricting the set of vertices of $\mathcal{F}$ such that all hyperedges have size between $r$ and $3r-4$. Hence, by Lemma~\ref{lemma:low_uniformity} applied with $k=r$ we find a subset $S\subseteq V(\mathcal{F}')$ with the property that $1\leq |S\cap e|\leq 100\log r$ for all $e\in E(\mathcal{F}')$. The set of hyperedges in $\mathcal{H}$ dual to $S$ then gives a subhypergraph in which every vertex $v\in V(\mathcal{H})$ has degree between $1$ and $100\log r$.

In the case $d\leq 3r$ we argue similarly, by directly applying Lemma~\ref{lemma:low_uniformity} with $k=\lceil d/3\rceil$ to obtain a set $S$ of vertices for which $1\leq |S\cap e|\leq 100\log r$. As we noted above, considering its dual gives the subhypergraph we need in $\mathcal{H}$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:2-shallow}
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a $3$-regular tripartite $3$-uniform hypergraph. Then, $\mathcal{H}$ has a spanning subhypergraph whose degrees are all either $1$ or $2$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- claim-1: body_md -->
\label{claim:minimal}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a hypergraph, and suppose that every proper subhypergraph of $\mathcal{F}$ has property $B$. Let us assign a vector $x_v\in \mathbb{R}^{|E(\mathcal{F})|}$ to each vertex of the hypergraph $\mathcal{F}$, where $x_v(e)=1$ if $v\in e$ and $x_v(e)=0$ otherwise. If the vectors $x_v$ are linearly dependent, then $\mathcal{F}$ has property $B$. In particular, if $|E(\mathcal{F})|<|V(\mathcal{F})|$, then $\mathcal{F}$ has Property $B$.
<!-- end claim-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
If the vectors $x_v$ are linearly dependent, there exists a linear dependency $\sum_{v} \alpha_v x_v=0$ with not all $\alpha_v$ equal to zero. In other words, for every edge $e\in E(\mathcal{F})$ we have $\sum_{v\in e} \alpha_v=0$.

Let $R=\{v:\alpha_v<0\}$ and $B=\{v:\alpha_v>0\}$, and color the vertices of $R$ red and vertices of $B$ blue. Note that some vertices may have $\alpha_v=0$ and thus remain uncolored. Since $\sum_{v\in e} \alpha_v=0$, any edge containing a red vertex also must contain a blue vertex. Let $U$ be the set of uncolored vertices, and let $\mathcal{F}[U]$ be the induced subhypergraph on this set. By assumption, $\mathcal{F}[U]$ can be bicolored without monochromatic edges. Extending the coloring using the sets $R, B$ gives a bicoloring of $\mathcal{F}$ without monochromatic edges, as we wanted.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:property_B}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a $3$-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree $3$ with two edge-disjoint perfect matchings. Then, $\mathcal{F}$ has Property $B$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Suppose that $\mathcal{F}$ does not have Property $B$, and let $\mathcal{C}\subseteq \mathcal{F}$ be the minimal subgraph of $\mathcal{F}$ which also does not have Property $B$. 

To start, let us show that $|E(\mathcal{C})|\leq |V(\mathcal{C})|$. Indeed, note that the average degree of $\mathcal{C}$ is at most $3$, since this is the maximum degree of $\mathcal{C}$. On the other hand, the average degree of $\mathcal{C}$ is at least ${3|E(\mathcal{C})|}/{|V(\mathcal{C})|}$, since $\mathcal{C}$ is a $3$-uniform hypergraph. Combining these two bounds gives $|E(\mathcal{C})|\leq |V(\mathcal{C})|$.

On the other hand, since $\mathcal{C}$ is a minimal subhypergraph not having Property $B$, by Claim~\ref{claim:minimal}, we must have $|E(\mathcal{C})|\geq |V(\mathcal{C})|$. Hence, all of the inequalities of the previous paragraph must be equalities, and in particular all vertices of $\mathcal{C}$ must have degree $3$ in $\mathcal{C}$. This means that no edge contains vertices of both $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{F}\backslash \mathcal{C}$, and so every perfect matching of $\mathcal{F}$ restricts to a perfect matching of $\mathcal{C}$. Hence, $\mathcal{C}$ has two distinct perfect matchings.

This implies that the vectors $\{x_v:v\in V(\mathcal{C})\}$ cannot be linearly independent, as follows. Consider the vertex-edge incidence matrix $A$ of the hypergraph $\mathcal{C}$, which has the vectors $x_v$ as rows. If the rows of this square matrix are linearly independent, so are the columns. In other words, if $y_e\in \mathbb{R}^{|V(\mathcal{C})|}$ is the indicator vector of the edge $e$, the vectors $\{y_e:e\in E(\mathcal{C})\}$ are linearly independent.

However, this is not the case: if $M, M'$ are the perfect matchings of $\mathcal{C}$, then $\sum_{e\in M} y_e=\sum_{e\in M'} y_e$ is a nontrivial linear dependence between the columns of $A$ (both sums give an all-ones vector since $M, M'$ are perfect). Hence, the rows of $A$ must also be linearly dependent, which is what we aimed to show. By Claim~\ref{claim:minimal} this means that $\mathcal{C}$ has property B, contradiction.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
To prove Proposition~\ref{prop:2-shallow}, it suffices to observe that the dual $\mathcal{F}$ of a tripartite $3$-uniform hypergraph $\mathcal{H}$ has three disjoint perfect matchings. Indeed, the vertices inside each of the three partite sets of $\mathcal{H}$ are incident to distinct edges, and thus they correspond to a perfect matching in the dual. Hence, Proposition~\ref{prop:2-shallow} follows directly by dualizing Lemma~\ref{lemma:property_B}.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:hypergraph_matching}
Suppose that $\mathcal{H}$ is a bipartite $3$-uniform hypergraph. If every set $S\subseteq A$ has $\nu(L_S)\geq 2|S|$, then there exists a matching $M$ in $\mathcal{H}$ saturating $A$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:5-shallow}
If $\mathcal{H}$ is a nonempty regular $3$-uniform hypergraph, then $\mathcal{H}$ has a spanning subhypergraph with degrees in the set $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Consider the following auxiliary hypergraph $\mathcal{A}$. The vertex set of $\mathcal{A}$ consists of five copies of $V(\mathcal{H})$, i.e. for each vertex $v\in V(\mathcal{H})$ we add the vertices $v^{(1)}, v^{(2)}, v^{(3)}, v^{(4)}, v^{(5)}$ to $V(\mathcal{A})$. The edge set of $\mathcal{A}$ is defined as follows: for each edge $e=\{x, y, z\}\in E(\mathcal{H})$, we add to $E(\mathcal{A})$ the following six edges
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
$\{x^{(1)}, y^{(2)}, z^{(3)}\}$, & $\{y^{(1)}, z^{(2)}, x^{(3)}\}$, & $\{z^{(1)}, x^{(2)}, y^{(3)}\}$,\\
$\{x^{(1)}, y^{(4)}, z^{(5)}\}$, & $\{y^{(1)}, z^{(4)}, x^{(5)}\}$, & $\{z^{(1)}, x^{(4)}, y^{(5)}\}$.

The goal is to show that there exists a matching covering $A$ in this auxiliary hypergraph. To show this, note that for any $S\subseteq A$, the link graph $L_S$ is a bipartite multigraph between $B$ and $C$ with maximum degree $d$ and $2|S|d$ edges, since every vertex of $S$ contributes at least $2d$ edges to the link graph. Hence, the cover number of this bipartite graph must be at least $2|S|$, and so by K\H{o}nig's theorem we have $\nu(L_S)=\tau(L_S)\geq 2|S|$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:hypergraph_matching}, this shows that $\mathcal{A}$ contains a matching $M$ saturating $A$.

In other words, for every $v\in V(\mathcal{H})$, there exists a unique edge of $M$ containing $v^{(1)}$, say $\{v^{(1)}, u^{(2)}, w^{(3)}\}$ (or $\{v^{(1)}, u^{(4)}, w^{(5)}\}$). We construct the subhypergraph $\mathcal{C}$ by adding the edge $\{v, u, w\}$ for each $v$. We claim that every vertex appears in at most $5$ edges of $\mathcal{C}$. Indeed, if a vertex $v$ appears in an edge of $\mathcal{C}$, it must be that one of its copies in $\mathcal{A}$ was present in one of the edges of the matching $M$. But there are $5$ copies of $v$ in $\mathcal{A}$, and so $v$ can appear in at most $5$ edges of $\mathcal{C}$. This shows that $\mathcal{C}$ has maximum degree at most $5$, as we wanted.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:4-shallow}
If $\mathcal{H}$ is a nonempty regular $3$-uniform hypergraph, then $\mathcal{H}$ has a spanning subhypergraph with degrees in the set $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We start from the subhypergraph $\mathcal{C}$ obtained in Proposition~\ref{prop:5-shallow} and remove some edges to reduce its maximum degree by one. Note that we treat $\mathcal{C}$ as a multihypergraph.

The main observation is that if a vertex $v$ has $\deg_{\mathcal{C}}v=5$, then we may delete the edge containing $v^{(1)}$ in the matching $M$ (which we included in order to cover $v$) and reduce the degree of $v$ in this way. However, one must be careful in which order the vertices $v$ are chosen, in order to ensure that every vertex is still covered after several edges get deleted in this process.

We call the edge containing $v^{(1)}$ in the matching $M$ the \textit{special edge} of $v$, and we use the same term to refer to the corresponding edge in $\mathcal{C}$.

Let $D$ be an auxiliary directed graph on the set of vertices of degree $5$. We put a directed edge in $D$ from $u$ to $v$ if $v$ is contained in the special edge of $u$ and $v\neq u$. The goal is to delete some special edges associated to the vertices of $D$, and we will use $D$ to guide the order in which we do this.

The maximum outdegree in $D$ is $2$, and hence there exists a vertex $v$ of indegree at most $2$. We now delete the special edge $e_v$ of the vertex $v$ from $\mathcal{C}$. 

We claim that even though $e_v$ was deleted, $v$ will still be covered at the end of this procedure. This is simple to see: since $v$ had at most $2$ inedges, at most two additional edges covering $v$ will be deleted. Since $v$ had appeared in $5$ edges of $\mathcal{C}$, even after all the deletions, $v$ will still be covered. 

We further claim that no other vertex $w$ of $e_v$ is left uncovered. If $w$ never had degree $5$, then the special edge $e_w$ will not be deleted from $\mathcal{C}$ and $w$ will remain covered. If $w$ had degree $5$ and the special edge $e_w$ was deleted in the past, we have just argued in the previous paragraph that $w$ will remain covered.

We iterate this process on the remaining vertices that are covered at least $5$ times, until all vertices are covered at most $4$ times, which completes the proof.
