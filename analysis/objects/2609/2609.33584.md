<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
\label{C1}
If $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal A'$ are line arrangements in $\mathbb P^2$ with isomorphic intersection lattices,
then $\nu(\mathcal A)=\nu(\mathcal A')$.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thmA}
Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal A'$ be a counterexample as in \eqref{eq:C2}. Then
\[
4\le m\le r<\frac{d-2}{2},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
d\ge 2m+3 .
\]
If moreover $m=4$, then $r=\floor*{(d-3)/2}$. If $r=m$, then $d\le3m+1$. There
is no counterexample with $d\le12$, and none with $r=m=4$. Finally, for every
line $L\in\mathcal A$, either $L$ contains at most $r+1$ singular points of $\mathcal A$, or
deleting $L$ from $\mathcal A$ and the corresponding line from $\mathcal A'$ produces a
counterexample with $d-1$ lines and minimal degree of a Jacobian relation
$r-1$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:rm}
Let $\mathcal A$ be an arrangement of $d$ lines with $m=m(\mathcal A)\ge3$, $d\ge2m+1$ and
$r(\mathcal A)=m$. Then $\mathcal A$ is either free with exponents $(m,d-m-1)$ or POG with
exponents $(m,d-m,d_3)$, where $d-m\le d_3\le d-2$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $d_1\le d_2\le\cdots$ be the degrees of a minimal system of generators of
$D_0(f)$, so $d_1=r=m$, and let $\rho_1$ be a generator of degree $m$. Let $p$
be a point of multiplicity $m$, and let $\tilde D_p\in D_0(f)_{d-m}$ be the
local derivation associated with $p$ in \cite[Theorem 1.3]{DimcaDer}. By
\cite[Theorem 1.3(5)]{DimcaDer}, the three coefficients of $\tilde D_p$ vanish
simultaneously only at the multiple points of the subarrangement formed by the
lines of $\mathcal A$ not passing through $p$, that is at finitely many points of
$\mathbb P^2$. If $D_0(f)_{d-m}=(S\rho_1)_{d-m}$, then $\tilde D_p=u\rho_1$ with
$\deg u=d-2m\ge1$, and the three coefficients of $\tilde D_p$ would vanish
along the curve $u=0$, a contradiction. Hence $d_2\le d-m$, and so
$d_1+d_2\le d$. On the other hand $d_1+d_2\ge d-1$, and $\mathcal A$ is free if and
only if $d_1+d_2=d-1$, and POG if and only if $d_1+d_2=d$, by
\cite[Theorem 2.3]{3syz}, see also \cite[Theorem 1.1]{ADP}. The bounds on $d_3$
are those recalled above.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The lemma above is also contained in \cite[Theorem 1.12(2)]{DimcaDer}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:galois}
Let $K\subset\mathbb C$ be a subfield and let $\mathcal A\colon f=0$ be an arrangement of $d$
lines with $f\in K[x,y,z]$, all lines of $\mathcal A$ being defined over $K$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\roman*)}]
\item For every field extension $K'$ of $K$ and every $r$ one has
\[
\dim_{K'}\operatorname{AR}(f\otimes_KK')_r=\dim_K\operatorname{AR}(f)_r .
\]
In particular $r(\mathcal A)$ does not
depend on the field over which it is computed, and a Jacobian relation of
minimal degree may be chosen with all its coefficients in $K$.
\item Let $\sigma\colon K\to\mathbb C$ be a field embedding and let $\mathcal A^\sigma\colon
f^\sigma=0$ be the arrangement obtained by applying $\sigma$ to the coefficients
of the linear forms. Then $\mathcal L(\mathcal A^\sigma)\cong\mathcal L(\mathcal A)$ and
$r(\mathcal A^\sigma)=r(\mathcal A)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
(i) The graded piece $\operatorname{AR}(f)_r$ is the kernel of the $K$-linear map
$S_r^3\to S_{r+d-1}$ sending $(a,b,c)$ to $af_x+bf_y+cf_z$. In the monomial
bases this map is given by a matrix $M_r$ with entries in $K$, and the rank of a
matrix is unchanged by extension of the base field. Hence so is the dimension of
the kernel, and a $K$-basis of $\ker M_r$ remains a basis after extension.

(ii) Applying $\sigma$ to the coefficients of the linear forms of $\mathcal A$ carries
$M_r$ entrywise to the corresponding matrix for $f^\sigma$. As $\sigma$ is an
isomorphism of $K$ onto its image, it preserves the vanishing of minors, so
$\operatorname{rank}M_r^\sigma=\operatorname{rank}M_r$ and therefore
$\dim\operatorname{AR}(f^\sigma)_r=\dim\operatorname{AR}(f)_r$ for every $r$, whence $r(\mathcal A^\sigma)=r(\mathcal A)$
by (i). The same argument applied to the $2\times2$ and $3\times3$ minors of the coefficient
matrix of the linear forms shows that a set of lines of $\mathcal A$ is concurrent if
and only if the corresponding set of lines of $\mathcal A^\sigma$ is, and that distinct
lines remain distinct. Hence the two intersection lattices are isomorphic.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rk:galois}
Lemma \ref{lem:galois}(ii) says that a pair of Galois-conjugate arrangements
can never provide a counterexample to Conjecture \ref{C1}: the two members have
isomorphic intersection lattices, but also equal $r$, hence equal $\nu$ by
\eqref{eq:nu}. This is worth keeping in mind below, since the two
lattices described in Section \ref{sec:extremal} admit exactly two
realizations up to projective equivalence, conjugate over a quadratic field,
and one might otherwise be tempted to regard such a pair as a potential
candidate.
<!-- end remark-3 -->

\label{P:basic}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
If $2m>d$ then $r=d-m$ by \eqref{eq:d-m} is a lattice invariant, so $r=r'$,
which is impossible and hence $d\ge2m$. If $m=2$ then $r=d-2$ by
\eqref{eq:nodal}, contradicting $r<(d-2)/2$. If $m=3$, then $d\ge6$ and
\eqref{eq:bb} and \eqref{eq:C2} give $2d/3-2\le r<(d-2)/2$, whence $d\le5$, a
contradiction. Hence $m\ge4$.

Next, $r\ge m-1$ always holds, with equality if and only if $\mathcal A$ is free with
exponents $(m-1,d-m)$. If $r=m-1$ then $\tau(\mathcal A)=\tau(d,r)_{\max}$, while
$r'\ge r+1$ and $r+1\le(d-1)/2$ give
$\tau(\mathcal A')\le\tau(d,r+1)_{\max}<\tau(d,r)_{\max}$ by \eqref{eq:taunu}, so that
$\tau(\mathcal A)>\tau(\mathcal A')$, contradicting $\mathcal L(\mathcal A)\cong\mathcal L(\mathcal A')$. Hence $m\le r$, and
$m\le r<(d-2)/2$ yields $d\ge2m+3$.

Finally, let $m=4$. Then \eqref{eq:bb} gives $d/2-2\le r<(d-2)/2$. If $d=2d'+1$, then
the unique integer solution is $r=d'-1$, and if $d=2d'$ it is $r=d'-2$. Both
are $\floor*{(d-3)/2}$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{P:rm}
Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal A'$ be a counterexample with $r=m$. Then $d\le3m+1$. Moreover, there is
no counterexample with $r=m=4$, and none with $d\le12$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since $r=m$, Lemma \ref{lem:rm} shows that either $\mathcal A$ is free with exponents
$(m,d-m-1)$, which is excluded exactly as in the proof of Proposition
\ref{P:basic}, or $\mathcal A$ is POG with exponents $(m,d-m,d_3)$. In the latter case \eqref{eq:d3} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TA}
\tau(\mathcal A)=(d-1)^2-m(d-m-1)-(d_3-d+m+1).
\end{equation}
On the other hand $m=r<(d-2)/2$ implies $m+1\le(d-1)/2$, so that $r'\ge m+1$
and \eqref{eq:taunu} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TAp}
\tau(\mathcal A')\le\tau(d,m+1)_{\max}-\nu(\mathcal A').
\end{equation}
Expanding, $\tau(d,m+1)_{\max}-\tau(\mathcal A)=d_3-2d+3m+3$, so \eqref{eq:TAp} and
$\tau(\mathcal A)=\tau(\mathcal A')$ force $d_3\ge2d-3m-3+\nu(\mathcal A')$. Since $d_3\le d-2$ by \cite{Sch},
we obtain $d-2\ge2d-3m-3$, that is $d\le3m+1$.

Suppose now $r=m=4$. By Proposition \ref{P:basic} we have
$4=r=\floor*{(d-3)/2}$, so $d\in\{11,12\}$, and $d=11$ is excluded by
Theorem \ref{thm:eleven} below. For $d=12$ we know that Terao's Conjecture and
its nearly free analogue hold, by \cite{BK} and \cite{DIM2}: if $\mathcal A'$ were free
or nearly free, then so would be $\mathcal A$, with the same exponents, contradicting
$r<r'$. Hence $\nu(\mathcal A')\ge2$, and $d_3\le d-2$ yields $d\le3m-1=11$, a
contradiction.

Finally, if $d\le12$ then Proposition \ref{P:basic} forces $m=4$ and hence
$r=4=m$, which has just been excluded.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:nofree}
Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal A'$ be a counterexample with $d\le15$. Then, for
every line $L\in\mathcal A$, the deleted arrangement $\mathcal B=\mathcal A\setminus\{L\}$ is not free.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose that $\mathcal B$ is free with exponents $(d_1,d_2)$, $d_1\le d_2$, so that
$d_1+d_2=d-2$, since $\mathcal B$ consists of $d-1$ lines. By the addition theory for free arrangements,
see \cite[Theorem 1.4]{POG}, the arrangement $\mathcal A=\mathcal B\cup\{L\}$ is free with
exponents $(d_1,d_2+1)$ if $|\mathcal B^L|=d_1+1$, free with exponents $(d_1+1,d_2)$ if
$|\mathcal B^L|=d_2+1$, and POG with exponents $(d_1+1,d_2+1,|\mathcal B^L|-1)$ otherwise. In
all three cases
\[
r(\mathcal A)\in\{d_1,\,d_1+1\},
\]
and which of the three occurs is decided by $d_1$, $d_2$ and $|\mathcal B^L|$ alone.

Let $L'\in\mathcal A'$ and $\mathcal B'=\mathcal A'\setminus\{L'\}$ be as in the Conventions, so that
$\mathcal L(\mathcal B)\cong\mathcal L(\mathcal B')$. As $\deg\mathcal B=d-1\le14$, Terao's Conjecture \cite{BK}
makes $\mathcal B'$ free with the same exponents $(d_1,d_2)$. Moreover $|\mathcal B^L|=r_L$ is
the number of singular points of $\mathcal A$ lying on $L$, hence a lattice invariant,
so $|\mathcal B'^{L'}|=|\mathcal B^L|$. Therefore $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal A'$ fall into the same case of the
trichotomy above and have the same exponents. In particular, $r(\mathcal A)=r(\mathcal A')$,
contradicting $r(\mathcal A)<r(\mathcal A')$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:del}
Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal A'$ be a counterexample, let $L\in\mathcal A$,
$\mathcal B=\mathcal A\setminus\{L\}$, and let $\mathcal B'=\mathcal A'\setminus\{L'\}$. Then
$r(\mathcal B)\in\{r-1,r\}$ and $r(\mathcal B')\ge r$. Moreover
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\roman*)}]
\item if $r(\mathcal B)=r$, then $r_L\le r+1$;
\item if $r(\mathcal B)=r-1$, then $\mathcal B,\mathcal B'$ is a counterexample as in \eqref{eq:C2},
of degree $d-1$, with $r(\mathcal B)=r-1$ and $m(\mathcal B)\le m$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, if there is no counterexample of degree $d-1$ with minimal
degree of a Jacobian relation $r-1$ and maximal multiplicity at most $m$, then
every line of $\mathcal A$ contains at most $r+1$ singular points of $\mathcal A$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By \eqref{eq:mono} we have $r(\mathcal B)\le r\le r(\mathcal B)+1$ and
$r(\mathcal B')\ge r(\mathcal A')-1\ge r$.

(i) If $r(\mathcal B)=r$, then $D_0(f_\mathcal B)_{r-1}=0$, so \eqref{eq:AD} with $k=r$ embeds
$D_0(f_\mathcal A)_r\ne0$ into $H^0\bigl(L,\mathcal O_L(r+1-r_L)\bigr)$. Hence this
space is non-zero, that is $r_L\le r+1$.

(ii) We have $\mathcal L(\mathcal B)\cong\mathcal L(\mathcal B')$, and $\mathcal B$ is essential, since
$m(\mathcal B)\le m<d-1$. Moreover, $r(\mathcal B)=r-1<(d-2)/2-1<\bigl((d-1)-2\bigr)/2$, and $r(\mathcal B)=r-1<r\le r(\mathcal B')$.
Hence $\mathcal B,\mathcal B'$ satisfies \eqref{eq:C2} in degree $d-1$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:twoline}

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:ziegler}
Let $\mathcal A$ be an arrangement of $d$ lines and $L\in\mathcal A$. Let $\mathcal A^L$ be the set of
singular points of $\mathcal A$ on $L$, let $m^L(p)=m_p-1$ for $p\in\mathcal A^L$, and let
$d_1^L\le d_2^L$ be the exponents of the Ziegler multirestriction
$(\mathcal A^L,m^L)$, so that $d_1^L+d_2^L=d-1$. Then
\[
r(\mathcal A)\ \ge\ d_1^L .
\]
In particular, if $d\ge5$ and every singular point of $\mathcal A$ on $L$ is a triple
point, then $r(\mathcal A)\ge(d-1)/2$. Hence no line of a counterexample $\mathcal A$ as in
\eqref{eq:C2} contains only triple points of $\mathcal A$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $\alpha_L$ be a linear form defining $L$, let $D(\mathcal A)$ be the module of
logarithmic derivations of $\mathcal A$, $\theta_E$ the Euler derivation, and
$D_L(\mathcal A)=\{\theta\in D(\mathcal A)\ :\ \theta(\alpha_L)=0\}$. For $\theta\in D(\mathcal A)$ we
have $\theta(\alpha_L)=g\alpha_L$ for some $g\in S$, and
$\theta-g\theta_E\in D_L(\mathcal A)$, so $D(\mathcal A)=S\theta_E\oplus D_L(\mathcal A)$. In the same
way $D(\mathcal A)=S\theta_E\oplus D_0(f)$, where $D_0(f)$ is viewed as the module of
derivations annihilating $f$. Hence $D_L(\mathcal A)\cong D(\mathcal A)/S\theta_E\cong D_0(f)$
as graded $S$-modules, and the initial degree of $D_L(\mathcal A)$ is $r(\mathcal A)$.

By \cite{ZieMulti}, reducing the coefficients modulo $\alpha_L$ defines a
degree preserving $S$-linear map
\[
\pi\colon D_L(\mathcal A)\longrightarrow D(\mathcal A^L,m^L),
\]
where $D(\mathcal A^L,m^L)$ is a free module over $S/\alpha_LS$ with a basis of degrees
$d_1^L,d_2^L$. If $\pi(\theta)=0$, then $\theta=\alpha_L\theta'$ for a
derivation $\theta'$, and $\theta'\in D_L(\mathcal A)$, since $\alpha_L$ is prime to
the linear forms of the other lines of $\mathcal A$. Thus $\ker\pi=\alpha_LD_L(\mathcal A)$.
Now let $0\ne\theta\in D_L(\mathcal A)_{r(\mathcal A)}$. As $D_L(\mathcal A)_{r(\mathcal A)-1}=0$, we have
$\theta\notin\ker\pi$, so $\pi(\theta)$ is a nonzero element of degree $r(\mathcal A)$
of $D(\mathcal A^L,m^L)$, and therefore $r(\mathcal A)\ge d_1^L$.

If every singular point on $L$ is a triple point, then $|\mathcal A^L|=(d-1)/2\ge2$ and
$m^L\equiv2$, so $(\mathcal A^L,m^L)$ has exponents $\bigl((d-1)/2,(d-1)/2\bigr)$ by
\cite{Wakamiko}, see also \cite[Proposition 1.23]{Yoshinaga}. Hence
$r(\mathcal A)\ge(d-1)/2$, which is incompatible with $r(\mathcal A)<(d-2)/2$.

\qquad

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:edim}
Modulo $\mathrm{PGL}(3,\mathbb C)$, the realization space of $\mathcal L(\mathcal A)$ in the
space of $d$-tuples of lines has dimension at least $e(\mathcal A)$ at each of its
points, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edim}
e(\mathcal A):=2d-8-\sum_{p\,:\,m_p\ge3}\bigl(m_p-2\bigr).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Let $X\subset(\mathbb P^{2\vee})^d$ be the set of $d$-tuples of lines realizing
$\mathcal L(\mathcal A)$. The ambient space is smooth of dimension $2d$. Fix a point
$(\ell_1,\dots,\ell_d)$ of $X$. For each point $p$ of multiplicity $m_p\ge3$
choose two of the lines through $p$. In a neighborhood of the fixed point
these two lines remain distinct, their intersection point $p(\ell)$ depends
regularly on the lines, and the condition that each of the remaining $m_p-2$
lines through $p$ passes through $p(\ell)$ is a single equation. Since the
absence of further incidences and the distinctness of the lines are open
conditions, $X$ is defined near the fixed point by these
$N=\sum_p(m_p-2)$ equations, so every irreducible component of $X$ through
that point has dimension at least $2d-N$. The orbits of
$\mathrm{PGL}(3,\mathbb C)$ have dimension at most $8$, and this leads to \eqref{eq:edim}.

For a line $L$ let $s_L$ be the number of points of multiplicity at least three
on $L$, so that $\sum_Ls_L=S$. Two such points span a unique line, whence
$\sum_L\binom{s_L}{2}\le\binom k2$. As $x\mapsto\binom x2$ is convex, Jensen's
inequality gives $\sum_L\binom{s_L}{2}\ge d\binom{S/d}{2}$, that is
$S(S-d)/(2d)\le k(k-1)/2$. Equality in Jensen's inequality forces all $s_L$ to
coincide.

\label{ex:nearmiss}
Among the surviving types, exactly five satisfy \eqref{eq:incid} with
equality, all in degree $15$ and all with $k=6$ and $S=30=2d$: the types
$(n_2,\dots,n_6)=(43,0,2,2,2)$, $(42,0,3,0,3)$, $(42,1,0,3,2)$ and
$(41,1,1,1,3)$, with $r=m=6$ and $\tau(\mathcal A)=143$, $144$, $144$, $145$, and the
type $(n_2,\dots,n_5)=(45,0,0,6)$ with $m=5$ and $\tau(\mathcal A)=141$. By
Proposition \ref{P:incid} every line then carries exactly two of the six
multiple points, so these points are the vertices of a graph with fifteen
edges on six vertices, necessarily $K_6$, and every multiple point has
multiplicity $5$. This excludes the first four types (which are also excluded
by Theorem \ref{thmD}, as $\tau(\mathcal A)>141$). The last type is realized precisely
by the fifteen lines joining six points no three of which are collinear, in
positions where no further concurrences occur. For six general points one
finds $\operatorname{AR}(f)_8=0$, so that $r=9$ by \eqref{eq:dpwp}, since
$\tau(15,9)'_{\max}=141$: the general member is maximal Tjurina of type
$(15,9)$ and balanced. The same value $r=9$ is obtained when the six points
lie on a smooth conic, so this natural specialization produces no jump. A
counterexample of this type would require a special position of the six points
for which $r$ drops to $6$.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rk:edim}
The condition $e(\mathcal A)\ge1$ is sufficient for the realization space to be
positive-dimensional, provided it is non-empty, but not necessary: the
concurrency conditions may be dependent, as happens for symmetric
configurations, and then the actual dimension exceeds $e(\mathcal A)$.
Table \ref{tab:dim} therefore does not exclude a counterexample -- it only
identifies the $381$ and $346$ numerical types in degrees $15$ and $16$ whose
non-empty realization spaces are automatically positive-dimensional. It should
also be said that a jump of $r$ does not require a positive-dimensional family
at all: what is needed is a lattice whose realization space has two components
carrying different values of $r$, and Section \ref{sec:extremal} exhibits
lattices in degree $13$ whose realizations are isolated and defined over
quadratic fields. A rigid lattice with two such components would escape this
subsection entirely.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rk:chmn}
Conjecture \ref{C1} is equivalent to a positive answer to
\cite[Question 7.12]{Cook+}, which asks whether the splitting type $(a,b)$ of
the derivation bundle is a combinatorial invariant. Indeed, by
\cite[Corollary 3.3]{AbeDimcaSplit} one has $\nu(\mathcal A)=(d-1)^2-\tau(\mathcal A)-ab$, which
is \eqref{eq:nu} rewritten, and $(d-1)^2-\tau(\mathcal A)$ is the second Chern class of
the derivation bundle, see also \cite[(7.1)]{Cook+}, hence a lattice
invariant. Since $a+b=d-1$, knowing $ab$ is the same as knowing $(a,b)$. This
is implicit in \cite[\S1]{AbeDimcaSplit}, where a positive answer to
\cite[Question 7.12]{Cook+} is described as a stronger form of Terao's
Conjecture.

The splitting type also governs unexpected curves. Let $Z$ be the set of $d$
points dual to $\mathcal A$. By \cite[Lemma 3.5(a) and Corollary 5.7]{Cook+}, $Z$
admits an unexpected curve if and only if $2a+2<d$, that is $b-a\ge2$, and no
$a+2$ points of $Z$ are collinear, that is $m(\mathcal A)\le a+1$. In this case $Z$ has
an unexpected curve of degree $j$ exactly for $a<j\le b-1$. For a
counterexample $\mathcal A,\mathcal A'$ one has $a=r\ge m$, so the dual configuration $Z$ of
$\mathcal A$ admits unexpected curves of degrees $r+1,\dots,d-r-2$, while the dual
configuration $Z'$ of $\mathcal A'$ has none of degree $r+1$, since $a'\ge r+1$. If
moreover $\mathcal A'$ is balanced, which is automatic when $d=2r+3$, for instance in
the case $d=13$, $r=5$ of Section \ref{sec:thirteen}, then $Z'$ admits no
unexpected curve at all. Thus a counterexample amounts to a pair of
lattice-isomorphic point configurations of which only one admits an unexpected
curve of degree $r+1$. Conversely, Conjecture \ref{C1} would imply that the
existence and the degrees of unexpected curves of $Z$ depend only on
$\mathcal L(\mathcal A)$.

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\label{rk:baer}
The method of Section \ref{sec:104} can also be applied to this type. In the
extremal case in which a single line $L_0$ absorbs the whole defect, that is
$n_4^{L_0}=0$ and $n_4^L=4$ for the other fourteen lines (a case excluded at
once by Proposition \ref{P:2114}, since $r_{L_0}=14$), it proceeds as follows.
Dually, $L_0$ becomes a point on no block, while the remaining
fourteen points carry fourteen blocks of size four, each point lying on
exactly four of them, so that the uncovered pairs form a perfect matching. The
search produces $1920$ labelled solutions forming a single isomorphism class,
namely
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\{0,2,4,6\},\ \{0,3,8,10\},\ \{0,5,9,12\},\ \{0,7,11,13\},\ \{1,2,9,11\},\\
&\{1,3,5,7\},\ \{1,4,8,13\},\ \{1,6,10,12\},\ \{2,5,10,13\},\ \{2,7,8,12\},\\
&\{3,4,11,12\},\ \{3,6,9,13\},\ \{4,7,9,10\},\ \{5,6,8,11\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This configuration is the complement of a Baer subplane $\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_2)$ in
$\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)$: the fourteen lines of $\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)$ meeting the subplane in
exactly one point carry four points of the complement each, and the seven
lines of the subplane give the perfect matching of uncovered pairs. Its
automorphism group has order $336$, in accordance with
$1920=2^7\cdot7!/336$. In particular, it is realizable over $\mathbb F_4$, and our
realizability test in characteristic zero, which leaves one parameter $s$ and an
ideal containing $s^2-s+1$ and $s^2-s-1$, returns the unit ideal, while in
characteristic two it returns $(s^2+s+1)$. Non-realizability over $\mathbb C$ can
also be seen very directly: the restriction to the points
$\{0,2,4,7,8,10,13\}$ is the Fano plane, with lines
$\{0,2,4\}$, $\{0,8,10\}$, $\{0,7,13\}$, $\{4,8,13\}$, $\{2,10,13\}$,
$\{2,7,8\}$, $\{4,7,10\}$. This is a small reminder that a matroid search must always
be completed by a realizability test in characteristic zero, and that the test
must verify the recovered lattice.
