<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Petri net theory is a vast area and the word `unfolding' is sometimes used for other purposes. Here we are concerned only with the process of unfolding a Petri net to an occurrence net or an event structure. We also note that the symmetry issues we discuss are similar to issues in the representation of unmarked nets as certain monoidal categories. We refer the reader to \cite{WGPN} for a thorough comparison.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A Petri net $P$ consists of:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item a set $S$ of \emph{places} and a set $T$ of \emph{transitions};
  \item multirelations $\mathrm{Pre} : S \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\rotatebox{90}{$-$}}\hfil\cr}} T$ and $\mathrm{Post} : S \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\rotatebox{90}{$-$}}\hfil\cr}} T$ such that for every $t\in T$ there are finitely many $s \in S$ such that $\mathrm{Pre}(s, t) > 0$ and finitely many such that $\mathrm{Post}(s, t) > 0$;
  \item a multiset $\mu \in \mathcal{M}(S)$ of places, the \emph{marking};
  \end{itemize}
satisfying the following two conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The net is \emph{grounded}: for every $t \in T$, there is $s \in S$ such that $\mathrm{Pre}(s,
      t) > 0$.
    \item The net has no \emph{isolated places}: for every $s \in S$, there exists $t \in T$ such that $\mathrm{Pre}(s,t) > 0$ or $\mathrm{Post}(s, t) > 0$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:whole-grain}
  A whole-grain Petri net $P$ consists of:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item a set $S$ of places and a set $T$ of transitions;
  \item two sets $I$ and $O$ together with spans $S \leftarrow I \rightarrow T$
  and $S \leftarrow O \rightarrow T$;
    \item a finite set $M$ together with a map $M \to S$, the \emph{marking};
  \end{itemize}
such that the functions $I \to T$ and $O \to T$ have finite fibres. Additionally, we require that the maps $I \rightarrow T$ and $I + O \rightarrow S$ are
surjective.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:Qonobjects}
  For a whole-grain Petri net $P$, with components labelled as in
Definition~\ref{def:whole-grain}, there is an ordinary Petri net $\mathsf{Q}(P)$ with transition set $T$, place set $S$, marking $\mu = \mathsf{Q}(M)$, and multirelations
$\mathrm{Pre} = \mathsf{Q}(S \leftarrow I \rightarrow T)$ and $\mathrm{Post} = \mathsf{Q}(S \leftarrow O \rightarrow T)$. This Petri net is denoted $\mathsf{Q}(P).$
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Both kinds of Petri nets in this paper are assumed to be grounded and to have no isolated places. These two conditions are common but not systematically imposed, so this deserves a brief comment. In the context of net unfoldings, the grounded-ness condition seems essential, to avoid tokens being uncontrollably produced. However, one could likely do away with the condition on isolated places, at the cost of a slightly more complex 2-category of whole-grain Petri nets (because Theorem~\ref{thm:discreteness} below would not hold).
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->

  $P \to P'$ consists of a function $\eta : T \rightarrow T'$  and a multirelation $\beta : S \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\rotatebox{90}{$-$}}\hfil\cr}} S'$ such
  that the diagrams

	\arrow["\beta"{inner sep=.8ex}, "\shortmid"{marking}, from=1-2, to=3-2]

	\arrow["{\mu'}"'{inner sep=.8ex}, "\shortmid"{marking}, from=2-1, to=3-2]
\end{tikzcd}
\quad
\begin{tikzcd}

	\arrow["{\mathrm{Pre}'}"'{inner sep=.8ex}, "\shortmid"{marking}, from=2-1, to=2-2]
\end{tikzcd}\quad
\begin{tikzcd}
	S & T \\
	{S'} & {T'}
	\arrow["\mathrm{Post}"{inner sep=.8ex}, "\shortmid"{marking}, from=1-1, to=1-2]

commute. (The multisets $\mu$ and $\mu'$ are seen as multirelations from a singleton set, and the function $\eta$ is seen as a multirelation with

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Equivalent to the above diagrammatic conditions are the following equations, for every $p' \in P'$ and $t \in T$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\sum_{p \in P} \beta(p, p')\mu(p) = \mu'(p')$;
    \item $\sum_{p \in P}\beta(p, p') \mathrm{Pre}(p, t) = \mathrm{Pre}'(p',\eta(t))$; and 
    \item $\sum_{p \in P}\beta(p, p')\mathrm{Post}(p, t) = \mathrm{Post}'(p',\eta(t))$.
\end{itemize}

	\arrow["{(c)}"{description}, draw=none, from=1-3, to=2-4]

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Our cabling maps generalise those of Kock \cite{WGPN}, who only considered
  maps between Petri nets having the same initial marking. It is important for
  our purposes to allow for varying markings (subject to the pullback condition
  $(e)$---compare with the first condition in
  Definition~\ref{def:map-whole-grain})  for a proper
  connection with event structures.

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
For whole-grain Petri nets $P$ and $P'$, a \emph{rational map}
  $P \to P'$ is a span
  \[
     P \xleftarrow{\varphi} R \xrightarrow{\psi} P'
  \]
  where $R$ is another whole-grain net, $\varphi$ is a cabling map and $\psi$ is
  an \'etale map.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:Qonmor}
  For $P, P' \in \mathbf{Petri}^{\mathsf{WG}}$, every rational map $P \xleftarrow{\varphi} R \xrightarrow{\psi} P'$
  induces a morphism of ordinary Petri nets $(\eta, \beta): \mathsf{Q}(P) \to \mathsf{Q}(P')$ where $\eta$ is the
  function $T_R \to T_{P'}$ and $\beta$ is the finitary multirelation
  $\mathsf{Q}(S_P \leftarrow S_R \to S_{P'})$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We verify the first condition in  Definition~\ref{def:morphisms-petri} and omit the other two, which use similar arguments. Explicitly, we must show that for every $p' \in S_{P'}$, $\sum_{p \in S_P} \beta(p, p')\mu(p) = \mu'(p')$, where $\mu$ and $\mu'$ are the respective markings of $\mathsf{Q}(P)$ and $\mathsf{Q}(P')$. 

Recall that, by definition of $\mathsf{Q}$, for $p \in S_P$ and $p' \in S_{P'}$, $\beta(p, p')$ is the cardinality of the set $\varphi_S^{-1}\{p\} \cap \psi_S^{-1}\{p'\}$; $\mu(p)$ is the cardinality of the fibre $m_P^{-1}\{ p\}$ for the marking function $m_P : M_P \to S_P$; and similarly $\mu'(p')$ is the cardinality of $m_{P'}^{-1}\{p'\}$. 

By the axioms of rational maps we have the following situation:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	S_P & S_R & S_P' \\
	M_P & {M_R} & {M_{P'}} 
	\arrow[from=1-2, to=1-1, "\varphi_S"']
	\arrow[from=1-2, to=1-3, "\psi_S"]
	\arrow[from=2-1, to=1-1, "m_P"]
	\arrow["\lrcorner"{anchor=center, pos=0.125, rotate=180}, draw=none, from=2-2, to=1-1]
	\arrow[from=2-2, to=1-2, "m_R"]
	\arrow[from=2-2, to=2-1]
	\arrow[equals, from=2-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=2-3, to=1-3, "m_{P'}"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Thus, by the characterization of pullbacks in $\mathbf{Set}$ we may identify $M_R$ with the coproduct $\sum_{p \in S_P} \varphi_S^{-1}\{p\} \times m_P^{-1}\{p \}$, and under this identification the map $m_R : M_R \to S_R$ is given by $(p, r, j) \mapsto r$. For $p' \in S_{P'}$ we obtain the desired equation by considering the bijection 
\begin{align*}
m_{P'}^{-1}\{p'\} &\cong m_R^{-1} \left( \psi_S^{-1} \{ p' \} \right) \\
&\cong \{ (p, r, j) \mid  p \in S_P, r \in \varphi_S^{-1}\{p \}, j \in m_P^{-1}\{p\} \text{ and } r \in \psi_S^{-1} \{p'\} \} \\ 
&= \{ (p, r, j) \mid  p \in S_P, r \in \varphi_S^{-1}\{p \} \cap \psi_S^{-1}\{p'\}, j \in m_P^{-1}\{p\} \} \\ 
&\cong \coprod_{p \in P} \left(\varphi_S^{-1}\{p \} \cap \psi_S^{-1}\{p'\}\right) \times m_P^{-1}\{p\}
\end{align*}
whose domain has cardinality $\mu(p')$ and whose codomain has cardinality $\sum_{p \in S_P} \beta(p, p') \times \mu(p)$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:folding}
    For $P, P' \in \mathbf{Petri}^{\mathsf{WG}}$, if $\psi : P \rightarrow P'$ is an \'etale map (seen as a degenerate rational map), then $\mathsf{Q}(\psi)$ is a folding map.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For a span of sets of the form $S_P =\!\!=\!\!= S_P \to S_{P'}$ the multirelation $S_P \mathrel{\ooalign{$\longrightarrow$\cr\hfil\!\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\rotatebox{90}{$-$}}\hfil\cr}} S_{P'}$ given by $\mathsf{Q}$ is a total function.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:pullback-cabling-etale}
 For whole-grain Petri nets $P', R, R'$, if $\psi : R \rightarrow P'$ is a cabling map, and $\varphi : R' \rightarrow P'	$ is an \'etale map, then there is a $\mathbf{Petri}^{\mathsf{WG}}_{\mathrm{gen}}$-pullback square as below for which $\psi''$ is a cabling map and $\varphi''$ is an \'etale map.
 \[\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1em, column sep=1em]
	&& {R''} && \\
	& R && {R'} \\
	 && {P'} &&
	\arrow["{\varphi''}"', from=1-3, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{\psi''}", from=1-3, to=2-4]
	\arrow["\lrcorner"{anchor=center, pos=0.125, rotate=-45}, draw=none, from=1-3, to=3-3]
	\arrow["\psi"', from=2-2, to=3-3]
	\arrow["{\varphi'}", from=2-4, to=3-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The diagram
\[\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=0.8em, column sep=0.8em]
	& {I_R} && {I_{P'}} \\
	{S_{R}} && {S_{P'}} \\
	& {I_{R''}} && {I_{R'}} \\
	{S_{R''}} && {S_{R'}}
	\arrow[from=1-2, to=1-4]
	\arrow[from=2-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow[from=2-1, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=2-3, to=1-4]
	\arrow[from=3-2, to=1-2]
	\arrow[from=3-2, to=3-4]
	\arrow[from=3-4, to=1-4]
	\arrow[from=4-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow[from=4-1, to=3-2]
	\arrow[from=4-1, to=4-3]
	\arrow[from=4-3, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=4-3, to=3-4]

<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
\label{def:occurrence-net}
  Let $P = (S, T, \mathrm{Pre}, \mathrm{Post}, \mu)$ be a Petri net with `no parallel edges': the multirelations $\mathrm{Pre}$ and $\mathrm{Post}$ are ordinary relations. Say two
  transitions $t, t' \in T$ are in \emph{immediate conflict}, written
  $t \mathbin{\#_0} t'$, if there exists a place $s$ with $\mathrm{Pre}(s, t)$ and
  $\mathrm{Pre}(s, t')$. Define binary relations $<$ and $\#$ on the set $S \uplus T$ as
  follows:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $<$ is the transitive closure of  $\mathrm{Pre} \cup \mathrm{Post}$, so $u < v$ iff there is a non-empty path from $u$ to $v$ in the graph underlying $P$;
    \item $\#$ is the \emph{hereditary closure} of $\#_0$ under ${<}$, that is,
          the smallest relation containing $\#_0$ and such that if $u \mathbin{\#} u'$ and
          $u < v$ then $v \mathbin{\#} u'$.
        \end{itemize}
        The Petri net $P$ is an \emph{occurrence net} if: 
		\begin{itemize}
			\item the relations $<$ and
        $\#$ are irreflexive; 
		\item each place $s$ has at most one $t$ with $\mathrm{Post}(s, t)$;
		\item the set $\{ u \mid u < v \}$ is finite for every
        $v \in S \uplus T$; and
		\item the multiset $\mu$ is a set (at most one token
        per place) containing precisely the $\leq$-minimal places: those with no incoming transitions. This set is also called the \emph{in-boundary} of $P$. 
		\end{itemize}

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:whole-grain-unfolding}

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
We will show that the counit of the restricted adjunction of Theorem~\ref{ref:thmunfolding-etale}, a family of \'etale maps $\varepsilon_P : \mathsf{U} P \to P$, extends to the rational setting. 

First recall that we can regard $\varepsilon_P$ as a degenerate rational map
 $\mathsf{U} P \xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}} \mathsf{U} P \xrightarrow{\varepsilon_P} P$, and it follows easily from properties of pullbacks that pre-composing this with another rational map $P_1 \xleftarrow{\psi} P_2 \xrightarrow{\varphi} \mathsf{U} P$ gives the span $P_1 \xleftarrow{\psi} P_2 \xrightarrow{\varepsilon_P \circ \varphi} P$. (This is a well-defined rational map: $\psi$ is a cabling map and both $\varphi$ and $\varepsilon_P$ are \'etale.)

We show that $(\mathrm{id}, \varepsilon_P)$ is a universal arrow in the following sense: for every $O \in \mathbf{Occ}^{\mathsf{WG}}$ and $P \in \mathbf{Petri}^{\mathsf{WG}}$, the post-composition functor 
\[
(\mathrm{id}, \varepsilon_P) \circ - : \mathbf{Occ}^{\mathsf{WG}}(O, \mathsf{U} P) \longrightarrow \mathbf{Petri}^{\mathsf{WG}}(O, P)
\]
is an equivalence of (essentially discrete) categories. It suffices to show it is essentially surjective and full, because faithfulness is immediate for functors between essentially discrete categories.

\emph{Essentially surjective.} Consider a rational map $O \xleftarrow{\psi} R \xrightarrow{\varphi} P$ (with $\varphi$ \'etale, $\psi$ cabling). By Lemma~\ref{lem:cabling-to-occ}, $R$ is an occurrence net. Thus we can instantiate Theorem~\ref{ref:thmunfolding-etale}: $\varphi$ must factor through $\varepsilon_P : \mathsf{U} P\to P$ via an \'etale map $\varphi' : R \to \mathsf{U} P$, so $\varphi = \varepsilon_P \circ \varphi'$. We have constructed a rational map $O \xleftarrow{\psi} R \xrightarrow{\varphi'} \mathsf{U} P$ in the pre-image of $(\varphi, \psi)$. 

\emph{Full.} Let $O \xleftarrow{\psi_1} R_1 \xrightarrow{\varphi_1} \mathsf{U} P$ and $O \xleftarrow{\psi_2} R_2 \xrightarrow{\varphi_2} \mathsf{U} P$ be rational maps such that $(\mathrm{id}, \varepsilon_P) \circ (\psi_1, \varphi_1)$ and $(\mathrm{id}, \varepsilon_P) \circ (\psi_2, \varphi_2)$ are related by a 2-cell $\alpha$ in $\mathbf{Petri}^{\mathsf{WG}}(O, P)$. We must show $(\psi_1, \varphi_1)$ and $(\psi_2, \varphi_2)$ are themselves related by a 2-cell. 

By Theorem~\ref{thm:discreteness}, the 2-cell $\alpha$ is an isomorphism $R_1 \to R_2$ such that
\[\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=tiny]
	& {R_1} & {\mathsf{U} P} & \\
	O &&& P \\
	& {R_2} & {\mathsf{U} P}

	\arrow["{\varepsilon_P}", from=1-3, to=2-4]
	\arrow["{\psi_1}", from=2-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{\psi_2}"', from=2-1, to=3-2]
	\arrow["{\varphi_2}"', from=3-2, to=3-3]
	\arrow["{\varepsilon_P}"', from=3-3, to=2-4]
\end{tikzcd}\]
commutes. But in particular $\alpha$ is \'etale, and so we must have $\varphi_1 = \varphi_2 \circ \alpha$ by the universal property of $\varepsilon_P$ (Theorem~\ref{ref:thmunfolding-etale}). Thus $\alpha$ also provides the required 2-cell $(\psi_1, \varphi_1) \to (\psi_2, \varphi_2)$.

An \emph{event structure} is a tuple $(E, {\leq}, \#)$ where $\leq$ is a partial order on $E$, and $\#$  is an irreflexive and symmetric binary relation on $E$, satisfying the axioms below:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For every $e \in E$  the set $\{e' \in E \mid e' \leq e \}$ is finite.
    \item For $e, e', e'' \in E$, if $e \leq_{E} e'$ and $e \mathrel{\#} e''$ then $e' \mathrel{\#} e''$.
\end{itemize}

<!-- definition-14: body_md -->
For an event structure $E$, an isomorphism family  is a set $\mathbb{S}$ of bijections $\theta:x \cong y$ between finite configurations of $x, y \in \mathscr{C}(E)$, such that:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\mathbb{S}$ contains all identity bijections, and is stable under composition and inverse.  
        \item For $\theta : x \cong y \in \mathbb{S}$, if $x' \subseteq x$, then the restriction of $\theta$ to $x'$ is in $\mathbb{S}$.
        \item For $\theta : x \cong y \in \mathbb{S}$, if $x \subseteq x'$, then there exists an extension $y \subseteq y'$ and a bijection $\theta' : x' \cong y' \in \mathbb{S}$ such that $\theta'$ restricts to $\theta$.
    \end{itemize}
    The pair $(E, \mathbb{S})$ is called an \emph{event structure with symmetry}.

<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
For event structures with symmetry $(E,\mathbb{S})$ and $(E', \mathbb{S}')$, a map $f : E \rightarrow E'$ \emph{preserves symmetry} if for all $\theta : x \cong y \in \mathbb{S}$, $f \theta : fx \cong fy \in \mathbb{S}'$, where $f\theta : f(e) \mapsto f(\theta(e))$.
<!-- end definition-15 -->
<!-- definition-16: body_md -->
For event structures with symmetry $(E,\mathbb{S})$ and $(E', \mathbb{S}')$ and symmetry-preserving maps $f, g : E \rightarrow E'$, say that $f$ and $g$ are \emph{symmetric}, denoted $f \sim g$, if for every $x \in \mathscr{C}(E)$ the bijection $fx \cong gx$ defined by $ f(e) \mapsto g(e)$ is in $\mathbb{S}'$.
<!-- end definition-16 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:sem-symmetry}
For a Petri net $P$, the collection
\[
\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{U}_P)} = \{\theta : x \cong y \mid \theta \in \mathbf{Ev}(x, y) \text{\ and\ } \alpha_y \circ \mathcal{N}(\theta) = \alpha_x\} \] is an isomorphism family on the event structure $\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{U}_{P})$ with respect to which the following holds: for every $E \in \mathbf{Ev}$ and for all pairs of maps $f_1, f_2 \in \mathbf{EvSym}(E, \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{U}_P))$  such that $f_1 \sim f_2$, $\varepsilon_P \circ \mathcal{N}(f_1) =\varepsilon_P \circ \mathcal{N}(f_2)$.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We only detail the extension axiom for isomorphism families. It suffices to look at a one-event extension $x \subseteq x'$ with $x' = x \cup \{ e \}$ and $e \notin x$. The general axiom then follows by induction. Let $e_1, \ldots, e_n$ be the immediate predecessors of $e$ w.r.t.~the order $\leq$. First note that $\gamma_x(e_i) = \gamma_{x'}(e_i)$ for every $i \leq n$, a consequence of naturality.

		Recall the construction of the unfolding: for every $i \leq n$, $\gamma_x(e_i)$ is a transition of $\mathcal{U}_P$ and so of the form $(A_i, a_i)$. Observe that we must have $\gamma_y(\theta(e_i)) = (A_i', a_i)$ for some $A_i'$, since by assumption both $\gamma_x(e_i)$ and $\gamma_y(\theta(e_i))$ map to the same transition of $P$ (namely, $a_i$). 

	Now let $(B, b)$ denote the transition $\gamma_{x'}(e)$, so (by construction) $B$ is its pre-set of places in $\mathcal{U}_P$. We partition $B$ as follows: let $B_{\mathrm{int}} \subseteq B$ be the subset of places sitting in between $\gamma_x(e_i)$ and $\gamma_{x'}(e)$ for some $i$ (formally,
       $B_{\mathrm{int}} = \{ s \in B \mid \mathrm{Post}((A_i, a_i), s) \text{ for some } i \leq n \})$ and  
   let $B_{\mathrm{inn}} = B \setminus B_{\mathrm{int}}$. We note that every place $s \in B_{\mathrm{inn}}$ is initial: indeed, any transition of $\mathcal{U}_P$ having $s$ in its post-set would correspond to an event of $x$ immediately below $e$, thus one of the $e_i$, and we would have $s \in B_{\mathrm{int}}$.
		
   We claim that $(B', b)$ is a well-defined transition of $\mathcal{U}_P$, where the set $B'$ is defined as $B_{\mathrm{inn}} \cup \{   ((A_i', a_i), s, j) \mid ((A_i, a_i), s, j) \in B_{\mathrm{int}} \}$. To see why this holds, note that every element of $B'$ is a well-defined place: $((A_i', a_i), s, j)$ is valid just because $((A_i, a_i), s, j)$ is, since $\eta(A_i', a_i)= \eta(A_i, a_i)$. Then observe that $\mathrm{co}~B'$ (because the transitions $(A_i', a_i)$ are concurrent and initial places are never involved in a conflict) and the arity condition transfers easily from $B$. So the claim holds and we can let $y' = y \cup \{ (B, b)\}$ and easily verify that the resulting extension of $\theta$ is in $\mathbb S$.

We return to the `problematic' example in \S\ref{example-intro}. Applying the above construction, we obtain the same event structure (with two concurrent events), but this time it comes equipped with an isomorphism family that makes the two events symmetric. This is one way to resolve the universality issue by restoring the equivalence, up to symmetry.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:crucial}
    For $O \in \mathbf{Occ}$ and $P \in \mathbf{Petri}$, if $(\eta, \beta): O \rightarrow P$ is a map of Petri nets and there are two maps $(\eta'_1, \beta'_1), (\eta'_2, \beta'_2):  O \rightarrow \mathcal{U}_P$ satisfying the commutative diagram of Lemma~\ref{lemmawinskel}, 
then $\eta'_1 \sim \eta'_2$ as maps of event structures with symmetry $J\mathcal{E} O \to \llbracket P \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}}$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
By assumption the two maps of ocurrence nets are equal when postcomposed by $\varepsilon_P$. The result then follows from our definition of the symmetry in $\mathcal{U}_P$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:pseudo-adj}
  For $E \in \mathbf{Ev}$ and $P \in \mathbf{Petri}$, the function 
  \[ 
  \mathbf{Ev}(E, \mathcal{E}{\mathcal{U}_P}) \xrightarrow{\cong} \mathbf{Occ}(\mathcal{N} E, \mathcal{U}_P) \xrightarrow{\varepsilon_P \circ -} \mathbf{Petri}(\mathcal{N} E, P) 
  \]
extends to an equivalence of categories $\mathbf{EvSym}(JE, \llbracket P \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}}) \simeq \mathbf{Petri}(\mathcal{N} E, P)$, natural in $E$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
First recall that the domain category $\mathbf{EvSym}(JE, \llbracket P \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}})$ is a setoid and the codomain $\mathbf{Petri}(\mathcal{N} E, P)$ is a set, i.e. a discrete category. The function extends to a functor, i.e. symmetric pairs have the same image, by Proposition~\ref{prop:sem-symmetry}. To establish the equivalence it suffices to prove that the function is surjective, which follows from Lemma~\ref{lemmawinskel}.
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- definition-17: body_md -->
For locally small 2-categories $\mathbf{D}$ and $\mathbf{E}$, a $2$-functor $J : \mathbf{D} \rightarrow \mathbf{E}$ is \emph{2-dense} if the 2-functor $\tilde{J} : \mathbf{E} \to [\mathbf{D}^\mathrm{op}, \mathbf{Cat}] : e \mapsto \mathbf{E}(J-, e)$ is locally an isomorphism of categories, where $[\mathbf{D}^\mathrm{op}, \mathbf{Cat}]$ is the 2-category of 2-functors, strict natural transformations, and modifications.
<!-- end definition-17 -->
<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
The embedding 2-functor $J : \mathbf{Ev} \to \mathbf{EvSym}$ is 2-dense.
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
For event structures with symmetry $A$ and $B$, we show that the functor $\tilde{J}_{A ,B} : \mathbf{EvSym}(A, B) \to \mathbf{Cat}^{\mathbf{Ev}^\mathrm{op}}(\mathbf{EvSym}(J-, A), \mathbf{EvSym}(J-, B))$ is an isomorphism of categories by constructing an inverse. Write $A = (|A|, \mathbb{S}_A)$ and $B = (|B|, \mathbb{S}_B)$. 

Observe that, for a natural transformation $\alpha : \mathbf{EvSym}(J-, A) \to \mathbf{EvSym}(J-, B)$, applying component $\alpha_{|A|}$ to the `identity' function $\mathrm{id}_{|A|} : J|A| \to A$ gives a map of event structures with symmetry $\alpha_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|}) : J|A| \to B$. We claim that $\alpha_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|})$ preserves the symmetry in $A$. For any bijection $\theta: x \cong y$ in $\mathbb{S}_A$, we have $(\mathrm{id}_{|A|} \circ J\iota_y \circ J\theta) \sim (\mathrm{id}_{|A|} \circ J\iota_x)$, writing $\iota_x : x \to |A|$ and $\iota_y : y \to |A|$ for the inclusion maps (and recalling that $\theta \in \mathbf{Ev}(x, y)$). Since $\alpha$ is a natural transformation and each component preserves the symmetry relation on maps,  $\alpha_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|}) \circ J{\iota_x} = \alpha_x (\mathrm{id}_{|A|} \circ J{\iota_x}) \sim \alpha_x(\mathrm{id}_{|A|} \circ J{\iota_y} \circ J\theta) = \alpha_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|}) \circ J\iota_y \circ J\theta.$ In other words, $\alpha_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|}) \theta \in \mathbb{S}_B$, and we have proven the claim. Therefore, we take $\tilde J_{A, B}^{-1}(\alpha) = \alpha_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|})$. For functoriality of $J_{A, B}^{-1}$, note that any modification $\alpha \to \beta$ must be unique and gives $\alpha_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|})\sim \beta_{|A|}(\mathrm{id}_{|A|})$. We omit the verification that $\tilde J_{A, B}^{-1}$ and $\tilde J_{A, B}$ are inverses.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{def:sem}
The unfolding semantics $P \mapsto \llbracket P \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}}$ determines a pseudo-functor $\llbracket - \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}} : \mathbf{Petri} \to \mathbf{EvSym}$     (i.e. composition and identities are only preserved up to symmetry).
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Every map $(\eta, \beta) : P \to P'$ induces a natural transformation $\mathbf{EvSym}(J -, \llbracket P \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}}) \xrightarrow{\simeq} \mathbf{Petri}(\mathcal{N} -, P) \xrightarrow{ (\eta, \beta)\circ - } \mathbf{Petri}(\mathcal{N} -, P') \xrightarrow{\simeq} \mathbf{EvSym}(J-, \llbracket P' \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}})$, which corresponds to a map $\llbracket P \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}} \to \llbracket P' \rrbracket^{\mathrm{Sym}}$  under the density isomorphism. The non-strictness is because pseudo-inverses are only determined up to isomorphism.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
Let $L : \mathbf{D} \to \mathbf{C}$ and $J : \mathbf{D} \to \mathbf{E}$ be 2-functors and suppose that $J$ is 2-dense. If $L$ has two $J$-relative pseudo-adjoint pseudo-functors $R, R' : \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{E}$, then $R$ and $R'$ are equivalent.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->

By 2-density of $J$ this lifts to an internal equivalence $R c \simeq R'c$ in $\mathbf{E}$. Several naturality conditions must be verified but this is straightforward diagram chasing.
