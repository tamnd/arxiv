<!-- defi-1: body_md -->
The zero-boundary \textbf{discrete Gaussian free field} (\textbf{DGFF}) $\Gamma$ is a measure on $\ell^2(\pi)$, such that
		\[ \forall f\in\ell^2(\pi),~~~\Gamma(f) \propto \exp{{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal {E}(f)\right)}}. \]
		Note that $\Gamma$ is a multivariate Gaussian in the space $\ell^2(\pi)$, with mean 0 and covariance matrix $g$. The covariance matrix in the standard Euclidean space is given by ${{\left(\frac{g(x,y)}{\pi(y)}\right)}}_{x,y\in V}$.
<!-- end defi-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The assumption of reversibility is not necessary since the distribution of $\Gamma$ depends only on the energy functional. The energy associated with $\Delta$ is the same as its symmetrized in $\ell^2(\pi)$, which is reversible.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The DGFF is a \textit{Gibbs measure} with Hamiltonian given by the energy $\mathcal {E}$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
One can define the DGFF with a general boundary condition $f_{\partial V} : \partial V \to \mathbb {R}$, using the energy
		\[
			\tilde{\mathcal {E}}(f) := \mathcal {E}(f) + \sum_{x\in V, y\in \partial V}\Delta(x,y)f(x)f_{\partial V}(y)\pi(x).
		\]
		However, one can easily translate any result to this general case using the following fact. If $\gamma$ is this distributed as the DGFF with boundary condition $f_{\partial V}$ and $\overline{f_{\partial V}}$ is the harmonic extension of $f_{\partial V}$ to $V$, then $\gamma-\overline{f_{\partial V}} \sim \Gamma$.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
If $\gamma\sim \Gamma$ and $x\in V$, then the law of $\gamma(x)$ conditionally on $\{\gamma(y),y\neq x\}$ is that of a normal distribution with mean $P\gamma(x)$ and variance $1/\pi(x)$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We can decompose the energy using reversibility
		\begin{align*}
			\mathcal {E}(f) &= \pi(x)f(x)(f(x)-Pf(x)) - \underbrace{\sum_{y\neq x}f(y)P(y,x)f(x)\pi(y)}_{=\pi(x)f(x)Pf(x)} + C\\
			&= \pi(x)(f(x) - Pf(x))^2 + C',
		\end{align*}
		where $C$ and $C'$ do not depend on the value of $f$ at site $x$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- defi-2: body_md -->
Let $\overline{Q}$ be a stochastic matrix on $\overline{V}$ and $Q$ its restriction to $V$.
		The asynchronous DeGroot dynamics is a continuous time Markov chain $(\mu_t)_{t\geq 0}$ on the set of functions $V\to\mathbb {R}$. \\
		It has the following evolution:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Put an exponential clock of parameter 1 at each site in $V$.
			\item If the clock at site $x$ rings at time $t$, the value of $\mu_t(x)$ is updated to $Q\mu_{t^{-}}(x)$.
		\end{itemize}
<!-- end defi-2 -->
<!-- defi-3: body_md -->
The \textit{total variation distance} between two probability measures $\mu,\nu$ on the same measurable space $(\Omega,\mathcal {A})$ is given by
		\[ \|\mu-\nu\|_{TV} = \sup_{A\in\mathcal {A}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|. \]
		An equivalent definition can be given using optimal transport duality:
		\[\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV} = \inf_{(X,Y) \text{ coupling of }\mu,\nu} \mathbb{P}(X\neq Y).\]
		If $\mu$ and $\nu$ both have a density with respect to a reference measure $\lambda$, then we also have
		\[
		\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV} = \frac{1}{2}\int |\mu(x)-\nu(x)|d\lambda(x).
		\]
<!-- end defi-3 -->
<!-- defi-4: body_md -->
Let $\varepsilon>0$ be a precision and $r>0$ a chosen size. 
		The \textit{mixing time} at precision $\varepsilon$ from an initial condition of size $r$, denoted $t_{\text{mix}}^r(\varepsilon)$ is 
		\[
		t_{\text{mix}}^r(\varepsilon) = \max_{\substack{h : V\to\mathbb {R}\\ \mathcal {E}(h) = r}}\inf\{t\geq 0|\|\delta_{h}\mathscr{P}_t-\Gamma\|_{TV} \leq \varepsilon\}.
		\]
		Note that $\sqrt{\mathcal {E}(h)}$ is a norm on $\ell^2(\pi)$. The reason behind this choice of norm will become apparent in the proof.
<!-- end defi-4 -->
<!-- defi-5: body_md -->
Let $(G_n)_{n\geq 0}$ be a sequence of connected and simple graphs with interior vertices $(V_n)_{n\geq0}$. For $n\in\mathbb {N}$, let $\Gamma_n$ be the DGFF on $G_n$. Assume the size of the graphs diverges. \\
		Let $r(n)$ be a real function of $n$. For $\varepsilon >0$, let $t_{\text{mix}}^{r,n}(\varepsilon)$ be the mixing time associated with $\Gamma_n$. We say that the dynamics of the DGFF started from an initial condition of size $r$, on the sequence of graphs $(G_n)_{n\in\mathbb {N}}$, exhibits a \textit{cutoff phenomenon}, if for any fixed $\varepsilon\in (0,1/2)$ we have
		\[
		\frac{t_{\text{mix}}^{r,n}(1-\varepsilon)}{t_{\text{mix}}^{r,n}(\varepsilon)} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 1.
		\]
		In our case the function $r$ should be chosen as a polynomial function of the size of the graph.
<!-- end defi-5 -->
<!-- defi-6: body_md -->
We say that a function $h: V\to\mathbb {R}$ is harmonic if $\Delta h$ is identically zero. If $\Delta h \geq 0$ (resp. $\Delta h\leq 0$) then we say that $h$ is super-harmonic (resp. $h$ is sub-harmonic).

Here we can conclude that deterministically $\mu_{t_i}(x) = \mu_{t_{i-1}}(X^x_{t_i-t_{i-1}})$.

		\end{figure}\newpage
		Next, we choose the site $x$ that is exactly the location of the last clock (here $x=4$). In this case the value of $\mu_{t_{i}}(x)$ is the average of $\mu_{t_{i-1}}$ over the neighborhood of $x$. The walk cannot jump in the time interval $[0,t_{i}-t_{i-1}[$, but will jump exactly at time $t_{i-1}-t_{i-1}$. Since the walk jumps randomly to a neighbor of $x$ we still have $\mu_{t_i}(x) = \mathbb{E}[\mu_{t_{i-1}}(X^x_{(t_i-t_{i-1})^+})]$. Continuing the recursion we can argue that $\mu_{t_i}(x) = \mathbb{E}[\mu_0(X_{t_i}^x)]$.

			\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{Example2.png}

		\paragraph{Mean} Let $i = \iota_t$, such that $\mu_t = \mu_{t_i}$. Take $x\in V$. \\
		First if $x\neq x_i$, the entry is unchanged, so $\mu_{t_i}(x) = \mu_{t_{i-1}}(x)$. As explained above, the key feature of this backward transformation is that the walk started at $x$ will not jump on the time interval $[0,t-t_{i-1}[$. Thus, if we remove the last point $(x_i,t_i)$ from $\mathcal {P}_0$ both $\mu_t(x)$ and $\mathbb{E}[\mu_0(X_t^x)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} | \mathcal {P} = \theta_t\mathcal {P}_0]$ will remain unchanged. \\
		Using the inductive hypothesis on $\mu_{t_{i-1}}(x)$, we have
		\begin{align*}
			\mu_{t_i}(x) = \mu_{t_{i-1}}(x) &= \mathbb{E}[\mu_0(X_t^x)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} | \mathcal {P} = \theta_t(\mathcal {P}_0\setminus\{(x_i,t_i)\})] \\
			&= \mathbb{E}[\mu_0(X_t^x)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} | \mathcal {P} = \theta_t\mathcal {P}_0].
		\end{align*}
		If $x = x_i$ then the entry is an average of its neighbors, that is
		\[
		\mu_{t_i}(x_i) =  P\mu_{t_{i-1}}(x_i).
		\]
		On the other hand $X^{x_i}$ will jump at time $t-t_i$ at some location $y\neq x_i$ according to $P(x,\cdot)$. However any other walks started at $y\neq x_i$ do not jump before time $t-t_i$. They are therefore unchanged if we remove $(x_i,t_i)$ from $\mathcal {P}_0$. In conclusion 
		\[
		\mathbb{E}[\mu_0(X_t^{x_i})\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} | \mathcal {P} = \theta_t\mathcal {P}_0]
		= \sum_{y\in V}P(x_i, y)\mathbb{E}[\mu_0(X_t^y)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} | \mathcal {P} = \theta_t(\mathcal {P}_0\setminus\{(x_i,t_i)\})].
		\]

		\paragraph{Covariance matrix}Again set $\iota_t = i$. Only the $x_i$-th row/column of the covariance matrix of $\sigma_{t_i}$ differs from $\sigma_{t_{i-1}}$. The idea to deal with the other entries of $\Sigma_{t_i}$ is the same as what was presented for the mean.\\
		Consider the $x_i$-th row of $\Sigma_{t_i}$. Recall the reversibility formula
		\[ \forall x,y\in V,~ \pi(x)P(x,y) = \pi(y)P(y,x). \] 
		We will be using this formula extensively in the following.
		\begin{itemize}
			\item First, for the diagonal we have
			\begin{align*}
				\Sigma_{t_i}(x_i,x_i) &= \frac{\pi(x_i)}{\pi(x_i)} +\pi(x_i)\text{Cov}(P\sigma_{t_{i-1}}(x_i), P\sigma_{t_{i-1}}(x_i))\\
				&=1+\sum_{x,y\in V}P(x_i,x)\Sigma_{t_{i-1}}(x,y)\frac{\pi(x_i)}{\pi(y)}P(x_i, y)\\
				&= 1 + [P\Sigma_{t_{i-1}}P](x_i,x_i).
			\end{align*}
			By definition, both $X^{x_i}$ and $\tilde{X}^{x_i}$ will jump independently at time $t_i$. Using the inductive hypothesis on $\mathcal {P}_0\setminus\{(x_i,t_i)\}$, we have
			\begin{align*}
				-\mathbb{E}[g(X_t^{x_i},\tilde{X_t}^{x_i})\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} |\mathcal {P} = \theta_t\mathcal {P}_0] &= -\sum_{x,y\neq x_i}\mathbb{E}[g(X_t^x,\tilde{X_t}^y)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} |\mathcal {P} =  \theta_t(\mathcal {P}_0\setminus\{(x_i,t_i)\})]P(x_i, x)P(x_i, y)\\
				&= -\sum_{x,y\in V}P(x_i, x)[g(x,y)-\Sigma_{t_{i-1}}(x,y)]\frac{1}{\pi(y)}P(x_i, y)\\
				&=\frac{1}{\pi(x_i)}{{\left[P\Sigma_{t_{i-1}}P(x_i,x_i)-PgP(x_i, x_i)\right]}}.
			\end{align*}
			We can conclude by expanding $PgP(x_i,x_i) = (I_V-\Delta)g(I_V-\Delta)(x_i,x_i) = g(x_i,x_i)-1$.
			\item Next, for the off-diagonal entries, take $x\neq x_i$ :
			\[
			 \pi(x)\Sigma_{t_i}(x,x_i) =\pi(x_i)\Sigma_{t_i}(x_i,x) = \pi(x)\pi(x_i)\text{Cov}(\sigma_{t_{i-1}}(x), P\sigma_{t_{i-1}}(x_i)) = \pi(x)[\Sigma_{t_{i-1}}P](x,x_i).
			\]
			Recall that $X^{x}$ cannot jump at time $t_i$ so only $X^{x_i}$ jumps. Again using the inductive hypothesis we conclude
			\begin{align*}
				-\mathbb{E}[g(X_t^{x},\tilde{X_t}^{x_i})\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} |\mathcal {P} = \theta_t\mathcal {P}_0] &=-\sum_{y\neq x_i}\mathbb{E}[g(X_t^x,\tilde{X_t}^y)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} |\mathcal {P} =  \theta_t(\mathcal {P}_0\setminus\{(x_i,t_i)\})]P(x_i,y)\\
				&= -\sum_{y\in V}[g(x,y)-\Sigma_{t_{i-1}}(x,y)]\frac{1}{\pi(y)}P(x_i, y)\\
				&=\frac{1}{\pi(x_i)}{{\left[\Sigma_{t_{i-1}}P(x,x_i)-gP(x,x_i)\right]}}.
			\end{align*}
			We can conclude by expanding $gP(x,x_i) = g(I_V-\Delta)(x,x_i) = g(x,x_i)$.
		\end{itemize}
		In any case for all $x,y\in V$:
		\[
		\Sigma_{t_i}(x,y) = g(x,y) -\pi(y)\mathbb{E}[g(X_t^{x},\tilde{X_t}^{x_i})\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_\dagger > t\}} |\mathcal {P} = \theta_t\mathcal {P}_0],
		\]
		which finishes the induction.

\label{lem:law}

		\mathbb{P}(S_{k+1} = z | S_k = y, S_{k-1}, \dots, S_0) = \mathds{1}_{z = y}\mathds{1}_{y\neq x_{k+1}}+P(y,z)\mathds{1}_{y=x_{k+1}}.
		\]

		\mathbb{P}(S_{k+1} = z | S_k = x_{k+1}, S_{k-1}, \dots, S_0) = \mathbb{P}(X_{t_{k+1}} = z|X_{t_k} = x_{k+1}) =  P(x_{k+1},z).

Let $P_t^\mathcal {P}(x,y)$ be the transition kernel of \textit{killed random walk with clocks} $\mathcal {P}$. We recall the result Subsection \ref{subsec:comb} that there exists a universal constant $C$ such that
		\[
			\|\delta_{h_0}\mathscr{P}_t-\mathcal {N}(h_0,g)\mathscr{P}_t\|_{TV} \leq C\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta g_t\|_F],
		\]
		where
		\begin{align*}
			g_t(x,y) = g(x,y) - \Sigma_t(x,y) = \sum_{u\in V} P_t^\mathcal {P}(x,u)\mathbb{E}[g(u, X_t^y)|\mathcal {P}]\pi(y).
		\end{align*}
		Since $g\Delta = I_V$, for any fixed $x\in V$ the function $y\mapsto g(x,y)$ is super-harmonic. We can therefore apply our result on super-harmonic functions.\\
		Take $s<t$ and $\varepsilon >0$, to be fixed later. We know by Proposition \ref{prop:bound} that
		\[
		\forall u,y\in \Lambda_n,~~~ [P^{{{\left\lfloort+s\right\rfloor}}+1}g](u,y) - \varepsilon \leq \mathbb{E}[g(u, X_t^y)|\mathcal {P}] \leq [P^{{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}}g](u,y) + \varepsilon ,
		\] 
		holds with probability at least
		\[
		1-\frac{2|V|\|g\|_\infty}{\varepsilon}e^{-s^2/4t}.
		\]
		Notice that $P$ and $g$ commute (since $g= (I_V-P)^{-1}$). This means that again, for any fixed $x\in V$, the function $y\mapsto [P^{{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}}g](y,x) +\varepsilon$ is super-harmonic. By apply the Proposition \ref{prop:bound} again and following the same principle, we obtain the bound
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundG}
			\pi(y)P^{2{{\left\lfloort+s\right\rfloor}}+2}g(x,y) - 2\pi(y)\varepsilon \leq g_t(x,y) \leq \pi(y)P^{2{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}}g(x,y) + 2\pi(y)\varepsilon,
		\end{equation}
		which holds with probability at least
		\[
		1-\frac{4|V|\|g\|_\infty}{\varepsilon}e^{-s^2/4t}.
		\]
		We can find a polynomial bound for $\|g\|_\infty$, using the same estimate as the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:conc}:
		\begin{align*}
			\|g\|_\infty&\leq \frac{1}{\sqrt{|V|\pi(V)}}\sqrt{\sum_{x,y\in V}\pi(x)g(x,y)^2}\\
			&\leq\frac{1}{\lambda\sqrt{|V|\pi(V)}}\sqrt{\sum_{x,y\in V}\pi(x)(\Delta g)(x,y)^2}\\
			&\leq2|V|^{5/2}.
		\end{align*}
		If \eqref{eq:boundG} holds, then 
		\[
			\forall x,y\in V,~~~ {{\left| \Delta g_t(x,y) \right|}} \leq \pi(y){{\left| P^{2{{\left\lfloort+s\right\rfloor}}+2}g(x,y) - P^{2{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}}g(x,y) \right|}} + \pi(y){{\left| P^{2{{\left\lfloort+s\right\rfloor}}+1}g(x,y) - P^{2{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}+1}g(x,y) \right|}}+ 4\pi(y)\varepsilon.
		\]
		In the following we set $N=\max(|V|,\pi(V))$. Fix now $\varepsilon=1/N^2$ and $s=100\log N/\sqrt{\lambda}$ such that \eqref{eq:boundG} holds with probability at least $1-N^{-10}$. On that event we then get
		\[
			\|\Delta g_t\|_F \leq \|(P^{2{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}}-P^{2{{\left\lfloort+s\right\rfloor}}+2})g\pi^T\|_F + \|(P^{2{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}+1}-P^{2{{\left\lfloort+s\right\rfloor}}+1})g\pi^T\|_F + 4\varepsilon\sqrt{|V|\pi(V)}.
		\]
		Using the same principle as in the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:conc}, we can write these Frobenius norms as sum of eigenvalues. For an arbitrary $\beta>0$ we separate these eigenvalues depending on if they are larger than $(1+\beta)\lambda$ or not

			\|(P^{2{{\left\lfloort-s\right\rfloor}}}-P^{2{{\left\lfloort+s\right\rfloor}}+2})g\pi^T\|_F^2 &= \sum_{\mu\in{\rm Sp}\Delta}{{\left[\frac{(1-\mu)^{2(t-s)}}{\mu}{{\left(1-(1-\mu)^{4s+2}\right)}}\right]}}^2\\

			&\leq \frac{Ce^{-2x}}{\lambda^2N}{{\left(\beta\sqrt{\lambda}\log N+N^{-\beta}\right)}}^2,

		\[

		\]
