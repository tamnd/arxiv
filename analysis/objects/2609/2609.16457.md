<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
A Riemannian nilmanifold $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ is cyclic if and only if its
Lie algebra $\mathfrak{n}$ is at most two-step nilpotent and $J_{\mathfrak{z}}$ is an Abelian
subalgebra of $\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{a})$. Consequently, every cyclic Riemannian nilmanifold is
naturally reductive.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:wilson}
Let $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ be a Riemannian nilmanifold with data triple
$(\mathfrak{n},\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle,L)$, and let $G$ be its full isometry group.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $G$ contains a unique simply transitive nilpotent subgroup $N$; moreover,
$N$ coincides with the nilradical of $G$.
\item The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ of $G$ is the vector space direct sum
$\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}$, where the isotropy algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ is given by
\[
  \mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{n},\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)\cap \operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{n})\cap \operatorname{Ann}(L).
\]
Here $\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{n},\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ is the Lie algebra of skew-symmetric
endomorphisms of $\mathfrak{n}$, $\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{n})$ is the Lie algebra of derivations of $\mathfrak{n}$,
and $\operatorname{Ann}(L)=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{n}): \varphi|_{L}=0\}$ is the annihilator of $L$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:gordon}
Assume that $\mathfrak{n}$ is two-step nilpotent and that $J$ is injective. Then the
isotropy algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ of the full isometry group acts as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\mathfrak{h}$ leaves each of the subspaces $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{z}$ invariant.
\item For every $\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}$,
\[
  \varphi|_{\mathfrak{z}}=J^{-1}\circ \operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{a})}(\varphi|_{\mathfrak{a}})\circ J .
\]
In particular, the restriction map $\varphi\mapsto \varphi|_{\mathfrak{a}}$ is an
isomorphism of $\mathfrak{h}$ onto a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{a})$.
\item Let $\varphi\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{a})$. Then $\varphi$ extends to an element of $\mathfrak{h}$ if
and only if $[\varphi,J_{\mathfrak{z}}]\subset J_{\mathfrak{z}}$ and
\[
  J^{-1}\circ \operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{a})}(\varphi)\circ J \in \mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{z})\cap \operatorname{Ann}(L).
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:gordon}
A Riemannian nilmanifold $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ with data triple
$(\mathfrak{n},\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle,L)$ is naturally reductive if and only if $\mathfrak{n}$ is at
most two-step nilpotent and both of the following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $J_{\mathfrak{z}}$ is a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{a})$;
\item $\tau_Z\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{z}_0)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(L)$ for every $Z\in\mathfrak{z}_0$, where the linear
map $\tau_Z:\mathfrak{z}_0\to\mathfrak{z}_0$ is defined by
\[
  [J_Z,J_{Z'}]=J_{\tau_Z Z'}, \qquad Z,Z'\in\mathfrak{z}_0 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:tau}
The map $\tau_Z$ in \cref{thm:gordon} is well defined because $J$ is injective
on $\mathfrak{z}_0$. Note that condition (ii) in \cref{thm:gordon}  is equivalent to the
requirement that $(J_Z,\tau_Z,0|_{\ker J})\in\mathfrak{h}$ for every $Z\in\mathfrak{z}_0$; this is
exactly the content of \cref{lem:gordon}(iii). It follows directly from
$[J_Z,J_{Z'}]=-[J_{Z'},J_Z]$ and the injectivity of $J$ on $\mathfrak{z}_0$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tauantisym}
  \tau_Z Z'=-\tau_{Z'}Z, \qquad Z,Z'\in\mathfrak{z}_0 .
\end{equation}
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:twostep}
Every cyclic Riemannian nilmanifold is at most two-step nilpotent.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $(\mathfrak{n},\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle,L)$ be a data triple for the Riemannian nilmanifold
$(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$. Suppose that $\mathfrak{n}$ is $s$-step nilpotent and consider
the lower central series
\[
  \mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}^{(0)}\supset \mathfrak{n}^{(1)}\supset \mathfrak{n}^{(2)}\supset \cdots \supset
  \mathfrak{n}^{(s)}=\{0\}, \qquad \mathfrak{n}^{(i+1)}=[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}].
\]
Let $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ be cyclic with respect to a subgroup $G_0$ of the
full isometry group $G$ and a reductive decomposition $\mathfrak{g}_0=\mathfrak{h}_0\oplus\mathfrak{m}$,
where $\mathfrak{h}_0=\mathfrak{g}_0\cap\mathfrak{h}$. As in \cite[Theorem 4.3]{Gordon}, there exists a
linear map
\[
  \rho:\mathfrak{n}\longrightarrow \mathfrak{h}_0
\]
such that
\[
  \mathfrak{m}=\{X+\rho(X): X\in\mathfrak{n}\}.
\]
Define $\lambda:\mathfrak{n}\to\mathfrak{m}$ by $\lambda(X)=X+\rho(X)$. Since $\mathfrak{m}$ is a
complement to $\mathfrak{h}_0$ and $\mathfrak{g}_0=\mathfrak{h}_0\oplus\mathfrak{n}$ at the vector-space level, $\lambda$
is a linear isomorphism; moreover, relative to the induced inner product
$\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}$ on $\mathfrak{m}$, $\lambda$ is an isometry, that is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isom}
  \langle \lambda(X), \lambda(Y) \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=\langle X, Y \rangle, \qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{n} .
\end{equation}

For each $i\in\{0,1,\dots,s-1\}$, let $\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$ denote the orthogonal
complement of $\mathfrak{n}^{(i+1)}$ in $\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}$, so that
\[
  \mathfrak{n}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}\oplus \mathfrak{n}^{(i+1)}
\]
and
\[
  \mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{s-1}\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}
\]
is an orthogonal decomposition. Correspondingly, set
$\mathfrak{m}_{(i)}=\lambda(\mathfrak{n}_{(i)})$, so that $\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{s-1}\mathfrak{m}_{(i)}$.

We shall need three elementary facts.

\emph{Fact 1.} For every $i$, one has $[\mathfrak{h}_0,\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}]\subset\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$.
Indeed, $\mathfrak{h}_0\subset\mathfrak{h}\subset\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{n})$, and every derivation preserves the
lower central series: $\varphi(\mathfrak{n}^{(i+1)})=\varphi([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}])\subset
[\varphi(\mathfrak{n}),\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}]+[\mathfrak{n},\varphi(\mathfrak{n}^{(i)})]\subset[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}]=\mathfrak{n}^{(i+1)}$
by induction on $i$. Since $\mathfrak{h}_0\subset\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{n})$ consists of skew-symmetric
endomorphisms, it also preserves orthogonal complements, whence
$[\mathfrak{h}_0,\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}]\subset\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$.

\emph{Fact 2.} For $X\in\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$ and $Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{(j)}$, one has
$[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{n}^{(i+j+1)}$. This is the standard inclusion
$[\mathfrak{n}^{(i)},\mathfrak{n}^{(j)}]\subset\mathfrak{n}^{(i+j+1)}$ for the lower central series.

\emph{Fact 3.} For $X\in\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$ and $Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{(j)}$, the projection onto
$\mathfrak{m}$ of $[\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)]$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proj}
  [\,\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}=
  \lambda\bigl([X,Y]+[\rho(X),Y]-[\rho(Y),X]\bigr)
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.2}
  [\,\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}\ \in\
  \Bigl(\bigoplus_{k\ge i+j+1}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}\Bigr)\oplus \mathfrak{m}_{(j)}\oplus \mathfrak{m}_{(i)} .
\end{equation}
Indeed, since $\rho(X),\rho(Y)\in\mathfrak{h}_0$ and $\mathfrak{h}_0$ is a subalgebra,
\begin{align*}
  [\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)]
  &=[X+\rho(X),\,Y+\rho(Y)]\\
  &=[X,Y]+[\rho(X),Y]-[\rho(Y),X]+[\rho(X),\rho(Y)],
\end{align*}
where we used $[X,\rho(Y)]=-[\rho(Y),X]$. The last term lies in $\mathfrak{h}_0$, hence
projects to $0$ in $\mathfrak{m}$, while $[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{n}^{(i+j+1)}$ by Fact 2,
$[\rho(X),Y]\in\mathfrak{n}_{(j)}$ and $[\rho(Y),X]\in\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$ by Fact 1. Applying the
isometry $\lambda$ yields \eqref{eq:3.2}.

We now exploit the cyclic condition \eqref{eq:cyclic}. Fix $X\in\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$ and
let $B\in\bigoplus_{k<i}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}$ and $Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{(l)}$ with $l\ge i+1$. By
\eqref{eq:cyclic} applied to the triple $(\lambda(X),B,\lambda(Y))$,
\[
  \langle [\,\lambda(X),B\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, \lambda(Y) \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  =-\langle [\,B,\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, \lambda(X) \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
   -\langle [\,\lambda(Y),\lambda(X)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, B \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}} .
\]
We claim that both terms on the right-hand side vanish. Writing $B=\lambda(U)$
with $U\in\mathfrak{n}_{(k)}$ and $k<i$, \eqref{eq:3.2} gives
\[
  [\,B,\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}=[\,\lambda(U),\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}\in
  \Bigl(\bigoplus_{r\ge k+l+1}\mathfrak{m}_{(r)}\Bigr)\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{(l)}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{(k)} .
\]
Since $k<i$ and $l\ge i+1$, all indices occurring on the right are different
from $i$; hence this element is orthogonal to $\lambda(X)\in\mathfrak{m}_{(i)}$, and the
first term vanishes. Likewise, by \eqref{eq:3.2} with the roles of $X$ and $Y$
interchanged,
\[
  [\,\lambda(Y),\lambda(X)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}\in
  \Bigl(\bigoplus_{r\ge l+i+1}\mathfrak{m}_{(r)}\Bigr)\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{(i)}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{(l)} ,
\]
all of whose components have index at least $i$; in particular it is orthogonal
to $B\in\bigoplus_{k<i}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}$, so the second term vanishes. Therefore
\[
  \langle [\,\lambda(X),B\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, \lambda(Y) \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=0 .
\]
As $\lambda(Y)$ ranges over $\bigoplus_{l\ge i+1}\mathfrak{m}_{(l)}$ and the inner product
is nondegenerate, we deduce that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
  \Bigl[\lambda(X),\,\bigoplus_{k<i}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}\Bigr]_{\mathfrak{m}}\subset
  \bigoplus_{k\le i}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)} .
\end{equation}

Now take $X\in\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}$ and $Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{(j)}$ with $j<i$. On the one hand,
\eqref{eq:lower} gives
$[\,\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\bigoplus_{k\le i}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}$. On the other
hand, by \eqref{eq:3.2},
\[
  [\,\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}=
  \lambda([X,Y])+\lambda([\rho(X),Y])-\lambda([\rho(Y),X])
\]
with $\lambda([X,Y])\in\bigoplus_{k\ge i+j+1}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}\subset
\bigoplus_{k>i}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}$, $\lambda([\rho(X),Y])\in\mathfrak{m}_{(j)}$ and
$\lambda([\rho(Y),X])\in\mathfrak{m}_{(i)}$. Since the decomposition $\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus
\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}$ is direct, the component $\lambda([X,Y])$, which lies in
$\bigoplus_{k>i}\mathfrak{m}_{(k)}$, must vanish. As $\lambda$ is injective, we obtain
$[X,Y]=0$. Interchanging the roles of $i$ and $j$, we conclude that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crosszero}
  [\mathfrak{n}_{(i)},\mathfrak{n}_{(j)}]=\{0\}, \qquad i\neq j .
\end{equation}

Consequently,
\[
  \mathfrak{n}^{(1)}=[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]=\sum_{i,j}[\mathfrak{n}_{(i)},\mathfrak{n}_{(j)}]
  =\sum_{i}[\mathfrak{n}_{(i)},\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}] .
\]
Finally, using the Jacobi identity and \eqref{eq:crosszero},
\[
  \mathfrak{n}^{(2)}=[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{(1)}]
  =\sum_{i}\bigl[\mathfrak{n},[\mathfrak{n}_{(i)},\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}]\bigr]
  \subset \sum_{i}\Bigl(\bigl[[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}],\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}\bigr]
  +\bigl[\mathfrak{n}_{(i)},[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}]\bigr]\Bigr) .
\]
Now $[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}]\subset\mathfrak{n}^{(i+1)}=\bigoplus_{k\ge i+1}\mathfrak{n}_{(k)}$, and by
\eqref{eq:crosszero} we have $[\mathfrak{n}_{(k)},\mathfrak{n}_{(i)}]=\{0\}$ for all $k\ge i+1$
(since $k\neq i$). Hence both terms vanish, and $\mathfrak{n}^{(2)}=\{0\}$. This proves
that $\mathfrak{n}$ is at most two-step nilpotent.

\label{lem:suff}
Let $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ be a Riemannian nilmanifold whose Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{n}$ is two-step nilpotent. If $J_{\mathfrak{z}}\subset\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{a})$ is Abelian, then
$(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ is cyclic.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We exhibit explicitly a reductive decomposition making the cyclic condition
hold. Let $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}$ be the full isometry algebra given by
\cref{lem:wilson}, and define
\[
  \mathfrak{h}_0:=\{(J_Z,\,0|_{\mathfrak{z}}): Z\in\mathfrak{z}\}.
\]
We first check that $\mathfrak{h}_0\subset\mathfrak{h}$. For $Z\in\mathfrak{z}$ and $Z'\in\mathfrak{z}_0$ one has,
by the Abelian hypothesis,
\[
  [J_Z,J_{Z'}]=0=J_{0}\, ,
\]
so that the map $\tau_Z$ of \cref{thm:gordon} vanishes on $\mathfrak{z}_0$; moreover
$\tau_Z=0\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{z}_0)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(L)$. By \cref{lem:gordon}(iii),
$(J_Z,0|_{\mathfrak{z}})\in\mathfrak{h}$, as required. Note also that $\mathfrak{h}_0$ is an Abelian
subalgebra of $\mathfrak{h}$, since $[J_Z,J_{Z'}]=0$ for all $Z,Z'\in\mathfrak{z}$.

Define a linear map $\rho:\mathfrak{n}\to\mathfrak{h}_0$ by
\[
  \rho(a+Z)=-\tfrac12 (J_Z,\,0|_{\mathfrak{z}}), \qquad a\in\mathfrak{a},\ Z\in\mathfrak{z},
\]
and set $\lambda(X)=X+\rho(X)$, $\mathfrak{m}=\lambda(\mathfrak{n})$. Then
\[
  \mathfrak{m}=\Bigl\{a+Z-\tfrac12(J_Z,0|_{\mathfrak{z}}) : a\in\mathfrak{a},\ Z\in\mathfrak{z}\Bigr\},
\]
and $\mathfrak{g}_0:=\mathfrak{h}_0\oplus\mathfrak{m}$ is a reductive decomposition of the Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{g}_0$ of a subgroup $G_0$ of the isometry group. Indeed, $\mathfrak{h}_0\cap\mathfrak{m}=\{0\}$
and, since $\mathfrak{h}_0$ is Abelian and $\rho(\mathfrak{n})=\mathfrak{h}_0$, we have
$[\mathfrak{h}_0,\mathfrak{h}_0]\subset\mathfrak{h}_0$ and $[\mathfrak{h}_0,\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{m}$; a direct computation
using \eqref{eq:Jmap} also shows $[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_0$, as is required for a
reductive decomposition.

Let us verify the cyclic condition. Take
\[
  X=a+x-\tfrac12 J_x,\quad
  Y=b+y-\tfrac12 J_y,\quad
  Z=c+z-\tfrac12 J_z ,
\]
with $a,b,c\in\mathfrak{a}$ and $x,y,z\in\mathfrak{z}$, where we write $J_x$ for the element
$(J_x,0|_{\mathfrak{z}})\in\mathfrak{h}_0$. Since $[J_x,J_y]=0$, $[J_x,b]=J_x b\in\mathfrak{a}$,
$[J_x,y]=0$ (because $\mathfrak{h}_0$ acts trivially on $\mathfrak{z}$) and $[x,b]=[a,y]=[x,y]=0$
(because $\mathfrak{z}$ is central), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracketXY}
  [X,Y]=[a,b]-\tfrac12 J_x b+\tfrac12 J_y a \ \in\ \mathfrak{n} .
\end{equation}
Projecting onto $\mathfrak{m}$ (equivalently, applying $\lambda$ to the $\mathfrak{n}$-component)
and using the isometry property \eqref{eq:isom}, we get
\begin{align}
  \langle [\,X,Y\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, Z \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  &=\Bigl\langle [a,b]-\tfrac12 J_x b+\tfrac12 J_y a,\ c+z\Bigr\rangle
  \nonumber\\
  &=\langle [a,b], z \rangle-\tfrac12\langle J_x b, c \rangle+\tfrac12\langle J_y a, c \rangle
  \nonumber\\
  &=\langle [a,b], z \rangle-\tfrac12\langle [b,c], x \rangle-\tfrac12\langle [c,a], y \rangle,
  \label{eq:cyccomp}
\end{align}
where in the last step we used \eqref{eq:Jmap}, namely
$\langle J_x b, c \rangle=\langle [b,c], x \rangle$ and $\langle J_y a, c \rangle=\langle [a,c], y \rangle=-\langle [c,a], y \rangle$.

Now cyclically permuting $(X,Y,Z)$, i.e.\ permuting
$(a,x)\mapsto(b,y)\mapsto(c,z)\mapsto(a,x)$, we obtain
\[
  \langle [\,Y,Z\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, X \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  =\langle [b,c], x \rangle-\tfrac12\langle [c,a], y \rangle-\tfrac12\langle [a,b], z \rangle
\]
and
\[
  \langle [\,Z,X\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, Y \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  =\langle [c,a], y \rangle-\tfrac12\langle [a,b], z \rangle-\tfrac12\langle [b,c], x \rangle.
\]
Adding the three identities, the coefficient of each of the three terms
$\langle [a,b], z \rangle$, $\langle [b,c], x \rangle$ and $\langle [c,a], y \rangle$ is
$1-\tfrac12-\tfrac12=0$. Hence
\[
  \langle [\,X,Y\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, Z \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  +\langle [\,Y,Z\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, X \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  +\langle [\,Z,X\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, Y \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=0 ,
\]
which is precisely \eqref{eq:cyclic}. Thus $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ is cyclic.

\label{lem:necc}
Let $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ be a cyclic Riemannian nilmanifold. Then $\mathfrak{n}$ is
at most two-step nilpotent and $J_{\mathfrak{z}}$ is Abelian.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By \cref{prop:twostep}, $\mathfrak{n}$ is at most two-step nilpotent, so the $J$-map is
defined. Let $\mathfrak{g}_0=\mathfrak{h}_0\oplus\mathfrak{m}$ be a reductive decomposition with respect to
which $(N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ is cyclic, and let $\rho:\mathfrak{n}\to\mathfrak{h}_0$ be a linear
map with $\mathfrak{m}=\{\lambda(X): X\in\mathfrak{n}\}$, where $\lambda(X)=X+\rho(X)$ and
$\lambda$ is an isometry as in \eqref{eq:isom}.

We first derive an identity that will be used throughout. For
$X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{n}$, expanding as in Fact 3 of the proof of \cref{prop:twostep},
\begin{align*}
  \langle [\,\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, \lambda(Z) \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  &=\Bigl\langle [X,Y]+[\rho(X),Y]-[\rho(Y),X],\, Z\Bigr\rangle\\
  &=\langle [X,Y], Z \rangle+\langle [\rho(X),Y], Z \rangle-\langle [\rho(Y),X], Z \rangle .
\end{align*}
Since $\rho(Y)\in\mathfrak{h}_0\subset\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{n})$ is skew-symmetric,
$\langle [\rho(Y),X], Z \rangle=\langle \rho(Y)X, Z \rangle=-\langle X, \rho(Y)Z \rangle
=-\langle [\rho(Y),Z], X \rangle$. Therefore
\[
  \langle [\,\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)\,]_{\mathfrak{m}}, \lambda(Z) \rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
  =\langle [X,Y], Z \rangle+\langle [\rho(X),Y], Z \rangle+\langle [\rho(Y),Z], X \rangle.
\]
Summing over the cyclic permutations of $(X,Y,Z)$ and using the cyclic condition
\eqref{eq:cyclic}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.3}
  0=\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\Bigl(\langle [X,Y], Z \rangle+2\,\langle [\rho(X),Y], Z \rangle\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ denotes the sum over the three cyclic permutations of
$(X,Y,Z)$.

\emph{Step 1: the action of $\rho$ on $\mathfrak{a}$.} Take $X,Y\in\mathfrak{a}$ and $Z\in\mathfrak{z}$.
Since $\mathfrak{z}$ is central, $[Y,Z]=[Z,X]=0$ and $[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{z}$. Moreover
$[\rho(X),Y]\in\mathfrak{a}$ and $[\rho(Y),Z]\in\mathfrak{z}$, because $\rho(X),\rho(Y)\in\mathfrak{h}$
preserve both $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{z}$ (see \cref{lem:gordon}(i)). Hence
$\langle [\rho(X),Y], Z \rangle=0$ and $\langle [\rho(Y),Z], X \rangle=0$, while
$\langle [Y,Z], X \rangle=\langle [Z,X], Y \rangle=0$. Equation \eqref{eq:3.3} thus reduces to
\[
  \langle [X,Y], Z \rangle+2\langle [\rho(Z),X], Y \rangle=0 .
\]
Using \eqref{eq:Jmap}, $\langle [X,Y], Z \rangle=\langle J_Z X, Y \rangle$, so that
\[
  \langle J_Z X, Y \rangle+2\langle \rho(Z)X, Y \rangle=0
\]
for all $X,Y\in\mathfrak{a}$ and $Z\in\mathfrak{z}$. By nondegeneracy of the inner product on
$\mathfrak{a}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rhoZ}
  \rho(Z)X=-\tfrac12 J_Z X, \qquad X\in\mathfrak{a},\ Z\in\mathfrak{z} .
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: the action of $\rho$ on $\mathfrak{z}_0$.} Let $Z\in\mathfrak{z}_0$. By
\cref{lem:gordon}(ii) and \eqref{eq:rhoZ}, the restriction of $\rho(Z)$ to
$\mathfrak{z}_0$ is determined by $\rho(Z)|_{\mathfrak{a}}=-\tfrac12 J_Z$; precisely, there is a
linear map $\tau_Z:\mathfrak{z}_0\to\mathfrak{z}_0$ with
\[
  [J_Z,J_{Z'}]=J_{\tau_Z Z'}, \qquad Z'\in\mathfrak{z}_0 ,
\]
such that
\[
  \rho(Z)=-\tfrac12\,(J_Z,\tau_Z,0|_{\ker J})\in\mathfrak{h} .
\]
Here $\tau_Z\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{z}_0)$, because $\rho(Z)\in\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{n})$ is
skew-symmetric on $\mathfrak{z}$.

\emph{Step 3: vanishing of $\tau$.} Take $X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{z}_0$. Since $\mathfrak{z}_0\subset\mathfrak{z}$
is central, $[X,Y]=[Y,Z]=[Z,X]=0$, and \eqref{eq:3.3} gives
\[
  0=\langle [\rho(X),Y], Z \rangle+\langle [\rho(Y),Z], X \rangle+\langle [\rho(Z),X], Y \rangle.
\]
Using $\rho(X)|_{\mathfrak{z}_0}=-\tfrac12\tau_X$ and the analogous formulas for $Y,Z$,
this becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tausum}
  0=\langle \tau_X Y, Z \rangle+\langle \tau_Y Z, X \rangle+\langle \tau_Z X, Y \rangle.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:tauantisym} we have $\tau_X Y=-\tau_Y X$ and $\tau_Z X=-\tau_X Z$.
Using also $\tau_X,\tau_Y,\tau_Z\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{z}_0)$, we compute
\[
  \langle \tau_X Y, Z \rangle=-\langle \tau_Y X, Z \rangle=\langle X, \tau_Y Z \rangle=\langle \tau_Y Z, X \rangle
\]
and
\[
  \langle \tau_Z X, Y \rangle=-\langle \tau_X Z, Y \rangle=\langle Z, \tau_X Y \rangle=\langle \tau_X Y, Z \rangle.
\]
Substituting into \eqref{eq:tausum} yields
$3\langle \tau_X Y, Z \rangle=0$, hence $\langle \tau_X Y, Z \rangle=0$ for all $Z\in\mathfrak{z}_0$, and
therefore $\tau_X Y=0$ for all $X,Y\in\mathfrak{z}_0$. Thus $\tau_X=0$ for every
$X\in\mathfrak{z}_0$, and consequently
\[
  [J_X,J_Y]=J_{\tau_X Y}=0
\]
for all $X,Y\in\mathfrak{z}_0$. Since $J$ vanishes on $\ker J$, we conclude that
$[J_Z,J_{Z'}]=0$ for all $Z,Z'\in\mathfrak{z}$, i.e.\ $J_{\mathfrak{z}}$ is Abelian.

Let $(\widetilde N,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ be a connected and simply connected
cyclic Riemannian nilmanifold without Euclidean factor. Then the isotropy
subgroup of its full isometry group is
\[
  H=\widetilde\Gamma\cdot H_0 ,
\]
where $H_0\cong U(r_1)\times\cdots\times U(r_k)$ is the compact connected
normal subgroup described in \eqref{eq:H0}, and $\widetilde\Gamma$ is a finite
group acting on $\mathfrak{z}$ by $\varphi(Z_p)=\pm Z_q$ ($r_p=r_q$) and on $\mathfrak{a}$ by the
explicit formulas above. In particular,
\[
  \dim H=\sum_{p=1}^{k} r_p^{2}
\]
and the component group $H/H_0$ is a subgroup of the signed permutation group
of the weight lines $\{\pm Z_1,\dots,\pm Z_k\}$ preserving the multiplicities
$r_p$ and the norms $\|Z_p\|$.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:isom}
For the three-dimensional Heisenberg group, $k=r_1=1$, so
$H_0\cong U(1)\cong S^{1}$ and $\widetilde\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$ (generated by
$Z\mapsto-Z$, acting on $\mathfrak{a}$ by $X\mapsto X$, $Y\mapsto-Y$). Thus
$H\cong O(2)$, the isotropy group of the round metric on $\mathbb{R}^2$.
 More generally, when all weights have equal norm and all
multiplicities are $1$, $\widetilde\Gamma$ is the full signed permutation group
$(\mathbb{Z}_2)^{k}\rtimes S_k$.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:heisenberg}
Let $\mathfrak{n}=\operatorname{span}\{X,Y,Z\}$ with the single nontrivial bracket $[X,Y]=Z$, and
endow $\mathfrak{n}$ with the inner product for which $\{X,Y,Z\}$ is orthonormal. Let
$N=H_3$ be the corresponding simply connected Lie group. Then $\mathfrak{z}=\mathbb{R} Z$ is the
center, $\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{span}\{X,Y\}$, and \eqref{eq:Jmap} gives
\[
  J_Z X=Y,\qquad J_Z Y=-X.
\]
Thus $J_{\mathfrak{z}}=\mathbb{R}\cdot J_Z$ is one-dimensional, hence Abelian. By
\cref{thm:main}, $(H_3,\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ is cyclic and naturally reductive.

Let us make the cyclic decomposition explicit. In the notation of
\cref{lem:suff}, $\mathfrak{h}_0=\mathbb{R}\cdot(J_Z,0)$ and
\[
  \mathfrak{m}=\operatorname{span}\Bigl\{X,\ Y,\ Z-\tfrac12 J_Z\Bigr\}.
\]
The full isometry group has isotropy $H\cong O(2)$, in agreement with
\cref{thm:isom} and \cref{rem:isom}.

<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{ex:diagonal}
More generally, let $\mathfrak{a}=\mathbb{R}^{2k}$ with orthonormal basis
$\{X_1,Y_1,\dots,X_k,Y_k\}$, let $\mathfrak{z}=\mathbb{R}^{k}$ with orthonormal basis
$\{Z_1,\dots,Z_k\}$, and define a two-step nilpotent Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{z}$ by
\[
  [X_p,Y_p]=\mu_p Z_p,\qquad \mu_p>0,
\]
all other brackets being zero. Then $J_{Z_p}$ acts on the plane
$\operatorname{span}\{X_p,Y_p\}$ by $J_{Z_p}X_p=\mu_p Y_p$, $J_{Z_p}Y_p=-\mu_p X_p$, and acts
trivially on the other planes. Hence $J_{Z_p}J_{Z_q}=J_{Z_q}J_{Z_p}$ for all
$p,q$ and $J_{\mathfrak{z}}$ is Abelian. By \cref{thm:main}, the associated simply
connected nilpotent Lie group is cyclic and naturally reductive for every choice
of the positive constants $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_k$. This family is exactly the
Riemannian product (with rescaled factors) of $k$ copies of the
three-dimensional Heisenberg group.

<!-- example-3: body_md -->
\label{ex:lowdim}
The simply connected nilpotent Lie groups of dimension at most four are the
Abelian groups $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the three-dimensional Heisenberg group $H_3$, the direct
product $H_3\times\mathbb{R}$, and the four-dimensional filiform (three-step nilpotent)
group. Abelian groups and $H_3$ (and its product with $\mathbb{R}$) are cyclic by
\cref{ex:heisenberg}. By \cref{prop:twostep}, the
four-dimensional filiform group, being three-step nilpotent, carries no cyclic
left-invariant metric. This recovers, for nilmanifolds, part of the
classification of simply connected cyclic homogeneous Riemannian manifolds of dimension at
most four obtained in \cite{Gadea2016}.

<!-- example-4: body_md -->
\label{ex:htype}
A Heisenberg-type (or $H$-type) Lie algebra is a two-step nilpotent
$\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{z}$ with inner product satisfying
\[
  J_Z^{2}=-\|Z\|^{2}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{a}}, \qquad Z\in\mathfrak{z},
\]
equivalently $J_Z J_{Z'}+J_{Z'}J_Z=-2\langle Z, Z' \rangle\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{a}}$; see
\cite{Kaplan}. If $\dim\mathfrak{z}\ge 2$ and $Z,Z'\in\mathfrak{z}$ are nonzero and orthogonal,
then $J_Z J_{Z'}=-J_{Z'}J_Z$, so that $J_Z$ and $J_{Z'}$ anticommute; in
particular they do not commute, and $J_{\mathfrak{z}}$ is not Abelian. By
\cref{thm:main}, such a group is not cyclic. Thus the Heisenberg-type condition
and the cyclic condition are, in a precise sense, opposite extremes: the former
forces the $J$-maps to satisfy the Clifford (anti)commutation relations, while
the latter forces them to commute. The three-dimensional Heisenberg group, where
$\dim\mathfrak{z}=1$, is the unique overlap of the two families.
Indeed,  a Heisenberg-type group is naturally reductive if and only if $\dim\mathfrak{z}=1,3$ \cite{Kaplan}.
