\label{thm:warm-uniform}
Let $\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ be a convex body with $B(0,1)\subset\mathcal{K}$ and
$\pi$ uniform on $\mathcal{K}$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:uniform-annealing} outputs

\label{thm:warm-main}
Let $V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}$ be a convex function, and suppose
that $\pi\propto e^{-V}$ satisfies $B(0,1)\subseteq\mathsf{L}_{g}$.
Given access to an evaluation oracle for $V$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:lifted-annealing}

\label{lem:score-identity} Let $\nu$ be a probability measure, $q:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}$
measurable, and define an exponential tilt $\nu_{s}(\mathrm{d} x)\propto e^{-sq(x)}\,\nu(\mathrm{d} x)$
on an interval where the tilt is well-defined. If $f_{s}$ is differentiable
and differentiation can be passed under the defining integrals, then
$\partial_{s}\mathbb{E}_{s}f_{s}=\mathbb{E}_{s}[\partial_{s}f_{s}]-\operatorname{cov}_{s}(f_{s},q)$,
where the identity is interpreted entrywise when $f_{s}$ is vector-
or matrix-valued.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Recall an elementary property of the score function: $\mathbb{E}_{s}[\partial_{s}\log\nu_{s}]=\int\partial_{s}\nu_{s}=0$
due to $\partial_{s}\nu_{s}=\nu_{s}\,\partial_{s}\log\nu_{s}$. Differentiating
the expectation under the integral gives
\[
\partial_{s}\mathbb{E}_{s}f_{s}=\int(\partial_{s}f_{s})\,\nu_{s}+\int f_{s}\,\partial_{s}\nu_{s}=\mathbb{E}_{s}[\partial_{s}f_{s}]+\mathbb{E}_{s}[f_{s}\,\partial_{s}\log\nu_{s}]=\mathbb{E}_{s}[\partial_{s}f_{s}]+\operatorname{cov}_{s}(f_{s},\partial_{s}\log\nu_{s})\,,
\]
where the last equality follows from $\mathbb{E}_{s}[\partial_{s}\log\nu_{s}]=0$.
The claim follows from $\partial_{s}\log\nu_{s}=-(q-\mathbb{E}_{s}q)$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
Let $u_{i}=u_{i}(s)$ be a suitable unit eigenvector of $M_{s}$ such
that $M_{s}u_{i}=\lambda_{i}(s)\,u_{i}$, and let $Z_{i}:=u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}Z$.
Then,
\begin{equation}
\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}(Z^{2}_{i},q)\,.\label{eq:unshifted-eigenvalue-derivative}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:score-identity} for the centered covariance,
\begin{equation}
B_{s}'=\mathbb{E}_{s}\bigl[\partial_{s}\bigl((X-b_{s})(X-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\bigr]-\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl((X-b_{s})(X-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}},q\bigr)=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl((X-b_{s})(X-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}},q\bigr)\,,\label{eq:unshifted-covariance-derivative}
\end{equation}
where the terms containing $\partial_{s}b_{s}$ vanish as $\mathbb{E}_{s}(X-b_{s})=0$.
Since $M_{s}=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}$, \eqref{eq:unshifted-covariance-derivative}
implies
\begin{equation}
\partial_{s}M_{s}=-M^{1/2}\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl((X-b_{s})(X-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}},q\bigr)\,M^{1/2}\,.\label{eq:Ms-derivative}
\end{equation}

By perturbation theory for symmetric matrix paths \cite[Theorem 5.4 and Theorem 6.8]{Kato95perturbation},
the eigenvalues $\lambda_{i}(s)$ of $M_{s}$ may be labeled differentiably,
and at each $s$, one may choose an orthonormal eigenbasis $(u_{i})^{n}_{i=1}$
of $M_{s}$ such that $\partial_{s}\lambda_{i}(s)=u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}(\partial_{s}M_{s})u_{i}$.
Hence, by \eqref{eq:Ms-derivative},
\begin{equation}
\partial_{s}\lambda_{i}(s)=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl\{\bigl(u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}M^{1/2}(X-b_{s})\bigr)^{2},q\bigr\}\,.\label{eq:deriv-lda}
\end{equation}
Using $M^{1/2}(X-b_{s})=M^{1/2}_{s}Z$ and $M^{1/2}_{s}u_{i}=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(s)}\,u_{i}$,
\[
u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}M^{1/2}(X-b_{s})=u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}M^{1/2}_{s}Z=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(s)}\,Z_{i}\,.
\]
Substituting this into \eqref{eq:deriv-lda} yields $\partial_{s}\lambda_{i}(s)=-\lambda_{i}(s)\operatorname{cov}_{s}(Z^{2}_{i},q)$,
which completes the proof.

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:property-ms-ys}
The barycenter $y_{s}=M^{1/2}b_{s}$ and covariance $M_{s}=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}$
satisfy the following bounds for $s\geq0$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $P_{s}+\lVerty_{s}\rVert^{2}\le N_{s}\leq s\cdot\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}$
\item $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})$
\item $y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}$
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For every unit vector $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$, by Cauchy--Schwarz followed
by the definition of $\mathsf{Q}_{n}$ for the quadratic form $\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}Z^{2}_{i}$,
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\,\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)\Bigr|=\Bigl|\operatorname{cov}_{s}\Bigl(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}Z^{2}_{i},q\Bigr)\Bigr|\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}}\,\lVert\alpha\rVert\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q}\,.
\]
Hence, $\sum_{i}[\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)]^{2}\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q$.
Substituting this into \eqref{eq:sum-log2},
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i}\log^{2}\frac{\lambda_{i}(s)}{\lambda_{i}(0)}\le s\int^{s}_{0}\sum_{i}\bigl(\partial_{r}\log\lambda_{i}(r)\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d} r\leq s\mathsf{Q}_{n}\int^{s}_{0}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{r}}q\,\mathrm{d} r=s\mathsf{Q}_{n}\Delta_{s}\,.\label{eq:unshifted-action}
\end{equation}
Using $\Delta_{s}\leq N_{s}$ and substituting this into \eqref{eq:unshifted-deficit},
$N^{2}_{s}\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\sum_{i}\log^{2}\frac{\lambda_{i}(s)}{\lambda_{i}(0)}\leq s\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}N_{s}$.
Hence,
\[
(P_{s}+\lVerty_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq)N_{s}\leq s\cdot\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}\,.
\]
Thus, $P_{s},N_{s},\lVerty_{s}\rVert^{2}=O(s)$ as desired. Substituting
this into \eqref{eq:final-bound}, we obtain $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})$
and $y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
The proof controls the eigenvalues
of $M_{s}$ \emph{collectively}, rather than individually. For example,
considering the total continuous change in the trace (specifically,
$N_{s}$ and $P_{s}$ terms), we obtain a useful bound. If we attempt
to do this for each eigenvalue, then we may obtain some bound on each,
but summing them up in a na\"ive way would lead to a factor of $n$.
A further consequence is the second item of Lemma~\ref{lem:property-ms-ys}
(i.e., the stability of the Schatten $2$-norm along the Gaussian
tilt):
\[
\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,.
\]
As noted earlier, this type of bound does not hold for individual
eigenvalues such as the largest eigenvalue (i.e., the operator norm).

Assume $M\succ0$ and
abbreviate $q=q_{u}$. Define $v_{s}:=\operatorname{Var}_{s}q$,  $G:=\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu$,
and
\[
b_{s}:=\mathbb{E}_{s}X\,,\qquad B_{s}:=\operatorname{cov}_{s}X\,,\qquad M_{s}:=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}\,,\qquad y_{s}:=M^{1/2}b_{s}+u\,.
\]
By isotropy, we have $B_{0}=\mathrm{Id}$, $M_{0}=M$, and $y_{0}=u$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:score-identity}, we similarly have
\[
(\mathbb{E}_{s}q)'=-v_{s}\,,\qquad\Delta_{s}:=\mathbb{E}_{0}q-\mathbb{E}_{s}q=\int^{s}_{0}v_{r}\,\mathrm{d} r\,,\qquad B_{s}'=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl((X-b_{s})(X-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}},q\bigr)\,.
\]
Let $\lambda_{i}(s)$ be an absolutely continuous labeling of the
eigenvalues of $M_{s}$. The calculation in the preceding subsection
gives the same identity: $\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}(Z^{2}_{i},q)$.
Hence,
\[
\sum_{i}\bigl(\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)\bigr)^{2}\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}v_{s}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{i}\log^{2}\frac{\lambda_{i}(s)}{\lambda_{i}(0)}\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}s\Delta_{s}\,.
\]

Define $N_{s}$ and $P_{s}$ as before. Since $\mathbb{E}_{s}q=\operatorname{tr} M_{s}+\lVerty_{s}\rVert^{2}$,
\[
\Delta_{s}=N_{s}-P_{s}+\lVertu\rVert^{2}-\lVerty_{s}\rVert^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta_{s},P_{s},\lVerty_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVertu\rVert^{2}+N_{s}\,.
\]
Then, $N_{s}\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s\Delta_{s}}\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s\,(\lVertu\rVert^{2}+N_{s})}$.
Solving this,
\[
N_{s}\le\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s+\lVertu\rVert\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s}\,.
\]
Combining this with the bounds on $P_{s}$ and $y_{s}$ gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\frac{\lVertu\rVert^{2}}{s}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\lesssim\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\frac{\lVertu\rVert^{2}}{s}\,.
\]
Substituting these bounds into \eqref{eq:var-decomp},
\begin{equation}
v_{s}\lesssim(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+\mathsf{Q}^{2}_{n})\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,\frac{\lVertu\rVert^{2}}{s}\,.\label{eq:general-bound}
\end{equation}
The last term is the only singular term introduced by the shift, and
it becomes vacuous as $s$ goes to $0$. However, at $s=0$, the same
decomposition gives $v_{0}\leq(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+4)\,G$. To combine the two
regimes, we propagate the endpoint bound to small $s$.

  {\normalfont\normalsize\bfseries} 
{Handling small $s$.}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:score-identity}, $v_{s}'=-\mathbb{E}_{s}[(q-\mathbb{E}_{s}q)^{3}]$.
By the polynomial reverse H\"older inequality \cite[Theorem 7]{CW01distributional},
$|v_{s}'|\lesssim v^{3/2}_{s}$. Let $\bar{v}:=(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+4)\,G$
and $s_{0}=c\bar{v}^{-1/2}$ for sufficiently small $c>0$. Since
$M\succ0$ and $\nu_{s}$ is full-dimensional, $v_{s}>0$. Integrating
the differential inequality, for $s\in[0,s_{0}]$, we have $v_{s}\leq4\bar{v}$.
Since $u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu\leq\lVertu\rVert^{2}\lVertM\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\leq\lVertu\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G}$,
we have $G=\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\lVertu\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G}$.
Hence, $v_{s}\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,(\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\lVertu\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G})$. 

For $s\geq s_{0}$, the pointwise bound \eqref{eq:general-bound}
yields
\[
v_{s}\lesssim(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+\mathsf{Q}^{2}_{n})\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,\lVertu\rVert^{2}\sqrt{(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+4)\,G}\,.
\]
Therefore,
\[
v_{s}\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})^{2}\,\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\lVertu\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G}\bigr)\,.
\]
This proves the claim when $M\succ0$. For $M\succeq0$, apply the
result to $M+\varepsilon I_{n}$ and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$;
dominated convergence follows from the finite fourth moment of $\nu$.

\label{lem:renyi-exact} Let $q>1$ and $s\ge s'\ge0$. Then, 
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{s'})\le\frac{q}{2}\,(s-s')^{2}\sup_{t\in[s',qs-(q-1)\,s']}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{t}}(\lVertX\rVert^{2})\,.\label{eq:renyi-curvature}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $Z(s):=\int e^{-s\,\lVertx\rVert^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)$. For $\Delta:=s-s'$,
the density ratio is $\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s'}}(x)=\frac{Z(s')}{Z(s)}\,e^{-\Delta\,\lVertx\rVert^{2}}$,
and
\[
\int\bigl(\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s'}}\bigr)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{s'}=\frac{Z(s')^{q-1}\,Z\bigl(q\,s-(q-1)\,s'\bigr)}{Z(s)^{q}}\,.
\]
Thus,
\[
\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{s'})=\frac{F\bigl(s+(q-1)\,\Delta\bigr)-qF(s)+(q-1)\,F(s-\Delta)}{q-1}\,.
\]
Let $I:=[s',s+(q-1)\Delta]$ and $V_{I}:=\sup_{t\in I}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{t}}(\lVertX\rVert^{2})=\sup_{t\in I}F''(t)$.
By Taylor expansion,
\begin{align*}
F\bigl(s+(q-1)\,\Delta\bigr) & \le F(s)+(q-1)\,\Delta F'(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,(q-1)^{2}\Delta^{2}\,,\\
F(s-\Delta) & \le F(s)-\Delta F'(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,\Delta^{2}\,.
\end{align*}
Substituting these estimates into $\mathsf{R}_{q}$,
\begin{align*}
\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{s'}) & \leq\frac{F(s)+(q-1)\,\Delta F'(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,(q-1)^{2}\Delta^{2}-qF(s)+(q-1)\,\bigl(F(s)-\Delta F'(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,\Delta^{2}\bigr)}{q-1}\\
 & =\frac{qV_{I}}{2}\,\Delta^{2}\,,
\end{align*}
which completes the proof.

\label{rem:comparison-closeness}
\cite[Lemma 4.5]{KV25faster} bounds $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu_{\sigma^{2}}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)})$
by $qD^{2}\alpha^{2}/\sigma^{2}$ for compactly supported logconcave
$\nu$ with support diameter $D$. Later, \cite[Lemma 4.2]{KV26zeroLC}
gives the bound $q^{2}R^{2}\alpha^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})$ for the same
quantity when $\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\lVertX\rVert^{2}]=R^{2}\leq D^{2}$.

Our estimate~\eqref{eq:renyi-curvature} yields an improved bound.
For every $t>0$, the measure $\nu_{t}$ is $2t$-strongly logconcave.
By Brascamp--Lieb or \eqref{eq:pi} with $C_{\mathsf{PI}}(\nu_{t})\leq1/(2t)$,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{t}}(\lVertX\rVert^{2})\leq\frac{1}{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}}[4\,\lVertX\rVert^{2}]=\frac{2}{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}}[\lVertX\rVert^{2}]\leq\frac{2R^{2}}{t}\,,
\]
where the last inequality follows from $\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}}[\lVertX\rVert^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\lVertX\rVert^{2}]$.
For $s=(2\sigma^{2})^{-1}$ and $s'=[2\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)]^{-1}$
with $t\geq s'$, we have
\[
\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu_{\sigma^{2}}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}(1+\alpha)})\leq\frac{q}{2}\,\bigl(\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)}\bigr)^{2}\,\frac{2R^{2}}{s'}=\frac{qR^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)}\,.
\]
This improves the $q^{2}$-dependence of the second bound to $q$.
It also recovers the compact-support bound in the present convex-body
setting (due to $R^{2}\leq D^{2}$).

\label{prop:truncated-gaussian-relay}
Let $\mu_{\sigma^{2}}\propto e^{-\lVertx\rVert^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\,\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{K}}(x)$
for a convex body $\mathcal{K}\supset B(0,1)$ given by a membership oracle.
Given $\varepsilon>0$, $\eta\in(0,1/2)$, $q\ge q_{0}\ge2$, and
an initial distribution $\mu_{0}$ with $M_{q_{0}}:=\lVert\nicefrac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}}{\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sigma^{2}}}\rVert_{L^{q_{0}}(\mu_{\sigma^{2}})}\leq10$,
initialize $\mathsf{PS}_{\textup{Gauss}}$ with $\mu_{0}$ and iterate it $N=\widetilde{O}(n^{2}\sigma^{2}\log\frac{q}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\eta})$
times with $h=(10n^{2}\log Z)^{-1}$ and $\tau=Z^{2}\log^{3}Z$, where
$Z=16NM_{2}/\eta$. Assume $M_{q_{0}}\leq10$ and $q_{0}\geq1+\log\tau$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The latter condition is ensured by $q_{0}\ge2\vee O(\log\frac{N}{\eta})$.
\item With probability at least $1-\eta$, $\mathsf{PS}_{\textup{Gauss}}$ completes all $N$
iterations without failure. Conditioned on this event, its output
has law $\nu$ satisfying $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}})\le\varepsilon+2\log\frac{1}{1-\eta}$,

Let $\Phi(s):=\log\int_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}\exp(-n\,\lVertx\rVert^{2}/2-st)\,\mathrm{d} x\mathrm{d} t$.
As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:renyi-exact}, we obtain
\[
\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{(1+\alpha)s})=\frac{\Phi\bigl(s\,(1-(q-1)\,\alpha)\bigr)-q\Phi(s)+(q-1)\,\Phi\bigl((1+\alpha)\,s\bigr)}{q-1}\,.
\]
Set $s_{-}:=s\,(1-(q-1)\,\alpha)>0$, $s_{+}:=s\,(1+\alpha)$, $I:=[s_{-},s_{+}]$,
and $V_{I}:=\sup_{u\in I}\Phi''(u)=\sup_{u\in I}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{u}}T$.
By Taylor expansion,
\begin{align*}
\Phi(s_{-}) & \leq\Phi(s)-(q-1)\,\alpha s\Phi'(s)+\frac{(q-1)^{2}\alpha^{2}s^{2}}{2}\,V_{I}\,,\\
\Phi(s_{+}) & \leq\Phi(s)+\alpha s\Phi'(s)+\frac{\alpha^{2}s^{2}}{2}\,V_{I}\,.
\end{align*}
Substituting these estimates into the $\mathsf{R}_{q}$-identity yields
\[
\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{(1+\alpha)s})\leq\frac{(q-1)^{2}\alpha^{2}s^{2}V_{I}+(q-1)\,\alpha^{2}s^{2}V_{I}}{2\,(q-1)}=\frac{q\alpha^{2}s^{2}}{2}\,V_{I}\,.

h_{u}(x) & \le\int^{a+b}_{a-10}e^{-ut}\,\mathrm{d} t\le(b+10)\,e^{-u(a-10)}\,.
\end{align*}

u\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{u}}T}\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{u}}\bigl(Q(X)+uT\bigr)}+\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{u}}Q}\lesssim\sqrt{n}\,.

\[

\]
This completes the proof.
