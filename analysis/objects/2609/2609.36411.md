<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let \(A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m,n}\) be arbitrary, and let
\(\psi:\mathbb R_+\to\mathbb R_+\) be non-increasing and satisfy \eqref{eq:D}{}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-mass}
 \mathcal L^m( W_A(\psi))=
 \begin{cases}
  0&\displaystyle\text{if }
     \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}M_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)<\infty,\\[6pt]
  1&\displaystyle\text{if }
     \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}M_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)=\infty.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:volume-clustering}
The summand in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has the form
\[
 M_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)
 =\frac{2^{kn}\psi(2^k)^m}
        {N_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)}
 =\frac{\text{Khintchine--Groshev block volume}}
        {\text{homogeneous clustering multiplicity}}.
\]
The numerator is the \(k\)-th term of the dyadic
Khintchine--Groshev series in \eqref{eq:KG-volume}, while the
denominator is the homogeneous clustering multiplicity.
The explicit inclusion of this factor is the principal feature distinguishing
our criterion from the earlier formulations discussed above.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- rem-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:geometric-scale}
The base \(2\) is inessential.  For any \(\beta>1\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} remains valid with \(2^k\) replaced by
\(\beta^k\), provided that
\(\psi(\beta^{k+1})\leqslant\varrho\psi(\beta^k)\) for some
\(\varrho\in(0,1)\) and all sufficiently large \(k\).
<!-- end rem-2 -->
<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:pgn-form}
Uniformly in \(k\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pgn-mass}
 M_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)
 \asymp
 \prod_{i=1}^{d}\min\{R_k,\lambda_i(a_{t_k}\Lambda_A)\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the zero--one law in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is determined
by the convergence or divergence of
\begin{equation*} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
 \prod_{i=1}^{d}\min\{R_k,\lambda_i(a_{t_k}\Lambda_A)\}.
\end{equation*}
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Equation~\eqref{eq:pgn-mass} follows directly from
Lemma~\ref{lem:minima-mass}.

<!-- rem-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:kurzweil}
Suppose that \(A\) is badly approximable and that \(\psi\) satisfies
the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  By the Dani correspondence
\cite[Theorem~2.20]{Dan85}, the forward orbit
\(\{a_t\Lambda_A:t\geqslant0\}\) is bounded in the space of unimodular lattices.
Hence all of its successive minima are bounded above and
away from zero, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:pgn-form} gives, for all sufficiently large \(k\),
\[
 M_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)
 \asymp
 \min\{1,R_k^d\}
 =\min\{1,2^{kn}\psi(2^k)^m\}.
\]
This series converges if and only if the dyadic series in
\eqref{eq:KG-volume} does.  Indeed, both diverge if
\(2^{kn}\psi(2^k)^m\geqslant1\) for infinitely many \(k\); otherwise their
tails agree.  Thus
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} recovers the
dyadically regular case of Kurzweil's theorem.

Since the dimensions \(m,n\), the matrix \(A\), and the function \(\psi\)

<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:normalized-error}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, for
\(\mathcal L^m\)-almost every \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb T^m\),
\[
 \mathfrak c_{A,\psi}(\mathbf b)=
 \begin{cases}
  0&\text{if }\sum_kM_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)=\infty,\\
  +\infty&\text{if }\sum_kM_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)<\infty.
 \end{cases}
\]
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
To apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to the rescaled functions
\(j^{-1}\psi\) and \(j\psi\) for every \(j\geqslant1\), first note that its series criterion is
invariant under fixed positive rescaling.  Indeed, \(c\psi\) satisfies
\eqref{eq:D}{} with the same contraction factor, and for every fixed \(c>0\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:dilation} gives
\begin{equation*} M_A(c\psi(2^k),2^k)\asymp_cM_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)
\end{equation*}
In the divergent case,
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives full measure to
\( W_A(j^{-1}\psi)\) for every \(j\geqslant1\).  Hence their countable intersection
has full measure and is contained in
\(\{\mathfrak c_{A,\psi}=0\}\), which proves the divergent case.

In the convergent case, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} shows that
\( W_A(j\psi)\) is null for every \(j\geqslant1\).  Outside
their countable union, for each \(j\geqslant1\) the inequality
\[
 \left|A\mathbf q-\mathbf b\right|_{\mathbb Z^m}<j\psi(\left|\mathbf q\right|)
\]
has only finitely many solutions.  Thus
\(\mathfrak c_{A,\psi}(\mathbf b)\geqslant j\) for every \(j\), so
\(\{\mathfrak c_{A,\psi}=+\infty\}\) has full measure, proving the
convergent case.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:Bad-classification}
For every \(A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m,n}\), uniformly in \(k\geqslant0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-equivalence}
 M_A(\psi_{n/m}(2^k),2^k)
 =\frac{1}{N_A(\psi_{n/m}(2^k),2^k)}
 \asymp\Delta_k(A).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for \(\mathcal L^m\)-almost every \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb T^m\),
\[
 \mathfrak c_A(\mathbf b)=
 \begin{cases}
  0&\text{if }\sum_k\Delta_k(A)=\infty,\\
  +\infty&\text{if }\sum_k\Delta_k(A)<\infty.
 \end{cases}
\]
Equivalently, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), the sets \(\operatorname{Bad}_A(\varepsilon)\),
\(\operatorname{Bad}_A\), and \(\operatorname{Bad}_A(\infty)\) are all null in the first case and
all conull in the second.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Take \(\psi=\psi_{n/m}\).  Then
\[
 \psi_{n/m}(2^{k+1})=2^{-n/m}\psi_{n/m}(2^k),
\]
so \eqref{eq:D}{} holds with contraction factor \(2^{-n/m}\).  Moreover,
\(R_k=1\) and \(t_k=nk\log2\).  The definition of \(M_A\) and
Corollary~\ref{cor:pgn-form} give \eqref{eq:critical-equivalence}.
The conclusion about \(\mathfrak c_A\) follows from
Corollary~\ref{cor:normalized-error}.  If the almost-sure value is zero,
each \(\operatorname{Bad}_A(\varepsilon)\), their countable union \(\operatorname{Bad}_A\), and
\(\operatorname{Bad}_A(\infty)\subset\operatorname{Bad}_A\) are null.  If the almost-sure value is
infinite, \(\operatorname{Bad}_A(\infty)\), and hence all the other displayed sets,
are conull.

(equivalently, every) \(\varepsilon>0\) if and only if
\(\sum_k\Delta_k(A)=\infty\).  It also settles the Lebesgue-measure case of
\cite[Question~1.3]{MRS24}.

\item \emph{Hausdorff dimension.}  Although not needed here, it is worth
mentioning that \cite[Theorem~1.3]{KKL25} proves
\[
 A\text{ is \emph{singular on average}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \dim_H\operatorname{Bad}_A(\varepsilon)=m\quad\text{for some }\varepsilon>0.
\]
In dimension one, see
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BKLR21}; see also \cite{LSS19}.
For results on \(\operatorname{Bad}_A(\infty)\), see also \cite{ET11,Kim25}.
\end{enumerate}

\label{cor:nonsingular}
If \(A\) is nonsingular, then
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta_k(A)=\infty.
\]
Consequently, \(\mathcal L^m(\operatorname{Bad}_A)=0\), equivalently

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By the Dani correspondence \cite[Theorem~2.14]{Dan85}, singularity of
\(A\) is equivalent to divergence of the forward orbit
\(\{a_t\Lambda_A:t\geqslant0\}\) in the space of unimodular lattices.  Since
\(A\) is nonsingular, this orbit meets some compact set at arbitrarily
large times.  By Mahler's compactness criterion, its first minimum is
bounded away from zero there.  Thus there are \(c_0>0\) and arbitrarily
large \(t\) such that
\[
 \lambda_1(a_t\Lambda_A)\geqslant c_0.
\]
Since the first minimum changes by at most a fixed multiplicative factor
over bounded time intervals, sampling at \(t=nk\log2\) gives \(c>0\)
and infinitely many \(k\) such that
\(\lambda_1(a_{nk\log2}\Lambda_A)\geqslant c\).  Since
\(\lambda_i\geqslant\lambda_1\) for every
\(i\), at each such index
\[

\]
so \(\sum_k\Delta_k(A)=\infty\), and the remaining conclusions follow
from Proposition~\ref{prop:Bad-classification}.

\label{cor:hausdorff}
Let \(A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m,n}\), let \(\psi:\mathbb R_+\to\mathbb R_+\) be non-increasing
and satisfy \eqref{eq:D}{}, and let \(0<s<m\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hausdorff-cases}
 \mathcal H^s\bigl( W_A(\psi)\bigr)=
 \begin{cases}
  0&\displaystyle\text{if }
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
  \frac{2^{kn}\psi(2^k)^s}
       {N_A(\psi(2^k),2^k)}<\infty,\\[10pt]
  \infty&\displaystyle\text{if }
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
  \frac{2^{kn}\psi(2^k)^s}
       {N_A(\psi(2^k)^{s/m},2^k)}=\infty.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
These are separate implications; the two series conditions need not be
complementary.
<!-- end cor-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The convergence case is proved together with that of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in Section~\ref{subsec:convergence-case}.

For the divergence case, put \(\varphi=\psi^{s/m}\).
If \(\varrho\) is a contraction factor for \(\psi\), then
\(\varphi(2^{k+1})\leqslant\varrho^{s/m}\varphi(2^k)\) for all sufficiently
large \(k\).  Thus \(\varphi\) is non-increasing and satisfies
\eqref{eq:D}{}, while the divergent series in \eqref{eq:Hausdorff-cases} is
\[
 \sum_k M_A(\varphi(2^k),2^k).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} therefore gives
\(\mathcal L^m( W_A(\varphi))=1\).

For each \(\mathbf0\ne\mathbf q\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 B_{\mathbf q}:=B(\langle A\mathbf q\rangle,\psi(\left|\mathbf q\right|)),
 \qquad r(B_{\mathbf q}):=\psi(\left|\mathbf q\right|),
\]
where \(r(B_{\mathbf q})\) denotes the radius of \(B_{\mathbf q}\).
The torus with the supremum metric satisfies
\(\mathcal H^m(B(\mathbf b,r))\asymp r^m\) for all sufficiently small \(r\).
We apply the general mass transference principle
\cite[\S6.1, Theorem~3]{BV06} with
\(X=\mathbb T^m\), \(g(r)=r^m\), and \(f(r)=r^s\).  In its notation,
\[
 B_{\mathbf q}^m=B_{\mathbf q},
 \qquad
 B_{\mathbf q}^s=B(\langle A\mathbf q\rangle,\varphi(\left|\mathbf q\right|)).
\]
Since \(r(B_{\mathbf q})\to0\), \(r\mapsto r^{s-m}\) is monotonic, and
\(\limsup_{\left|\mathbf q\right|\to\infty}B_{\mathbf q}^s= W_A(\varphi)\) is conull,
the mass transference principle gives
\[
 \mathcal H^s( W_A(\psi))
 =\mathcal H^s\!\left(\limsup_{\left|\mathbf q\right|\to\infty}B_{\mathbf q}^m\right)
 =\infty.
\]

\label{cor:power-dimension}
Let \(A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m,n}\), and suppose that
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta_k(A)=\infty.
\]
Then, for every \(v>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-dimension}
 \dim_H W_A(\psi_v)
 =\min\left\{m,\frac nv\right\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \eqref{eq:power-dimension} holds for every nonsingular
matrix \(A\).
<!-- end cor-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For every \(v>0\), the function \(\psi_v\) is non-increasing and
\[
 \psi_v(2^{k+1})=2^{-v}\psi_v(2^k),
\]
so it satisfies \eqref{eq:D}{}.

Suppose first that \(0<v\leqslant n/m\).  Since
\(\psi_v(q)\geqslant\psi_{n/m}(q)\) for \(q\geqslant1\),
\[
  W_A(\psi_{n/m})\subseteq W_A(\psi_v),
\]
while Proposition~\ref{prop:Bad-classification} shows that
\( W_A(\psi_{n/m})\) is conull.  It follows that
\( W_A(\psi_v)\) is conull and has Hausdorff dimension \(m\), as
asserted in \eqref{eq:power-dimension}.

Now suppose that \(v>n/m\), and put \(s=n/v\), so \(0<s<m\).  Since
\[
 2^{kn}\psi_v(2^k)^s=1,
 \qquad
 \psi_v(2^k)^{s/m}=\psi_{n/m}(2^k),
\]
we have, by \eqref{eq:critical-equivalence},
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{2^{kn}\psi_v(2^k)^s}
      {N_A(\psi_v(2^k)^{s/m},2^k)}
 =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{1}{N_A(\psi_{n/m}(2^k),2^k)}
 \asymp\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Delta_k(A)=\infty.
\]
The divergence part of Corollary~\ref{cor:hausdorff} therefore gives
\(\mathcal H^{n/v}( W_A(\psi_v))=\infty\), and hence
\(\dim_H W_A(\psi_v)\geqslant n/v\).

For the reverse inequality, fix \(s'\) with \(n/v<s'<m\).  The zero
vector counted in \(N_A\) gives
\(N_A(\psi_v(2^k),2^k)\geqslant1\), while \(n-vs'<0\).  Hence
\[
 \sum_k
 \frac{2^{k(n-vs')}}{N_A(\psi_v(2^k),2^k)}
 \leqslant\sum_k2^{k(n-vs')}<\infty,
\]
and the convergence part of Corollary~\ref{cor:hausdorff} gives
\(\mathcal H^{s'}( W_A(\psi_v))=0\).  Since this holds for every
\(s'>n/v\), it follows that \(\dim_H W_A(\psi_v)\leqslant n/v\).
The last assertion follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:nonsingular}.

\label{prop:scalar}
Let \(\psi:\mathbb R_+\to\mathbb R_+\) be non-increasing and satisfy \eqref{eq:D}{}.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-equivalence}
 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}M_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\ \sum_{q=q_k}^{q_{k+1}-1}
 \min\{\psi(q),\left|q_k\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\}<\infty.
\end{equation}
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For \(q_s\leqslant q<q_{s+1}\), put
\[
 h(q):=\min\{\psi(q),\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\}.
\]
The function \(h\) is non-increasing, so Cauchy condensation gives
\[
 \sum_qh(q)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_j2^jh(2^j)<\infty.
\]
For all large \(j\), we have \(\psi(2^j)<1/2\);
choose \(s\) with \(q_s\leqslant2^j<q_{s+1}\).

Suppose first that the series on the left of
\eqref{eq:scalar-equivalence} converges.
The best approximation property of the convergent denominator \(q_s\)
gives \(\left|q\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\geqslant\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\) for
\(1\leqslant q<q_{s+1}\) \cite[\S2.2]{FK16}.
Hence the points \(q\alpha-p\in[-\psi(2^j),\psi(2^j)]\), where
\(q,p\in\mathbb Z\) and \(0\leqslant q\leqslant2^j\), are
\(\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\)-separated.
Counting them and restoring both signs gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)
 &\leqslant1+\frac{4\psi(2^j)}{\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}}
 \leqslant\frac{5\psi(2^j)}{h(2^j)},\\
 M_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)&\geqslant\frac15 2^jh(2^j).
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand series therefore converges by condensation.

Conversely, suppose that \(\sum_qh(q)<\infty\).
The estimate in \cite[proof of Lemma~2.1]{FK16} implies
\(\sum_sq_s\psi(q_s)<\infty\).
By the homogeneous counting bound \cite[\S6.1, (6.9)]{BHV20},
adjoining the zero vector and using symmetry gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)
 &\geqslant1+2\left\lfloor
       \min\left\{\psi(2^j)q_{s+1},\frac{2^j}{q_s}\right\}
       \right\rfloor\\
 &\gg\max\left\{1,
       \min\left\{\frac{\psi(2^j)}{\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}},
                   \frac{2^j}{q_s}\right\}
       \right\},
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used \(q_{s+1}\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\geqslant1/2\).
If \(\psi(2^j)\leqslant\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\), then
\(h(2^j)=\psi(2^j)\), and \(N_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)\geqslant1\) gives
\[
 M_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)
 =\frac{2^j\psi(2^j)}{N_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)}
 \leqslant2^j\psi(2^j)=2^jh(2^j).
\]
If \(\psi(2^j)>\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\), then
\(h(2^j)=\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}\), and the preceding counting bound gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)
 &\ll\frac{2^j\psi(2^j)}
 {\min\left\{\dfrac{\psi(2^j)}{\left|q_s\alpha\right|_{\mathbb Z}},
                    \dfrac{2^j}{q_s}\right\}}\\
 &=\max\{2^jh(2^j),q_s\psi(2^j)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, in both cases,
\[
 M_\alpha(\psi(2^j),2^j)\ll2^jh(2^j)+q_s\psi(2^j).
\]
Finally, let \(j_s=\lceil\log_2q_s\rceil\), so that \(2^{j_s}\geqslant q_s\).
For all sufficiently large \(s\), iterating \eqref{eq:D}{} and using
monotonicity gives
\[
 \psi(2^{j_s+\ell})\leqslant\varrho^\ell\psi(2^{j_s})
 \leqslant\varrho^\ell\psi(q_s)\qquad(\ell\geqslant0).
\]
Since every \(j\) with \(q_s\leqslant2^j<q_{s+1}\) satisfies \(j\geqslant j_s\),
we obtain
\[
 \sum_{\{j:q_s\leqslant2^j<q_{s+1}\}}q_s\psi(2^j)
 \leqslant q_s\psi(q_s)\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\varrho^\ell
 =\frac{q_s\psi(q_s)}{1-\varrho}.
\]
Summing over \(s\), and using the convergence of
\(\sum_sq_s\psi(q_s)\) and \(\sum_j2^jh(2^j)\), proves convergence
of the left-hand series.

\label{lem:basic-lattice-counting}
Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a full-rank lattice.  For every centrally
symmetric convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) centered at the origin and \(C\geqslant1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lattice-doubling}
 \#(\Lambda\cap CK)
 \ll_C\#(\Lambda\cap K).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every \(R>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standard-lattice-count}
 \#(\Lambda\cap[-R,R]^d)
 \asymp\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,R/\lambda_i(\Lambda)\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->

\(CK\) and the sublattice \(a\Lambda\) gives
\[
 \#(\Lambda\cap CK)
 \leqslant a^d\#\bigl(\Lambda\cap(2C/a)K\bigr)
 \leqslant a^d\#(\Lambda\cap K),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:lattice-doubling}.

For the lower bound in \eqref{eq:standard-lattice-count}, choose
linearly independent \(\mathbf v_i\in\Lambda\) with
\(\left|\mathbf v_i\right|\leqslant\lambda_i(\Lambda)\).
The distinct vectors \(\sum_i a_i\mathbf v_i\), where \(a_i\in\mathbb Z\) and
\(\lvert a_i\rvert\leqslant R/(d\lambda_i(\Lambda))\), lie in \([-R,R]^d\).

 \geqslant\prod_{i=1}^{d}
 \left(2\left\lfloor\frac{R}{d\lambda_i(\Lambda)}\right\rfloor+1\right)
 \gg\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,R/\lambda_i(\Lambda)\}.
\]
The upper bound follows from \cite[Theorem~1.5]{Henk02} applied to
\([-R,R]^d\), whose successive minima are \(\lambda_i(\Lambda)/R\).

\label{lem:dense-translations}
Let \(\mathbf v_1,\ldots,\mathbf v_r\in\mathbb T^m\), and suppose that
\(\Gamma:=\sum_{j=1}^r\mathbb Z\mathbf v_j\) is dense in \(\mathbb T^m\).  If a measurable set
\(E\subset\mathbb T^m\) satisfies
\[
 E-\mathbf v_j\subset E\qquad(1\leqslant j\leqslant r),
\]
then \(E\) is null or conull.
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
By translation invariance of Lebesgue measure, each inclusion is an equality
modulo null sets.  The indicator \(f:=\mathbf1_E\) satisfies
\(f(\,\cdot+\mathbf v_j)=f\) almost everywhere for every \(j\).  The Fourier
coefficients of \(f\) are
\[
 \widehat f(\mathbf k)
 :=\int_{\mathbb T^m}f(\mathbf x)e^{-2\pi i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}
 \,d\mathcal L^m(\mathbf x)\qquad(\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^m).
\]
For every \(j\), a change of variables gives
\[
 \bigl(1-e^{2\pi i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v_j}\bigr)
 \widehat f(\mathbf k)=0.
\]
Fix \(\mathbf k\ne0\).  If \(\widehat f(\mathbf k)\ne0\), the identity
above would force
\(e^{2\pi i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v_j}=1\) for every \(j\), and hence
\(e^{2\pi i\mathbf k\cdot\gamma}=1\) for every \(\gamma\in\Gamma\).
Since \(\Gamma\) is dense and the character
\(\mathbf x\mapsto e^{2\pi i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\) is continuous, it would follow
that this character is identically \(1\) on \(\mathbb T^m\), contradicting
\(\mathbf k\ne0\).  Hence \(\widehat f(\mathbf k)=0\) for every
\(\mathbf k\ne0\).  Uniqueness of Fourier series gives
\(f=\widehat f(\mathbf0)\) almost everywhere.  Since \(f\) is an indicator
function, \(E\) is null or conull.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:zero-one}
Let \(A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m,n}\), and let \(\psi(N)\to0\) be non-increasing.
Then
\[
 \mathcal L^m( W_A(\psi))\in\{0,1\}.
\]
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We first decompose according to which coordinates escape to infinity.
Write \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\).
For \(\varnothing\ne I\subset[n]\),
\(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_i)_{i\in I}\in\{\pm1\}^{I}\), and
\(\mathbf c=(c_j)_{j\notin I}\in\mathbb Z^{I^c}\), set
\[
 \mathcal Q_R(I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c)
 :=\left\{\mathbf q\in\mathbb Z^n:
          q_j=c_j\ (j\notin I),\ \varepsilon_iq_i\geqslant R\ (i\in I)\right\}
\]
For this set, define
\[
 E_{I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c}
 :=\bigcap_{R=1}^{\infty}
   \bigcup_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_R(I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c)}
   B(\langle A\mathbf q\rangle,\psi(\left|\mathbf q\right|)).
\]
\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Claim 1.}
We have the decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:claim-one-decomposition}
  W_A(\psi)=
 \bigcup_{\varnothing\ne I\subset[n]}
 \ \bigcup_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\{\pm1\}^I}
 \ \bigcup_{\mathbf c\in\mathbb Z^{I^c}}
 E_{I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Claim 1]
To prove the inclusion \(\subseteq\), suppose that
\(\mathbf b\in W_A(\psi)\), and choose pairwise distinct solutions
\(\mathbf q^{(s)}\) of \eqref{eq:psi-approximable}, so that
\[
 \mathbf b\in B(\langle A\mathbf q^{(s)}\rangle,\psi(\lvert\mathbf q^{(s)}\rvert))
 \qquad(s\geqslant1).
\]
Successively passing to subsequences in
each coordinate, we may arrange that every \(q_i^{(s)}\) is either constant
or tends to infinity with a fixed sign.  Let \(I\) be the set of coordinates
of the latter type, let \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) record their signs, and let
\(\mathbf c\) record the constant coordinates.  At least one coordinate is of
the latter type, since otherwise \(\mathbf q^{(s)}\) would be constant in \(s\).
Hence \(I\ne\varnothing\).  For every \(R\), all
sufficiently large \(s\) satisfy
\(\mathbf q^{(s)}\in\mathcal Q_R(I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c)\); hence
\(\mathbf b\in E_{I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c}\).

For the inclusion \(\supseteq\), suppose that
\(\mathbf b\in E_{I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c}\).  Then for every \(R\) there is
\(\mathbf q_R\in\mathcal Q_R(I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c)\) such that
\[
 \mathbf b\in B(\langle A\mathbf q_R\rangle,\psi(\left|\mathbf q_R\right|)).
\]
Since
\(I\ne\varnothing\), we have \(\left|\mathbf q_R\right|\geqslant R\).  Thus the collection
\(\{\mathbf q_R:R\geqslant1\}\) is infinite and consists of nonzero vectors, so
\(\mathbf b\in W_A(\psi)\).
\end{proof}

The union in \eqref{eq:claim-one-decomposition} is countable.  Hence, to prove
the proposition, it suffices to establish the following claim.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Claim 2.}
For each triple \((I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c)\) as above, the set
\(E_{I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c}\) is null or conull.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Claim 2]
Fix a triple \((I,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\mathbf c)\), and abbreviate the
corresponding sets by \(\mathcal Q_R\) and \(E\).  Put
\[
 \Gamma_I:=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb Z\langle A\mathbf e_i\rangle,
 \qquad H_I:=\overline{\Gamma_I},
 \qquad
 \mathbf x_{\mathbf c}:=\left\langle
     \sum_{j\notin I}c_jA\mathbf e_j\right\rangle.
\]
For every \(\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_R\), we have
\[
 \langle A\mathbf q\rangle
 =\mathbf x_{\mathbf c}+\sum_{i\in I}q_i\langle A\mathbf e_i\rangle
 \in\mathbf x_{\mathbf c}+\Gamma_I.
\]
Moreover, if \(\mathbf b\in E\), then for every \(R\) there is a
\(\mathbf q_R\in\mathcal Q_R\) such that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(\mathbf b,\mathbf x_{\mathbf c}+H_I)
 \leqslant \psi(\left|\mathbf q_R\right|)\leqslant\psi(R).
\]
Since \(\psi(R)\to0\), we obtain
\(E\subset\mathbf x_{\mathbf c}+H_I\).  If \(H_I\ne\mathbb T^m\), this proper closed
coset has Lebesgue measure zero, and hence \(E\) is null.

It remains to consider \(H_I=\mathbb T^m\).  Fix \(i\in I\).  For
\(\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_{R+1}\), put
\[
 \mathbf q'=\mathbf q-\varepsilon_i\mathbf e_i.
\]
Then \(\mathbf q'\in\mathcal Q_R\) and
\(\left|\mathbf q'\right|\leqslant\left|\mathbf q\right|\).  With
\(\mathbf v_i:=\varepsilon_i\langle A\mathbf e_i\rangle\), translation invariance of
the metric and monotonicity of \(\psi\) give
\begin{align*}
 B(\langle A\mathbf q\rangle,\psi(\left|\mathbf q\right|))-\mathbf v_i
 &=B(\langle A\mathbf q'\rangle,\psi(\left|\mathbf q\right|))\\
 &\subset B(\langle A\mathbf q'\rangle,\psi(\left|\mathbf q'\right|)).
\end{align*}
Taking the union over \(\mathbf q\in\mathcal Q_{R+1}\), and then the intersection
over \(R\), gives
\[
 E-\mathbf v_i\subset E.

We first relate \(\Phi_\Gamma(r,T)\) to the covolume of
\(a_t\Gamma\).  The map \(a_t\) scales the first \(m\) standard basis
vectors by \(R/r\) and the remaining \(n\) by \(R/T\).  Hence

\(a_t\Lambda_A\), and let \(\mu_1\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\mu_\sigma\) be those of

the Euclidean balls of radii \(1\) and \(\sqrt d\).

\[
 \Phi_\Gamma(r,T)
 \asymp R^{d-\sigma}\operatorname{covol}(a_t\Gamma)

the covolume both equal to one.

 &\asymp\prod_{i=1}^{d}\min\{R,\lambda_i\}

Lemma~\ref{lem:wedge-mass} gives \(M_j\ll\Phi_j\).

For \((k,\ell)\in\mathcal R\), let \(h=\ell-k\).
Evaluating the maximum at \(p_k\) and \(p_\ell\), respectively, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-lower-bounds}
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)
 &\geqslant \left|\omega_{\Gamma,p_k}\right|r_k^{b_k}T_\ell^{a_k}
 =\Phi_k2^{a_kh},\\
 \Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)
 &\geqslant\Phi_\ell\left(\frac{r_k}{r_\ell}\right)^{b_\ell}
 \geqslant\Phi_\ell\varrho^{-b_\ell h},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the last inequality uses \eqref{eq:radius-decay}.
Consequently,
\begin{equation*} \frac{M_kM_\ell}{\Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)}
 \ll\min\{M_\ell2^{-a_kh},\,
             M_k\varrho^{b_\ell h}\}.
\end{equation*}

For fixed \(\ell\), sum the first bound over \(k\) with \(a_k>0\).
Since then \(a_k\geqslant1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-geometric-arm}
 \sum_{\substack{k:(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R\\a_k>0}}
 M_\ell2^{-a_k(\ell-k)}
 \leqslant M_\ell\sum_{h\geqslant1}2^{-h}
 \ll M_\ell.
\end{equation}
Among the remaining pairs, fix \(k\) and sum the second bound over
\(\ell\) with \(b_\ell>0\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-geometric-arm}
 \sum_{\substack{\ell:(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R\\a_k=0,\ b_\ell>0}}
 M_k\varrho^{b_\ell(\ell-k)}
 \leqslant M_k\sum_{h\geqslant1}\varrho^h
 \ll M_k.
\end{equation}
It remains to consider
\[
 \mathcal R_0:=\left\{(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R:a_k=b_\ell=0\right\}
\]
when this set is nonempty.  For \((k,\ell)\in\mathcal R_0\), the
equalities \(a_k=b_\ell=0\) give \(p_k=\sigma-n\) and \(p_\ell=m\).
Since \(p_-(\sigma)\leqslant p_j\leqslant p_+(\sigma)\),
\[
 0\leqslant p_-(\sigma)\leqslant\sigma-n,\qquad
 m\leqslant p_+(\sigma)\leqslant\sigma.
\]
Thus \(\sigma\geqslant\max\{m,n\}\), and in fact
\(p_k=p_-(\sigma)\) and \(p_\ell=p_+(\sigma)\).
As \(\omega_\Gamma\ne0\), the maximum in \eqref{eq:Phi} is positive.
Hence the maximizing components
\(\omega_{\Gamma,\sigma-n}\) and \(\omega_{\Gamma,m}\) are nonzero.
Consequently,
\[
 \Phi_k=\left|\omega_{\Gamma,\sigma-n}\right|r_k^{d-\sigma},\qquad
 \Phi_\ell=\left|\omega_{\Gamma,m}\right|T_\ell^{d-\sigma}.
\]
Since \(d-\sigma>0\), \eqref{eq:radius-decay} gives geometric decay of
\(\Phi_k\) along the left endpoints of \(\mathcal R_0\), with ratio at
most \(\varrho^{d-\sigma}\), while \(T_\ell=2^\ell\) gives geometric
growth of \(\Phi_\ell\) along its right endpoints, with ratio at least
\(2^{d-\sigma}\).
With \(a_k=b_\ell=0\), \eqref{eq:Phi-lower-bounds} gives
\[
 \Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)\geqslant\max\{\Phi_k,\Phi_\ell\}.
\]
First fix \(k\) and consider the pairs with \(\Phi_\ell\leqslant\Phi_k\).
Using \(\Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)\geqslant\Phi_k\) and then
\(M_\ell\ll\Phi_\ell\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-residual-arm}
 \sum_{\substack{\ell:(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R_0\\\Phi_\ell\leqslant\Phi_k}}
 \frac{M_kM_\ell}{\Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)}
 \ll \frac{M_k}{\Phi_k}
       \sum_{\substack{\ell:(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R_0\\\Phi_\ell\leqslant\Phi_k}}
       \Phi_\ell
 \ll M_k.
\end{equation}
The last bound follows by summing in decreasing \(\ell\)-order:
the values \(\Phi_\ell\) grow geometrically and do not exceed \(\Phi_k\).

For pairs with \(\Phi_k<\Phi_\ell\), fix \(\ell\).
Using \(\Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)\geqslant\Phi_\ell\) and then
\(M_k\ll\Phi_k\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-residual-arm}
 \sum_{\substack{k:(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R_0\\\Phi_k<\Phi_\ell}}
 \frac{M_kM_\ell}{\Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)}
 \ll \frac{M_\ell}{\Phi_\ell}
       \sum_{\substack{k:(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R_0\\\Phi_k<\Phi_\ell}}
       \Phi_k
 \ll M_\ell.
\end{equation}
For the last bound, we sum in increasing \(k\)-order, as the values
\(\Phi_k\) decrease geometrically and are less than \(\Phi_\ell\).

These four cases partition \(\mathcal R\).  Summing
\eqref{eq:first-geometric-arm}, \eqref{eq:second-geometric-arm},
\eqref{eq:first-residual-arm}, and \eqref{eq:second-residual-arm}
over their respective fixed indices in \(\partial\mathcal R\) gives
\[
 \sum_{(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R}
 \frac{M_kM_\ell}{\Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)}
 \ll\sum_{j\in\partial\mathcal R}M_j.\qedhere
\]

<!-- lem-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:incidence}
For every \(1\leqslant j\leqslant N\),
\[
 \#\left\{\Gamma:j\in\partial\mathcal R_\Gamma\right\}\leqslant2d.
\]
<!-- end lem-11 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
If \(j\in\partial\mathcal R_\Gamma\), then
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_{j,\ell}\) for some \(\ell>j\), or
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_{k,j}\) for some \(k<j\).
For the first family, we have
\[
 \mathcal B(r_j,T_{\ell_1})\subset \mathcal B(r_j,T_{\ell_2}),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{j,\ell_1}\subset\Gamma_{j,\ell_2}
 \qquad(j<\ell_1<\ell_2).
\]
For the second, monotonicity of \(r_k\) gives
\[
 \mathcal B(r_{k_2},T_j)\subset \mathcal B(r_{k_1},T_j),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{k_2,j}\subset\Gamma_{k_1,j}
 \qquad(k_1<k_2<j).
\]
In either chain, equal ranks force equal spans and hence equal
sublattices, by the definition of \(\Gamma_{k,\ell}\).
The proper sublattices have ranks in \(\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\), so at most
\(d\) distinct ones occur in each chain, giving at most \(2d\) in total.
<!-- end proof-22 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
We prove \eqref{eq:mixed-kernel-target}, which implies the proposition
by \eqref{eq:second-moment-reduction}.

For a full-rank pair, \(\Gamma_{k,\ell}=\Lambda_A\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:wedge-mass} gives \(M_{k,\ell}\asymp 1\).  Hence all
full-rank pairs together contribute
\[
 \sum_{\substack{1\leqslant k<\ell\leqslant N\\
                  \Gamma_{k,\ell}=\Lambda_A}}
 \frac{M_kM_\ell}{M_{k,\ell}}
 \ll\sum_{1\leqslant k<\ell\leqslant N}M_kM_\ell
 \leqslant S_N^2.
\]

For each \(\Gamma\) with \(\mathcal R_\Gamma\ne\varnothing\),
Lemmata~\ref{lem:wedge-mass} and
\ref{lem:fixed-module} give
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R_\Gamma}
 \frac{M_kM_\ell}{M_{k,\ell}}
 &\asymp\sum_{(k,\ell)\in\mathcal R_\Gamma}
 \frac{M_kM_\ell}{\Phi_\Gamma(r_k,T_\ell)}\\*
 &\ll\sum_{j\in\partial\mathcal R_\Gamma}M_j.
\end{align*}
Summing over these \(\Gamma\) and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:incidence} gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\substack{1\leqslant k<\ell\leqslant N\\\Gamma_{k,\ell}\ne\Lambda_A}}
 \frac{M_kM_\ell}{M_{k,\ell}}
 &\ll\sum_\Gamma\sum_{j\in\partial\mathcal R_\Gamma}M_j\\*
 &=\sum_{j=1}^N M_j\,\#\left\{\Gamma:j\in\partial\mathcal R_\Gamma\right\}
 \leqslant2dS_N.
\end{align*}
Together with the full-rank estimate, this proves
\eqref{eq:mixed-kernel-target}.
