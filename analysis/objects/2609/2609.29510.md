<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{htophiProp} If, for each $f\in\mathcal{L}_u$, there exists a sequence $(h_n)$ in $\mathcal{L}$ that monotonically increases to $f$ for which the sequence $(I(h_n))$ converges to some $I(f)\in\mathbb{F}$, then $I$ has a continuous extension to $\mathcal{L}_u$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
If $(x_m)$ is a monotonically increasing sequence in $\mathcal{L}$ and $L\in\mathbb{F}$, then, by a routine argument, the sequence $(L-(x_m\wedge L))$ monotonically decreases to $0$. Since $I$ is a Daniell functional, $\displaystyle{\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}}I(L-(x_m\wedge L))=0$, and by the linearity of $I$,
\begin{flalign}
    &&\displaystyle{\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}}I(x_m\wedge L)&=I(L),&((x_m)\in\mathcal{L}^\mathbb{N},\  \forall m\in\mathbb{N}\  x_m\leq x_{m+1}).\label{wedgeLim}
\end{flalign}

If $(\varphi_k)$ is a sequence in $\mathcal{L}$ such that $\varphi_k\uparrow f$, then setting $m=n$, $x_m=h_n$, $L=\varphi_k$ in \eqref{wedgeLim}, we obtain 
\begin{equation}
    {\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}} I(h_n\wedge\varphi_k)=I(\varphi_k),
\end{equation}
so, given $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $N\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $I(f)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<I(h_N)$, which implies
\begin{equation}
    I(f)-\varepsilon<I(h_N)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.\label{DaniellExtend1}
\end{equation}
Setting $m=k$, $x_m=\varphi_k$, $L=h_N$ in \eqref{wedgeLim}, we find that there exists $K\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $k\geq K$ implies $I(h_N)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<I(h_N\wedge \varphi_k)$, which, in conjunction with \eqref{DaniellExtend1}, gives us
\begin{flalign}
    && I(f)-\varepsilon&<I(h_N\wedge \varphi_k), & (k\geq K).\label{DaniellExtend2}
\end{flalign}
By an epsilon argument, the fact that $\varphi_k\uparrow f$ implies $\varphi_k\leq f$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$, which further implies $h_N\wedge\varphi_k\leq \varphi_k\leq f$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$. By the linearity of $I$, we have $I(h_N\wedge\varphi_k)\leq I(\varphi_k)\leq I(f)$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$, which, in conjunction with \eqref{DaniellExtend2}, gives us $I(f)-\varepsilon<I(\varphi_k)\leq I(f)$ for all $k\geq K$. Therefore, the sequence $(I(\varphi_k))$ converges to $I(f)\in\mathbb{F}$, and $I$ has a continuous extension to $\mathcal{L}_u$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{heuRem}
As a guiding principle, any proof of a measure-theoretic or integration-theoretic statement concerning an arbitrary ordered field $\mathbb{F}$ must be established independently of any completeness axiom for $\mathbb{F}$, unless the current goal is precisely to prove that the statement in question is equivalent to a completeness axiom.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
The collection of all measurable functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ in the sense of \cite[p.~114]{can24} and the collection of all step functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ are vector lattices, and the collection of all measurable subsets of $\left[ a,b\right]$ in the sense of \cite[p.~115]{can24} is an algebra on $\left[ a,b\right]$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $c\in\mathbb{F}$, and if $f$ and $g$ are measurable functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$, then there exist monotonically decreasing sequences $(\varphi_n)$ and $(\psi_n)$ of step functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ that converge pointwise to $f$ and $g$, respectively, almost everywhere on $\left[ a,b\right]$. Without loss of generality, we may assume the underlying set of measure zero to be the same set $Z$. That is, $x\in\left[ a,b\right]\backslash Z$ implies ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\varphi_n(x)=f(x)$ and ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\psi_n(x)=g(x)$. Since the rules for operations on convergent sequences are valid in $\mathbb{F}$, we have ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}[\varphi_n(x)+\psi_n(x)]=f(x)+ g(x)$, ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\varphi_n(x)\vee\psi_n(x)=f(x)\vee g(x)$, ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\varphi_n(x)\wedge\psi_n(x)=f(x)\wedge g(x)$, ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}} |c|\varphi_n(x)=|c|f(x)$ and ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}} c=c$, where the sequences $(\varphi_n+\psi_n)$, $(\varphi_n\vee\psi_n)$, $(\varphi_n\wedge\psi_n)$, $(|c|\varphi_n)$ and the constant sequence $(c)$ are all monotonically decreasing. Thus, $f+g$, $f\vee g$, $f\wedge g$, $|c|f$ and the constant function $c$ are all measurable. Since the difference of two nonnegative measurable functions is measurable \cite[p.~115]{can24}, so are $|c|(f\vee 0)-|c|(f\wedge 0)$ and $|c|(f\wedge 0)-|c|(f\vee 0)$, where $cf$ is always one of these two functions. Thus, the collection of all measurable functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ form a vector lattice, a vector sublattice of which, is the collection of all step functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$. If $E$ and $F$ are measurable subsets of $\left[ a,b\right]$, the characteristic functions $\chi_E$ and $\chi_F$ are measurable, and hence, so are $\chi_E\wedge\chi_F=\chi_{E\cap F}$, $(\chi_E-\chi_F)\vee(\chi_F-\chi_E)=|\chi_E-\chi_F|=\chi_{E\Delta F}$ and $1=\chi_{\left[ a,b\right]}$ are all measurable. That is, $E\cap F$, $E\Delta F$ and $\left[ a,b\right]$ are measurable. Therefore, the measurable subsets of $\left[ a,b\right]$ form an algebra.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
The function $\varphi\mapsto\int_a^b\varphi$ is a positive linear functional on the vector lattice of all step functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Denote two step functions $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ by $\phi=\sum_{h=1}^mc_h\chi_{A_h}$ and $\psi=\sum_{k=1}^ne_k\chi_{B_k}$, where the sets $A_h$ are pairwise disjoint sets each of which is either a singleton or an open interval with $\left[ a,b\right]=\bigcup_{h=1}^mA_h$, and similarly for the sets $B_k$. The sets
\begin{flalign}
    && A_h\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^nB_t\right),\quad B_k\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^mA_t\right), &&\nonumber\\
    && A_h\cap B_k, &&(h\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\},\  k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}),\nonumber
\end{flalign}
are pairwise disjoint, and we have the relations
\begin{flalign}
    && \chi_{A_h}&=\chi_{A_h\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^nB_t\right)}+\sum_{k=1}^n\chi_{A_h\cap B_k}, &(h\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}),\nonumber\\
    && \chi_{B_k}&=\chi_{B_k\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^mA_t\right)}+\sum_{h=1}^m\chi_{A_h\cap B_k}, &(k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}),\nonumber
\end{flalign}
for the characteristic functions. Thus, we may represent $\phi$ and $\psi$ as
\begin{flalign}
    && \phi&=\sum_{h=1}^mc_h\chi_{A_h\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^nB_t\right)}+\sum_{k=1}^n0\chi_{B_k\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^mA_t\right)}+\sum_{h=1}^m\sum_{k=1}^nc_h\chi_{A_h\cap B_k}, &\label{steprep1}\\
    && \psi&=\sum_{h=1}^m0\chi_{A_h\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^nB_t\right)}+\sum_{k=1}^ne_k\chi_{B_k\backslash\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^mA_t\right)}+\sum_{k=1}^n\sum_{h=1}^me_k\chi_{A_h\cap B_k}, &\label{steprep2}
\end{flalign}
and using these representations of $\phi$ and $\psi$, the coefficients for the corresponding representations for $\phi+\psi$, $\kappa\phi$ (for all $\kappa\in\mathbb{F}$), $\phi\vee\psi$ and $\phi\wedge\psi$ may be obtained by performing these operations on the corresponding coefficients, term-by-term, according to how they are written in \eqref{steprep1}--\eqref{steprep2}. Also, the relations \eqref{steprep1}--\eqref{steprep2} may be used to compute for $\int_a^b(\phi+\psi)$ and $\int_a^b(\kappa\phi)$ and show that these are equal to $\int_a^b\phi+\int_a^b\psi$ and $\kappa \int_a^b\phi$, respectively. If $\phi\geq 0$, then, for each $H\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}$, if $x_H\in A_H$, then $0\leq \phi(x_H)=\sum_{h=1}^mc_h\chi_{A_h}(x_h)=c_H\cdot 1$, because the sets $A_h$ are pairwise disjoint. Thus, $c_H\geq 0$ for all $H$, and because each $\chi_{A_H}$ is a nonnegative-valued function, $\int_a^b\phi$ is the finite sum of the nonnegative products $c_H\chi_{A_H}$, so $\int_a^b\phi\geq 0$. Therefore, $\varphi\mapsto\int_a^b\varphi$ is a positive linear functional.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
The statements in Figure~\ref{TheFig} are each equivalent to the Dedekind completeness of the ordered field $\mathbb{F}$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
As discussed in Section~\ref{ImpliesSec}, only two implications remain, which we now prove. Consider an interval $\left[ a,b\right]\subseteq\mathbb{F}$.\\

\noindent$\ensuremath{{\bf(DFP)}}\implies\ensuremath{{\bf(LIP)}}$. Let $f:\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ be a Lebesgue measurable function, and suppose $(\varphi_n)$ and $(\psi_n)$ are a monotonically decreasing sequences of step functions that converge to $f$ almost everywhere on $\left[ a,b\right]$. The sequence $(\varphi_n\wedge\psi_n)$ also monotonically decreases to $f$, and the sequence $(\varphi_n-\varphi_n\wedge\psi_n)$ monotonically decreases to the zero function. If \ensuremath{{\bf(DFP)}}\ is true, then we have ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\int_a^b(\varphi_n-\varphi_n\wedge\psi_n)=0$, which implies ${\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\int_a^b\varphi_n={\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\int_a^b(\varphi_n\wedge\psi_n)$. Since $(\varphi_n)$ and $(\psi_n)$ are arbitrary, we may interchange $\varphi_n$ and $\psi_n$ in the previous equation, and we further have
\[{\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\int_a^b\varphi_n={\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\int_a^b(\varphi_n\wedge\psi_n)={\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\int_a^b\psi_n.\]
That is, for any two sequences of step functions that monotonically decrease to $f$ almost everywhere on $\left[ a,b\right]$, the corresponding sequences of integrals converge to the same value. From the definition in \cite[pp.~114--115]{can24}, this means that $f$ has a Lebesgue integral over $\left[ a,b\right]$, which proves \ensuremath{{\bf(LIP)}}.\\ 


\noindent$\ensuremath{{\bf(DCP)}}\implies\ensuremath{{\bf(LMP)}}$. Let $E$ be a measurable subset of $\left[ a,b\right]$, and let $(\varphi_n)$ be a sequence of step functions (which are measurable functions) $\left[ a,b\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ that monotonically decrease to $\chi_E$ almost everywhere on $\left[ a,b\right]$. Such a sequence is guaranteed to exist by the definition of measurable subset of $\left[ a,b\right]$ from \cite[pp.~114--115]{can24}. The fact that $(\varphi_n)$ is monotonically decreasing may be used in a routine epsilon argument, valid in the ordered field $\mathbb{F}$, to show that $\chi_E\leq\varphi_n$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$. Also for the same reason that $(\varphi_n)$ is monotonically decreasing, we further have $\chi_E\leq \varphi_n\leq \varphi_1$, but since the left-most function in these inequalities is nonnegative, we may replace $\varphi_n$ by $|\varphi_n|$. That is, $|\varphi_n|\leq \varphi_1$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$. If \ensuremath{{\bf(DCP)}}\  holds, then $\int_a^b\chi_E={\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\int_a^b\varphi_n$. Hence, $\chi_E$ has a Lebesgue integral over $\left[ a,b\right]$, or equivalently, $E$ has Lebesgue measure, which proves \ensuremath{{\bf(LMP)}}.
