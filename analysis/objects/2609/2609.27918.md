\label{Definition1} \emph{Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be a fixed open set, and for $k,n,N\in\mathbb{N}$, consider a Carath\'eodory function 
\[
\mathrm{H} \ :\ \Omega\times \Big(\mathbb R^N \times\mathbb R^{N \times n} \times \cdots  \times \mathbb R^{N \times n^{\otimes k}}_{\mathrm s} \Big)  \longrightarrow \mathbb R.
\]
The $k$-th order supremal functional (arising from the ``supremand" $\mathrm{H}$) is given by
\begin{equation}
\label{1.6}
\left\{  \ \ \ \ 
\begin{split}
\mathrm{E}_\infty& \  : \ \mathrm W^{k,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N) \times \mathcal{L}(\Omega) \longrightarrow \mathbb{R},
\\
&\mathrm{E}_\infty(u,\mathcal{O}) := \underset{\mathcal{O}}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\, \mathrm{H} \big(\cdot, \mathrm{D}^{\vec{k}}u \big) .
\end{split}
\right. \ \ \ \
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal{L}(\Omega)$ symbolises the $\sigma$-algebra of Lebesgue-measurable subsets of $\Omega$, and 
\[

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{Remark17} If the measure $\lambda$ is supported everywhere on $\Omega$, then the supremum and the essential supremum coincide for continuous functions. Due to its significant role herein, we do provide a simple proof of this fact. Since $f \leq \sup_\Omega f$ everywhere on $\Omega$, we readily have
\[
\lambda \text{-} \underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess} \sup} \, f  \leq \sup_\Omega f.
\]
Conversely, by the definition of the essential supremum, we have 
\[
\lambda \text{-} \underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess} \sup} \, f  \geq t, \ \ \ \forall\, t\in \mathbb{R} : \ \lambda(\{f>t\})>0.
\]
We set $T(\delta):=\sup_\Omega f -\delta$, for $\delta>0$. If $\delta$ is small enough, the continuity of $f$ implies that the open set $\{f > {\sup}_\Omega f -\delta\}$ is non-empty in $\Omega$. Then, since $\mathrm{supp} (\lambda) =\Omega$, we have that $\lambda\big(\{f > {\sup}_\Omega f -\delta\}\big) >0$. This implies for the choice of $t=T(\delta)$ that
\[
\lambda \text{-} \underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess} \sup} \, f  \geq T(\delta)= \sup_\Omega f -\delta.
\]
We conclude by letting $\delta\to 0^+$.

\label{Definition21}
Consider the supremal functional \eqref{1.6}, and a given mapping $u\in \mathrm W^{k,\infty}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^N)$. We say that $u$ is an absolute minimiser of $\mathrm{E}_\infty$ on $\Omega$ when
\[
\ \ \mathrm{E}_\infty(u,\mathcal{O}) \leq \mathrm{E}_\infty(u+\phi,\mathcal{O}), \ \ \ \ \forall\,\, \mathcal{O} \subseteq \Omega \text{ open, }\ \forall\,\, \phi \in \mathrm W^{k,\infty}_0(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^N).
\]
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
(i) Definition \ref{Definition21} is rather standard in the area, but it creates more problems that it actually solves, as it throws out the direct method of the Calculus of Variations \cite{D}. Absolute minimisers in general cannot be obtained in this way because minimising sequences may converge subsequentially to \emph{any} of the global minimisers. Alternative methods are needed, and the standing idea is to approximate $\mathrm L^\infty$ functionals by the corresponding integral $\mathrm L^p$ functionals. By studying the limit as $p\to\infty$, the expectation is that, since integral functionals have the desired locality properties, the approximation method will select in the limit the correct object, namely a global absolute minimiser (more on this method below). 

\smallskip

\noindent (ii) Definition \ref{Definition21} is at slight variance to other ``localised" versions of this notion in the literature, which often require the minimality condition to be satisfied only on compactly contained $\mathcal{O}\Subset \Omega$, rather than all open $\mathcal{O} \subseteq \Omega $. The benefit of our slightly stronger version is that it allows to combine into a single definition absolute plus global minimisers, as typically the former are constructed for given boundary data by selecting one of the latter. In the scalar case for a bounded domain plus structural assumptions, absolute minimisers are unique (see e.g.\ \cite{ACJS, BJW1, C1}), hence the two notions coincide, but in general this is not so clear. Anyhow, for the results established herein, it makes no difference in the proofs which version of absolute minimality is used, and Definition \ref{Definition21} is preferable as it is more suitable for our purposes.

\label{Example23} Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be any open bounded interval, and consider the supremal functional \eqref{5.1} for $N=1$.
In this case, it is easy to confirm that absolute minimisers of $\mathrm{I}_\infty$ are unique (given endpoint boundary data), coincide with global minimisers and consist of the class of affine functions on $\mathbb{R}$. Fix any affine function $A : \mathbb{R} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ with $A(x):=ax+b$, $a>0$, $b\in \mathbb{R}$, which is an absolute minimiser of $\mathrm{I}_\infty$ on $\Omega$, and also a global minimiser on $\Omega$ for its own boundary values on $\partial\Omega$. By Remark \ref{Remark17} we have
\[
\mathrm{I}_\infty (u,\mathcal{O}) = \max_{\overline{\Omega}} | u'|^2, \ \ \text{ for all } u\in \mathrm{C}^1(\overline{\Omega}).
\]
Fix $\phi \in (\mathrm{C}^0_0 \cap \mathrm{C}^1)(\overline{\Omega})$. By minimality we have
\[
\mathrm{I}_\infty (A,\Omega) \leq \mathrm{I}_\infty (A+t\phi,\Omega), \ \ \text{ for all }t\neq 0, 
\]
which implies
\[
\liminf_{t\to0^+}\frac{\mathrm{I}_\infty (A+t\phi,\Omega) - \mathrm{I}_\infty (A,\Omega)}{t} \geq 0 \geq \limsup_{t\to0^-}\frac{\mathrm{I}_\infty (A+t\phi,\Omega) - \mathrm{E}_\infty (A,\Omega)}{t}.
\]
By Danskin's theorem applied to $\mathrm{F} : \mathbb{R} \times \overline{\Omega} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ given by $ \mathrm{F}(t,x) := |a+t\phi'(x)|^2$, we have that the left and right semi-differentials exist. Since $|A'|=A' \equiv a>0$ on $\overline{\Omega}$, we have that $\mathrm{Argmax}\{|A'| :  \overline{\Omega}\} = \overline{\Omega}$. This implies
\[
\left\{ \ \ 
\begin{split}
\big( \mathfrak{D} \mathrm{I}_\infty(\cdot,\Omega)\big)^+_A (\phi) &=\max_{\mathrm{Argmax}\{|A'| \,: \, \overline{\Omega}\}} [A'\, \phi'] = a \max_{\overline{\Omega}} [  \phi']  \geq 0,
\\
\big( \mathfrak{D} \mathrm{I}_\infty(\cdot,\Omega)\big)^-_A (\phi) &=\min_{\mathrm{Argmax}\{|A'| \,:\, \overline{\Omega}\}} [A'\, \phi' ] =a \min_{\overline{\Omega}} [  \phi'] \leq 0.
\end{split} 
\right. \ \ \  \
\]
Since $\phi =0$ on $\partial\Omega$, we have $\int_\Omega \phi'=0$. If $\phi \not\equiv 0$, there must exist points $x^\pm \in \Omega$ such that $\phi'(x^+)>0>\phi'(x^-)$. Thus, the left/right semi-derivatives do not match:
\[
\big( \mathfrak{D} \mathrm{I}_\infty(\cdot,\Omega)\big)^+_A (\phi) >\big( \mathfrak{D} \mathrm{I}_\infty(\cdot,\Omega)\big)^-_A (\phi), \ \ \text{ for \emph{all} }\phi \in (\mathrm{C}^0_0 \cap \mathrm{C})^1(\overline{\Omega})\setminus\{0\}.
\]
Therefore $\mathrm{I}_\infty(\cdot,\Omega)$ is not Gateaux differentiable at any affine (absolute and global) minimiser, along any direction. By the same reasoning, it follows that $\mathrm{I}_\infty(\cdot,\mathcal{O})$ is not Gateaux differentiable at any minimiser for any open subdomain $\mathcal{O} \subseteq \Omega$ and along any direction.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Now we discuss briefly the vectorial case. Since it presents several additional layers of complexity, we will only consider the archetypal case of the $\infty$-Laplacian. It was first derived from $\mathrm L^p$-approximations  in \cite{K10}. For maps $u : \mathbb{R}^n \supseteq \Omega \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^N$, it reads
\[
\Delta_\infty u := \Big( \mathrm{D} u \otimes \mathrm{D} u + |\mathrm{D} u|^2[\![ \mathrm{D} u ]\!]^\bot\! \otimes \mathrm{I} \Big) \!:\! \mathrm{D}^2 u =0 \ \ \text{ in }\Omega,
\]
where for any matrix $\mathrm{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$, $[\![ \mathrm{X} ]\!]^\bot := \mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{R}(\mathrm{X} : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^N)^\bot}$ is the orthogonal projection on the orthogonal complement of its range. $\Delta_\infty u =0$ is the simplest non-trivial Aronsson system, but it has discontinuous coefficients even for smooth solutions, which have interfaces whereon the dimension of the tangent space to $u(\Omega)$ changes \cite{K7, K8, K9, K10}. The system actually consists of two differential operators orthogonal to each other, the tangential one $\mathrm{D} u \mathrm{D}(|\mathrm{D} u|^2)=0$, and the normal one $[\![ \mathrm{D} u ]\!]^\bot\Delta u=0$, which expresses that $\Delta u$ is tangential to $u(\Omega)$. Further, it appears that $\infty$-Harmonic maps \textbf{cannot} be characterised through absolute minimisers, as classical solutions to $\Delta_\infty u =0$ are characterised in \cite{K8} through different classes of variations, specifically rank-one absolute minimals and variations normal to $u(\Omega)$ but free on the boundary (see also  \cite{AyK1, AyK2} for extensions). In \cite{KS} it was shown that the Dirichlet problem admits smooth non-minimising solutions, therefore, similarly to the situation in Example \ref{Example36}, $\Delta_\infty $ is clearly not sufficient for (absolute) minimality. Finally, viscosity solutions do not apply to this non-divergence non-monotone system, and one needs alternative novel approaches to study it rigorously, as solutions are typically non-classical (\cite{CKP, K4}).

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{Proposition16}  Let $(\Omega,d,\mathfrak L, \lambda)$ be a BMMSDP and let $f : \Omega \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a $\lambda$-measurable function, $\lambda$-essentially bounded above. Then:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The essential argmax set of $f$ over $\Omega$ can be characterised in the following way:
\[
\lambda \text{-} \mathrm{Argmax} \{ f : \Omega \} = \bigg\{ x\in \Omega \ : \ \lambda\text{-}\mathrm{ap}\limsup_{y\to x} f(y) = \lambda \text{-} \underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess} \sup}\, f \bigg\},
\]
and the approximate limsup above coincides with the corresponding approximate limit. In particular, if $f$ is $\lambda$-approximately continuous in an open neighbourhood of the set $\big\{f\geq \lambda\text{-}{\mathrm{ess} \sup}_{\Omega} f -\varepsilon_0 \big\}$ for some $\varepsilon_0>0$, then the essential argmax set  is non-empty.

\item If $f \in \mathrm{C}(\Omega)$ is continuous, then 
\[
\overline{\Big\{f \geq \lambda \text{-} \underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess} \sup}\, f -\varepsilon\Big\}}^\lambda = \Big\{x\in \Omega : f(x) \geq \underset{\Omega}{\sup} \, f -\varepsilon\Big\},
\]
and both sets are closed. Further,
\[
\lambda \text{-} \mathrm{Argmax} \{ f : \Omega \} = \Big\{ x\in \Omega : f(x) = \sup_{\Omega} f \Big\}.
\]
\item If $f \in \mathrm{C}(\Omega)$ is continuous and $\Omega$ is compact, then we additionally have 
\[
\lambda \text{-} \mathrm{Argmax} \{ f : \Omega \} = \mathrm{Argmax} \{ f : \Omega \},
\]
and both sets are non-empty.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
Even though a priori it is not obvious, it is indeed possible to derive the non-negativity of the semi-differential of $\mathrm{E}_\infty$ asserted in Theorems \ref{Theorem3} and \ref{Theorem5} from the Euler-Lagrange equations of the corresponding $\mathrm L^p$ functional as $p\to\infty$. For the sake of illustration we provide a \emph{formal argument}. Consider the $k$-th order functional
\[
{\mathrm{E}_p(u,\Omega) := \left(\, -\hspace{-13pt}\displaystyle\int_\Omega  \mathrm{H}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u \big) ^p\,\mathrm d\mathcal{L}^n \! \right)^{\!\!1/p} },
\]
placed in $\mathrm W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$, where we assume $\Omega\subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is open bounded, and $\mathrm{H} \geq 0$. Let $u_p$ be a global minimiser for some Dirichlet data on $\partial\Omega$. For a fixed $\phi \in \mathrm W^{k,\infty}_0(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)^\varobslash$ and $\varepsilon>0$, we set
\[
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\phi}(u): = \{\phi \neq 0\} \bigcap \Big\{  \mathrm{H}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u \big) \geq \mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u, \{\phi \neq 0\}) -\varepsilon \Big\}.
\]
Then, the weak form of the Euler-Lagrange equations reads
\[
\int_{\{\phi \neq 0\} }  \mathrm{H}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big)^{p-1} \mathrm{H}_{,\mathbf{X}}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big)   \!:\!   \mathrm{D}^{\vec k} \phi =0.
\]
Suppose $u_p \, -\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u_\infty$ in $\mathrm W^{k,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$ as $p\to\infty$ for all $q\in(1,\infty)$, and $\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u_\infty, \{\phi \neq 0\})>0$, which gives that $\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u_p, \{\phi \neq 0\})>0$ for large $p$. From the above, by using the definition of $\Omega_{\varepsilon,\phi}(u)$ for $\varepsilon>0$ small enough and rescaling, we rewrite as
\[ 
\begin{split}
0 &= \int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\phi}(u_p)} \bigg( \frac{\mathrm{H}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big) }{\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u_p, \{\phi \neq 0\})}\bigg)^{p-1} \mathrm{H}_{,\mathbf{X}}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big)  \!:\!   \mathrm{D}^{\vec k} \phi \,\mathrm d\mathcal{L}^n
\\
& \ \ \  + \int_{\{\phi \neq 0\} \setminus \Omega_{\varepsilon,\phi}(u_p)}  \bigg( \frac{\mathrm{H}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big)}{\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u_p, \{\phi \neq 0\})}\bigg)^{p-1}  \mathrm{H}_{,\mathbf{X}}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big)  \!:\!   \mathrm{D}^{\vec k} \phi \,\mathrm d\mathcal{L}^n.
\end{split}
\]
This gives the estimate
\[ 
\begin{split}
0 &\leq \bigg( \underset{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\phi}(u_p)}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}  \, \mathrm{H}_{,\mathbf{X}}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big) \!:\!   \mathrm{D}^{\vec k} \phi \bigg) \int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\phi}(u_p)} \bigg(\frac{\mathrm{H}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big)}{\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u_p, \{\phi \neq 0\})}\bigg)^{p-1} \,\mathrm d\mathcal{L}^n
\\
& \ \ \  +   \bigg( 1-\frac{ \varepsilon}{\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u_p, \{\phi \neq 0\})} \bigg)^{p-1} \int_{\{\phi \neq 0\} } \Big|\mathrm{H}_{,\mathbf{X}}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_p \big)   \!:\!   \mathrm{D}^{\vec k} \phi \Big| \,\mathrm d\mathcal{L}^n,
\end{split}
\]
from where, formally, we deduce as $p\to\infty$ that
\[
\underset{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\phi}(u_\infty)}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}   \, \mathrm{H}_{,\mathbf{X}}\big(\mathrm{D}^{\vec k} u_\infty \big) \!:\!   \mathrm{D}^{\vec k} \phi  \geq 0,
\]
for $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small and $\phi \in \mathrm W^{k,\infty}_0(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)^\varobslash$, which is equivalent to $(\mathfrak{D} \mathrm{E}_\infty)^+_u(\phi) \geq 0$.

\label{remark28}  (i) Special cases of Theorem \ref{Theorem26} have previously been established in  \cite{K10, DK, KP} in the first and second order case, but except for the more general context, this result also has a streamlined proof. The latter contains two crucial ideas: firstly, every arbitrary open set satisfies interior sphere conditions at a dense set of boundary points (without any regularity hypotheses), and secondly even though supremal functionals are typically non-differentiable, when restricted to the $\mathrm{C}^k$ subspace, one can select \emph{special subsets} $\mathcal{O} \Subset \Omega$ such that $\mathrm{E}_\infty(\cdot,\mathcal{O})$ actually \emph{is differentiable}. (Interestingly this happens only for $n\geq 2$. For $n=1$, Example \ref{Example23} shows that this is not possible and we always have non-differentiability!). The former fact is based on viscosity solution type ideas of ``touching sets", whilst the latter is not really essential for the method of proof to work, but it is interesting nonetheless, shortening the argument. A slight reworking of the method as in \cite{DK} works for all $n\in \mathbb{N}$, but the one-dimensional case is not especially interesting anyway.

\smallskip

\noindent (ii) Assumption \eqref{8.1} implies that \eqref{1.18}-\eqref{1.19} are valid on any $\mathcal{O} \Subset \Omega$, and $\mathrm{E}_\infty(\cdot, \mathcal{O})$ is semi-differentiable in $\mathrm W^{k,\infty}(\mathcal{O})$ (in fact \eqref{6.5} is satisfied as well on $\mathcal{O}$, and hence $\mathrm{E}_\infty$ is actually semi-differentiable in the stronger sense of Hadamard and Penot). These hypotheses are weaker than those required by Theorem \ref{Theorem3} to be semi-differentiable on $\mathrm W^{k,\infty}(\Omega)$, and can be restated as that $\mathrm{E}_\infty$ is semi-differentiable in $\smash{\mathrm W_{\textrm{loc}}^{k,\infty}(\Omega)}$.

\smallskip

\noindent (iii) To derive the fully nonlinear PDE \eqref{8.3} we actually only need to evaluate $( \mathfrak{D} \mathrm{E}_\infty)^+_u$ to $\mathrm{C}^k$-test functions which are supported on balls, and not on all the test functions of \eqref{8.2}. Namely, by inspecting the proof that follows, we will only need $( \mathfrak{D} \mathrm{E}_\infty)^+_u(\phi) \geq 0$ only for the following class of functions $\phi$:
\[
\Big\{\phi \in \mathrm{C}^k (\overline{\mathbb{B}})\cap \mathrm W_0^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{B})\, :\, \mathbb{B} \Subset \Omega \text{ is a ball} \Big\}.
\]
\noindent (iv) Finally, by Theorem \ref{Theorem5} we have that \eqref{8.2} is equivalent to $u$ being a (localised) absolute minimiser of \eqref{1.6} on $\Omega$, if additionally $\mathrm{H}(x,\cdot)$ is level-convex and non-degenerate.
