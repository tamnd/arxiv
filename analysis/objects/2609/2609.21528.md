<!-- head-1: body_md -->
{\em Let $(M^n,I,J,K,\Omega)$ be a compact HKT manifold such that $K_I(M)$ is holomorphically trivial. Then, for a given $F\in C^{\infty}(M)$ such that
\begin{equation*}\label{intF}
\int_M {\rm e}^F\,\Omega^n=\int_M \Omega^n \,,
\end{equation*}
the equation 
\begin{equation}\label{eq_QMA}
 (\Omega +\partial \partial_J \varphi)^n={\rm e}^F\,\Omega^n\,,\quad \int_M \varphi \,\Omega^n=0 
\end{equation}
has a unique solution $\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)$.}
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $ (M^n,I,J,K,\Omega) $ be a compact locally flat HKT manifold such that $K_I(M)$ is holomorphically trivial. Then the quaternionic Monge--Ampère equation \eqref{eq_QMA} has always a unique continuous solution $\varphi$.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
Given a domain $A\subseteq \mathbb{H}^n$, a function $ \varphi \colon A \to [-\infty,\infty) $ is called {\em quaternionic plurisubharmonic} (quaternionic psh for short) if it is upper semicontinuous, $ \varphi \not \equiv -\infty $ and it is either subharmonic or constant $ -\infty $ on each affine right quaternionic line, i.e. for every $ x\in A $ and $ v\in \mathbb{H}^n $ the function $ y\mapsto \varphi(x+vy) $ is either subharmonic in the usual sense or constant $ -\infty $. We denote by $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)$ the set of quaternionic plurisubharmonic functions on $A$.
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
A function $ \varphi \in L^1(M)$ on a locally flat HKT manifold $(M,I,J,K,\Omega)$ is called $\Omega $-{\em quaternionic plurisubharmonic} ($\Omega$-quaternionic psh for short) if in any local quaternionic chart such that $\Omega=\partial \partial_J u$ the function $ u+\varphi $ is quaternionic plurisubharmonic. 
                 
                                                                                                                                                                                   
               
                                                                                                                                                                         
We denote by
\[
\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega):= \{ \varphi \in L^1(M) \mid \varphi \text{ is $\Omega$-quaternionic psh} \}
\]
the space of $ \Omega $-quaternionic psh functions on $(M,I,J,K,\Omega)$.
<!-- end defn-2 -->
<!-- oss-1: body_md -->
{\em 
The results in \cite{Wan-Kang,Wan-Wang,Wan-Zhang} are stated in terms of certain operators $ d_0, d_1, $ and their composition $ \Delta:=d_0d_1 $. Even if in general $d_0$ and $d_1$ differ from $\partial$ and $\partial_J$, it was proved by Sroka \cite[Prop. 1]{Sro} that after a suitable choice of coordinates, we have
\[
d_0=2\partial_J\,, \qquad d_1=-2\partial\,, \qquad \Delta=4\partial \partial_J\,.
\]
For this reason we will freely refer to the results contained in \cite{Wan-Kang,Wan-Wang,Wan-Zhang}, also when working with the operators $\partial$ and $\partial_J$.
}
<!-- end oss-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:1}
Let $(M,I,J,K,\Omega)$ be a compact locally flat HKT manifold. If $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$, then $\Omega+\partial\partial_J \varphi$ is a q-closed q-positive current. Conversely if $\varphi\in L^1(M)$ is such that the q-closed current $\Omega +\partial \partial_J \varphi$ is q-positive, then there exists $\psi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ such that $\varphi=\psi$ almost everywhere.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) $ and let $\alpha\in \Lambda^{2n-2,2n}(M)$ be a q-positive form. Consider a finite cover $\mathcal{U}:=\{U_j\}_{j=1}^N$ such that $\Omega_{|U_j}=\partial \partial_J u_j$ for local smooth potentials $u_j$, and denote by $\{\rho_j\}_{j=1}^N$ a partition of unity associated to $\mathcal{U}$. Then we have
$$
(\Omega+\partial\partial_J \varphi)(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^N (\partial \partial_J (u_j+\varphi))(\rho_j \alpha)\geq 0\,,
$$
where the last inequality follows from \cite[Prop. 3.7]{Wan-Wang}. 

Conversely, let $\varphi\in L^1(M)$ be such that $\Omega+\partial \partial_J\varphi\geq 0$. It follows that
$$
0\leq (\Omega+\partial\partial_J \varphi)(\alpha)=(\partial \partial_J (u+\varphi))(\alpha)\,,
$$
for any q-positive form $\alpha\in \Lambda^{2n-2,2n}(M)$ that has compact support contained in a local quaternionic chart $U$, where $\Omega\vert_U=\partial\partial_J u_U$.
Take a local standard regularisation $ \psi_{U,\varepsilon}=(u_U+\varphi) \star \chi_\varepsilon $. By \cite[Prop. 2.1.6]{Alesker 2003} we see that $ \partial \partial_J \psi_{U,\varepsilon}=(\partial \partial_J (u_U+\varphi))\star \chi_\varepsilon\geq 0 $ on $U_\varepsilon:=\{ x\in U \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,\partial U) > \varepsilon\}$ as $ \psi_{U,\varepsilon} \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(U_\varepsilon)\cap C^{\infty}(U_\varepsilon)$ (Proposition \ref{prop_convolution}). Hence, for $ \varepsilon \searrow 0 $ the regularisation $ \psi_{U,\varepsilon} $ decreases to a quaternionic psh function $ \psi_U $ (Proposition \ref{3}), but since $ \psi_{U,\varepsilon} \to u_U+\varphi $ in $ L^1_{\rm loc}(U) $ we must have $ \psi_U=u_U+\varphi $ almost everywhere on $U$.
Next, we observe that for any two quaternionic charts $U_1,U_2$ such that $U_1\cap U_2\neq \emptyset$, we have
$$
\psi_{U_1}=\psi_{U_2}+(u_{U_1}-u_{U_2})
$$
almost everywhere on $U_1\cap U_2$. But $\psi_{U_1}, \psi_{U_2}+(u_{U_1}-u_{U_2})\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(U_1\cap U_2)$, thus they are equal on $U_1\cap U_2$ (this immediately follows from Proposition \ref{prop_convolution} as their local regularisations coincide). Hence the functions $\psi_{U_j}-u_{U_j}$ glue together to a global function $\psi$ that is equal to $\varphi$ almost everywhere and belongs to $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$.

\label{lem:rel_cptness}
Let $(M,I,J,K,\Omega)$ be a compact locally flat HKT manifold.	The set
\[
\left\{ \varphi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) \mid  \sup_M \varphi=0 \right\}
\]
is compact in $ \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) $. Furthermore, let $\mu$ be a positive Radon measure that satisfies $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) \subseteq L^1(\mu)$, then the subset $ \{ \varphi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) \mid \int_M \varphi\, d\mu=0 \} $ is relatively compact. In particular there exists $C$ such that for any $ \varphi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$,
	\begin{equation}
	    \label{eqn:L1_sup_comparison}
        -C+ \mu(M)\sup_M \varphi \leq \int_M \varphi\, d\mu \leq \mu(M)\sup_M \varphi\,.
	\end{equation}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Set $K:=\left\{\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\, |\, \sup_M \varphi=0\right\}$. Letting $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}\subset K$, Proposition \ref{prop_basic}.(4) yields a subsequence $(\varphi_{j_k})_{k\in \mathbb{N}}\subset K$ that converges to a function $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$. Then, as immediate consequence of Proposition \ref{prop_basic}.(3), we have that necessarily $\sup_M \varphi=0$, i.e. $\varphi\in K$ and $K$ is compact.
                                                                                                                                                                                      

The right inequality in \eqref{eqn:L1_sup_comparison} is trivial. Let now assume by contradiction to have a sequence $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}$ in  $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ such that $ \int_M \varphi_j\, d\mu=0 $ and such that $\mu(M)\sup_M \varphi_j\geq 2^j$ for all $j\in\mathbb{N}$. Setting $\psi_j:=\varphi_j-\sup_M \varphi_j $, by the first part of the proof, up to passing to a subsequence, $ \psi_{j_k} \to \psi \in L^1(M) $. We observe that 
\[
\|\psi_j\|_{L^1(\mu)}=\int_M \sup_M \varphi_j\, d\mu-\int_M \varphi_j\, d\mu =\mu(M)\sup_M \varphi_j\geq 2^j\,.
\]
To derive the contradiction it then remains to prove that $\| \psi_j \|_{L^1(\mu)}$ is uniformly bounded in $j$.

Assume first that $\mu$ is smooth. Then $\psi_j \mu \to \psi \mu$ in the weak sense of measures. Consequently $\int_M \psi_j\, d \mu \to \int_M \psi\, d \mu>-\infty$, implying that $ \|\psi_j\|_{L^1(\mu)} $ is uniformly bounded.
    
   
                                                                                             
   
                                                                                                                                           

If $\mu$ is not smooth, 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       
set $\psi=\sum_{j = 1}^\infty 2^{-j} \psi_j$. As the $ L^1 $ norm of $ \psi_j $ with respect to a smooth positive Radon measure is uniformly bounded, we have $ \psi \not \equiv -\infty $. Moreover $\psi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$, being decreasing limit of a sequence of functions in $ \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) $ (see Proposition \ref{prop_basic}.(1)). In particular $\psi\in L^1(\mu)$ and we get a contradiction observing that by the monotone convergence theorem we have $\int_M \psi\, d\mu=\sum_{j = 1 }^\infty 2^{-j} \int_M \psi_j\, d\mu=-\infty$.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{Prop:max_princ}
Let $ \varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n,\psi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M) $, then
$$
\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi_1>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_1,\varphi_2,\dots,\varphi_n}=\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi_1>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\max(\varphi_1,\psi),\varphi_2,\dots,\varphi_n}.
$$
In particular, for every $\varphi,\psi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) \cap L^\infty(M)$
$$
\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}=\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\max(\varphi,\psi)}\,.
$$
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since the nature of the result is local and the manifold is locally flat, it is enough to prove the statement on a domain $ A\subseteq \mathbb{H}^n $. Namely we want to show that
	\[
	\mathbf{1}_{\{u>v\}}(\partial \partial_J u)\wedge T=\mathbf{1}_{\{u>v\}}(\partial \partial_J \max(u,v))\wedge T
	\]
	as Borel measures, where $ u,v\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)\cap L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(A) $ and $ T $ is a q-closed q-positive $ (2n-2,0) $-current.
	
	If $ u $ is continuous, $ \{u >v \} $ is an open subset of $ A $ and the result follows. If $ u $ is not continuous we take a sequence $ (u_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}\subset \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A_\varepsilon)\cap C^\infty(A_\varepsilon) $ decreasing to $ u $ (Proposition \ref{prop_convolution}). We have
	\[
	\mathbf{1}_{\{u_j>v\}}(\partial \partial_J \max(u_j,v))\wedge T=	\mathbf{1}_{\{u_j>v\}}(\partial \partial_J u_j)\wedge T\,.
	\]
	Let $ w_j=\sup(u_j-v,0) $ and $ w=\sup(u-v,0) $ and observe that $ (w_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} $ decreases to $ w $. Therefore, by continuity of the Monge--Amp\`{e}re operator (cf. \cite[Prop. 3.2]{Wan-Zhang})
	\[
	w(\partial \partial_J \max(u,v))\wedge T=\lim_{j\to +\infty} w_j(\partial \partial_J \max(u_j,v))\wedge T=	\lim_{j\to +\infty} w_j (\partial \partial_J u_j)\wedge T=	w(\partial \partial_J u)\wedge T
	\]
	in the sense of Borel measures. Since $ 1/(w+\varepsilon) $ is bounded for every $ \varepsilon>0 $ we have
	\[
	\frac{w}{w+\varepsilon}(\partial \partial_J \max(u,v))\wedge T = \frac{w}{w+\varepsilon}(\partial \partial_J u)\wedge T\,,
	\]
	and this allows to conclude letting $ \varepsilon $ decrease to $0 $ because $ w/(w+\varepsilon) $ increases to $ \mathbf{1}_{\{u>v\}} $.

<!-- defn-3: body_md -->
For $ \varphi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$, we define the \emph{Monge--Ampère measure of $\varphi$} as
$$
\mathrm{MA}_\varphi:=\lim_{j\to +\infty} \mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi>-j\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\max(\varphi,-j)}\,.
$$
More generally, given $\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$, we define the \emph{quaternionic mixed Monge--Ampère measure of $\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n$} as
    $$
    \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_1,\dots, \varphi_n}:=\lim_{j\to +\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{s=1}^n\{\varphi_s>-j\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\max(\varphi_1,-j),\dots,\max(\varphi_n,-j)}\,.
    $$
The functions $ \max (\varphi,-j)  \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M)$ are called the {\em canonical approximants} of $\varphi$.

\label{Lem:MAdef}
Let $\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$, then the sequence of positive Borel measures $ \mu_j:=\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{s=1}^n\{\varphi_s>-j\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\max(\varphi_1,-j),\dots, \max(\varphi_n,-j)} $ is non-decreasing and $\mu_j(M)\leq 1$. In particular, $\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_1,\dots, \varphi_n}$ is a positive Borel measure such that $ \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n}(M)\leq 1$.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We prove the result when $\varphi_s=\varphi$ for all $s=1,\dots,n$ as the general case follows by a simple adaptation of the proof. Denoting then by $\varphi_j:=\max(\varphi,-j)$ the canonical approximants of $\varphi$, Proposition \ref{Prop:max_princ} implies
\[
{\mathbf 1}_{\{\varphi_j>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}={\mathbf 1}_{\{\varphi_j>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\max(\varphi_j,-k)}\,;
\]
hence, for $ j\geq k $ we obtain
\[
{\mathbf 1}_{\{\varphi>-j\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}
\geq
{\mathbf 1}_{\{\varphi>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}={\mathbf 1}_{\{\varphi>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k}\,.
\]
This shows that the sequence $ \mu_j:=\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi>-j\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j} $ is non decreasing. As $\mu_j\leq \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}$, by \eqref{eq:MAmass} we get $\mu_j(M)\leq 1$.
                                                                                                                                                   
Thus, 
\[
\mathrm{MA}_\varphi:=\lim_{j\to +\infty }\mu_j=\lim_{j\to +\infty} \mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi>-j\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}
\]
is itself a positive Borel measure with total mass bounded from above by $ 1 $.

\label{prop:NonPluripolar}
    Let $\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$, then the mixed Monge--Ampère operator $\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n}$ does not put mass on $Q$-polar sets, i.e. $ \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n}(E)=0 $ for all $Q$-polar sets $E\subseteq M$.

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By definition of the quaternionic mixed Monge--Ampère measure $\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n}$ it is sufficient to treat the case $\varphi_i\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M)$ (cf. Lemma \ref{Lem:MAdef}). Moreover, as a consequence of the quaternionic version of Josefson's Theorem, see \cite[Thm. 1.1]{Wan-Kang}, it is enough to work locally on a domain $A\subset \mathbb{H}^n$. <!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{prop_Echi_incr}
The energy \eqref{eqn:energy} is non-decreasing and concave. Furthermore for any non-positive $ \varphi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M) $ we have 
\[
                          
\int_M \varphi\, \mathrm{MA}_\varphi \leq E(\varphi) \leq \frac{1}{n+1                
} \int_M \varphi\, \mathrm{MA}_\varphi\,.
\]
Moreover, $ E $ is upper semicontinuous in the $ L^1 $-topology and it is continuous along decreasing sequences.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $ \varphi,\psi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M) $ and assume $ \varphi\leq \psi $. Set $ \varphi_t:=(1-t)\varphi+t\psi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M) $. From a straightforward computation and Lemma \ref{lem:fakegradient} we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq_primitive}
	\frac{d}{dt} E(\varphi_t)=                          
	\int_M \dot \varphi _t\, \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_t}\geq 0\,,
\end{equation}
\[
	\frac{d^2}{dt^2}E(\varphi_t)=-n\int_M \partial\dot \varphi_t \wedge \partial_J\dot \varphi_t \wedge (\Omega+\partial \partial_J \varphi_t)^{n-1}\leq 0\,,
\]
showing that the energy is non-decreasing and concave.

Let $ \varphi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M) $ be non-positive, then it is clear that $ E(\varphi) \leq \frac{1}{n+1                
}\int_M \varphi \mathrm{MA}_\varphi $. The other inequality is implied by the following
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trick}
\begin{split}
	\int_M \varphi\, \Omega_\varphi^{j+1}\wedge \Omega^{n-j-1}-\int_M \varphi\,\Omega_\varphi^{j}\wedge \Omega^{n-j}& =\int_M \varphi\partial \partial_J \varphi \wedge \Omega_\varphi^{j}\wedge \Omega^{n-j-1}\\
	&=- \int_M \partial\varphi \wedge \partial_J \varphi \wedge \Omega_\varphi^{j}\wedge \Omega^{n-j-1}\leq 0\,,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where we wrote $\Omega_\varphi:=\Omega+ \partial \partial_J \varphi$ and the last inequality is again given by Lemma \ref{lem:fakegradient}.
It remains to prove upper semicontinuity and continuity along decreasing sequences. Assume first that $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}$ decreases to $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M)$. From \eqref{eq_primitive} a simple calculation gives
$$
0\leq E(\varphi_j)-E(\varphi)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^n \int_M (\varphi_j-\varphi)\left(\Omega+\partial \partial_J \varphi_j\right)^k\wedge\left(\Omega+\partial \partial_J \varphi\right)^{n-k}\leq \int_M (\varphi_j-\varphi)\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}\,,
$$
   
                                                                                                                                                                                                
   
   
                                                                                                                                                        
   
where the last inequality follows from an integration by parts argument together with Lemma \ref{lem:fakegradient}, similarly to \eqref{eq:trick}.
               
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     
                                                                                                                                                              
             
                              
                
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        
              
By the Dominated Convergence Theorem it follows that $E(\varphi_j)\to E(\varphi)$.

Let us now consider $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) $ and set $\varphi^k:=\max(\varphi,-k)$ for the canonical approximants. For any $u\in\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M)$ such that $u\geq \varphi$ there exists $k\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $u\geq \varphi^k$. In particular $E(u)\geq \lim_k E(\varphi^k)$, from which we deduce that $E(\varphi^k)\to E(\varphi)$ taking the infimum over all the $u$'s and using the monotonicity of the Monge--Ampère energy.

Next take a sequence $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}\subset \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ such that $\varphi_j\to \varphi\in\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ in $L^1$ and set $\varphi_j^k:=\max(\varphi_j,-k), \varphi^k:=\max(\varphi, -k)$. The sequence $\psi_j^k:=(\sup_{l\geq j}\varphi_l^k)^*$ belongs to $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ and decreases to $\varphi^k$ as $j\to +\infty$ for any $k\in\mathbb{N} $ fixed (Proposition \ref{prop_basic}.(3)). As $\varphi^k$ is bounded, we obtain
$$
\lim_j E(\psi^k_j)= E(\varphi^k)
$$
by what was proved above. By monotonicity of the Monge--Ampère energy we deduce
$$

<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{Prop:projection_thm}
	For every $ \varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(M,\Omega) $ and $ v\in C^0(M) $
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:Proj}
	\frac{d}{dt} E (P(\varphi+tv))\big\vert_{t=0}=\int_M v\, \mathrm{MA}_\varphi\,.
	\end{equation}
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
First, we observe that it is enough to prove that
	\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ProjNew}
	E(P(\psi+u))-E(P(\psi))=\int_0^1\left(\int_M u\, \mathrm{MA}_{P(\psi+tu)}\right) dt
	\end{equation}
	for every pair of continuous functions $ \psi,u $ on $ M $. Indeed, take a sequence $ (\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} $ of continuous functions on $ M $ that decrease to $ \varphi $.  Such a sequence exists because $ \varphi $ is upper semicontinuous (observe that the $ \varphi_j $'s need not be in $ \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) $).
	                                                                                                                                
	By continuity along decreasing sequences of the Monge--Ampère energy (Proposition \ref{prop_Echi_incr}) and the Monge--Amp\`{e}re operator (see Lemma \ref{propE} below), we get
	\[
	E(P(\varphi+u))-E(\varphi)=\lim_{j\to +\infty}\bigl(E(P(\varphi_j+u))-E(P(\varphi_j))\bigr)
	\]
    as $P(\varphi)=\varphi, P(\varphi_j)\searrow P(\varphi), P(\varphi_j+u)\searrow P(\varphi+u)$, and
	\[
	\int_0^1\left(\int_M u \,\mathrm{MA}_{P(\varphi+tu)}\right)dt=\lim_{j\to +\infty}\int_0^1\left(\int_M u\, \mathrm{MA}_{P(\varphi_j+tu)}\right)dt\,.
	\]
    Therefore from \eqref{eq:ProjNew} we obtain
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:DagliUnNome}
    E(P(\varphi+u))-E(\varphi)=\int_0^1\left(\int_M u\,\mathrm{MA}_{P(\varphi+tu)}\right)dt
    \end{equation}
    for any continuous function $u$. In particular, assuming first $v\geq 0$, \eqref{eq:Proj} follows from \eqref{eq:DagliUnNome} and Lemma \ref{propE} by replacing $u$ with $sv$, dividing by $s$, and taking the limit as $s\to 0$. For general $v\in C^0(M)$ we can replace $v$ by $v-\inf_M v$, observing that
    $$
    \frac{d}{dt}E(P(\varphi+t(v+C)))_{|t=0}= \frac{d}{dt}E(P(\varphi+tv))_{|t=0}+C
    $$
    for any $C\in \mathbb{R}$ (since $P(f+C)=P(f)+C$ by definition) and that $\mathrm{MA}_\varphi(M)=1$.
	
	It remains to prove \eqref{eq:ProjNew} for $ \psi \in C^0(M) $, which in turn we claim will follow from
    \begin{equation}\label{eqn:New}
        \frac{d}{dt} E( P(\psi+tu))_{|t=0^+}=\int_M u \mathrm{MA}_{P(\psi)}.
    \end{equation}
    Indeed, replacing $\psi$ by $\psi+su$ and the direction $u$ by $-u$, from \eqref{eqn:New} we obtain
    $$
    \frac{d}{dt} E(P(\psi+tu))_{|t=s}=\int_M u \mathrm{MA}_{P(\psi+su)}
    $$
    for any $s\in [0,1]$. Integrating over the interval $[0,1]$ we deduce $\eqref{eq:ProjNew}$.
                                                                  
    Let us now prove \eqref{eqn:New}. From the concavity of the energy we deduce
	\begin{align*}
	E( P(\psi+tu))&\leq E(P(\psi))+E'(P(\psi))(P(\psi+tu)-P(\psi))\,,\\
	E (P(\psi))&\leq E(P(\psi+tu))+E'(P(\psi+tu))(P(\psi)-P(\psi+tu))\,,
	\end{align*}
	which, together with \eqref{eq_primitive}, give
	\[
		\int_M \frac{P(\psi+tu)-P(\psi)}{t}\mathrm{MA}_{P(\psi+tu)}\leq \frac{E( P(\psi+tu))-E(P(\psi))}{t}\leq \int_M \frac{P(\psi+tu)-P(\psi)}{t}\mathrm{MA}_{P(\psi)}\,.
	\]
	Using Lemma \ref{Lem:support_MA_P(psi)} and the inequality $ P(\psi+tu)\leq \psi+tu $ we then obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq_proj_thm1}
		\int_M u\,\mathrm{MA}_{P(\psi+tu)}\leq \frac{E (P(\psi+tu))-E(P(\psi))}{t}\leq \int_M u\,\mathrm{MA}_{P(\psi)}\,.
	\end{equation}
	Observe that the projection is uniformly Lipschitz as, by definition $P(\psi+u)\leq P(\psi+\sup_M \lvert u\rvert)=P(\psi)+\sup_M \lvert u\rvert$, and similarly $P(\psi +u)\geq P(\psi)-\sup_M \lvert u\rvert$ for any $u\in C^0(M)$. Thus we see that
	\[
	\sup_M |P(\psi+tu)-P(\psi)|\leq t\sup_M |u|\,.
	\]
	It follows that $ P(\psi+tu) \to P(\psi) $ uniformly as $ t\to 0^+ $.
	                                                                                                                                                                                                        
	By continuity of the Monge--Amp\`{e}re operator of Lemma \ref{eqn:Conv_unif} below, taking the limit in \eqref{eq_proj_thm1} as $ t\to 0^+ $ yields \eqref{eqn:New}, as required.

<!-- lem-6: body_md -->
\label{eqn:Conv_unif}
    Let $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap C^0(M)$ and let $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}\subset \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap C^0(M)$ be such that $\varphi_j\to \varphi$ uniformly. Then $\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}\to \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}$ weakly, as $j\to +\infty$.

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Approximating uniformly $f\in C^0(M)$ by $f_k\in C^\infty(M)$ we observe that
\begin{align*}
    \left\lvert \int_M f (\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j} - \mathrm{MA}_\varphi)\right\rvert&\leq \left\lvert \int_M (f-f_k)\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j} \right\rvert +\left\lvert \int_M f_k (\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j} - \mathrm{MA}_\varphi)\right\rvert + \left\lvert \int_M (f-f_k)\mathrm{MA}_\varphi \right\rvert \\
    &\leq 2\left\lVert f-f_k \right\rVert_\infty + \left\lvert \int_M f_k (\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j} - \mathrm{MA}_\varphi)\right\rvert
\end{align*}
as $\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}(M)=\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}(M)=1$. Thus it suffices to show that 
$$
\int_M f \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}\to \int_M f\mathrm{MA}_\varphi\,
$$
for every $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$. 

    By using a partition of unity we can reduce to the case when $f$ has compact support in the domain $A$ of a coordinate chart. Then, letting $u$ be a local potential for $\Omega$ in $A$, we have $\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}=(\partial \partial_J \psi_j)^n$, and $\mathrm{MA}_\varphi=(\partial \partial_J \psi)^n$, where we set $\psi_j:=u+\varphi_j, \psi:= u+\varphi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)$. Clearly $\lVert\psi_j-\psi \rVert_{\infty, K}\leq \lVert \varphi_j-\varphi \rVert_\infty$ for any compact set $K\subset A$, for instance $K=\mathrm{Supp}(f)$.
    Let $k=1,\dots,n$, and fix a subdomain $A'\Subset A$ such that $\mathrm{Supp}(f)\Subset A'$. Then, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &\left\lvert\int_A f(\partial \partial_J \psi_j)^k\wedge (\partial \partial_J\psi)^{n-k}-\int_A f(\partial \partial_J \psi_j)^{k-1}\wedge (\partial \partial_J \psi)^{n-k+1}  \right\rvert\\
        &\qquad = \left\lvert \int_A (\psi_j-\psi)(\partial\partial_J \psi_j)^{k-1}\wedge (\partial \partial_J \psi)^{n-k} \wedge \partial \partial_J f \right\rvert\\
        &\qquad \leq C \lVert\varphi_j-\varphi \rVert_{\infty} \int_{A'} (\partial \partial_J\psi_j)^{k-1}\wedge (\partial \partial_J \psi)^{n-k} \wedge \partial\partial_J u\\
        &\qquad \leq C \lVert\varphi_j-\varphi \rVert_{\infty} \int_M(\Omega+\partial\partial_J \varphi_j)^{k-1}\wedge (\Omega+\partial \partial_J \varphi)^{n-k}\wedge \Omega =C\lVert \varphi_j-\varphi\rVert_{\infty}\,,
    \end{aligned}
$$
where $-C\partial\partial_J u\leq \partial\partial_J f\leq C \partial \partial_J u$ and in the last equality we used \eqref{eq:MAmass}.
We deduce that
$$
\left\lvert \int_A f (\partial \partial_J\psi_j)^n-\int_A f(\partial \partial_J\psi)^n \right\rvert\leq n C \lVert \varphi_j-\varphi\rVert_\infty\,,
$$
which concludes the proof.

<!-- lem-7: body_md -->
\label{propE}
Let $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} $ be a decreasing sequence in $ \mathcal{E}^1(M,\Omega)$ converging to $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(M,\Omega)$. Then $ \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}\to \mathrm{MA}_\varphi $ weakly, as $ j\to+ \infty $.
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Assume without loss of generality that $\varphi\leq 0$ and $\varphi_j\leq 0$ for all $j$. Consider the canonical approximants $\varphi^k:=\max(\varphi,-k)$, $\varphi_j^k:=\max(\varphi_j,-k)$. For any fixed $k$ we clearly have $\varphi_j^k \searrow \varphi^k$ and from continuity of the Monge--Ampère operator along decreasing sequences in $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)\cap L^\infty(M)$ (Theorem \ref{thm:cont}) we deduce that $\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j^k} \to \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi^k}$.

Let $C>0$ be such that $E(\varphi)\geq -C$. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:contE1}
\int_{\{\varphi_j\leq -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}\leq \frac{n+1}{k}C
\end{equation}
for any $j,k\in\mathbb{N}$. Indeed, by Proposition \ref{prop_Echi_incr} we have
$$
-C\leq E(\varphi)\leq E(\varphi_j)\leq \frac{1}{n+1}\int_M \varphi_j \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}\leq \frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\{\varphi_j\leq -k\}}\varphi_j\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}\leq \frac{-k}{n+1}\int_{\{\varphi_j\leq -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}
$$
and the claim \eqref{eqn:contE1} follows. Then we have
\begin{multline*}
    \left\lvert\int_M \chi \left(\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}-\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}\right) \right\rvert\\
    \leq \underbrace{\left\lvert  \int_M\chi \left(\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}-\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j^k}\right) \right\rvert}_{\mathrm{(I)}_{j,k}} + \left \lvert  \int_M\chi \left(\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j^k}-\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi^k}\right) \right \rvert +\underbrace{\left \lvert  \int_M\chi \left(\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi^k}-\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}\right) \right \rvert}_{\mathrm{(II)}_{k}}\,.
\end{multline*}
The second term on the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $j\to +\infty$ thanks to the first part of the proof. Thus, we need to show that $\limsup_k\limsup_j\mathrm{(I)}_{j,k}+\mathrm{(II)}_{k}=0$.

For any $j,k\in\mathbb{N}$ we have
\[
\int_{\{\varphi_j\leq -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}=\int_M \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}-\int_{\{\varphi_j>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}= \int_M \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j^k}-\int_{\{\varphi_j>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j^k}=\int_{\{\varphi_j\leq -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j^k}\,,
\]
where the second equality follows since $\varphi_j, \varphi_j^k\in\mathcal{E}(M,\Omega)$ and the maximum principle of Lemma \ref{lem:max_princ_Unbounded}. Combining this with \eqref{eqn:contE1} we obtain
\begin{align*}
                                                                                      
    \mathrm{(I)}_{j,k}\leq \lVert \chi\rVert_{\infty}\int_{\{\varphi_j\leq -k\}}\left(\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}+\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j^k}\right)=2\lVert\chi \rVert_{\infty}\int_{\{\varphi_j\leq -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_j}\leq \frac{2(n+1)C\lVert \chi\rVert_\infty }{k}\,,
\end{align*}
and similarly
$$
\mathrm{(II)}_k\leq 2\lVert \chi \rVert_{\infty}\int_{\{\varphi\leq -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}\,.
$$
We deduce that $\limsup_k\limsup_j\mathrm{(I)}_{j,k}+\mathrm{(II)}_k\leq 0 $ as $\mathrm{MA}_\varphi$ does not put mass on the Q-polar set $\{\varphi=-\infty\}$ (Proposition \ref{prop:NonPluripolar}).

\label{lem:properness}
$ \mathcal{F} $ is proper with respect to $ E $. More precisely there is a constant $ C>0 $ such that
\begin{equation}
    \label{eqn:Properness}
    \mathcal{F}(\varphi)\leq E(\varphi)-\sup_M \varphi +C \left \lvert E(\varphi)-\sup_M \varphi \right \rvert^{1/2}\,,
\end{equation}
for all $ \varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(M,\Omega) $.

Set $ \varepsilon=|E(\varphi)|^{-1/2} $ so that $ \psi=\varepsilon \varphi $ is still $ \Omega $-quaternionic psh. For any $ 1\leq j \leq n  $ we have
\[
\Omega_\psi^j\wedge \Omega^{n-j}= \Omega^n+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\varepsilon^k(\partial \partial_J \varphi)^k \wedge \Omega^{n-k}\leq \Omega^n+N\varepsilon \sum_{k=0}^{n}\Omega_\varphi^k \wedge \Omega^{n-k}
\]
for some $ N\in \mathbb{N} $, therefore, since $\{\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) \mid \sup_M \varphi=0\}$ is compact by Lemma \ref{lem:rel_cptness}, we have
\[
E(\psi)=\frac{\varepsilon}{n+1                
}\sum_{j=0}^n\int_M \varphi\, \Omega_\psi^j \wedge \Omega^{n-j} \geq                           
\frac{\varepsilon}{n+1}\int_M \varphi\, \Omega^n + N\varepsilon^2 E(\varphi)\geq \frac{1}{n+1}\int_M\varphi \, \Omega^n-N\geq -\tilde C\,,
\]
proving that $ \psi \in \mathcal{E}_{\tilde C}^1(M,\Omega) $. Moreover the same Lemma \ref{lem:rel_cptness} gives $ \int_M \psi\, \mathrm{e}^F \Omega^n\geq -C' $ for every $ \psi \in \mathcal{E}^1_{\tilde C}(M,\Omega) $. Therefore
\[
\int_M \varphi \,\mathrm{e}^F \Omega^n =|E(\varphi)|^{1/2}\int_M \psi \, \mathrm{e}^F \Omega^n\geq -C'|E(\varphi)|^{1/2}\,,
\]
which clearly gives \eqref{eqn:Properness} and concludes the proof.

<!-- prop-9: body_md -->
\label{Comparison}
	                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
	    
	                                                                                                    
	    
    For any $\varphi,\psi \in\mathcal{E}(M,\Omega)$
	\[
	\int_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_\varphi\leq \int_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_\psi\,.

<!-- end prop-9 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Consider the canonical approximants $ \varphi_k:=\max(\varphi,-k),\psi_j:=\max(\psi,-j) $. By the comparison principle for bounded $ \Omega $-quaternionic psh functions (Proposition \ref{Prop:cps_principle}) we have
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:incomp}
\int_{\{\varphi>\psi_j\}} \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k} \leq \int_{\{\varphi_k>\psi_j\}} \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k} \leq \int_{\{\varphi_k>\psi_j\}} \mathrm{MA}_{\psi_j} \leq \int_{\{\varphi_k>\psi\}} \mathrm{MA}_{\psi_j}\,.
\end{equation}
Note that for any Borel set $B$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{k\to +\infty}\int_{B }\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k}&=\lim_{k\to +\infty }\left( \int_{B \cap \{ \varphi> -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k}+\int_{B\cap \{ \varphi\leq -k\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k}\right)=\int_{B}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi}\,, 
\end{aligned}
\]
since $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}(M,\Omega)$, and by definition
\[
\lim_{k\to +\infty} \int_{\{\varphi\leq -k\}} \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k}=1-\lim_{k\to +\infty} \int_{\{\varphi>-k\}} \mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k} =1-\int_M \mathrm{MA}_\varphi =0\,. 
\]
Similarly, $\lim_{j\to +\infty}\int_{B} \mathrm{MA}_{\psi_j}=\int_{B} \mathrm{MA}_{\psi}$. Thus, letting first $j\to +\infty$ and then $k\to +\infty$ in \eqref{eqn:incomp} yields
\begin{align*}
    \int_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_\varphi&=\lim_{k\to +\infty}\int_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k}=\lim_{k\to +\infty}\lim_{j\to +\infty}\int_{\{\varphi>\psi_j\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\varphi_k}\\
    &\leq \lim_{k\to +\infty}\lim_{j\to +\infty} \int_{\{\varphi_k>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\psi_j}=\lim_{k\to +\infty}\int_{\{\varphi_k>\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_\psi\leq \int_{\{\varphi\geq \psi\}}\mathrm{MA}_\psi.
\end{align*}
Replacing $ \psi $ with $ \psi+\varepsilon $ and letting $ \varepsilon $ decrease to $ 0 $ we conclude.

    \int_M \partial f \wedge \partial_J f \wedge \Omega_h\wedge T= \frac{1}{4}\int_M (\psi-\varphi)\left(\Omega_\varphi-\Omega_\psi\right)\wedge \Omega_h\wedge T\leq \int_M (-h)\Omega_h^2\wedge T \,,
    $$
    where we used $(-h)\Omega_h+\frac{1}{4}(\varphi-\psi)(\Omega_\varphi-\Omega_\psi)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi\Omega_\varphi+\varphi \Omega_\psi\right)\geq 0$. Hence
    \begin{equation}\label{ineq1}
                    
        \int_M \partial f \wedge \partial_J f \wedge \Omega \wedge T
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 
        \leq  5\left(\int_M \partial f\wedge \partial_J f\wedge \Omega_h \wedge T\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_M(-h)\Omega_h^2\wedge T\right)^{1/2}\,.
                  
    \end{equation}
    For $T=\Omega_h^l\wedge \Omega^{n-2-l}$, where $l=0,\dots,n-2$, we also have that
    \begin{equation}\label{ineq2}
    \int_M(-h)\Omega_h^2\wedge T\leq\int_M(-h)\Omega_h^n\,.
    \end{equation}
    Thus, applying iteratively the inequalities \eqref{ineq1}, \eqref{ineq2} to $T=\Omega_h^l \wedge \Omega^{n-2-l}$ for all $l=0,\dots,n-2$ we deduce
    \[
    \begin{split}
        \int_M \partial f\wedge \partial_J f \wedge \Omega^{n-1}&\leq 5^{\sum_{j=0}^{n-2} \frac{1}{2^j}}\left(\int_M \partial f\wedge \partial_J f \wedge \Omega_h^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}}\left(\int_M(-h)\Omega_h^n\right)^{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2^j}}\\
        &\leq 25 \left(\int_M \partial f\wedge \partial_J f \wedge \Omega_h^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}}\int_M(-h)\Omega_h^n\,,
    \end{split}
    \]
    where we also used that $\int_M (-h)\Omega_h^n\geq 1$. But, since $\binom{n-1}{l}\leq 2^{n-1}$,
    $$
    \int_M \partial f \wedge \partial_J f \wedge \Omega_h^{n-1}\leq \int_M \partial f \wedge \partial_J f \wedge \sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\Omega_\varphi^l \wedge \Omega_\psi^{n-1-l}=\int_M \frac{-f}{2}\left(\Omega_\varphi^n-\Omega_\psi^n\right)=0\,.
    $$
    Hence we have
    $$
                                        
    \int_M \partial f \wedge \partial_J f\wedge \Omega^{n-1}=0\,,
    $$
    i.e. $\partial f=0$, which concludes the proof.

\label{prop_convolution}
Let $A\subseteq \mathbb{H}^n$ be a domain, $ \varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A) $ and denote $\varphi_\varepsilon=\varphi\star \chi_\varepsilon $ the standard regularisation on $ A_\varepsilon=\{ x\in A \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,\partial A)>\varepsilon \} $ via mollification. As $\varphi$ is subharmonic, the sequence $\varphi_\varepsilon$ is subharmonic and decreases to $\varphi$ as $\varepsilon\searrow 0$. To conclude the proof it then remains to show that $\varphi_\varepsilon$ satisfies the submean inequality when restricted on an affine right quaternionic line. Namely, we want
    $$
    \varphi_\varepsilon(x)\leq \frac{1}{\lvert \mathbb{B}\rvert}\int_{\mathbb{B}} \varphi_\varepsilon(x+vy) \, d\lambda_1(y)
    $$
    for any $x\in A_\varepsilon$ and any $v\in \mathbb{H}^n$ such that $x+v y\in A_\varepsilon$ for any $y\in\mathbb{B}$, where $\mathbb{B}$ is the $4$-dimensional unit ball and $d\lambda_r$ is the Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^{4r}$.     $$

    $$

    $$
    concluding the proof.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If $ (\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A) $ is a decreasing sequence then $ \lim_j \varphi_j $ is either quaternionic plurisubharmonic or constant $ -\infty $.

Let $(M,I,J,K,\Omega)$ be a compact locally flat HKT manifold:
\begin{enumerate}
                                                                                                                                                                                            

                
\item If $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} $ is a decreasing sequence in $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ then either it converges uniformly to $-\infty$ or $ \lim_j \varphi_j \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$.

\item If $ (\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} $ is a sequence in $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ such that $ \varphi=\sup_{j\in \mathbb{N}}\varphi_j $ is bounded from above, then the upper semicontinuous regularisation $ \varphi^* $ belongs to $ \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega) $.

\item If $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} $ is a sequence in $\operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ convergent to $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(M,\Omega)$ then the sequence $\psi_j:=(\sup_{l\geq j}\varphi_l)^*$ decreases to $\varphi$.

\end{enumerate}

The first three points are immediate consequences of Proposition \ref{3} since on any local quaternionic chart such that $\Omega=\partial \partial_J u$ the functions $u+\varphi_j$ are quaternionic psh.
    

    Also for the last point it is enough to work locally. So let us assume that $\varphi_j\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)$, where $A$ is a domain of $\mathbb{H}^n$ and suppose that they do not converge locally uniformly to $-\infty$.
    Without loss of generality we may suppose $\varphi_j \leq 0$. Since $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}$ does not converge to $-\infty$ locally uniformly, there exist a constant $C>0$ and a compact subset $K\subseteq A$ such that
    $\limsup_{j\to +\infty} \sup_K \varphi_j >-C$. Up to a subsequence, there exist points $x_j\in K$ such that $\varphi_j(x_j) \geq -2C$ and $x_j\to x\in K$. Since quaternionic psh functions are subharmonic, it follows that
\[
\int_{\mathbb{B}} \varphi_j d\lambda \geq \int_{\mathbb{B}_j} \varphi_j d\lambda \geq \lambda(\mathbb{B}_j)\varphi_j(x_j) \geq -2C\lambda(\mathbb{B}_j)\,,

where $\lambda$ is the Lebesgue measure on $A$.

Clearly $X$ is open and non-empty by what was proved above, we aim to show that it is also closed. Let $y$ be a point in the closure of $X$ and fix a ball $\mathbb{B}_\varepsilon(y)$ centred at $y$ of radius $\varepsilon>0$ small enough so that $\mathbb{B}_\varepsilon(y)\subseteq A$. There exists a point $z\in \mathbb{B}_\varepsilon(y)\cap X$ and thus $z$ has a neighbourhood $U\subseteq A$ such that $\int_U \varphi_jd\lambda$ is uniformly bounded from below. We deduce that $\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)$ is a neighborhood of $y$ and that $\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)}\varphi_jd\lambda\geq \int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon+\sigma}(z_j)}\varphi_jd\lambda$ is uniformly bounded in $j\in\mathbb{N}$.
                                       
We conclude that $y\in X$ and thus $X$ is closed. Consequently, $X=A$ and the sequence $\varphi_j$ is bounded in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)$.

Indeed the sequence of non-negative measures $\mu_j:=(-\varphi_j)\lambda$ is bounded in the weak topology of Radon measures on $A$ and thus admits a convergent subsequence. By the classical theory of distribution the positive limit Radon measure $\mu$ can be identified with a distribution that we will denote $-\psi$. $$
(\partial \partial_J \psi)(\alpha):=\psi(f_\alpha)\,,
$$
by the usual extension of derivation to distributions (as in \eqref{eqn:curr}) and the duality between top forms and functions given by the Lebesgue measure, i.e. $ \psi(f\, d\lambda):= \psi(f). $

\[
\begin{split}

\begin{equation}\label{inH}
\int_{K} |\varphi_j-\varphi| \, d \lambda \leq  \int_{A} \eta (\varphi_j\star \chi_\varepsilon-\varphi_j) \, d \lambda  + \int_{A} \eta |\varphi_j\star \chi_\varepsilon-\varphi\star \chi_\varepsilon | \, d \lambda + \int_{A} \eta (\varphi \star \chi_\varepsilon -\varphi) \, d \lambda \,,
\end{equation}

\[

\]
In particular, by Ascoli-Arzelà Theorem $\varphi_j \star \chi_\varepsilon \to \varphi \star \chi_\varepsilon $ locally uniformly on $A$ as $j\to +\infty$. As a consequence, taking the $\limsup$ as $j\to +\infty$ of the inequality \eqref{inH} we get
\[
\limsup_{j\to+ \infty}\int_{K} |\varphi_j-\varphi| \, d \lambda  \leq 2 \int_A \eta (\varphi \star \chi_\varepsilon-\varphi) \, d\lambda \,,
\]
where we also used that $\varphi_j$ converges to $\varphi$ weakly. Taking the limit as $\varepsilon \searrow 0$ concludes the proof, as the monotone convergence theorem guarantees that $\int_A \eta (\varphi \star \chi_\varepsilon - \varphi) \, d \lambda \to 0$.

\label{Cor:Hartogs}
Let $A\subseteq \mathbb{H}^n$ be a domain. Let $(\varphi_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}} \subset \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)\cap C^0(A)$ be a uniformly bounded sequence that converges pointwise to a positive function $\varphi\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)$. If there exist $c>0$ and $t>1$ such that
\[
\varphi-t\varphi_*<c
\]
on a compact set $K\subseteq A$, then there exists an index $j_0\in \mathbb{N} $ and open neighbourhood $U\subseteq A$ of $K$ such that
\[
\varphi_j-t\varphi<c
\]
on $U$, for all $j\geq j_0$.
<!-- end lem-15 -->
<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
Take $x_0\in K$. Then there exists $0<c_1<c$ such that $
\varphi(x_0)-t\varphi_*(x_0)<c_1$. Since $\varphi$ is upper semicontinuous there exists an open neighbourhood $V$ of $x_0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{Hart1}
\varphi(x)<t\varphi_*(x_0)+c_1 \text{ for all }x\in V\,.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, by definition of lower semicontinuous regularisation, up to shrinking $V$, we may assume that there exists $c_1<c_2<c$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{Hart2}
t\varphi_*(x_0)-c_2+c_1<t\inf_V \varphi\,.
\end{equation}
Thus, combining \eqref{Hart1} and \eqref{Hart2} we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{Hart3}
\varphi(x)<t\inf_V \varphi + c_2 \text{ for all }x\in V\,.
\end{equation}
By Hartogs' Lemma for subharmonic functions (see e.g. \cite[Thm. 2.6.4]{Klimek}), for each $\varepsilon >0$ and each compact neighbourhood $K_0 \subseteq V$ of $x_0$ there exists an index $j_0$ such that $\varphi_j\leq \sup_V \varphi +\varepsilon $ on $K_0$ for any $j\geq j_0$. Choosing $\varepsilon=c-c_2$ and recalling \eqref{Hart3} we obtain
\[
\varphi_j(x)\leq \sup_V \varphi +c-c_2<t\inf_V \varphi + c \leq t\varphi(x)+c
\]
for all $x\in K_0$ and $j\geq j_0$. Since $K$ is compact and the point $x_0\in K$ was chosen arbitrarily the inequality remains true on an open neighbourhood $U$ of $K$, up to increasing $j_0$.

\label{Prop:supCap}
Let $A\subseteq \mathbb{H}^n$ be a bounded domain with the standard HKT structure $\Omega_0$. Let $w \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)\cap L^\infty(\bar A)$ and $\psi \in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)\cap C^0(\bar A)$ be such that
\[
\liminf_{A\ni x\to y}(w(x)-\psi(x)) \geq 0 \qquad \text{for any }y\in \partial A\,.
\]
If 
\[
(\partial \partial_J w)^n=h\,\Omega^n_0 \qquad \text{on }A\,,
\]
for some $h\in L^\infty(A)$, then for any $\alpha \in (0,1)$ there exists a constant $C>0$ such that
\[
\sup_A(\psi-w)\leq \varepsilon +C \cdot \mathrm{Cap^{WK}}\bigl(\{\psi-w>\varepsilon\},A\bigr)^{\alpha/n}\,,
\]
for any $\varepsilon >0$.
<!-- end prop-14 -->
<!-- proof-33: body_md -->
For $s\geq 0$ define
\[
f(s):=\mathrm{Cap^{WK}}\bigl(\{w-\psi<-s-\varepsilon\}, A\bigr)^{1/n}\,.
\]
The function $f$ is right-continuous, decreasing and satisfies $\lim_{s\to +\infty}f(s)=0$ (because $w$ and $\psi$ are bounded). Now, for any $t\in [0,1]$, the following inequality holds
\begin{equation}\label{ineq}
t^nf(s+t)^n\leq \int_{\{w-\psi<-s-\varepsilon\}} (\partial \partial_J w)^n\,.
\end{equation}
The proof is similar to that of Lemma \ref{Lem_CapMA}. Take $u\in \operatorname{\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathbb{H}}}(A)$ such that $0\leq u\leq 1$ and note the inclusions
\[
\{w-\psi<-s-t-\varepsilon\} \subseteq \left\{ w -\psi< tu-s-t-\varepsilon \right\} \subseteq \{w-\psi <-s-\varepsilon\}\,.
\]
Thanks to these, the fact that $(\partial \partial_J (tu))^n\leq(\partial \partial_J (\psi+tu-s-t-\varepsilon))^n $ and the comparison principle of \cite[Lem. 2.5]{Wan-Kang} we deduce
\[
\begin{split}
t^n\int_{\{w-\psi<-s-t-\varepsilon\}}(\partial \partial_J u)^n&\leq \int_{\{w-\psi<-s-t-\varepsilon\}}(\partial \partial_J (\psi+tu-s-t-\varepsilon))^n\\
&\leq\int_{\{ w -\psi< tu-s-t-\varepsilon\}}(\partial \partial_J (\psi+tu-s-t-\varepsilon))^n\\
&\leq\int_{\{ w -\psi< tu-s-t-\varepsilon\}}(\partial \partial_J w)^n \leq \int_{\{ w -\psi<-s-\varepsilon\}}(\partial \partial_J w)^n \,.
\end{split}
\]
Taking the supremum over all $u$'s yields \eqref{ineq}. Now, by assumption $(\partial \partial_J w)^n=h\, \Omega^n_0$ and thus, for any $\alpha \in (0,1)$, thanks to \cite[Lem. 9]{Sro}, we have
\[
\begin{split}
t^nf(s+t)^n&\leq \int_{\{w-\psi<-s-\varepsilon\}} h\,\Omega^n_0\leq \|h\|_{L^\infty} \int_{\{w-\psi<-s-\varepsilon\}}\Omega^n_0\\
&\leq C_\alpha \|h\|_{L^\infty} \mathrm{Cap^{WK}} \bigl(\{w-\psi<-s-\varepsilon\},A\bigr)^{1+\alpha}=C_\alpha \|h\|_{L^\infty}f(s)^{n(1+\alpha)} \,,
\end{split}
\]
where $C_\alpha>0$ is a constant only depending on $A, \Omega_0$ and $\alpha$. Hence, $f$ satisfies \eqref{eq_boundedness} with $B=(C_\alpha \|h\|_{L^\infty})^{1/n}$. We can now apply Lemma \ref{Lem:EGZ} and deduce that there exists $S>0$ such that 
\[
\sup_A (\psi-w)\leq S+\varepsilon\,.
\]
If  $f(0)^\alpha\geq (2B)^{-1}$ we deduce immediately the desired inequality:
\[
\sup_A (\psi-w)\leq S+\varepsilon\leq 2B S f(0)^\alpha+\varepsilon=2B S \mathrm{Cap^{WK}}\bigl(\{w-\psi<-\varepsilon\},A\bigr)^{\alpha/n}+\varepsilon\,.
\]
If instead $f(0)^\alpha<(2B)^{-1}$ we can take $s_0=0$ in \eqref{eqn:S} and conclude 
\[
\sup_A (\psi-w)\leq S+\varepsilon\leq \frac{2B}{1-2^{-\alpha}}\mathrm{Cap^{WK}}\bigl(\{w-\psi<-\varepsilon\},A\bigr)^{\alpha/n}+\varepsilon\,. \qedhere
\]
