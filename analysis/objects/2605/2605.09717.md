<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
The family of functions $g_\lambda:[0,\kappa^2]\to\mathbb{R}$, $\lambda\in (0,\kappa^2)$, is called a regulariser  if there exist $B,D,\gamma>0$ such that  $\sup_{0<\sigma\leq\kappa^2}\left|\sigma g_\lambda(\sigma)\right|<D$, $\sup_{0<\sigma\leq\kappa^2}\left|g_\lambda(\sigma)\right|<B/\lambda$ and $\sup_{0<\sigma\leq\kappa^2}\left|1-\sigma g_\lambda(\sigma)\right|<\gamma$ for all $0<\lambda\leq\kappa^2$. 
The qualification of $g_\lambda$ is the maximal $\nu>0$ such that there exists $\gamma_\nu>0$ satisfying for all $\lambda\in (0,\kappa^2)$
$$
\sup_{0<\sigma\leq\kappa^2}\left|1-\sigma g_\lambda(\sigma)\right|\sigma^\nu\leq \gamma_\nu \lambda^\nu.
                                                                                                                                            
$$

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem 1}
Let Assumptions (A2) and (A4) hold, $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $\eta\in (0,1)$. Then,  
 with probability at least $1-\eta$, it holds                  
$$
\left\|\hat{b}-b\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq \frac{6C}{\sqrt{n}}\log \frac{2}{\eta}.                                                                    
$$
where $C$ is given in Assumption (A4), $b$ in Equation \eqref{eq b} and $\hat{b}$ in Equation \eqref{eq hb}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem 2}
Let Assumption (A2) hold, $n_z,n,n_u\in\mathbb{N}$ and $\eta\in (0,1)$. Then, with probability at least $1-\eta$, it holds under (S1) 
$$
\left\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}-\mathcal{L}\right\|\leq \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_z}}\log\frac{2}{\eta} 
                                                                                         
             
$$
and it holds under (S2)

\left\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}-\mathcal{L}\right\|\leq \left(\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}\right)\log\frac{4}{\eta}                                                                                
$$
where $\kappa^2$ is given in Assumption (A2), $\mathcal{L}$ in Equation \eqref{eq LS} and $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$ in Equation \eqref{eq hLS}.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{theo main}
Consider either Setting (S1) with $n_z=m$ or (S2) with $n_u=m$. Let Assumptions (A1) to (A5) hold for some $r>0$ and $u\in \mathcal{H}$. Let $\bar{r}$ be the qualification of the regulariser $g_\lambda$ and $\hat{f}_\lambda$ be defined as in \eqref{def estimator}.                                                                                               
Let $\eta\in (0,1)$,                                               
choose $\lambda=(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2})^{1/(r+1)}$ and assume that 
$\lambda^{-r}>6\kappa^2\log(4/\eta)$. 
                                                                                     
Then, with probability at least $1-\eta$,                                 
if $r\in [0,\bar{r}]$ it holds                                                                           
$$
                                                                                                        
\left\|\hat{f}_\lambda-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C_1  \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\right)^{\frac{r}{r+1}}\log\left(\frac{4}{\eta}\right)
$$
and if $r\in [0,\bar{r}-1/2]$ it holds
$$
                                                                                                         
\left\|\hat{f}_\lambda-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|_{Z}\leq C_2 \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\right)^{\frac{2r+1}{2r+2}}\log\left(\frac{4}{\eta}\right)
$$
where $C_1$ and $C_2$ are constants that do not depend on $n$, $m$ and $\eta$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Setting $m=n$ in Theorem \ref{theo main} yields convergence rates of order $ n^{-{r}/{(2r+2)}}$ in $\mathcal{H}$ and $n^{-{(2r+1)}/{(4r+4)}}$ in $L^2(Z)$ for $\hat{f}_\lambda$. These rates can be improved if we add assumptions on the capacity of the hypothesis space, usually measured by the effective dimension                                                         
$$
\mathcal{N}(\lambda):=\mathrm{Tr}\left((\mathcal{L}+\lambda I)^{-1}\mathcal{L}\right),\quad \lambda>0.
$$
                                                                                              
For example, under the assumption that $\mathcal{N}(\lambda)\leq c \lambda^{-\beta}$ for some $\beta\in (0,1]$ and $c>0$, Corollary 5.1 in \citep{Lu} states that                                               
$$
\left\|\hat{f}_\lambda-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C_1 n^{-\frac{r}{2r+1+\beta}}\left(\log\frac{6}{\eta}\right)^2
$$
and                           
$$
\left\|\hat{f}_\lambda-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|_{Z}\leq C_2 n^{-\frac{2r+1}{4r+2+2\beta}}\left(\log\frac{6}{\eta}\right)^3
$$
with $\lambda=n^{-1/(2r+1+\beta)}$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\mathcal{L}$ is an integral operator with bounded symmetric kernel $k$, it is compact and self-adjoint on $L^2(Z)$. Hence, the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators tells us that the eigenvectors $\left(\tilde{e}_i\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ of $\mathcal{L}$ form an orthogonal basis of $L^2(Z)$. Moreover, the scalar product on $\mathcal{H}$ of two elements $f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}f_i \tilde{e}_i$ and $g=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}g_i \tilde{e}_i$ is given by $$\left\langle f,g \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{f_i g_i}{\sigma_i}$$ 
where $\left(\sigma_i\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ are the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{L}$ (see e.g. \citep{Liu}). Hence, an orthogonal basis of $\mathcal{H}$ is given by $\left(e_i\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ with $e_i=\sqrt{\sigma_i}\tilde{e}_i$. 
We can conclude that $\left\| \mathcal{L}\right\|_{L^2(Z)\to L^2(Z)}=\sigma_1=\left\| \mathcal{L}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}}$ where $\sigma_1$ is the largest eigenvalue of $\mathcal{L}$ and $\left\| \cdot\right\|_{L^2(Z)\to L^2(Z)}$ denotes the operator norm on $L^2(Z)$. 
                                                                                                                                         
Finally, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Assumption (A2), one obtains that  $\left\| \mathcal{L}\right\|_{L^2(Z)\to L^2(Z)}\leq  \kappa^2$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We define $\hat{D}:L^2(Z)\to\mathbb{R}$ and $V:\mathbb{R}^{n_z}\times \mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ by 
$$\hat{D}(f)=\frac{1}{n_z}\sum_{i=1}^{n_z} f(z_i)^2-\frac{2}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathcal{T} f(v_i)\quad\text{and}\quad
V(a,b)=\frac{1}{n_z}\sum_{i=1}^{n_z} a_i^2-\frac{2}{n} b.$$
We have 
$$\hat{D}(f)=V\left(\left\langle f,k(\cdot,z_1) \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\ldots,\left\langle f,k(\cdot,z_n) \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}, \left\langle f,\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathcal{T}_2 k(\cdot, v_i) \right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right)$$
and $\nabla V(a,b)=(2a_1/n_z,\ldots, 2a_{n_z}/n_z,-2/n)$. Theorem A.2 in \citep{Sriperumbudur} tells us that the solution of 
$\inf_{f\in \mathcal{H}}\hat{D}(f)+\left\|f \right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2$ is given by Equation \eqref{eq fGRS} with $f_0=0$ and $(\alpha,\beta)$ being the solution of 
$$
                                                                                                                            
\lambda (\alpha,\beta)+ \nabla V(K\alpha+\beta n\hat{B}, \alpha^\top n\hat{B}+\beta1_n^\top \mathcal{T}_1\mathcal{T}_2 K 1_n)=0
$$
where $\hat{B}=\left(\hat{b}\left(z_i\right)\right)_{i=1}^{n_z}$, $1_n$ is the vector of size $n$ whose components are all equal to 1 and $\mathcal{T}_1\mathcal{T}_2 K=\left[\mathcal{T}_1\mathcal{T}_2 k(v_i,v_j)\right]_{i,j=1}^n$. This equation simplifies as
$$
                                                                                                                        
\lambda\alpha+\frac{2}{n_z}\left( K\alpha+\beta n \hat{B}\right)=0\quad \text{and}\quad \beta =\frac{2}{n \lambda}
$$
from which the desired result follows.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\left\|\nabla F(f)\right\|^2_{Z}                                     
=\langle \mathcal{L} \nabla F(f),\nabla F(f)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\leq  \left\|\mathcal{L}\nabla F(f)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\left\|\nabla F(f)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}.
$$
Rearranging this inequality yields

\delta^{(2)}_{}=\frac{\left\|\nabla F(f)\right\|^2_{Z}}{2\left\|\mathcal{L}\nabla F(f)\right\|^2_{\mathcal{H}}}
                      
\leq\frac{\left\| \nabla F(f)\right\|^2_{\mathcal{H}}}{2\left\|\nabla F(f)\right\|^2_{Z}} =\delta^{(1)}_{}. 
\qedhere
\end{equation*}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators between $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{H}$ can be identified with $\mathcal{H}\otimes \mathcal{H}$, which is again a separable Hilbert space with norm $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\text{HS}}$. Define the operator $\zeta_z:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}$ by $\zeta_z f=\left\langle f,k(\cdot,z)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}k(\cdot,z)=f(z)k(\cdot,z)$. Observe that $\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}[\zeta_Z]$. To apply Proposition 2 from \citep{Caponnetto07}, we have to show that there exists $L>0$ such that $\left\|\zeta_z\right\|_{\text{HS}}\leq L/2$ for all $z\in\mathcal{Z}$. We first consider Setting (S1). Let $(e_i)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ be an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal{H}$.
                                                                                                                                             
                                                        
                    
                 
             
                                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                
               
                                                                                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                            
                                                                                                           
   
                                            
                                               
The Hilbert-Schmidt norm of $\zeta_z$ is 
\begin{align*}
\left\|\zeta_z\right\|_{\text{HS}}^2&=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\zeta_z e_i\right\|^2_{\mathcal{H}}
= \sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|k(\cdot,z)e_i(z)\right\|^2_{\mathcal{H}}
= \sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}} e_i(z)^2 k(z,z).
\end{align*}
Using that $k\leq \kappa^2$ and Parseval's identity, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\left\|\zeta_z\right\|_{\text{HS}}^2\leq \kappa^2 \sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}} e_i(z)^2 
= \kappa^2 \sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}  \left\langle e_i,k(\cdot,z)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^2
= \kappa^2\left\|k(\cdot,z)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 
= \kappa^4.
\end{align*}
                                              
                                                                                                      
                                                          
                                     
                                                                             
                                                  
Note that this implies $\mathbb{E}[\left\|\zeta_Z\right\|^2_{\text{HS}}]<\kappa^4$.
                                                                                                                                                                                                           
                                                                                                                                                                                                                         
All the assumptions of Proposition 2 in \citep{Caponnetto07} are thus verified with $L=2\kappa^2$ and $\sigma=\kappa^2$ and the desired result follows since $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}}\leq \left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$.                                                              

Now we consider the proof under Setting (S2). Let $(X_i)_{i=1}^n$ be an i.i.d.\ sample of $X$ and $(U_j)_{j=1}^{n_u}$ be an i.i.d.\ sample of $U$. Let 
$$
B_1=\left\|\frac{1}{n_u}\sum_{j=1}^{n_u}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\zeta_{(X_i,U_j)}-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\zeta_{(X_i,U)}|X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]\right\|_{\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}}\quad
$$
and
$$
                 
B_2=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{(X_i,U)}|X_i\right]-\mathcal{L}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}}.
$$
Observe that $\left\|{1}/{n}\sum_{i}\zeta_{(x_i,u)}\right\|\leq {1}/{n}\sum_{i}\left\|\zeta_{(x_i,u)}\right\|\leq \kappa^2$ for all $u\in \mathcal{U}$ and $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in \mathcal{X}$ by Jensen's inequality and the first part of the proof and similarly $\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{(X,U)}|X=x\right]\right\|\leq \kappa^2$ for all $x\in \mathcal{X}$. By Proposition 2 in \citep{Caponnetto07}, we have 
$\mathbb{P}[B_1\geq \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}|X_1,\ldots,X_n]\leq \eta/2$ and $\mathbb{P}[B_2\geq \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}]\leq \eta/2$. Finally, observe that
$$
\mathbb{P}\left[B_1\geq \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right]
                                                                                                             
=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\left[B_1\geq \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}\bigg|X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]\right]
\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\eta/2\right] \leq \eta/2.
$$
Now we compute
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{nn_u}\sum_{i,j}\zeta_{(x_i,u_j)}-\mathcal{L}\right\|
\geq \left(\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}+\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right]
&\leq \mathbb{P}\left[B_1+B_2 \geq \left(\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}+\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right]\\
&\leq  \mathbb{P}\left[\left\{B_1\geq  \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right\}
\cup \left\{B_2\geq  \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right\} \right]\\
&\leq \mathbb{P}\left[B_1 \geq  \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n_u}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right]
+\mathbb{P}\left[B_2 \geq  \frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right]\\
&\leq \eta. \qedhere
\end{align*}

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
In this proof, we write $\left\|\cdot\right\|$  instead of $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}$.                            
Let $m=n_z$ under Setting (S1) and $m=n_u$ under Setting (S2). We have
\begin{align*}
\left\|\hat{f}-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|&\leq \left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})(\hat{b}-b)\right\|+\left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\mathcal{L} f_\mathcal{H}-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|\\
&\leq \left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})(\hat{b}-b)\right\|+\left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})(\mathcal{L}-\hat{\mathcal{L}})f_\mathcal{H}\right\|+\left\|r_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})f_\mathcal{H}\right\|
\end{align*}
where $r_\lambda(\sigma)=g_\lambda(\sigma)\sigma-1$. Assume first that $r\leq 1$. On the one hand, as $\left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\right\|\leq B/\lambda$, we have using Lemmas \ref{lem 1} and \ref{lem 2}
\begin{align*}
\left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})(\hat{b}-b)\right\|+\left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})(\mathcal{L}-\hat{\mathcal{L}})f_\mathcal{H}\right\|
&\leq \left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\right\| \left\|\hat{b}-b\right\|+\left\|g_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\right\| \left\|\mathcal{L}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}\right\| \left\|f_\mathcal{H}\right\|\\
                                                                                                             
&\leq \left(\frac{A_1}{\lambda\sqrt{n}}+\frac{A_2}{\lambda\sqrt{m}}\right)\log \frac{4}{\eta}
\end{align*}
                                                  
where $A_1$ and $A_2$ are constants that do not depend on $n$, $m$, $\eta$ and $\lambda$. 
On the other hand, since $\left\|r_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\right\|\leq \gamma$ and $\left\|r_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\hat{\mathcal{L}}^r\right\|\leq \gamma_r\lambda^r$ by definition of a regulariser, we have
\begin{align*}
\left\|r_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})f_\mathcal{H}\right\|&= \left\|r_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\mathcal{L}^r u\right\|\\
&\leq \left\|r_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})\hat{\mathcal{L}}^r u\right\|+\left\|r_\lambda(\hat{\mathcal{L}})(\mathcal{L}^r-\hat{\mathcal{L}}^r) u\right\|\\
&\leq \gamma_r\lambda^r\left\|u\right\|+ \gamma \left\|\mathcal{L}^r-\hat{\mathcal{L}}^r\right\| \left\|u\right\|.
                                                                                                                   
\end{align*}
By Theorem 1 in \citep{Bauer}, there exists $c_r>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq Lr}
\left\|\mathcal{L}^r-\hat{\mathcal{L}}^r\right\|\leq c_r\left\|\mathcal{L}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}\right\|^r 
\leq c_r \left\{\left(\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{m}}\right)\log\frac{4}{\eta}\right\}^r
\leq c_r \lambda^r
                                                                                                
\end{equation}
                                                                                                              
where we have chosen $\lambda>6\kappa^2\left(1/{\sqrt{m}}+1/{\sqrt{n}}\right)\log({4}/{\eta})$ to obtain the last inequality. 
Gathering the above inequalities, we can conclude that 
$$
\left\|\hat{f}-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|
\leq \left(\frac{A_1}{\lambda \sqrt{n}}+\frac{A_2}{\lambda \sqrt{m}}+A_3\lambda^r \right)\log\frac{4}{\eta} 
                                                                                                                                                                     
                                                          
$$
                                                        
where $A_3$ does not depend on $n$, $m$, $\eta$ and $\lambda$. 
Choosing $\lambda=(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2})^{1/(r+1)}$, we obtain the desired bound.  
                                                                            
                                                                                                                                                   
To ensure that $\lambda>6\kappa^2\left(1/{\sqrt{m}}+1/{\sqrt{n}}\right)\log({4}/{\eta})$, we require that $(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2})^{-r/(r+1)}>6\kappa^2\log(4/\eta)$, which can be written as $\lambda^{-r}>6\kappa^2\log(4/\eta)$. 
                                       

When $r>1$, Theorem 1 in \citep{Bauer} cannot be applied but we have that $\left\|\mathcal{L}^r-\hat{\mathcal{L}}^r\right\|\leq c'_r\left\|\mathcal{L}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}\right\|$ by Lemma D.5 in \citep{Zhou} for some $c'_r>0$. Then, we can replace Equation \eqref{eq Lr} by
\begin{equation*}
\left\|\mathcal{L}^r-\hat{\mathcal{L}}^r\right\|\leq c'_r\left\|\mathcal{L}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}\right\| \leq c'_r \left(\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{6\kappa^2}{\sqrt{m}}\right)\log\frac{4}{\eta}                  
                                                                             
\end{equation*}
                                                        
and a similar reasoning applies.
The proof of the convergence in $L^2(Z)$ follows similar arguments and is based on the identity $$\left\|\hat{f}-f_\mathcal{H}\right\|_{L^2(Z)}= \left\|\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}\left(\hat{f}-f_\mathcal{H}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}.$$ A complete proof is provided in \citep{Bauer}. In their notation, it suffices to replace $T$ by $\mathcal{L}$, $T_\mathbf{x}$ by $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$, $S_\mathbf{x}^*y$ by $\hat{b}$ and to set $\phi(t)=t^r$.
