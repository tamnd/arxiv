<!-- que-1: body_md -->
\label{basic} 
Does $T$ have a nontrivial hyperinvariant subspace, that is a subspace ${\cal M}\in \hbox{\rm Lat}({\cal H}_1\oplus{\cal H}_2)$, different from $\{0\}\oplus\{0\}$ and ${\cal H}_1\oplus{\cal H}_2$, which is invariant for every $Q\in\{T\}' :\, Q{\cal M}\subset{\cal M}$?
<!-- end que-1 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{composition} 
We have $\; C_{{\cal E},{\cal F}} \circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}} = C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}}$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
For completeness we sketch the easy proof. 
Given any $X\in{\cal L}({\cal H})$, let $Y= C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}}(X)\in{\cal L}({\cal E})$ and $Z= C_{{\cal E},{\cal F}}(Y)\in{\cal L}({\cal F})$. 
For every $x\in{\cal F}$ we have $Zx= P_{\cal F} Yx= P_{\cal F} P_{\cal E} Xx= P_{\cal F} Xx$, and so $Z= C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}}(X)$. 
 
\rightline{$\square$
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{partial} 
For every $r\in\mathbb{N}$, we have 
\[P_{1,1}\circ M_{3,r}\circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r}}= M_{2,r+1}\circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}_{2,r+1}}= P_{3,3}\circ M_{3,r+1}\circ C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r+1}}.\]
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
Given any $\widetilde X\in{\cal L}({\cal H})$, let us consider its matrix $[\widetilde X]= [\xi_{i,j}]_\infty$, where 
\[\xi_{i,j}= \langle e_i,\widetilde X e_j\rangle \quad (i,j\in\mathbb{N}).\] 
For any $r\in\mathbb{N}$, let $\widetilde X_r:= C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r}}(\widetilde X)$ and $X_r= [\xi_{i,j}^r]_3= M_{3,r}(\widetilde X_r)$. 
Since 
\[\xi_{i,j}^r = \langle e_{r+i-1}, \widetilde X_r e_{r+j-1}\rangle = \langle e_{r+i-1}, \widetilde X e_{r+j-1}\rangle = \xi_{r+i-1,r+j-1} \quad (i,j\in \mathbb{N}_3),\] 
it follows that 
\[P_{1,1}(X_r)= \left[\begin{matrix} 
\xi_{2,2}^r & \xi_{2,3}^r\\ 
\xi_{3,2}^r & \xi_{3,3}^r\end{matrix}\right] = \left[\begin{matrix} 
\xi_{r+1,r+1}& \xi_{r+1,r+2}\\ 
\xi_{r+2,r+1}&\xi_{r+2,r+2}\end{matrix}\right] =: X_0.\] 
On the other hand, let $\widetilde Y_{r+1} := C_{{\cal H},{\cal F}_{2,r+1}}(\widetilde X)$ and $Y_{r+1} = [\eta_{i,j}^{r+1}]_2= M_{2,r+1}(\widetilde Y_{r+1})$. 
Since $\eta_{i,j}^{r+1}= \xi_{r+i,r+j}\; (i,j\in\mathbb{N}_2)$, it follows that $Y_{r+1}=X_0$. 
 
Finally, take $\widetilde X_{r+1}= C_{{\cal H},{\cal E}_{3,r+1}}(\widetilde X)$ and $X_{r+1}= [\xi_{i,j}^{r+1}]_3 = M_{3, r+1}(\widetilde X_{r+1})$, where $\xi_{i,j}^{r+1}= \xi_{r+i,r+j}\; (i,j\in\mathbb{N}_3)$. 
Then 
\[P_{3,3}(X_{r+1})= \left[\begin{matrix} 
\xi_{1,1}^{r+1} & \xi_{1,2}^{r+1}\\ 
\xi_{2,1}^{r+1}& \xi_{2,2}^{r+1}\end{matrix}\right] = \left[\begin{matrix} 
\xi_{r+1,r+1}& \xi_{r+1,r+2}\\ 
\xi_{r+2,r+1}& \xi_{r+2,r+2}\end{matrix}\right]= X_0,\] 
and the proof is completed. 
 
\rightline{$\square$
<!-- end head-2 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{connection} 
For every $r\in\mathbb{N}$, we have 
\begin{itemize} 
\item[\textup{(i)}] $\; q_{3,r}= q_{2,r} q_{2,r+1} \rho(C^{r+1}_{3,1}),$

where $(\widetilde z_1, \widetilde z_2, \widetilde w_1)\in\mathbb{C}^3$ is arbitrary. 
 
Given any $(z_2,w_2,w_3)\in\mathbb{C}^3$, there exists $(\widetilde z_1, \widetilde z_2, \widetilde w_1)\in\mathbb{C}^3$ such that  
\[X(z_2,0,w_2,w_3)= \widetilde X(\widetilde z_1, \widetilde z_2, \widetilde w_1, 0).\] 
The entrywise equations are 
\begin{itemize} 
\item[\textup{(1)}] $\;\widetilde z_1= z_2 x_r + w_2 (b_{\Delta_r}(3) - q_r/x_r),$ 
\item[\textup{(2)}] $\; \widetilde z_2=w_2$, 
\item[\textup{(3)}] $\; \widetilde z_1 x_{r+1} + \widetilde z_2 b_{\Delta_{r+1}}(3) x_{r+1} + \widetilde w_1 q_{r+1}/b_{r+2}= z_2y_r +w_3(b_{\Delta_r}(2) -q_r/x_r)$, 
\item[\textup{(4)}] $\; \widetilde z_2 x_{r+1} + \widetilde w_1 q_{r+1}=w_3$. 
\end{itemize} 
From (3) and (4) we may derive 
\begin{itemize} 
\item[\textup{(5)}] $\; \widetilde z_1b_rb_{r+1}x_{r+1} - \widetilde z_2 b_r x_{r+1} = z_2 b_rb_{r+1}y_r -w_3(b_{r+1}+b_rb_{r+1} q_r/x_r).$ 
\end{itemize} 
Substituting (1) and (2) into (5) we may deduce 
\begin{itemize} 
\item[\textup{(6)}] $\; z_2b_rb_{r+1}(x_rx_{r+1}-y_r) - w_2 x_{r+1}b_{r+1} (1+ b_rq_r/x_r) + w_3 b_{r+1}(1+ b_r q_r/x_r)=0$. 
\end{itemize} 
However (6) can hold for every $(z_2,w_2,w_3)\in\mathbb{C}^3$ only if all coefficients are zero: 
\[x_{r+1}= \frac{y_r}{x_r} \quad \hbox{ and } \quad \frac{q_r}{x_r}= - \frac{1}{b_r}.\] 
Straightforward computations show that under the conditons 
\[\frac{q_r}{x_r}= - \frac{1}{b_r} \quad \hbox{ and } \quad q'_r = \frac{q_r}{b_{r+1}},\] 
the $\Delta_r$-normal subspace $M_{3,r}(\widehat{\cal L}_r)$ is actually strongly $\Delta_r$-normal with parameters $x_r, y_r, q_r\in\mathbb{C}_0$. 
 
\rightline{$\square$
