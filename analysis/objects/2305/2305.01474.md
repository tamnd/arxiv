<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{defKZ}
	A 2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace=(M,\mu,\eta)$ on a 2-category $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{K}}$ is called \emph{colax idempotent}
	$($or \emph{coKZ}$)$ if either of the following equivalent conditions holds: 
	\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm{(\roman*)}]
		\item the multiplication $\mu$ is right adjoint left inverse of $\eta M$; 
		\item any pseudo-algebra structure map $\alpha:M(K)\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} K$ for $M$ is right adjoint left
		inverse of the unit $\eta_K$. 
	\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
We would like to produce a free Grothendieck fibration on a functor
	$F:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}}\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$. So, for every object $a$ in \ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}} and map $f:b\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} F(a)$, we want
	to force 
	the existence of a cartesian lifting of $f$ to $a$. The idea is then to freely add all
	pairs $\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b}{f}{a}{F(a)}$, and thus to consider the comma category
	$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F$.\footnote{We shall often write an object in a comma category $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F$ as a
		pair \@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]} bfa{F(a)} instead of the appropriate triple
		$(b,a,\xymatrix@1@=6ex{b\ar[r]|-{f}&F(a)})$ when no confusion arises.} This can be
	compared for example to the construction of the free monoid on a set.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{propdefmonad}
	The 2-functor $M:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}} \ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ that maps a functor
	$F:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}}\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ into the functor on the left 
	in the comma object diagram in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$
	$$\xymatrix{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{P_2}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_-{P_1}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_{}="f"^-{F}\\
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{}="g"_-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar|>{}|-*=0[@]{{\raisebox{.7ex}{\scalebox{.3}{$\bullet$}}}}"g";"f"}$$
	extends to a colax idempotent 2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace=(M,\mu,\eta)$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By the universal property of the comma object, the identity natural transformation induces the unit $\eta_F:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}} \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F$ over $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$. Explicitly,
	$$\eta_F(a)=\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{F(a)}{\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}_{}}}{a}{F(a)}.$$
	Then the pasting
	$$\xymatrix{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/{P_1}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{P'_2}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_-{P'_1}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{P_2}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_{}="w"^-{P_1}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_{}="f"^-{F}\\
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{}="q"_-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}}&
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{}="g"_-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}}\ar|>{}|-*=0[@]{{\raisebox{.7ex}{\scalebox{.3}{$\bullet$}}}}"q";"w".&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar|>{}|-*=0[@]{{\raisebox{.7ex}{\scalebox{.3}{$\bullet$}}}}"g";"f".}$$
	induces the multiplication $\mu_F:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/{P_1}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}} \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F$ over $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$. Explicitly,
	$$\mu_F(\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b_1}{f_1}{\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b_2}{f_2}{a}{F(a)}}{b_2})=\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}[8ex]{b_1}{f_2f_1}{a}{F(a)}$$
	and it acts on the arrows by pasting the two commutative squares.
	
	Using the universal property of the comma object, it is straightforward to prove that $(M,\mu,\eta)$ is a colax idempotent 2-monad. 
	
	Explicitly, $\eta M\dashv \mu$ with the unit of the adjunction being the identity and counit with component $u_F:\eta_{M(F)}\mu_F\xymatrix@1@C=1.3em{{}\ar|>{}|-*=0[@]{{\raisebox{.7ex}{\scalebox{.3}{$\bullet$}}}}[r]&{}} \ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{M(MF)}}$ the natural transformation given by the family of arrows in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/M(MF)$,
	which component at the index \@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b_1}{f_1}{\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b_2}{f_2}{a}{F(a)}}{b_2} is
	$$\xymatrix{
		b_1\ar[d]_-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}_{b_1}}}\ar[r]^-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}_{b_1}}}&b_1\ar[d]_-{f_1}&
		&b_1\ar[d]^-{f_1}\ar[r]^-{f_2 f_1}&F(a)\ar[d]^-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}_{F(a)}}}\\
		b_1\ar[r]_-{f_1}&b_2&
		&b_2\ar[r]_-{f_2}&F(a)}$$
	from \@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b_1}{\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}_{}}}{\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}[8ex]{b_1}{f_2 f_1}{a}{F(a)}}{F(a)} to \@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b_1}{f_1}{\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b_2}{f_2}{a}{F(a)}}{b_2}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{cps}
	The $2$-category of pseudo-algebras for the colax idempotent 2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ on
	$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ is isomorphic to the $2$-category $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Fib}}(\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}})$ of cloven
	Grothendieck fibrations over \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}} and functors that preserve the cleavage up to
	isomorphism.
	
	The 2-category of strict algebras for $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ is isomorphic to the 2-category
	$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{SpFib}}(\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}})$ of split Grothendieck fibrations over \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}} and functors that
	preserve the cleavage on the nose.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By the characterization theorem for pseudo-algebras for a colax idempotent monad of
	\citep{KockA:monfws} (see also Definition~\ref{defKZ}), a functor $F:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}}\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$
	sustains a structure of pseudo-algebra for $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ if and only if the unit 
	$$\xymatrix{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\ar[r]^-{\eta_F}\ar[d]_-{F}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F\ar[d]^-{M(F)}\\
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar[r]_-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}
	}$$
	has a right adjoint $\alpha$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$.\footnote{Indeed,
		\citet{KockA:monfws} shows just a bijection between the objects.}
	For every $a$ in \ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}} and $f:b\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} F(a)$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$, the object
	$\alpha\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b}{f}{F(a)}{a}$ is over $b$ and the component of the counit of $\eta_F\dashv
	\alpha$ on $\@ifnextchar[{\CCO}{\CCO[6ex]}{b}{f}{F(a)}{a}$ is a lifting of $f$ to $a$, since the counit is
	vertical. The couniversality of the counit precisely translates as such lifting being
	cartesian. 
	
	The strict axioms for strict algebras translates as the cloven fibration being split.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remarks-1: body_md -->
\label{remstreetfibrations}
	(a) Recall from \citep{StreetR:fibbic} that a functor $F :\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}} \mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ is a
	\emph{Street fibration} if, for every object $a$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}$ and arrow $f:b \mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} F(a)$,
	there are an isomorphism $h_a:b_a \mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} b$
	and a cartesian arrow into $a$ over the composite $fh_a : b_a \mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} F(a)$. Street fibrations
	can be characterized as pseudo-algebras for the ``same'' monad \ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace of Grothendieck
	fibrations, but lifted to the pseudo-fibre $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\!/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$, see
	\citep{StreetR:fibbic}. 
	The 2-category $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\!/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ has the same objects as $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$,
	1-cells are triangles filled with a natural isomorphism,
	and 2-cells are those 2-cells in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ which commute with the two natural
	isomorphisms. 
	For the characterization of Street fibrations,
	it is enough to observe that the underlying functor of \ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace lifts,
	and that unit and counit are still natural.
	The rest of the proof works the same as for Proposition~\ref{cps}.
	
	\noindent(b)
	As \ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace is a monad also on the whole 2-category $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$,
	we can also look at the pseudo-algebras there. It is easy to see that the strict algebras
	are again split Grothendieck fibrations. Following \citet{KockA:monfws}, 
	pseudo-algebras for \ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace on $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$ can also be characterized as those functors $F$
	such that the unit $(\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{}},\eta_F)$ has a right adjoint in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$. Let
	$$\xymatrix{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F\ar[r]^-{A}\ar[d]_-{F}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}}\ar[d]^-{M(F)}\\
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar[r]^-{B}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}$$
	be such a right adjoint.
	In particular,
	the functor $B$ is isomorphic to the identity on $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ via an isomorphism $\zeta:B\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}}\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}$.
	Unfolding the other conditions
	one sees that for every object $a$ in \ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}} and arrow $f:b \mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} F(a)$,
	there is a cartesian arrow into $a$ over $f\zeta_b:B(b) \mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} F(a)$.
	It follows that a pseudo-algebra for \ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$ is a Street fibration.
	However, the converse does not seem to hold.
	Indeed, in both cases, given $a$ in \ensuremath{\mathpzc{A}} and $f:b\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} F(a)$,
	we can choose an isomorphism $h$ such that the composite $fh$ has a cartesian lift to $a$.
	But in a Street fibration the choice of $h$ depends on the object $a$,
	whereas in a pseudo-algebra in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$ the choice is given uniformly for every object $a$ by the isomorphism $\zeta_b$.
	
	Notice also that a normal pseudo-algebra in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$, being a right adjoint right inverse to the unit,
	is in particular an arrow in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ and, in fact, a  right adjoint right inverse to the unit in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$.
	It follows that a normal pseudo-algebra in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$ is a normal Grothendieck fibration.
	This fact suggests that (normal) Street fibrations are strictly more general than pseudo-algebras in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^2$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{propcomp}
	The 2-functor underlying the 2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ coincides with the composition
	$$\xymatrix@C=5em{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^-{\mathord{{{\operator@font{cod}}}}^*}&
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}^{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{2}}}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^-{\mathord{{{\operator@font{dom}}}}_\bullet}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}$$
	of the 2-functor $\mathord{{{\operator@font{cod}}}}^*$ that calculates pullbacks along $\mathord{{{\operator@font{cod}}}}:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}^2\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}} \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ and the 2-functor $\mathord{{{\operator@font{dom}}}}_\bullet$ of postcomposition with $\mathord{{{\operator@font{dom}}}}:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}^2\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}} \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The comma object in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$
	$$\xymatrix{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/F\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{P_1}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_-{P_2}& \ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}} \ar@<-.2ex>[d]_{}="g"^-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}}\\

	from $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}$ to $F$ is equivalent to the pullback of $F$ along the lax limit of the

	$$\xymatrix{
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/{F} \ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_-{P_2} &\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}^2\ar@<-.2ex>[r]^{\mathord{{{\operator@font{dom}}}}}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_{}="w"_-{\mathord{{{\operator@font{cod}}}}}&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}\ar@<-.2ex>[d]_{}="f"^-{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Id}_{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}}}}\\

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The 2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ can thus be expressed as the composition of left adjoint 2-functors. This
	observation is original.  
	
	The adjunction $\mathord{{{\operator@font{dom}}}}_\bullet\dashv\mathord{{{\operator@font{dom}}}}^*$ is the usual change of base.
	And the functor $\mathord{{{\operator@font{cod}}}}^*$ has indeed a right 2-adjoint $\mathord{{{\operator@font{cod}}}}_*$ because
	$\mathord{{{\operator@font{cod}}}}$ is an opfibration. Hence it is 2-exponentiable as stated in
	\citet[Th\'eor\`eme~4.4]{GiraudJ:metdld} and 
	in \citet[2nd Proposition on p.~894]{ConducheF:sujdea},  
	but for an explicit proof of the characterisation of exponentiable functors, see
	\citet{StreetR:powf} where such functors are called \emph{powerful}. See also
	\citet[Theorem~2.16]{StreetR:comfat} for the extension to the case of internal categories
	in a cartesian closed category with pullbacks.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{propmhasrightadj}
	The 2-functor underlying the 2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ has a right 2-adjoint $N$, expressed as the

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{mthm}
	The $2$-category of pseudo-coalgebras for the lax idempotent 2-comonad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{N}}\xspace$ on
	$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ is isomorphic to the $2$-category $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Fib}}(\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}})$ of cloven
	Grothendieck fibrations over $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ and functors that preserve the cleavage up to
	isomorphism.  
	
	The 2-category of strict coalgebras for $\ensuremath{\mathbf{N}}\xspace$ is isomorphic to the 2-category
	$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{SpFib}}(\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}})$ of split Grothendieck fibrations over $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ and functors that
	preserve the cleavage on the nose.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The isomorphism between the double category of left adjoints in a
	2-category and the double category of right adjoints \citep{Kelly:reveltwocat} (via mating
	calculus) transforms the 2-category of pseudo-algebras for 
	\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace into the the 2-category of pseudo-coalgebras for \ensuremath{\mathbf{N}}\xspace. Indeed, \citet{LaudaA:frobalg} showed
	that a right pseudo-adjoint to a pseudo-monad in a Gray-category
	is a pseudo-comonad with pseudo-coalgebras 2-equivalent to the pseudo-algebras of the
	pseudo-monad. We notice that, when the pseudo-adjunction is a strict 2-adjunction and the
	interchange rule is strict, the 2-equivalence becomes a 2-isomorphism. We can then
	conclude by Theorem~\ref{cps}.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{propcatoverbhasdescentobj}
	$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ has all weighted 2-colimits, created by the domain functor into
	$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$. In particular, it has all codescent objects. 
	
	Moreover $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ also has all iso-inserters and equifiers, thus all descent
	objects.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
$\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ is the 2-category of strict coalgebras for the 2-comonad $-\times
	\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ on $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$. So the forgetful into $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$ creates all weighted
	2-colimits. Since $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$ is cocomplete as a 2-category, $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ is
	cocomplete as well. As shown in \citep{Lack:codoc}, codescent objects are
	particular weighted 2-colimits. 
	
	The iso-inserter of two functors $F,G:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{C}}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}}\ensuremath{\mathpzc{D}}$ over $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ is given by the
	category whose objects are all pairs ${(C,\phi_C)}$ with $C$ in \ensuremath{\mathpzc{C}} and
	$\phi_C:F(C)\cong G(C)$ an isomorphism in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{D}}$ over the identity, and whose morphisms
	${(C,\phi_C)}\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}}{(C',\phi_{C'})}$ are morphisms $f:C\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} C'$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{C}}$ such that
	$G(f)\phi_C=\phi_{C'}F(f)$. We are thus restricting the usual inserter in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$
	taking only those $\phi_C$ that are vertical.
	
	Equifiers in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ are just calculated in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$. By
	\citep{Lack:codoc}, $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ has then descent objects as well, as they
	can be produced via an iso-inserter followed by two equifiers.

\label{remrightsplitting}

	where $\mathord{{{\operator@font{Cart}}}}(u,X(f))$ is the chosen cartesian lifting of $u$ to $X(f)$. This triangle
	subsumes all the other conditions $\phi$ needs to satisfy, by cartesianity arguments. 
	
	So an arbitrary object of the iso-inserter is equivalently given by $\ple{b,X}$ equipped
	with chosen isomorphisms $\phi_{f,u}$ that satisfy the commutative triangle above. Under
	the axiom of choice, this is equivalent to restrict to those $\ple{b,X}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{G}}_p$
	such that $X$ is a cartesian functor, \emph{i.e.}\xspace a functor that preserves the cleavage up to
	isomorphism.
	
	The naturality-like condition that the morphisms of the iso-inserter need to satisfy is
	equally subsumed under the commutative triangle above. So that morphisms in the
	iso-inserter are simply all morphisms $\ple{h,\lambda}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{G}}_p$. 
	
	In order to produce a descent object, we should then take two equifiers to force the following two conditions (the second one is a cocycle condition):
	$$\phi_{f,\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}_{}}}=\mathord{{{\operator@font{\alpha_{\eta'}}}}}:X(f)\cong(\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}_{}})^*X(f)$$
	$$\begin{tikzcd}
		X(fuv)\arrow[r,"{\phi_{f,uv}}"]\arrow[d,"{\phi_{fu,v}}"'] & (uv)^*X(f) \arrow[d,iso,"{\alpha_{\mu'}}"{inner sep=1.15ex}]\\
		v^*X(fu)\arrow[r,"{v^*\phi_{f,u}}"'] & v^*u^*X(f)
	\end{tikzcd}$$
	{where the isomorphisms $\alpha_{\eta'}$ and $\alpha_{\mu'}$ are those of the
		pseudo-coalgebra structure $\alpha$ on $p$, \emph{i.e.}\xspace those given by the chosen cleavage of
		$p$ (see Remark~\ref{remcoalgstructoffib}). Both conditions are however true for all
		$(\ple{b,X},\phi)$, as they are subsumed by the commutative triangle above,
		by cartesianity arguments. So the iso-inserter calculated above is already the descent
		object we needed.}
	
	We conclude that the concrete recipe of \citet{Lack:codoc} to strictify pseudo-coalgebras 
	translates as taking $R(p)$ to be the restriction of $N(p)$ to those objects $\ple{b,X}$
	with $X$ that preserves the cleavage up to isomorphism (actually with chosen
	isomorphisms), without any further condition on morphisms. This is precisely
	the right splitting construction of \citet{GiraudJ:cohna}.

\label{remessimgivesrightsplitting}
	We note another way to recover the right adjoint splitting of
	fibrations. By Theorem~\ref{mthm}, the pseudo-coalgebra structure $\alpha$ of a cloven
	fibration $p$ is a right adjoint right inverse of the counit $\eta'_F:N(F)\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}} F$. This
	$\alpha$ is a homomorphism of 
	fibrations which embeds $F$ into the split fibration $N(F)$. Closing under isomorphism the
	image of $F$ into $N(F)$ provides a split fibration equivalent to $F$.
	
	This recovers also the right splitting construction of \citet{GiraudJ:cohna}, as by
	Remark~\ref{remcoalgstructoffib} such image is made of triangles 
	$$\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=4.75ex,column sep=4.75ex]
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}/{p(E)}\ar{rr}{(-)^* E}\ar{rd}[']{\partial_0}&&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{E}}\ar{ld}{p}\\
		&\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}
	\end{tikzcd}$$
	and $(-)^*E$ preserves the cleavage up to isomorphism.

\label{remleftsplitting}
	Recall that a factorization system on a 2-category is \emph{enhanced} when squares
	which are pseudo morphisms in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}^{\ensuremath{\mathpzc{2}}}$ with domain in \ensuremath{\mathpzc{E}} and codomain in
	\ensuremath{\mathpzc{M}}---so they commute up to an invertible 2-cell $\alpha$---can be filled with a unique
	diagonal such that the upper triangle commutes strictly and the lower one commutes up to a
	unique invertible 2-cell which coincides with $\alpha$ on the whole square.
	
	{The factorization system on $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}$ where \ensuremath{\mathpzc{E}} consists of the
		bijective-on-objects functors and 
		\ensuremath{\mathpzc{M}} consists of the fully-faithful functors, is enhanced. This lifts 
		to a factorization system on the 2-category $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$, which is easily seen to be
		enhanced.}
	
	The other two assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:cohmnd} are easily verified for the 2-monad
	$\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$. Indeed a fully faithful functor with a right quasi-inverse is clearly an
	equivalence. And it is easily seen from the definition of the 2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ (see
	Proposition~\ref{propdefmonad}) that $M$ preserves bijective-on-objects functors. In fact, the
	action of $M$ on morphisms in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{Cat}}/\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$, induced by the universal property of the
	comma object, is almost trivial. 
	
	Power's insight is that an enhanced factorization system can be used to extract a strict
	algebra from a pseudo one. In particular, the underlying object of the strict algebra can
	be computed factoring the pseudo-algebra map. 
	
	Let $p:\ensuremath{\mathpzc{E}}\xymatrix@1@C=1.6em{{}\ar@<-.2ex>[r]&{}}\ensuremath{\mathpzc{B}}$ be a cloven fibration, with pseudo-algebra structure for the
	2-monad $\ensuremath{\mathbf{M}}\xspace$ given by a map $\alpha:M(p)\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} p$ and isomorphisms $\alpha_\eta$ and
	$\alpha_\mu$ that regulate respectively liftings in the chosen cleavage of $p$ of an
	identity and of a composite. Using (the proof of) Theorem~\ref{thm:cohmnd}, we obtain that the
	underlying object of the left splitting $L(p)$ of $p$ is the full image of the
	pseudo-algebra map $\alpha:M(p)\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} p$. That is, the objects are the same of $M(p)$, but an
	arrow from $(f,a)$ to $(f',a')$ is an arrow $\alpha(f,a) \mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} \alpha(f',a')$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{E}}$
	and thus an arrow $f^*a\mathrel{\xymatrix@1@=2.5ex{*=0{}\ar[];[r]&*=0{}}} (f')^*a'$ in $\ensuremath{\mathpzc{E}}$. This is precisely the left splitting
	construction of \citet{GiraudJ:cohna}.
