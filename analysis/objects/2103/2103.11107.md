<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:tv-dist}
Let $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2 \in (0, 1)$ and $\tilde{S} \subseteq S \subseteq \mathcal{X}$. Then for $m \geq 1+ \frac{2}{\epsilon_1}\log \tfrac{1}{\epsilon_2}$, either $\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S) \leq \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})$ or $\left\|P^{(1)}(\cdot \;|\; S) - \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(\cdot \;|\; S)\right\|_{TV} \leq \epsilon_{2}t$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Consider the $l=1, t=1$ case of the above MCMC sampling procedure. In this case, the procedure outputs only one element of $\mathcal{X}$. This random element is picked by $m$ steps of the following random walk. We first pick $x$ with probability $q(x)$, then we sample a $y$ with probability $q(y)$, we compute $p(x), p(y)$ and sample a number uniformly at random from the interval $(0, 1)$, and if $p(y) q(x)/p(x) q(y) > \mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$, then the random walk moves from $x$ to $y$ and updates $p(x)$ as $p(y)$. Observe that the stationary distribution of the above random walk is the adaptive sampling distribution w.r.t. $S$ given by $p(x) = d(x, \operatorname{span}\left(S\right))^{p}/\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S)$. Using Corollary $1$ of \cite{cai}, the total variation distance after $m$ steps of the random walk is bounded by  
\[
\left(1 - \frac{1}{\gamma}\right)^{m-1} \leq e^{-(m-1)/\gamma} \leq \epsilon_{2}, \quad \text{where $\gamma = \max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}$}.
\]
The above bound is at most $\epsilon_{2}$ if we choose to run the random walk for $m \geq 1+\gamma \log \frac{1}{\epsilon_2}$ steps. Now suppose $\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S) > \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})$. Then, for any $x \in \mathcal{X}$
\[
\frac{p(x)}{q(x)} = \dfrac{\dfrac{d(x, \operatorname{span}\left(S\right))^{p}}{\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S)}}{\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d(x, \operatorname{span}\left(\tilde{S}\right))^{p}}{\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})} + \dfrac{1}{2 \left|\mathcal{X}\right|}} \leq \dfrac{2~ d(x, \operatorname{span}\left(S\right))^{p}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})}{d(x, \operatorname{span}\left(\tilde{S}\right))^{p}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S)} \leq \frac{2}{\epsilon_{1}},
\]
using $\tilde{S} \subseteq S$ and the above assumption $\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S) > \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})$. Therefore, $m > \frac{2}{\epsilon_{1}} \log\frac{1}{\epsilon_{2}}$ ensures that $m$ steps of the random walk gives a distribution within total variation distance $\epsilon_{2}$ from the adaptive sampling distribution for picking a single point.

Note that for $t > 1$ both the adaptive sampling and the MCMC sampling procedure pick an i.i.d. sample of $t$ points, so the total variation distance is additive in $t$, which means
\[
\left\|P^{(1)}(\cdot \;|\; S) - \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(\cdot \;|\; S)\right\|_{TV} \leq \epsilon_{2}t,
\]
assuming $\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S) > \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})$. This completes the proof the lemma.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:induction}
For any subset $S_{0} \subseteq \mathcal{X}$, any $\epsilon_{1}, \epsilon_{2} \in (0, 1)$ and any positive integers $t, l, m$ with $m \geq 1+\frac{2}{\epsilon_2}\log \frac{1}{\epsilon_1}$,
\[
\underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] \leq \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] + \left(\epsilon_{1} + \epsilon_{2} t l\right) \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0}).
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We show a slightly stronger inequality than the one given above, i.e., for any $S_{0}$ such that $\tilde{S} \subseteq S_{0}$,
\begin{align*}
& \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] \\
& \leq \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] +  \left(\epsilon_{1} \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] + \epsilon_{2} t l\right) \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}).
\end{align*}
The special case $S_{0} = \tilde{S}$ gives the lemma. We prove the above-mentioned stronger statement by induction on $l$. For $l=0$, the above inequality holds trivially. Now assuming induction hypothesis, the above holds true for $l-1$ iterations (instead of $l$) starting with any subset $S_{1} = S_{0} \cup A \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ because $\tilde{S} \subseteq S_{0} \subseteq S_{1}$. 
\begin{align}
& \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& = \underset{S_{1} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right]\right] \nonumber \\
& = \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 1} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad \qquad + \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right]. \label{eq:parts-by-ind}
\end{align}
If $\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 1$ then $\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l}) \leq \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) \leq \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0})$, so the first part of the above sum can be bounded as follows.
\begin{align}
& \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 1} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \leq \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0}) \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 1} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right]. \label{eq:part-1-ind}
\end{align}
Now, the second part can be bounded as follows.
\begin{align}
& \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& = \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \leq \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0}) \cdot \nonumber \\
& \qquad \left(\underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] +  \left(\epsilon_{1}~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] + \epsilon_{2} t (l-1)\right) \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})\right) \nonumber \\
& \qquad \qquad \text{by applying the induction hypothesis to $(l-1)$ iterations starting from $S_{1}$} \nonumber \\
& \leq \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} P^{(1)}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})~ \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{2} t (l-1)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})~ \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0}) \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \left|\tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0}) - P^{(1)}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})\right|~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \leq \sum_{S_{1}} P^{(1)}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})~ \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{2} t (l-1)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}) + \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \left|\tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0}) - P^{(1)}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})\right|~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}) \nonumber \\
& \leq \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})~ \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{2} t (l-1)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})  + \left\|\tilde{P}^{(1)}(\cdot \;|\; S_{0}) - P^{(1)}(\cdot \;|\; S_{0})\right\|_{TV}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}) \\
& \leq \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})~ \sum_{S_{1} \;:\; \mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1}) = 0} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{2} t (l-1)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})  + \epsilon_{2} t~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}), \label{eq:part-2-ind} 
\end{align}
using Lemma \ref{lem:tv-dist} about the total variation distance between $P^{(1)}$ and $\tilde{P}^{(1)}$ distributions. Plugging the bounds $\eqref{eq:part-1-ind}$ and $\eqref{eq:part-2-ind}$ into $\eqref{eq:parts-by-ind}$, we get
\begin{align*}
& \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] \\
& \leq \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] + \epsilon_{1}~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})~ \sum_{S_{1}} \tilde{P}^{(1)}_{m}(S_{1} \;|\; S_{0})~ \underset{T_{2:l} \;|\; S_{1}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{1} \cup T_{2:l})\right] \nonumber \\
& \qquad + \epsilon_{2} t (l-1)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S})  + \epsilon_{2} t~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}) \\
& = \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] +  \left(\epsilon_{1} \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{ind}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] + \epsilon_{2} tl\right)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}) \\
& \leq \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] +  \left(\epsilon_{1} + \epsilon_{2} t l\right)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, \tilde{S}),
\end{align*}
which completes the proof of Lemma \ref{lemma:induction}.

where $V^{*}$ minimizes $\mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V)$ over all linear subspaces $V$ of dimension $k$.

\label{thm:alpha-iterations}
Let $k$ be any positive integer and let $\epsilon \in (0, 1)$. Let $S_{0}$ be a subset of $k$ points sampled from $\mathcal{X}$ using $\alpha$-approximate volume sampling for $p=2$. Let $S_{l} = S_{0} \cup T_{1:l}$ be obtained by starting from $S_{0}$ and doing $l$ iterations of MCMC sampling, where in each iteration we add $t$ points from $\mathcal{X}$ by running $t$ independent random walks for $m$ steps each. Then for $t = 8k/\epsilon$, $l = \log(2\alpha(k+1)/\epsilon)/\log(8/\epsilon)$ and $m \geq 1 + 128k \alpha \log^{2}(2\alpha/\epsilon)/\epsilon^{2} \log(8/\epsilon)$, we have
\[
\underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] \leq (1+\epsilon)~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}), \]
where $V^{*}$ minimizes $\mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V)$ over all linear subspaces $V$ of dimension $k$.

We use the following setting of parameters $t, l, \epsilon_{1}, \epsilon_{2}, m$ as functions of $k, \epsilon, \alpha$.
\begin{align*}
t & = \frac{8k}{\epsilon}, \qquad l = \frac{\log(2\alpha(k+1)/\epsilon)}{\log(8/\epsilon)}, \qquad
\epsilon_{1} = \frac{\epsilon}{8\alpha (k+1)}, \qquad \text{and} \\
\epsilon_{2} & = \frac{\epsilon}{8tl\alpha(k+1)} = \frac{\epsilon^{2} \log (8/\epsilon)}{64k(k+1) \alpha \log(2\alpha/\epsilon)}, \\
m & = 1 + \frac{2}{\epsilon_{2}} \log\frac{1}{\epsilon_{1}} = 1 + \frac{128k(k+1) \alpha \log^{2}(2\alpha/\epsilon)}{\epsilon^{2} \log(8/\epsilon)}
\end{align*}

& \leq \underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{E}}\left[\mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})\right] + \left(\epsilon_{1} + \epsilon_{2} t l\right)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0}) \\
& \hspace{6cm} \text{by Lemma \ref{lemma:induction} and $m \geq 1+\frac{2}{\epsilon_{2}}\log \frac{1}{\epsilon_{1}}$} \\

& \hspace{6cm} \text{by Proposition \ref{prop:adaptive-iterations}} \\
& \leq \left(1 + \frac{\epsilon}{4}\right)~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}) + \frac{\epsilon}{2\alpha(k+1)} \alpha(k+1)~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}) \\
& \qquad \qquad + \left(\frac{\epsilon}{8\alpha(k+1)} + \frac{\epsilon}{8\alpha(k+1)}\right) \alpha (k+1)~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}) \\
& \hspace{6cm} \text{by Proposition \ref{prop:l2-vol} and plugging in $t, l, \epsilon_{1}, \epsilon_{2}$} \\
& \leq \left(1 + \frac{\epsilon}{4}\right)~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}) + \frac{\epsilon}{2}~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}) + \frac{\epsilon}{4}~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}) \\ 
& \leq (1+\epsilon)~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}).
\end{align*}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Anari et al. \cite{anari2016monte} give an MCMC algorithm to sample approximately from volume sampling. They start with any subset $S$, pick $i \in S$ and $j \notin S$ uniformly at random, and perform a lazy random walk over $k$-sized subsets, i.e., move to $T = S \setminus \{i\} \cup \{j\}$ with probability $\frac{1}{2} \min\{1, \frac{\mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{T})^{2}}{\mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{S})^{2}}\}$, and with the remaining probability stay at $S$. They show that in $\text{poly}(n, k, \log(1/\delta \mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{S}))$ steps of the above random walk starting at $S$, the resulting distribution is within $\delta$ total variation distance from the exact volume sampling distribution. Thus, using Proposition \ref{prop:l2-vol} with $\alpha = 1$ and adding the error due to total variation distance, we get that  the expected error $\mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, S)$ at the end of $\text{poly}(n, k, \log(1/\delta \mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{S}))$ steps of the above random walk is at most $(k+1)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*}) + \delta \max_{S \;:\; \left|S\right|=k} \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S)$, where $V^{*}$ minimizes $\mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, V)$ over all linear subspaces $V$ of dimension $k$. We choose $\delta$ as follows.
\[
\delta = \frac{1}{\kappa} =  \frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sigma_{\max}} \leq \frac{\min_{S \;:\; \left|S\right|=k} \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S)}{\max_{S \;:\; \left|S\right|=k} \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, S)},
\]
where $\sigma_{\min}$ and $\sigma_{\max}$ are the minimum and the maximum singular values, respectively, of the $n$-by-$d$ matrix whose rows are all the points in $\mathcal{X}$, and $\kappa$ is the condition number. Then the expected error of $\mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, S)$ is at most $(k+2)~ \mathrm{err}_{p}(\mathcal{X}, V^{*})$. Observe that $\min_{S \;:\; \left|S\right|=k} \mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{S})^{2} \geq \sigma_{\min}^{k}$. Note that for the above choice of $\delta$ and any $k$-sized subset $S \subseteq \mathcal{X}$, we can upper bound $\log(1/\delta \mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{S})^{2})$ by $O(k \log \kappa)$, where $\kappa$ is the condition number of the $n$-by-$d$ matrix whose rows are the points of $\mathcal{X}$. Thus, in total number of $\text{poly}(n, k, \log \kappa)$ steps of the random walk we get an initial subset $S_{0}$ with $\mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, S_{0})$ upper bounded by $(k+2)~ \mathrm{err}_{2}(\mathcal{X}, S^{*})$, in expectation. 

Another alternative to implement approximate volume sampling is to do a $(1+\epsilon)$ volume-preseving random projection using only one pass over the given data \cite{DBLP:conf/approx/MagenZ08}, and then do exact volume sampling on the projected data in $\mathrm{poly}(k/\epsilon)$ dimensions. This gives another alternative to obtain the initial subset $S_{0}$. 

The rest of the proof using MCMC sampling algorithm starting from $S_{0}$ using the same subset as a pivot is similar to the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:alpha-iterations}.

The running time of MCMC procedure is $O(tlm \cdot d(tl)^2)=d\cdot\mathrm{poly}(k/\epsilon)$, where $d\cdot(tl)^2$ is the time required to project a point onto $\mathrm{span}(S)$,  where $S$ is a subset of $O(tl)$ points of dimension $d$ each.

\underset{T_{1:l} \;|\; S_{0}}{\operatorname{\tilde{E}}}\left[\sum_{j\in N_{\beta}(S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})}d(x_j, S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})^2\right] \leq (1+\epsilon)~ \sum_{i\in I}d(x_i, S^{*}_I)^2, 
\]
  \text{where $S^{*}_I = \underset{S \;:\; \left|S\right| = k}{\operatorname{argmin}}~ \sum_{i\in I}d(x_i, S)^2$}, and $N_{\beta}(S_{0} \cup T_{1:l}) \subseteq [n]$ denotes the set  of the indices of the nearest $(1-\beta)n$ points to $(S_{0} \cup T_{1:l})$ among $x_{1}, x_{2}, \dotsc, x_{n}$.  This gives a near-optimal $O(k^2 \log k/\epsilon^2)$-sized subset selection with $(1+\epsilon)$ approximation guarantee over inliers in only $2$ passes over $\mathcal{X}$.
