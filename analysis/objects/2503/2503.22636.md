\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])= |P_f \cap M|.
\]

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:PL}
Let $\Sigma$ be a rational fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$. A function $f\colon |\Sigma|\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is \textbf{integral piecewise-linear on $\Sigma$} if, for every cone $\sigma\in\Sigma$, there exists an integral linear function $m\in M$ such that
\[
f|_\sigma=m|_\sigma.
\]
Denote by $\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ the group of integral piecewise-linear functions on $\Sigma$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{example:notlinear}
Let $N=\mathbb{Z}^2$, and let $\Sigma$ be the two-dimensional fan in $N_\mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$ with one maximal cone $\sigma$ spanned by the two rays $v_1=(1,0)$ and $v_2=(1,2)$. There does not exist an integral piecewise-linear function $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ such that $f(v_1)=0$ and $f(v_2)=1$. Indeed, if such a function existed, then linearity on $\sigma$ would imply that its value at
\[
(1,1)=\frac{1}{2}(1,0)+\frac{1}{2}(1,2) \in \sigma
\]
is $1/2$, but this is not an integer.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:uPL}
Let $\Sigma$ be a rational fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$. An element $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ is \textbf{integral linear on $\Sigma$} if there exists $m\in M$ such that
\[
f=m|_{|\Sigma|}.
\]
Let $\mathrm{L}(\Sigma)\subseteq\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ denote the subgroup of integral linear functions on $\Sigma$, and denote by
\[
\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)\coloneq\frac{\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)}{\mathrm{L}(\Sigma)}
\]
the \textbf{group of integral piecewise-linear functions on $\Sigma$ up to translation}.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{example:torsion}
Let $N=\mathbb{Z}^2$, and let $\Sigma$ be the one-dimensional fan in $N_\mathbb{R}\cong \mathbb{R}^2$ with two rays, one spanned by $v_1=(1,0)$ and one spanned by $v_2=(1,2)$. There is a unique piecewise-linear function $f$ such that $f(v_1)=0$ and $f(v_2)=1$. This piecewise-linear function is not the restriction of an integral linear function on $\mathbb{R}^2$ (as we saw in Example~\ref{example:notlinear}), but $2f$ \emph{is} the restriction of an integral linear function; specifically, $2f$ is the restriction of the integral linear function $(x,y)\mapsto y$. In other words,
\[
[f]\neq 0\;\;\;\text{ but }\;\;\;2[f]=0
\]
in $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$.  Thus, $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$ has torsion.
<!-- end example-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $X\subseteq N_\mathbb{R}$ be the support of a rational fan. A function $f\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is \textbf{(integral) piecewise-linear on $X$} if there is a rational fan $\Sigma$ with $|\Sigma|=X$ such that $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$. Denote by $\mathrm{PL}(X)$ the group of piecewise-linear functions on $X$ and by $\mathrm{L}(X)\subseteq \mathrm{PL}(X)$ the group of linear functions on $X$. Denote by
\[
\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(X)\coloneq \frac{\mathrm{PL}(X)}{\mathrm{L}(X)}
\]
the \textbf{group of (integral) piecewise-linear functions on $X$ up to translation}.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
$\mathrm{PL}(X)$ is closed (and therefore forms a group) under addition of functions.  To see this, suppose that $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ are rational fans with equal support $X$ and $f_1\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma_1)$ and $f_2\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma_2)$. We may then choose a common refinement $\Sigma$ of $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$, and since each cone of $\Sigma$ is contained in a cone of $\Sigma_1$ and a cone of $\Sigma_2$, it follows that $f_1$ and $f_2$ are each linear on the cones $\Sigma$. Thus, the sum $f_1+f_2$ is piecewise-linear on $\Sigma$, and since $|\Sigma|=X$, this implies that $f_1+f_2\in\mathrm{PL}(X)$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $\Sigma$ be a rational fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$. A function $F\colon |\Sigma|\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is \textbf{(integral) piecewise-exponential on $\Sigma$} if there exists $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ such that
\[
F=e^f.
\]
Denoting by $\mathrm{Fun}(|\Sigma|,\mathbb{R})$ the ring of all functions $|\Sigma| \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, the \textbf{ring of (integral) piecewise-exponential functions on $\Sigma$}, denoted $\mathrm{PE}(\Sigma)$, is the subring of $\mathrm{Fun}(|\Sigma|,\mathbb{R})$ generated by all (integral) piecewise-exponential functions on $\Sigma$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:expfunctions}
Let $\Sigma$ be a rational fan. We have equality
\[
\sum_{i=1}^ke^{f_i}=\sum_{j=1}^\ell e^{g_j}\in \mathrm{PE}(\Sigma)
\]
if and only if, for every $v\in |\Sigma|$, there is an equality of multisets
\[
\{f_i(v)\mid i=1,\dots,k\}=\{g_j(v)\mid j=1,\dots,\ell\}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The ``if'' direction is immediate. To prove the ``only-if'' direction by contrapositive, suppose that there exists some $v\in|\Sigma|$ such that the two multisets are not equal. Without loss of generality, assume that the elements of the multisets are ordered in nondecreasing order.  By successively removing the largest term from each multiset if they agree, one eventually arrives at two new multisets
\[
\{f_1(v),\dots,f_{k-d}(v)\}\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\{g_1(v),\dots,g_{\ell-d}(v)\}
\]
such that, without loss of generality, $f_{k-d}(v)>g_{\ell-d}(v)\geq\dots\geq g_1(v)$ and
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^ke^{f_i(v)}-\sum_{j=1}^\ell e^{g_j(v)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-d}e^{f_i(v)}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-d} e^{g_j(v)}.
\end{align*}
Note that it is possible to have $\ell-d=0$, in which case the final sum above vanishes. Either way, evaluating at $\lambda v$ for $\lambda\gg 0$, the term
\[
e^{f_{k-d}(\lambda v)}=e^{\lambda f_{k-d}(v)}
\]
dominates all other terms, so the difference cannot be zero.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $\Sigma$ be a rational fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$. Denote by
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:uPESigmadef}
\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(\Sigma)\coloneq\frac{\mathrm{PE}(\Sigma)}{\big\langle e^\ell-1 \mid \ell\in\mathrm{L}(\Sigma)\big\rangle}
\end{equation}
the \textbf{ring of (integral) piecewise-exponential functions on $\Sigma$ up to translation}.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:1inuPE}
Let $f \in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$.  Then $[e^f] = 1 \in \underline{\mathrm{PE}}(\Sigma)$ if and only if $f \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma)$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
As explained above, the only direction that requires proof is the ``only if'' direction, so suppose that $[e^f] = 1 \in \underline{\mathrm{PE}}(\Sigma)$.  This means that $e^f - 1 \in \langle e^\ell - 1 \; | \; \ell \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma) \rangle$, and unpacking the elements of this ideal, we find that
\[e^f - e^0 = \sum_{i=1}^k \left(e^{f_i + \ell_i} - e^{f_i}\right)\]
for some $f_1, \ldots, f_k \in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ and $\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_k \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma)$.  Rearranging and applying Lemma~\ref{lemma:expfunctions}, there is an equality of multisets
\[\{f(v), f_1(v), \ldots, f_k(v)\} = \{0, f_1(v) + \ell_1(v), \ldots, f_k(v) + \ell_k(v)\}\]
for all $v \in |\Sigma|$.  This, in particular, implies that the sums of the elements in these multisets agree, meaning that
\[f(v) = \ell_1(v) + \cdots + \ell_k(v)\]
for all $v \in |\Sigma|$.  That is, $f = \ell_1 + \cdots + \ell_k$, so the lemma is proved.

<!-- example-3: body_md -->
\label{example:petorsion}
Let $N=\mathbb{Z}^2$, and let $\Sigma$ be the one-dimensional fan in $N_\mathbb{R}\cong \mathbb{R}^2$ considered in Example~\ref{example:torsion}. In that example, we found a piecewise-linear function $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)\setminus\mathrm{L}(\Sigma)$ such that $2f\in\mathrm{L}(\Sigma)$. Thus, in $\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(\Sigma)$, we obtain an element
\[
[e^f]\neq 1\;\;\;\text{ such that }\;\;\;[e^f]^2=1.
\]
(The fact that $[e^f] \neq 1$ follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:1inuPE}, since $f \notin \mathrm{L}(\Sigma)$.)  We now use the function $f$ to show that $\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(\Sigma)$ has torsion as an additive group.

For brevity, denote a piecewise-linear function on $\Sigma$ as a pair $(a_1,a_2)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$, where $a_i$ is the value of the function at $v_i$; for instance, the function $f$ from Example~\ref{example:torsion} is represented as the pair $(0,1)$. Notice that a piecewise-linear function represented by $(a_1,a_2)$ is the restriction of a linear function if and only if $a_1-a_2\in2\mathbb{Z}$. 
Within the quotient ring $\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(\Sigma)$, we have, for example,
\[[e^f]=[e^{(0,1)}]=[e^{(1,0)}].\]
Using this observation, we compute
\begin{align*}
2[e^f-1]&=[e^{(0,1)}]+[e^{(0,1)}]-[e^{(0,0)}]-[e^{(0,0)}]\\
&=[e^{(0,1)}]+[e^{(1,0)}]-[e^{(0,0)}]-[e^{(0,0)}]\\
&=[e^{(1,1)}]+[e^{(0,0)}]-[e^{(0,0)}]-[e^{(0,0)}]\\
&=[e^{(1,1)}]-[e^{(0,0)}] \\&= 0,
\end{align*}
where the third equality follows from Lemma~\ref{lemma:expfunctions} and the last equality from Lemma~\ref{lem:1inuPE}.  Therefore, $\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(\Sigma)$ has nontrivial torsion.

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
Let $X\subseteq N_\mathbb{R}$ be the support of a rational fan. A function $F\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is \textbf{(integral) piecewise-exponential on $X$} if there is a rational fan $\Sigma$ with $|\Sigma|=X$ such that $F\in\mathrm{PE}(\Sigma)$. Denote by $\mathrm{PE}(X)$ the \textbf{ring of (integral) piecewise-exponential functions on $X$}, and denote by
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:uPEXdef}
    \underline{\mathrm{PE}}(X)\coloneq\frac{\mathrm{PE}(X)}{\big\langle e^\ell-1 \mid \ell\in\mathrm{L}(X)\big\rangle}
\end{equation}
the \textbf{ring of (integral) piecewise-exponential functions on $X$ up to translation}.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The ring of not-necessarily-integral piecewise-exponential functions, which we denote $\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(X)_\mathbb{R}$, was previously studied by Brion \cite{Brion} in relation to the polytope algebra and Chow rings of toric varieties. The natural inclusion of integral piecewise-exponential functions $\mathrm{PE}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{PE}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ induces a ring homomorphism from $\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(X)$ to $\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(X)_\mathbb{R}$, but the nontrivial torsion element constructed in Example~\ref{example:petorsion} is in the kernel, so this homomorphism is not generally an inclusion.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:K(X)presentation}
Let $X$ be the support of a rational fan.  There is an isomorphism of rings
\[
\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(X) \cong \frac{\mathbb{Z}\{\mathrm{PL}(X)\}}{\mathbb{Z}\bigg\{ \substack{\vspace{5bp}\{f\} + \{g\} - \{\max(f,g)\} - \{\min(f,g)\},\\     \{f+\ell\} - \{f\}}\;\Big|\;\substack{\vspace{5bp}f,g\in\mathrm{PL}(X),\\     \ell\in \mathrm{L}(X)}\bigg\}},
\]
where the notation $\mathbb{Z}\{ \cdot \}$ in the denominator represents the $\mathbb{Z}$-linear span.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
Let $X$ be the support of a rational fan. If $f,g\in\mathrm{PL}(X)$, then 
\[
\max(f,g), \; \min(f,g)\in\mathrm{PL}(X).
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since any two rational fans admit a common rational refinement, we may choose a fan $\Sigma$ with $|\Sigma|=X$ such that $f,g\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma).$ For each cone $\sigma\in\Sigma$, the points
\[
\{v\in\sigma\mid f(v)=g(v)\}
\]
are the intersection of $\sigma$ with a rational hyperplane in $\mathrm{span}_\mathbb{R}(\sigma)$. Upon subdividing each cone $\sigma$ along the corresponding hyperplane, we obtain a new rational fan $\Sigma'$ such that $\max(f,g)$ and $\min(f,g)$ are linear when restricted to each cone of $\Sigma'$. It then follows that $\max(f,g),\min(f,g)\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma')\subseteq\mathrm{PL}(X)$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:K(X)presentation}
Let $X$ be the support of a rational fan.  Then
\[
\mathrm{PE}(X) = \mathbb{Z}\{\mathrm{PL}(X)\}/I\]
where
\[I={\mathbb{Z}\big\{ \{f\} + \{g\} - \{\max(f,g)\} - \{\min(f,g)\}\mid f,g\in\mathrm{PL}(X)\big\}}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Equivalently, we prove that $I$ is the kernel of the surjective homomorphism $\tau$ defined by \eqref{eq:tau}.  For one inclusion, note that there is an equality of multisets
\[
\{f(v), g(v)\} = \{\max(f,g)(v), \min(f,g)(v)\}  
\]
for all $v \in X$.  Thus,
\[
e^f + e^g = e^{\max(f,g)} + e^{\min(f,g)} \in \mathrm{PE}(X),
\]
from which it follows that $I\subseteq\ker(\tau)$.

To prove the converse, we first prove the following ``reordering of values'' claim in the quotient ring $\mathbb{Z}\{\mathrm{PL}(X)\}/I$: given $f_1,\ldots, f_m \in \mathrm{PL}(X)$, there exist $\widetilde f_1,\ldots, \widetilde f_m\in \mathrm{PL}(X)$ such that $\sum \{f_i\} = \sum \{\widetilde f_i\} \mod I$ and such that
\[\widetilde f_1(v) \le \cdots \le \widetilde f_m(v) \text{ for all } v\in X.\]
This can be proved by induction on the number of $i \in \{1, \ldots, m-1\}$ such that $f_i(v) > f_{i+1}(v)$ for some $v\in X$.  If there are no such $i$, we are done.  Otherwise, let $i$ be as above, and for each $k=1,\ldots,m,$ let
\[\widetilde f_k = \begin{cases} \min (f_i, f_{i+1}) &\text{ if }k=i,\\ \max (f_i, f_{i+1}) & \text{ if }k=i+1,\\f_k &\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]
Then $\sum \{f_i\} = \sum \{\widetilde f_i\} \mod I$ and the number of $i$ with $\widetilde f_i (v) > \widetilde f_{i+1}(v)$ for some $v\in X$ has decreased compared to the same quantity for the $f$'s, proving the claim.

From here, suppose 
\[
F=\sum_{i=1}^k\{f_i\}-\sum_{j=1}^\ell\{g_j\}
\] 
lies in the kernel of $\tau$.  Then, by Lemma~\ref{lemma:expfunctions}, there is an equality of multisets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thing2}
\{f_i(v)\mid i=1,\dots,k\}=\{g_j(v)\mid j=1,\dots,\ell\}
\end{equation}
for each $v \in X$.  This implies that $k=\ell$, but it does not immediately imply that $f_i = g_i$ for each $i$; it could be the case, for instance, that $f_1(v) = g_2(v)$ and $f_2(v) = g_1(v)$.  However, the ``reordering of values'' claim above shows that, after adding an element of $I\subseteq\ker(\tau)$, we may assume that 
\begin{equation*}\label{eq:thing1}f_1(v) \le \cdots \le f_k(v)\text{ and } g_1(v) \le \cdots \le g_\ell(v) \text{ for all }v\in X.
\end{equation*}
Combining this with \eqref{eq:thing2} ensures that $f_i = g_i$ for all $i=1,\ldots,k$, so $F=0$, as desired.

<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:Ehrhart}
Let $\Sigma$ be a unimodular fan.  We say that $\Sigma$ is {\bf Ehrhart} if
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the star fan $\Sigma^\rho$ is Ehrhart for all $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$, and
    \item there exists a well-defined function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma\colon \underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma) \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}$ such that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(0) = 1$ and 
    \[
    \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f]) = \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f-\delta_\rho]) + \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)
    \]
    for all $f \in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ and all $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$.
\end{enumerate}
If $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart, we call the function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ the {\bf Ehrhart polynomial} of $\Sigma$.
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
If $\Sigma$ is an Ehrhart fan, then there is a unique function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma\colon\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ satisfying the conditions of Definition~\ref{def:Ehrhart}. Furthermore, the induced function 
    \begin{align*}
    \widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}&\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\\
    f&\mapsto\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])
    \end{align*}
agrees with a polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[x_\rho \; | \; \rho\in\Sigma(1)]$ of degree $\dim(\Sigma)$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The proof is by induction on dimension. When $\Sigma$ is $0$-dimensional,  $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma) = 0$ and thus the only function satisfying the conditions of Definition~\ref{def:Ehrhart} is the constant function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma=1$, which agrees with a polynomial function of degree zero, verifying the base case.  
    
    Now suppose that the proposition is true for all Ehrhart fans of dimension less than $d$, and let $\Sigma$ be an Ehrhart fan of dimension $d$. Let $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ be an Ehrhart function of $\Sigma$---which may not, a priori, be unique or given by a polynomial. 
    
    We first prove that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ is unique. To prove this, it suffices to observe that repeated applications of the Ehrhart recursion 
    \[
    \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f]) = \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f-\delta_\rho]) + \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)
    \]
    uniquely determine the value of $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])$ for any $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)=\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}$ from the Ehrhart functions $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}$ and the initial value $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(0)=1$. Since the Ehrhart functions $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}$ are all unique, by the induction hypothesis, it follows that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ is unique.
    
    Now consider the induced function
    \begin{align*}
    \widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}&\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\\
    f&\mapsto\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])
    \end{align*}
    By the induction hypothesis, for each $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$, there is a polynomial $P_\rho\in\mathbb{Q}[x_\tau \; | \; \tau\in\Sigma(1)]$ of degree $\dim(\Sigma^\rho)$ such that
    \[
    \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)=P_\rho(f)\;\;\;\text{ for every }\;\;\;f\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)},
    \]
    where $P_\rho(f)$ denotes the evaluation of $x_\tau$ at $f(u_\tau)$ for all $\tau\in\Sigma(1)$. Using that integer-valued polynomials in $\mathbb{Q}[x_\tau \; | \; \tau\in\Sigma(1)]$ have a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis given by products of univariate polynomials of the form
    \[
    {x_\tau\choose \alpha}=\frac{x_\tau(x_\tau-1)\dots (x_\tau-\alpha+1)}{\alpha!}
    \]
    with $\alpha\geq 0$ \cite[Proposition~XI.1.12]{CahenChabert}, it follows that there are integers $c_\alpha^\rho\in\mathbb{Z}$ such that
    \[
    P_\rho=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\Sigma(1)}\atop \alpha_{\rho}>0} c_\alpha^\rho{x_\rho-1\choose\alpha_\rho-1}\prod_{\tau\neq\rho}{x_\tau\choose \alpha_\tau}.
    \]
    We now claim that the integers $c_\alpha^\rho$ are independent of $\rho$.

    Let us compare coefficients $c_\alpha^\rho$ and $c_\alpha^\tau$ for distinct rays $\rho,\tau\in\Sigma(1)$. Since the $\alpha$ in question appears in a subscript of $c_\alpha^\rho$, then $\alpha_\rho>0$ by definition, and similarly, $\alpha_\tau>0$. Since $P_\rho(f)=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)$ only depends on the values $f(u_\tau)$ for which $\tau$ is a ray in the neighborhood $\Sigma^{(\rho)}$, and similarly for $P_\tau(f)$, it follows that $c_\alpha^\rho=c_\alpha^\tau=0$ if $\rho$ and $\tau$ do not lie on a common two-dimensional cone of $\Sigma$. Thus, suppose that there is a two-dimensional cone $\sigma\in\Sigma(2)$ such that $\sigma(1)=\{\rho,\tau\}$. Then the Ehrhart recursion applied to $\Sigma^\rho$ and $\Sigma^\tau$ implies that
    \[
    P_\rho(f)-P_\rho(f-\delta_\tau)=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\sigma}([f]^\sigma)=P_\tau(f)-P_\tau(f-\delta_\rho).
    \]
    Using that
    \[
    {x_\tau\choose \alpha_\tau}-{x_\tau-1\choose\alpha_\tau}={x_\tau-1\choose\alpha_\tau-1},
    \]
    this implies that
    \[
    \sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\Sigma(1)}\atop \alpha_{\rho},\alpha_\tau>0} c_\alpha^\rho{x_\rho-1\choose\alpha_\rho-1}{x_\tau-1\choose\alpha_\tau-1}\prod_{\tau'\neq\rho,\tau}{x_{\tau'}\choose \alpha_{\tau'}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\Sigma(1)}\atop \alpha_\tau,\alpha_{\rho}>0} c_\alpha^\tau{x_\tau-1\choose\alpha_\tau-1}{x_\rho-1\choose\alpha_\rho-1}\prod_{\tau'\neq\rho,\tau}{x_{\tau'}\choose \alpha_{\tau'}}
    \]
    Thus, we see that $c_\alpha^\rho=c_\alpha^\tau$ for all $\alpha$ with $\alpha_\rho,\alpha_\tau>0$, as desired. 
    
    Now for each $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\Sigma(1)}\setminus\{0\}$, define $c_\alpha\coloneq c_\alpha^\rho$ where $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$ is any ray for which $\alpha_\rho>0$. By our argument in the previous paragraph, the definition of $c_\alpha$ does not depend on the choice of $\rho$. Define the polynomial
    \[
    P=1+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\Sigma(1)}\setminus\{0\}}c_\alpha\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma(1)}{x_\tau\choose \alpha_\tau}\in\mathbb{Q}[x_\tau \; | \; \tau\in\Sigma(1)],
    \]
    which has degree
    \[
    1+\max_\rho\dim(\Sigma^\rho)=d. 
    \]
    For any $f\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}$ and $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$, we compute
    \[
    P(f)-P(f-\delta_\rho)=P_\rho(f)=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)=\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f)-\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f-\delta_\rho),
    \]
    and it follows that $P-\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ is a constant. Since both $P$ and $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ take value one at $0$, the two functions are thus equal. Therefore, $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma=P$, completing the proof of the induction step.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:onedimensional}
A one-dimensional unimodular fan $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart if and only if 
\[
\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}u_\rho=0.
\]
Moreover, if $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart, then for every $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, we have
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=1+\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}f(u_\rho).
\]
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $\Sigma$ be a one-dimensional fan.  Then condition (1) of Ehrharticity is satisfied, because the star fans $\Sigma^\rho$ are all zero-dimensional, and thus Ehrhart.  Thus, it remains to study whether the second condition of Ehrharticity holds for $\Sigma$. Define the function
\begin{align*}
G_\Sigma:\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)&\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\\
f&\mapsto 1+\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}f(u_\rho).
\end{align*}
Notice that $G_\Sigma(0)=1$, and since the Ehrhart polynomial of $\Sigma^\rho$ is the constant function $1$ for every $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$, it follows that $G_\Sigma$ satisfies the Ehrhart recursion:
\[
G_\Sigma(f)-G_\Sigma(f-\delta_\rho)=1=G_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)\;\;\;\text{ for every }\;\;\;\rho\in\Sigma(1).
\]
Thus, $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart if and only if $G_\Sigma$ descends to a well-defined function on the quotient $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$. But $G_\Sigma$ is well-defined on $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$ if and only if 
\[
\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}f(u_\rho)=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}(f+m)(u_\rho)
\]
for all $m\in M$, which is satisfied if and only if
\[
\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}u_\rho=0.\qedhere
\]

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:dimensiontwo}
Let $\Sigma$ be a pure, unimodular fan of dimension two. Then $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart if and only if there exist integers $a_\rho\in\mathbb{Z}$ for every $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:dim2prop1}
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma(2)\atop \rho\subseteq\sigma}u_{\sigma(1)\setminus\rho}=a_\rho u_\rho
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:dim2prop2}
\sum_\rho (2-a_\rho)u_\rho = 0.
\end{equation}
Moreover, if $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart, then for every $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, we have
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=1+\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}(1-a_\rho/2)f(u_\rho)+\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma(2)\atop \sigma(1)=\{\rho_1,\rho_2\}}f(u_{\rho_1})f(u_{\rho_2})-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\frac{a_\rho}{2} f(u_\rho)^2.
\]
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The first condition of Ehrharticity requires that all one-dimensional star fans $\Sigma^\rho$ are Ehrhart, but by Proposition~\ref{prop:onedimensional}, this is equivalent to the existence of integers $a_\rho\in\mathbb{Z}$ for every $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$ such that \eqref{eqn:dim2prop1} holds. Assuming that this is the case, define the function
\begin{align*}
G_\Sigma:\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)&\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\\
f&\mapsto 1+\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\big(1-\frac{a_\rho}{2}\big)f(u_\rho)+\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma(2)\atop \sigma(1)=\{\rho_1,\rho_2\}}f(u_{\rho_1})f(u_{\rho_2})-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\frac{a_\rho}{2} f(u_\rho)^2.
\end{align*}
Notice that $G_\Sigma(0)=1$, and for any $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$, a direct computation verifies that
\[
G_\Sigma(f)-G_\Sigma(f-\delta_\rho)=1+\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma(2)\atop\rho\subseteq\sigma}f(u_{\sigma(1)\setminus\rho})-a_\rho f(u_\rho)=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([(f-m)^\rho]),
\]
where $m$ is any linear function such that $m(u_\rho)=f(u_\rho)$. Thus, $G_\Sigma$ satisfies the necessary properties of an Ehrhart polynomial, so $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart if and only if $G_\Sigma$ descends to a well-defined function on $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$. A direct computation then shows that the quadratic part of $G_\Sigma$ descends if and only if \eqref{eqn:dim2prop1} holds, while the linear part of $G_\Sigma$ descends if and only if \eqref{eqn:dim2prop2} holds.

Let $\Sigma$ be a balanced fan of dimension $d$. The \textbf{volume polynomial} of $\Sigma$ is the function
\begin{align*}

    [f]&\mapsto \deg_\Sigma([f]^d),

where we identify $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)=\underline{\mathrm{PP}}^1(\Sigma)$ and carry out the multiplication $[f]^d$ within $\underline{\mathrm{PP}}^*(\Sigma)$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:volume}
Let $\Sigma$ be an Ehrhart fan of dimension $d$. Then  the leading term of the Ehrhart polynomial is $\frac{1}{d!}V_\Sigma$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The proof is by induction on $d$. If $d=0$, then $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ and $V_\Sigma$ are both the constant function $1$, proving the base case. Now suppose that $\Sigma$ has positive dimension $d$ and that the proposition holds for all fans of dimension less than $d$.

By precomposing the Ehrhart polynomial and the volume polynomial with the quotient map $\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)\rightarrow \underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$, we obtain polynomials $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ and $\widehat V_\Sigma$ from $\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)=\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}$ to $\mathbb{Z}$. Since a homogeneous polynomial is uniquely determined from its partial derivatives, we consider the partial derivatives of $\frac{1}{d!}\widehat V_\Sigma$ and the partial derivatives of the degree-$d$ homogeneous component of the inhomogeneous polynomial $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$.

Denoting elements of $\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ by $f=\sum_{\rho}z_\rho\delta_\rho$, the chain rule gives
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_\rho}\widehat V_\Sigma(f)=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_\rho}\deg_\Sigma([f]^d)=d\cdot \deg_\Sigma([f]^{d-1}[\delta_\rho]).
\]
A standard computation (see, for example, Equation (2.6) in \cite{RossLorentzian}) then gives
\[
\deg_\Sigma([f]^{d-1}[\delta_\rho])=\deg_{\Sigma^\rho}(([f]^\rho)^{d-1}),
\]
so that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq.partial1}
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_\rho}\frac{1}{d!}\widehat V_\Sigma(f)=\frac{1}{(d-1)!}\widehat V_{\Sigma^\rho}((f-m)^\rho),
\end{equation}
where $m$ is any linear function that agrees with $f$ at $u_\rho$. On the other hand, the derivative with respect to $z_\rho$ of the leading term of $\widehat \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f)$ is equal to the leading term of
\[
\widehat \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f)-\widehat \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f-\delta_\rho),
\]
which, by the Ehrhart condition, is equal to the leading term of $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}((f-m)^\rho)$. Thus, letting $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma^d$ denote the leading term of $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq.partial2}
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_\rho}\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma^d(f)=\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}^{d-1}((f-m)^\rho).
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq.partial1} and \eqref{eq.partial2}, along with the induction hypothesis, prove that $\frac{1}{d!}\widehat V_\Sigma$ and $\widehat\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma^d$ have the same partial derivatives, and since they are each homogeneous polynomials, Euler's homogeneous function theorem then implies that they are equal.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:complete}
Let $\Sigma$ be a complete unimodular fan.  Then $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart. Furthermore, for every convex $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, 
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=|P_f\cap M|,
\]
where $P_f$ is the lattice polytope
\[
P_f \coloneq \{m \in M_\mathbb{R} \; | \; m(u_\rho) \leq f(u_\rho) \;\; \forall \rho \in \Sigma(1)\},
\]
and for every concave $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$,
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=(-1)^n|\mathrm{Int}(P_{-f})\cap M|,
\]
where $\mathrm{Int}(P)$ denotes the topological interior of the polytope $P$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
Since complete unimodular fans correspond to smooth complete toric varieties, one could use known results in toric geometry to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:complete}, where the role of the Ehrhart polynomial would be played by the holomorphic Euler characteristic of line bundles. We take a different approach, modeled on techniques from Ehrhart theory; this more elementary proof does not require any tools from algebraic geometry. While the techniques below may be well-known to an expert in Ehrhart theory, it is useful to spell them out as we will generalize these ideas in the next section.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite}
    Let $\Sigma$ be a complete unimodular fan. For any $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, there are only finitely many $m\in M$ such that
    \[
    \sum_{\sigma\in \Sigma_{f,m}}(-1)^{\dim \sigma}\neq 0.
    \]

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:convex}
    Let $\Sigma$ be a complete unimodular fan. For any convex $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$,
    \[
    \sum_{\sigma\in \Sigma_{f,m}}(-1)^{\dim \sigma}=\begin{cases}
        (-1)^n & m\in P_f\\
        0 & \text{else,}
    \end{cases}
    \]
    and for any concave $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$,
    \[
    \sum_{\sigma\in \Sigma_{f,m}}(-1)^{\dim \sigma}=\begin{cases}
        1& m\in \mathrm{Int}(P_{-f})\\
        0 & \text{else.}
    \end{cases}
    \]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $\Sigma$ be a complete unimodular fan of dimension $n$, and define a function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma\colon \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)\to \mathbb{Z}$ by
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f) = (-1)^n \sum_{m\in M} \left(\sum_{\sigma\in \Sigma_{f,m}}(-1)^{\dim \sigma}\right)\in \mathbb{Z}.
\]
Note that the outer sum is finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}, so $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f)$ is a well-defined integer. Given any $m'\in M$, observe that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f+m') &=& (-1)^n \sum_{m\in M} \sum_{\sigma\in \Sigma_{f\!+\!m',m}} (-1)^{\dim \sigma} \\ &=& (-1)^n \sum_{m\in M} \sum_{\sigma\in \Sigma_{f\!+\!m',m\!+\!m'}} (-1)^{\dim \sigma} \\ &=& \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f),
\end{eqnarray*}
where the final equality uses that $\Sigma_{f,m} = \Sigma_{f+m', m+m'}$ for all $m'\in M$, which follows directly from the definition of $\Sigma_{f,m}$. Hence, $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ gives a well-defined function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma:\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb{Z}$. Moreover, for any convex $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} implies that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:countinglatticepoints}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=(-1)^n \sum_{m\in P_f}(-1)^n=|P_f\cap M|,
\end{equation}
and for any concave $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} implies that
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=(-1)^n \sum_{m\in \mathrm{Int}(P_{-f})}1=(-1)^n|\mathrm{Int}(P_{-f})\cap M|.
\]
It remains to prove that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart with respect to the function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$.

We prove that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart by induction on $n$.  First, if $\Sigma$ is complete then so is $\Sigma^\rho$ for all $\rho\in \Sigma(1)$.  Since $\dim \Sigma^\rho = \dim \Sigma - 1$, it follows inductively that $\Sigma^\rho$ is Ehrhart, verifying Property (1) of Ehrharticity.

In order to verify Property (2), we first note that
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(0)=|P_0\cap M|=1,
\]
where the first equality is a special case of \eqref{eq:countinglatticepoints} and the second equality is because $P_0=\{0\}.$ Lastly, we must show that, for any ray $\rho\in \Sigma(1)$, the function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ satisfies the recursion
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f]) - \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f-\delta_\rho]) = \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho} ([f]^\rho).
\]
The left-hand side above is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thingie}
    (-1)^n \sum_{m\in M}\left(\sum_{\sigma} (-1)^{\dim \sigma}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ ranges over cones in $\Sigma_{f,m} \setminus \Sigma_{f-\delta_\rho,m}$.  Note that the expression in parentheses is zero outside a finite subset of $M$, since it is a difference of two expressions enjoying the same property.  Using the definitions of $\Sigma_{f,m}$ and $\Sigma_{f-\delta_\rho,m}$, the expression~\eqref{eq:thingie} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thingie2}
(-1)^n \sum_{\substack{m\in M \\ m(u_\rho) =f(u_\rho)}} \sum_{\substack{\sigma \in \Sigma, \rho \preceq \sigma, \\ m(x) \le f(x) \\ \text{for all } x\in \sigma}} (-1)^{\dim \sigma}.
\end{equation}
Now the cones in $\Sigma$ containing $\rho$ are exactly the cones of the star fan $\Sigma^\rho$, so this expression can be rewritten in terms of $\Sigma^\rho$. In order to do so, let $m_0\in M$ be any element such that $m_0(u_\rho) = f(u_\rho)$, and substitute $m' = m-m_0$.  Then~\eqref{eq:thingie2} is
\begin{eqnarray*}
(-1)^{n-1} \sum_{\substack{m'\in M \\  m'(u_\rho) =0 }} \, \sum_{\substack{\sigma \in \Sigma^\rho,  \\ m'(x) \le (f-m_0)(x) \\ \text{for all } x\in \sigma}} (-1)^{\dim \sigma}, 
\end{eqnarray*}
and this expression is exactly $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho} ([f]^\rho)$. This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:complete}, modulo the proofs of Lemmas~\ref{lem:finite} and \ref{lem:convex}, which we provide below.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Given any subfan $\Sigma'\subseteq\Sigma$, define
\[
V\coloneq\{m\in M_\mathbb{R} \mid \Sigma_{f,m} = \Sigma'\}\subseteq M_\mathbb{R}.
\]
We prove that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_c(\Sigma')=0$ whenever $V$ is unbounded. Since there are finitely-many subfans of $\Sigma$, this implies that there are finitely-many $m\in M$ such that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_c(\Sigma_{f,m})\neq 0$. Toward this end, suppose that $V$ is unbounded;  we shall show that the link of $\Sigma'$ is contractible, implying that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_c(\Sigma') = 0$.  We begin by studying the structure of the set $V$.  

Given $m\in M_\mathbb{R}$, the condition on $m$ that $\Sigma_{f,m}=\Sigma'$ is equivalent to the condition that for all ray generators $u_\rho$ of rays $\rho\in \Sigma(1)$, 
\[
\langle m, u_\rho\rangle \le f(u_\rho)\text{ if and only if }\rho \in \Sigma'.
\]
Therefore, $V$ is the intersection of the closed halfspaces
\[
\{m \in M_\mathbb{R} \mid \langle m,u_\rho\rangle \le f(u_\rho)\}
\]
for each ray generator $u_\rho$ of $\rho \in \Sigma'(1)$, and the open halfspaces
\[
\{m \in M_\mathbb{R} \mid \langle m,u_\rho\rangle > f(u_\rho)\}
\]
for each ray generator $u_\rho$ of $\rho\in\Sigma(1)\setminus\Sigma'(1)$. In particular, $V$ is an intersection of finitely many closed and open half-spaces.  Thus, if $V$ is unbounded, as is assumed, then it must be unbounded in a particular linear direction; that is, there exists $v\in M_\mathbb{R}\setminus \{0\}$ such that $v+ V \subseteq V$.

Now let $H$ be the hyperplane in $N_\mathbb{R}$ defined by
\[
H = \{ x \in N_\mathbb{R}\mid \langle v,x\rangle = 0\},
\] 
and let 
\[
H^+ = \{ x \in N_\mathbb{R} \mid \langle v,x\rangle >0\}\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\; H^- = \{ x \in N_\mathbb{R} \mid \langle v,x\rangle <0\}
\]
be the open half-spaces bounded by $H$.  We claim that whether a ray $\rho\in \Sigma(1)$ is in $\Sigma'(1)$ is almost entirely determined by its position relative to the hyperplane $H$.  The precise claim is as follows: let $u_\rho$ denote the primitive ray generator of $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$.  Then we claim that 
if $u_\rho \in H^-$ then $u_\rho\in \Sigma'$, and if $u_\rho\in H^+$ then $u_\rho\not \in \Sigma'$.  (If $u_\rho \in H$ then we cannot conclude either way, a priori.)  

To prove this claim, suppose first that $u_\rho\in H^-$, so $\langle v,u_\rho\rangle < 0$.  We claim that $\rho\in\Sigma'(1)$. If not, then $V\subseteq \{m\in M_\mathbb{R}: \langle m,u_\rho\rangle > f(u_\rho)\}$. Since $V$ is unbounded in the direction $v$, there exists $v_0\in V$ such that $\langle v_0+\lambda v, u_\rho \rangle > f(u_\rho)$ for all $\lambda\ge 0$.  But this contradicts that $\langle v, u_\rho\rangle < 0$.  A similar argument shows that if $u_\rho \in H^+$ then $\rho\not\in\Sigma'(1)$, proving the claim. The claim implies, in particular, that all rays of $\Sigma'$ are contained in $H\cup H^-$, and it follows that $\Sigma'\subseteq H\cup H^-$.

We now prove that the link of $\Sigma'$ is contractible. Here it will be convenient to use the ``combinatorial'' link of the unimodular fan $\Sigma'$, which is the simplicial complex $L\Sigma'$ glued from the simplices 
\[
L\sigma = \mathrm{conv}\{u_\rho~|~\rho\in\sigma(1)\}
\] 
along faces. Consider the space 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Y-space}
Y \coloneq (L\Sigma' \cap H) \cup (L\Sigma\cap H^-).
\end{equation}
The space $L\Sigma\cap H^-$ is homeomorphic an open disk, and the whole space $Y$ is then homeomorphic to a partial closure of this open disk, which is contractible.  Note that $L\Sigma'$ is a subset of $Y$, since $\Sigma' \subseteq H \cup H^-$ as previously shown.  We shall construct a deformation retract from $Y$ to $L\Sigma'$.

We first set notation for certain deformation retracts of subsets of simplices. If $T$ is a finite set, write $\Delta^T$ for the standard simplex of dimension $|T|-1$ in $\mathbb{R}^T$, with vertices labeled by $T$.  If $S$ and $T$ are two nonempty sets then there is a canonical homeomorphism of the simplex $\Delta^{S\sqcup T}$ with the topological join $\Delta^S \ast \Delta^T$, defined as the quotient of $\Delta^S \times \Delta^T \times [0,1]$ by the equivalence relation $\sim$ generated by 
\[
(x,y,0) \sim (x',y,0) \text{ and } (x,y,1) \sim (x,y', 1)
\]
whenever $x,x'\in \Delta^S,$ and $y,y'\in \Delta^T$.  Thus there are canonical embeddings $\Delta^S \to \Delta^{S\sqcup T}$ and $\Delta^T \to \Delta^{S\sqcup T}$. We denote these images $\Delta^S$, respectively $\Delta^T$; they are closed faces of $\Delta^{S\sqcup T}$. 

The identification of $\Delta^{S\sqcup T}$ with the join $\Delta^S\ast\Delta^T$ makes evident a  deformation retraction of $\Delta^{S\sqcup T} \setminus \Delta^S$ onto $\Delta^T$.  In coordinates, it is the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:in-coords}
\Delta^{S\sqcup T} \setminus \Delta^S\times[0,1]\to\Delta^{S\sqcup T} \setminus \Delta^S, \quad ((x,y,a),b)\mapsto (x,y,a(1-b)).
\end{equation}
It is important to note that these deformation retracts glue along faces: if $S'\subseteq S$ and $T'\subseteq T$ are nonempty subsets, then the following diagram commutes:
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\label{eq:commutes}
{\xymatrix@R=8mm@C=8mm{
\Delta^{S'\sqcup T'}\times [0,1]\ar[d]\ar[r] & \Delta^{S'\sqcup T'}\ar[d] \\ \Delta^{S\sqcup T}\times [0,1]\ar[r] &\Delta^{S\sqcup T}.
}}.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}

Now we return to the main proof. For each cone $\sigma\in \Sigma$ that meets $H^-$, let $\sigma'$ be the face of $\sigma$ spanned by its rays in $\Sigma'$, and let $\sigma''$ be the face of $\sigma$ spanned by its rays not in $\Sigma'$.  Then $L\sigma \cong L\sigma' \ast L\sigma''$.  Moreover, the subset
\[
Y_\sigma \coloneq (L\sigma'\cap H)\cup (L\sigma \cap H^-) \subseteq L\sigma \setminus L\sigma''
\]
admits a deformation retraction onto $L\sigma'$. Indeed, the deformation retraction of $L\sigma \setminus L\sigma''$ onto $L\sigma'$, given in~\eqref{eq:in-coords}, restricts to a deformation retraction of $Y_\sigma$ onto $L\sigma'$.  That is because, under the identification of $L\sigma$ with $L\sigma' \ast  L\sigma'' = (L\sigma' \times L\sigma'' \times [0,1])/\sim$, the set $Y_\sigma$ has the property that if $(x,y,a_1)\in Y_\sigma$ then $(x,y,a_2)\in Y_\sigma$ for all $a_2 \in [ a_1, 1]$.

These deformation retractions are compatible along faces by~\eqref{eq:commutes}, and so glue to give a deformation retraction of $Y$ onto $L\Sigma'$.  Therefore $L\Sigma'$ is contractible since $Y$ is contractible, and we conclude that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_c(L\Sigma') = 0$, as desired, concluding the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}.

<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{def:fiberbundle}
Let $0\to N' \to N \xrightarrow{\pi} N'' \to 0$ be a short exact sequence of finitely-generated free abelian groups, and let $\Sigma'$ and $\Sigma''$ be unimodular fans in $N'_\mathbb{R}$ and $N''_\mathbb{R}$, respectively.  (We view $N'_\mathbb{R} \subseteq N_\mathbb{R}$ by tensoring the short exact sequence with $\mathbb{R}$.)  A \textbf{$\Sigma'$-bundle over $\Sigma''$} is a unimodular fan $\Sigma$ in $N_\mathbb{R}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $|\Sigma|\cap N_\mathbb{R}'=|\Sigma'|$;
    \item there exists a subfan $\widetilde\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma$ such that $\pi$ is injective on $\widetilde\Sigma$ and the function
    \begin{align*}
        \widetilde\Sigma&\rightarrow\Sigma''\\
        \widetilde\sigma&\mapsto\pi(\widetilde\sigma)
    \end{align*}
    is a bijection such that $\pi(\widetilde \sigma \cap N) = \pi(\widetilde \sigma) \cap N''$ for each $\widetilde \sigma \in \widetilde \Sigma$;
    \item the function
    \begin{align*}
        \Sigma'\times\widetilde\Sigma&\rightarrow\Sigma\\
        (\sigma',\widetilde\sigma)&\mapsto\sigma'+\widetilde\sigma
    \end{align*}
    is a bijection.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:fiberbundles}
Let $\Sigma'$ and $\Sigma''$ be unimodular fans. If $\Sigma'$ is complete, then a $\Sigma'$-bundle over $\Sigma''$ is Ehrhart if and only if $\Sigma''$ is Ehrhart.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Suppose that $\Sigma'$ is complete and that $\Sigma$ is a $\Sigma'$-bundle over $\Sigma''$. Throughout this proof, we use the notation of Definition~\ref{def:fiberbundle}.

First, let us assume that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart. Choose any maximal cone $\sigma\in\Sigma'\subseteq\Sigma$. It follows directly from the definitions of fiber bundles and star fans that $\Sigma^\sigma\cong \Sigma''$. Since the star fan of $\Sigma$ at an arbitrary cone $\sigma$ can be obtained iteratively from $\Sigma$ by taking star fans at a ray, the assumption that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart implies that $\Sigma''$ is Ehrhart.

Conversely, suppose that $\Sigma''$ is Ehrhart. We use induction on $\dim\Sigma$ to prove that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart. In other words, we assume that a fiber bundle is Ehrhart whenever it has complete fibers, an Ehrhart base, and dimension less than $\dim\Sigma$. To check Condition (1) of Ehrharticity, let $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$. Then either $\rho\in\Sigma'(1)$ or $\rho\in\widetilde\Sigma(1)$. In the first case, it follows from the definitions that $\Sigma^\rho$ is a $(\Sigma')^\rho$-bundle over $\Sigma''$, and in the second case, $\Sigma^\rho$ is a $\Sigma'$-bundle over $(\Sigma'')^{\pi(\rho)}$. In either case, $\Sigma^\rho$ is Ehrhart by induction on $\dim\Sigma$.

To prove Condition (2) of Ehrharticity for the fiber bundle $\Sigma$, we define an explicit function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma:\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$; to do so, we must first introduce additional notation. Given $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, define $f'\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma')$ as the restriction of $f$ to $\Sigma'$, and define $f''\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma'')$ as the unique piecewise-linear function such that
\[
f''(u_{\pi(\rho)})=f(u_\rho)\;\;\;\text{ for every }\;\;\;\rho\in\widetilde\Sigma(1).
\]
Choose a splitting of the quotient map $\pi:N_\mathbb{R}\rightarrow N_\mathbb{R}''$, allowing us to write $N_\mathbb{R}=N_\mathbb{R}'\oplus N_\mathbb{R}''$. Given any $m'\in M'$---in other words, an integral linear map on $N_\mathbb{R}'$---we can use the above splitting to view $m'$ to an integral linear map on $N_\mathbb{R}$. For any $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, define (using the notation from Section~\ref{sec:complete})
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f)=(-1)^{\dim\Sigma'}\sum_{m'\in M'}\Big(\Big (\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma'_{f',m'}}(-1)^{\dim(\sigma)}\Big)\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}([(f-m')''])\Big).
\]

We first prove that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ descends to $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$. Given any $\ell\in M$, we compute
\begin{align*}
    \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f+\ell)&=(-1)^{\dim\Sigma'}\sum_{m'\in M'}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma'_{f'+\ell',m'}}(-1)^{\dim(\sigma)}\Big)\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}([(f+\ell-m')''])\\
    &=(-1)^{\dim\Sigma'}\sum_{m'+\ell'\in M'}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma'_{f'+\ell',m'+\ell'}}(-1)^{\dim(\sigma)}\Big)\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}([(f+\ell-m'-\ell')''])\\
    &=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(f),
\end{align*}
where the final equality uses the fact that $\Sigma'_{f'+\ell',m'+\ell'}=\Sigma'_{f',m'}$, along with the observation that $(\ell-\ell')''$ is linear on $\Sigma''$. Thus, $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ descends to a function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma:\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$.

Next, we observe that 
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma(0)=(-1)^{\dim\Sigma'}\sum_{m'\in M'}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma'_{0,m'}}(-1)^{\dim(\sigma)}\Big)\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}([(-m')''])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}(0)=1,
\]
where the second equality uses Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} to conclude that $m'=0$ is the only element of $M'$ contributing to the sum. To complete the proof that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart, it now remains to verify that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiberbundlerecursion}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f]) - \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f-\delta_\rho]) = \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)
\end{equation}
for all $f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ and $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$. There are two cases to consider, depending on whether $\rho\in\Sigma'(1)$ or $\rho\in\widetilde\Sigma(1)$.

If $\rho\in\Sigma'(1)$, then the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:fiberbundlerecursion} can be written as 
\[
(-1)^{\dim\Sigma'}\sum_{m'\in M'}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma'_{f',m'}\setminus\Sigma'_{f'-\delta_\rho,m'}}(-1)^{\dim(\sigma)}\Big)\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}([(f-m')'']).
\]
As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:complete}, the cones $\sigma$ appearing in the sum above can be identified with the cones of $(\Sigma')^\rho$, and then interpreting $\Sigma^\rho$ as a $(\Sigma')^\rho$-bundle over $\Sigma''$, it then follows that this difference is equal to $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)$, verifying \eqref{eq:fiberbundlerecursion} in this case.

If $\rho\in\widetilde\Sigma(1)$, then the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:fiberbundlerecursion} can be written as 
\[
(-1)^{\dim\Sigma'}\sum_{m'\in M'}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma'_{f',m'}}(-1)^{\dim(\sigma)}\Big)\Big(\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}([(f-m')''])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma''}([(f-m')''-\delta_{\pi(\rho)}])\Big).
\]
Since $\Sigma''$ is Ehrhart, the final difference in the above expression is equal to 
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma'')^{\pi(\rho)}}(([f-m')]^{\pi(\rho)}),
\]
and then the interpretation of $\Sigma^\rho$ as a $\Sigma'$-bundle over $(\Sigma'')^{\pi(\rho)}$ implies the validity of \eqref{eq:fiberbundlerecursion} in this case, finishing the proof.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:products}
Let $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ be Ehrhart fans.  Then $\Sigma_1 \times \Sigma_2$ is Ehrhart, with Ehrhart polynomial given by the product of the Ehrhart polynomials of the factors:
\[
 \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_1\times\Sigma_2}([f_1 \times f_2])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_1}([f_1])\times\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_2}([f_2]).
 \]
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $d_i=\dim(\Sigma_i)$ and  $\Sigma = \Sigma_1 \times \Sigma_2$.  We proceed by induction on $d_1 + d_2$.

The base case is when $d_1 +d_2 = 0$, and hence both fans as well as their product are zero-dimensional and therefore Ehrhart. For the induction step, suppose products of Ehrhart fans are Ehrhart with the desired Ehrhart polynomial whenever the sum of their dimensions is less than $d$, and suppose that $d_1 + d_2 = d$.  Given a ray $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$, either $\rho\in\Sigma_1(1)$ or $\rho\in\Sigma_2(1)$. In the first case, we have
\[
\Sigma^\rho=\Sigma_1^{\rho}\times\Sigma_2,
\]
and similarly for the second case. It then follows from the induction hypothesis and the assumption that both $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ are Ehrhart that $\Sigma^\rho$ is Ehrhart for every $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$. Given a piecewise-linear function $f= f_1 \times f_2$ on $\Sigma$, we define
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_1}([f_1])\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_2}([f_2]).
\]
To show that this function satisfies the Ehrhart relation, let $\rho \in \Sigma(1)$, and suppose without loss of generality that $\rho\in\Sigma_1(1)$ so that $f-\delta_\rho=(f_1-\delta_\rho) \times f_2$. We then compute
\begin{align*}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f-\delta_\rho])&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_1}([f_1])\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_2}([f_2])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_1}([f_1-\delta_\rho])\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_2}([f_2])\\
&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_1)^\rho}([f_1]^\rho)\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_2}([f_2])\\
&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho),
\end{align*}
where we use the assumption that $\Sigma_1$ is Ehrhart in the second equality and the induction hypothesis in the third.

<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
Let $\Sigma$ be a unimodular fan and let $\tau\in\Sigma$. Define
\[
u_\tau=\sum_{\rho\in\tau(1)}u_\rho\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\rho_\tau=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u_\tau.
\]
The \textbf{stellar subdivision of $\Sigma$ at $\tau$}, denoted $\Sigma_\tau$, is the fan 
\[
\Sigma_\tau \coloneq \{\sigma \in \Sigma \; | \; u_\tau \notin \sigma\} \;\; \cup \;\;\bigcup_{\substack{ u_\tau \in \sigma }} \;\; \bigcup_{\substack{\pi \preceq \sigma,\\u_\tau \notin \pi}} \pi + \rho_\tau,
\]
where in the two unions, both $\sigma$ and $\pi$ range over cones of $\Sigma$.
<!-- end definition-10 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
In the language of toric geometry, a stellar subdivision, as defined above, corresponds to a blowup of a smooth toric variety along a toric subvariety. This perspective informs but is not essential to what follows.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:starfans}
Let $\Sigma$ be a unimodular fan in $N_\mathbb{R}$ and $\tau\in\Sigma$. Given any ray $\rho$ in the stellar subdivision $\Sigma_\tau$, the star fan $(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho$ is described by one of the following three cases.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\rho\notin\Sigma^{(\tau)}(1)$, then
\[
(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho=\Sigma^\rho.
\]
\item If $\rho\in\Sigma^{(\tau)}(1)\setminus\{\rho_\tau\}$, then $\tau$ projects onto a cone $\overline\tau\in\Sigma^\rho$, and the star fan of $\Sigma_\tau$ at $\rho$ is the stellar subdivision of the star fan $\Sigma^\rho$ at $\overline\tau$:
\[
(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho=(\Sigma^\rho)_{\overline\tau}.
\]
\item If $\rho=\rho_\tau$, then $(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho$ is a $\Pi_{\dim\tau-1}$-bundle over $\Sigma^\tau$, where $\Pi_k$ denotes the normal fan of the standard $k$-simplex in $\mathbb{R}^{k+1}/\mathbb{R}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
To justify the description of the star fan in Case (1), note that, in this case, the neighborhood $\Sigma^{(\rho)}$---and thus the star fan $\Sigma^\rho$---is unaffected by  stellar subdivision at $\tau$. 

To justify Case (2), note that any cone $\sigma$ in the neighborhood $(\Sigma_\tau)^{(\rho)}$ is either a cone of $\Sigma^{(\rho)}$ (if $u_\tau\notin\sigma$), or it is a cone of the form
\[
\pi + \rho_\tau
\]
for some $\pi\in\Sigma^{(\rho)}$ such that $\pi$ and $u_\tau$ lie in a common cone of $\Sigma^{(\rho)}$ and $u_\tau\notin\pi$. Upon projecting these two types of cones to $N_\mathbb{R}^\rho$, we obtain the two types of cones comprising the stellar subdivision of $\Sigma^\rho$ at $\overline\tau$.

Lastly, we consider Case (3). In order to describe $(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}\subseteq N_\mathbb{R}^{\rho_\tau}$ as a fiber bundle, we describe the fiber and the base explicitly. Define $\Sigma'$ to be the image of $\Sigma_\tau\cap\tau$ in the quotient space $N_\mathbb{R}^{\rho_\tau}$, and define $N_\mathbb{R}'=\mathrm{Span}_\mathbb{R}(\Sigma')\subseteq N_\mathbb{R}^{\rho_\tau}$. It follows from the definition of stellar subdivisions that $\Sigma'=(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}\cap N_\mathbb{R}'$ is the normal fan of the standard simplex. Let $\widetilde\Sigma$ be the maximal subfan of $(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}$ that omits the rays of $\Sigma'$, or in other words, omits the images of rays of $\tau$. It then follows from the definition of star fans that the natural quotient map 
\[
\pi:N_\mathbb{R}^{\rho_\tau}\rightarrow N_\mathbb{R}^{\rho_\tau}/N_\mathbb{R}' = N_\mathbb{R}^\tau
\]
is an injection of the cones of $\widetilde\Sigma$ onto the cones of the star fan $\Sigma^\tau$. Moreover, by the definition of stellar subdivisions, the cones of $(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}$ are in natural bijection with pairs $(\sigma',\widetilde\sigma)\in\Sigma'\times\widetilde\Sigma$. Putting this all together, it follows that $(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}$ is a fiber bundle over $\Sigma''=\Sigma^\tau$, with fibers $\Sigma'=\Pi_{\dim\tau-1}$, completing the proof.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:blowups}
If $\Sigma$ is a unimodular fan and $\tau\in\Sigma$, then $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart if and only if the stellar subdivision $\Sigma_\tau$ is Ehrhart. Moreover, if both $\Sigma$ and $\Sigma_\tau$ are Ehrhart, then their Ehrhart polynomials agree: for all $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, we have
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f]).
\]
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
We first prove the ``only if'' direction by induction on dimension. To do so, assume that Ehrharticity is preserved by stellar subdivisions of fans of dimension less than $d$, and assume that $\Sigma$ is a $d$-dimensional Ehrhart fan. We aim to prove that $\Sigma_\tau$ is Ehrhart.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:starfans}, the star fan $(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho$ for any $\rho\in\Sigma_\tau(1)$ is either a star fan of $\Sigma$, a stellar subdivision of a star fan of $\Sigma$, or a fiber bundle over a star fan of $\Sigma$ with complete fibers. It follows from the Ehrharticity of $\Sigma$, the induction hypothesis, and Proposition~\ref{prop:fiberbundles} that $(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho$ is Ehrhart for all $\rho\in\Sigma_\tau(1)$. We have thus proven Condition (1) in the definition of Ehrharticity for $\Sigma_\tau$, and we turn to Condition (2).  

Given any $\tilde f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma_\tau)$, there is a unique $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ and $a\in\mathbb{Z}$ such that 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tildeffa}
    \tilde f=f+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}
\end{equation}
Specifically, the function $f$ takes the same value as $\tilde f$ on all ray generators except $u_{\rho_\tau}$, whereas
\[f(u_{\rho_\tau}) = \sum_{\rho \in \tau(1)} \tilde f(u_\rho)\]
to ensure linearity across $\tau$; from here, taking $a = \tilde f(u_{\rho_\tau}) - f(u_{\rho_{\tau}})$ makes \eqref{eq:tildeffa} hold.  The pictorial example in Figure~\ref{fig:ftilde} may help to clarify the relationship between $\tilde f$ and $f$.

when $a < 1$, for any summands $s_i$; in particular in the example of Figure~\ref{fig:ftilde}, there are summands for $i=-2$, $i=-1$, and $i=0$, corresponding to the three perpendicular slices to $\rho_\tau$ along which the circled lattice points lie.)  One readily checks that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}(\tilde f+m)=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}(\tilde f)$ for any $m\in M$, so we obtain a well-defined function $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}:\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma_\tau)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ that agrees with $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma$ when restricted to $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)$.

We claim that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}$ satisfies the Ehrhart relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blowuprelation}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f-\tilde\delta_\rho])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_
\tau)^\rho}([\tilde f]^\rho).
\end{equation}
for every $\rho\in\Sigma_\tau(1)$. We consider four possible cases, in order of increasing complexity; for the reader's convenience, we illustrate the four cases in Figure~\ref{fig:cases}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[Case 1:] If $\rho=\rho_\tau$, then $\tilde f=f+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ with $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ implies that $\tilde f-\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}=f+(a-1)\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$. Directly from the definition of $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}$, we then compute
\begin{align*}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f-\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}])&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^{\rho_\tau})=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([\tilde f]^{\rho_\tau}),
\end{align*}
verifying \eqref{eq:blowuprelation} in this case.
 
\item[Case 2:] If $\rho\notin\Sigma^{(\tau)}$, then we observe that
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tilde\delta_\rho=\delta_\rho$,
\item $\tilde f=f+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ with $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ implies $\tilde f-\tilde\delta_\rho=(f-\delta_\rho)+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ with $f-\delta_\rho\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$, 
\item $[\tilde f]^\rho=[f]^\rho$ (since $\tilde f$ agrees with $f$ on the neighborhood of $\rho$),
\item $[f+i\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^{\rho_\tau}=[f-\delta_\rho+i\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^{\rho_\tau}$ (for a similar reason).
\end{itemize}
It then follows from the definition of $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}$ and the assumption that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart that
\begin{align*}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f-\tilde\delta_\rho])&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma}([f-\delta_\rho])\\
&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)\\
&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho}([\tilde f]^\rho),
\end{align*}
verifying \eqref{eq:blowuprelation} in this case.
 
\item[Case 3:] If $\rho\in\Sigma^{(\tau)}\setminus\tau(1)$, then we observe that
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tilde\delta_\rho=\delta_\rho$, and
\item $\tilde f=f+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ with $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ implies $\tilde f-\tilde\delta_\rho=(f-\delta_{\rho})+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ with $f-\delta_\rho\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$.
\end{itemize}
From the definition of $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}$, we then compute
\begin{align*}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([\tilde f-\tilde\delta_\rho])&=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma}([f-\delta_\rho])\\
&\hspace{20bp}+\sum_{i=1}^a\Big(\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f+i\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^{\rho_\tau})-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\delta_\rho+i\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^{\rho_\tau})\Big).
\end{align*}
Using the Ehrharticity of both $\Sigma$ and $(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}$, this expression simplifies to
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)+\sum_{i=1}^a\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^\pi}([f+i\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^\pi),
\]
where $\pi\in\Sigma_\tau(2)$ is the cone with rays $\rho$ and $\rho_\tau$. Using that $(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho=(\Sigma^\rho)_{\overline\tau}$, this final expression is then seen to be equal to $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho}([\tilde f]^\rho)$, verifying \eqref{eq:blowuprelation} in this case.
 
\item[Case 4:] If $\rho\in\tau(1)$, then we observe that
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tilde\delta_\rho+\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}=\delta_\rho$ and
\item $\tilde f=f+a\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ with $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ implies $\tilde f-\tilde\delta_\rho=(f-\delta_\rho)+(a+1)\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ with $f-\delta_\rho\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$.
\end{itemize}
We compute the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:blowuprelation} from the definition of $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}$:
\begin{align*}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma}([f-\delta_\rho])+\sum_{i=1}^a\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f+i\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^{\rho_\tau})-\sum_{i=1}^{a+1}\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_{\rho}+(i-1)\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]^{\rho_\tau}).
\end{align*}
Since $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart, the first difference above is $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)$, and, using the fact that the star fan $(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho
_\tau}$ is Ehrhart, the second difference works out to be
\[
-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho]^{\rho_\tau})+\sum_{i=1}^a\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^\pi}([f+i\tilde\delta_{\rho^\tau}]^\pi),
\]
where $\pi\in\Sigma_\tau(2)$ is the cone with rays $\rho$ and $\rho_\tau$. In order to verify \eqref{eq:blowuprelation} in this case, it remains to prove that
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho]^{\rho_\tau})=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho}([f]^\rho).
\]
Since $(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho=(\Sigma^\rho)_{\overline\tau}$ and $[f]^\rho$ is linear on the cones of $\Sigma^\rho$, it follows that
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^\rho}([f]^\rho)=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho),
\]
so it remains to show that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho]^{\rho_\tau})=0$. After translating $f$, we can assume that $f$ vanishes on $\tau$, so that $[f-\tilde\delta_\rho]^{\rho_\tau}=[f-\tilde\delta_\rho]$. For notational convenience, set $\Sigma'=\Pi_{\dim\tau-1}$. Using that $(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}$ is a $\Sigma'$-bundle over $\Sigma^\tau$, we can use the construction in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:fiberbundles} to write
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho])=(-1)^{\dim\Sigma'}\sum_{m'\in M'}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma'_{-\tilde\delta_\rho',m'}}(-1)^{\dim(\sigma)}\Big)\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\tau}([(f-m')'']),
\]
where $(f-\tilde\delta_\rho)'=-\tilde\delta_\rho'$ follows from the assumption that $f$ vanishes on $\tau$. However, noting that the the function $-\tilde\delta_\rho'\in\mathrm{PL}(\Sigma')$ is concave and that the simplex $P_{\tilde\delta_\rho'}$ does not have any interior lattice points, it follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} that the contribution from every $m'\in M'$ to the above sum is zero. Thus,
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho]^{\rho_\tau})=0,
\]
completing the verification of \eqref{eq:blowuprelation} in this case.
\end{enumerate}

We have now proved that the stellar subdivision of an Ehrhart fan is Ehrhart, and it remains to prove the converse. By induction, assume that the Ehrharticity of a stellar subdivision implies the Ehrharticity of the original fan in all dimensions less than $d$, and let $\Sigma$ be a unimodular $d$-dimensional fan such that $\Sigma_\tau$ is Ehrhart for some $\tau\in\Sigma$. We must prove that $\Sigma$ is Ehrhart.

To verify the first Ehrhart condition for $\Sigma$, we note that, for any $\rho\in\Sigma$, the star fan $\Sigma^\rho$ is either equal to a star fan of $\Sigma_\tau$ or has a stellar subdivision that is equal to a star fan of $\Sigma_\tau$. Using the assumption that $\Sigma_\tau$ is Ehrhart and the induction hypothesis, it follows that $\Sigma^\rho$ is Ehrhart for any $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$.

To verify the second condition of Ehrharticity for $\Sigma$, start by defining $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma:\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ by
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])\coloneq \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f]),
\]
where we are using the assumption that $\Sigma_\tau$ is Ehrhart. Thus, Condition (2) of Ehrharticity is equivalent to proving that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blowdownrelation}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f-\delta_\rho])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_
\tau)^\rho}([f]^\rho)
\end{equation}
for every $f\in \mathrm{PL}(\Sigma)$ and $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$. We consider two cases.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[Case 1:] If $\rho\notin\tau(1)$, then $\delta_\rho=\tilde\delta_\rho$, and it follows from the Ehrharticity of $\Sigma_\tau$ that
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f-\delta_\rho])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_
\tau)^\rho}([f]^\rho)
\]
verifying \eqref{eq:blowdownrelation} in this case.
 
\item[Case 2:] If $\rho\in\tau(1)$, then we observe that $\delta_\rho=\tilde\delta_\rho+\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}$ and it follows that
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f-\delta_\rho])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho-\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}]).
\]
Ehrharticity of $\Sigma_\tau$ implies that
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f])-\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma_\tau}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho-\tilde\delta_{\rho_\tau}])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho}}([f]^\rho)+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho]^{\rho_\tau}).
\]
The argument used in Case 4 above shows that 
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{(\Sigma_\tau)^{\rho_\tau}}([f-\tilde\delta_\rho]^{\rho_\tau})=0,
\]
verifying \eqref{eq:blowdownrelation} in this case, and completing the proof of the proposition.\qedhere
\end{enumerate}

Let $\Sigma$ be a common unimodular refinement of $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:wlodarczyk} implies that there exists a sequence of unimodular fans $\Sigma_1',\dots,\Sigma_k'$ such that

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:chiK}
If $X$ is the support of an Ehrhart fan, then the Ehrhart polynomial of $X$ induces a well-defined linear function $\widetilde\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X:\underline{\mathrm{PE}}(X)\rightarrow \mathbb{Z}$ defined on additive generators by
\[
\widetilde\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([e^{f}]) = \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([f]).
\]
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:K(X)presentation}, it suffices to prove that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel1}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([f])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([g])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([\max(f,g)])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([\min(f,g)])
\end{equation}
for all $f,g\in\mathrm{PL}(X)$, and that
\begin{equation*}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([f+\ell])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_X([f])
\end{equation*}
for all $f\in\mathrm{PL}(X)$ and $\ell\in\mathrm{L}(X)$, the latter of which is immediate from the observation that $[f+\ell]=[f]\in\mathrm{PL}(X)$. To prove \eqref{eq:rel1}, start by fixing a unimodular fan $\Sigma$ supported on $X$ such that $f$, $g$, $\max(f,g)$, and $\min(f,g)$ are all linear on the cones of $\Sigma$. Notice that, for each cone $\sigma\in\Sigma$, it must be the case that $f\geq g$ or $f\leq g$ on all of $\sigma$. By Definition~\ref{def:Ehrhartfansupport}, we see that \eqref{eq:rel1} is equivalent to the similar relation obtained by replacing $X$ with $\Sigma$ everywhere:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel3}
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([g])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([\max(f,g)])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([\min(f,g)]).
\end{equation}
We prove \eqref{eq:rel3} by induction on the nonnegative integer
\begin{equation}\label{eq:difference}
\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}|f(u_\rho)-g(u_\rho)|.
\end{equation}

When \eqref{eq:difference} is zero, then $f=g=\max(f,g)=\min(f,g)$, and \eqref{eq:rel3} follows immediately, proving the base case. To prove the induction step, suppose that \eqref{eq:difference} is positive and choose any $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$ such that (without loss of generality) $f(u_\rho)>g(u_\rho)$. By our assumption on $\Sigma$, it follows that $f(x)\geq g(x)$ for all $x$ in the neighborhood of $\rho$. In particular, it then follows that
\[
[f]^\rho=[\max(f,g)]^\rho\in\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma^\rho).
\]
and
\[
\max(f-\delta_\rho,g)=\max(f,g)-\delta_\rho.
\]
Applying the Ehrhart recursion to both $[f]$ and $[\max(f,g)]$ at $\rho$, we then obtain
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f-\delta_\rho])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho)
\]
and
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([\max(f,g)])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([\max(f-\delta_\rho,g)])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_{\Sigma^\rho}([f]^\rho).
\]
In particular, it then follows that \eqref{eq:rel3} is equivalent to 
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([f-\delta_\rho])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([g])=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([\max(f-\delta_\rho,g)])+\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\Sigma([\min(f,g)]),
\]
but this new relation has a strictly smaller value of \eqref{eq:difference}, and thus follows by the induction hypothesis.

\label{thm:matroid}
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a loopless matroid.  Then $\Sigma_\mathcal{M}$ is Ehrhart, with Ehrhart polynomial given by
\[
\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}([f]) = \raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\mathcal{M}\left(\bigotimes_{F}\mathcal{O}(D_F)^{\otimes f(u_F)}\right).
\]
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
We first note that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}$ is well-defined on $\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma_\mathcal{M})$ simply because 
\[
\underline{\mathrm{PL}}(\Sigma_\mathcal{M})\cong \mathrm{Pic}(Z_{\Sigma_\mathcal{M}})\subseteq K(\Sigma_{\mathcal{M}})= K(\mathcal{M}),
\]
where the isomorphism is given by (\cite[Theorems~4.2.1 and 4.2.12]{CoxLittleSchenck})
\[
[f]\mapsto \bigotimes_{F}\mathcal{O}(D_F)^{\otimes f(u_F)}.
\]

Next, for any ray $\rho_F$ of $\Sigma_\mathcal{M}$ corresponding to a proper flat $F$ of $\mathcal{M}$, the star fan of $\Sigma_\mathcal{M}$ at $\rho_F$ is isomorphic to a product of Bergman fans,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Bergmanstarfan}
    \Sigma_\mathcal{M}^{\rho_F} \cong \Sigma_{\mathcal{M}^F} \times \Sigma_{\mathcal{M}_F},
\end{equation}
by \cite[Proposition 3.5]{AHK}.  Here, $\mathcal{M}^F$ denotes the restriction of $\mathcal{M}$ to $F$ and $\mathcal{M}_F$ denotes the contraction, both of which are themselves loopless matroids. Thus, condition (1) in the definition of Ehrharticity follows by induction on the size of the ground set and the fact that products of Ehrhart fans are Ehrhart (Proposition~\ref{prop:products}), where the base case is given by the (zero-dimensional) Bergman fan of the unique loopless matroid on a one-element ground set.

The fact that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}$ satisfies the recursion in condition (2) of the definition of Ehrharticity follows directly from \cite[Proposition 8.6]{LLPP} via the identification \eqref{eq:Bergmanstarfan}. To help the reader parse notation, we note that \cite{LLPP} uses the notation 
$\mathrm{Snap}_\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{FY}}(\mathbf{a})$ for a tuple $\mathbf{a} = (a_F)$ of nonnegative integers, which is related to the above by
\[
\mathrm{Snap}_\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{FY}}(\mathbf{a})=\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}_\mathcal{M}\left(\bigotimes_{F}\mathcal{O}(D_F)^{\otimes a_F}\right).
\]
And finally, that $\raisebox{2bp}{$\chi$}(0)=1$ follows from setting $\mathbf{m}=0$ in \cite[Theorem~7.2]{LLPP}.
