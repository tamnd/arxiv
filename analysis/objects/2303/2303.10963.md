\label{thm: main1}
Fix a rational vector $(c_1,...,c_k)\in (0,1)^k$. Suppose $(X, r\sum_{j=1}^kc_jB_j)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair for some $(B_1,...,B_k)\in \mathbb{P}_1\times...\times \mathbb{P}_k$ and some rational $r>0$. Then $(D_1,...,D_k)\in \mathbb{P}_1\times...\times \mathbb{P}_k$ 
is GIT-(semi/poly)stable under ${\rm {Aut}}(X)$-action with respect to the linearization $\mathcal{O}(c_1,...,c_k)$ if and only if $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is K-(semi/poly)stable for $0<\epsilon\ll 1$. In other words, the chamber decomposition of $[0,1]^k$ near the original point $(0,0,...,0)\in [0,1]^k$ exactly gives the chamber decomposition for the variation of GIT.

{\rm{(\cite[Theorem 8.1]{Zhou23a})}}\label{thm: domain}
Consider the log smooth pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, S_1+S_2+...+S_k)$, where $S_j, j=1,...,k,$ are mutually distinct smooth hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{P}^n$ of degrees $d_j$ with $n\geq 2$. Suppose all the Fano complete intersections are K-semistable\footnote{This means that for any non-repeating subset $\{i_1,...,i_l\}\subset \{1,2,...,k\}$ such that $\sum_{p=1}^ld_{i_p}<n+1$, the complete intersection $\cap_{p=1}^l S_{i_p}$ is K-semistable.}. Then the K-semistable domain\footnote{See Definition \ref{def: kss domain}.} ${\rm {Kss}}(\mathbb{P}^n, S_1+...+S_k)$ is a rational polytope generated by the following equations
\begin{equation*}
\begin{cases}
0\leq x_i\leq 1, \ \ \  1\leq i\leq k\\

\beta_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^kx_jS_j}(S_i)\geq 0, \ \ \  1\leq i\leq k\\

\sum_{j=1}^k x_jd_j\leq n+1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}

with $\sum_{j=1}^kd_j<n+1$, we have the following conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the log Fano pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$ is K-semistable if and only if the complete intersection $\cap_{j=1}^{k}S_j$ is  K-semistable;
\item there exists a morphism $\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}\to \mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}}$ 
which descends to a surjective morphism $M^{K}_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}\to M^K_{n, \vec{d}}.$
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem: kss degeneration}
Let $(X, \Delta)$ be a K-semistable log Fano pair. Suppose $S$ is a prime divisor on $X$ such that $\beta_{X, \Delta}(S)=0$, then $S$ induces a special test configuration with vanishing generalized Futaki invariant (e.g. \cite{LX14, Fuj19}). By \cite{LWX21}, we know that the central fiber of the test configuration is also K-semistable. Assume further that $S$ is Cartier and $S\sim_\mathbb{Q} -\lambda(K_X+\Delta)$ for some rational $0<\lambda<1$, then it is not hard to see that 
the central fiber of the test configuration is isomorphic to the projective cone over $S$ with respect to the polarization $S|_S$. Actually, first note that 
$$X={\rm{Proj}} \bigoplus_{m\in \mathbb{N}} H^0(X, mS). $$
By \cite[Section 2.3.1]{BX19},  the special test configuration induced by $S$ could be formulated as
$$\mathcal{X}:={\rm{Proj}} \bigoplus_{m\in \mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in \mathbb{Z}} H^0(X, mS-iS)t^{-i}, $$
and the central fiber is
$$\mathcal{X}_0={\rm{Proj}} \bigoplus_{m\in \mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in \mathbb{Z}} \left(H^0(X, mS-iS)/H^0(X, mS-(i+1)S)\right) .$$
Note that for $i\leq m$ we have
$$H^0(X, mS-iS)/H^0(X, mS-(i+1)S)\cong  H^0(S, (m-i)S|_S).$$
Thus, $\mathcal{X}_0\cong {\rm{Proj}} \bigoplus_{m\in \mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in \mathbb{N}}H^0(S, mS|_S)s^i$, which is the projective cone over $S$ with respect to $S|_S$.

We define the CM-line bundles for the family $\pi: (\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\to T$ as follows:
$$\lambda_{{\rm {CM}}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L};\pi):=\lambda_{n+1}^{\frac{2a_1}{a_0}+n(n+1)}\otimes\lambda_n^{-2(n+1)}, $$
$$\lambda_{{\rm {CM}}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi):= \lambda_{n+1}^{\frac{2a_1-\tilde{a}_0}{a_0}+n(n+1)}\otimes\lambda_n^{-2(n+1)}\otimes\tilde{\lambda}_n^{n+1}.$$

$$\lambda_{{\rm {CM}}, \beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi):= \lambda_{n+1}^{\frac{2a_1-\beta\tilde{a}_0}{a_0}+n(n+1)}\otimes\lambda_n^{-2(n+1)}\otimes\tilde{\lambda}_n^{\beta(n+1)}.$$

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem: proportional cm}
Let $\pi: (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{D}; \mathcal{L})\to T$ be a family that satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{X}\to T$ is a flat family of projective normal varieties of dimension $n$ over a proper normal base $T$ such that $-K_{\mathcal{X}/T}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and relative ample over $T$; 
\item $\mathcal{D}$ is an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $\mathcal{X}$ such that every component is flat over $T$ and $\mathcal{D}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}, T}-\mu K_{\mathcal{X}/T}$ for some rational $\mu>0$;
\item $\mathcal{L}=- K_{\mathcal{X}/T}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Applying Definition \ref{def: CM}, we have
\begin{align*}
&\lambda_{{\rm {CM}}, \beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi) \\
=\ &\lambda_{n+1}^{\frac{2a_1-\beta\tilde{a}_0}{a_0}+n(n+1)}\otimes\lambda_n^{-2(n+1)}\otimes\tilde{\lambda}_n^{\beta(n+1)}\\
=\ & \left(\frac{}{}n(1-\beta \mu)+n(n+1)\right)\pi_*\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\\
\ &-2(n+1)\left(\frac{n}{2}\pi_*(\mathcal{L}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\pi_*(-K_{\mathcal{X}/T}\mathcal{L}^n) \right)+\beta (n+1) \pi_*(\mathcal{L}^n\mathcal{D})\\
=\ &-(1+\beta \mu n)\pi_*\mathcal{L}^{n+1}+\beta (n+1) \pi_*(\mathcal{L}^n\mathcal{D}).
\end{align*}
The proof is complete.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem: compute cm}
Notation as above, for a rational point $\vec{c}:=(c_1,...,c_k)\in (0,1)^k$, we have the following formula:
$$\lambda_{{\rm {CM}}, \beta}(\vec{c})= \mathcal{O}(\beta (n+1)vc_1,...,\beta(n+1)vc_k).$$
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
First, note that $\sum_{j=1}^k c_j\mathcal{B}_j\sim_{\mathbb{Q}, T} -(\sum_{j=1}^k c_jl_j)K_{X\times T/T}$.
By Lemma \ref{lem: proportional cm}, we have
\begin{align*}
\lambda_{{\rm {CM}}, \beta}(\vec{c})&=-(1+\beta n\sum_{j=1}^kc_jl_j){\pi_{\vec{c}}}_*(-K_{X\times T/T})^{n+1}+ (1+n)\beta{\pi_{\vec{c}}}_*\left((-K_{X\times T/T})^n.\sum_{j=1}^kc_j\mathcal{B}_j\right)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k (n+1)\beta c_j {\pi_{\vec{c}}}_*((-K_{X\times T/T})^n\mathcal{B}_j). 
\end{align*}
It suffices to compute ${\pi_{\vec{c}}}_*((-K_{X\times T/T})^n\mathcal{B}_j)$. Recall that 
$$\mathcal{B}_j:=\mathbb{P}_1\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{j-1}\times \mathcal{D}_j\times \mathbb{P}_{j+1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_k,$$ 
and 
${\pi_j}_*((-K_{X\times \mathbb{P}_j/\mathbb{P}_j})^n\mathcal{D}_j)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_j}(v)$
for $\pi_{j}: X\times \mathbb{P}_j\to \mathbb{P}_j$. Thus, we have
$${\pi_{\vec{c}}}_*((-K_{X\times T/T})^n\mathcal{B}_j)=\mathcal{O}(0_1,0_2...0_{j-1}, v,0_{j+1},...,0_k),$$
where $0_i$ means that the $i$-th position is $0$. The proof is finished.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop: GIT=K}
Fix a rational vector $(c_1,...,c_k)\in (0,1)^k$. Suppose $(X, r\sum_{j=1}^kc_jB_j)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair for some $(B_1,...,B_k)\in \mathbb{P}_1\times...\times \mathbb{P}_k$ and some rational $r>0$. Then $(D_1,...,D_k)\in \mathbb{P}_1\times...\times \mathbb{P}_k$ 
is GIT-(semi/poly)stable under ${\rm {Aut}}(X)$-action with respect to the linearization $\mathcal{O}(c_1,...,c_k)$ if and only if $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is K-(semi/poly)stable for $0<\epsilon\ll 1$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The idea of the proof is essentially the same as that of \cite[Theorem 1.1]{Zhou21a}. 

Suppose $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is K-semistable, to show that $(D_1,...,D_k)$ is GIT-semistable under the ${\rm {Aut}}(X)$-action with respect to the linearization $\mathcal{O}(c_1,...,c_k)$, by Remark \ref{rem: futaki}, it suffices to show that $\lambda_{{\rm {CM}}, \epsilon}(\vec{c})$ is proportional to $\mathcal{O}(c_1, ..., c_k)$. This is exactly Lemma \ref{lem: compute cm}. Now we suppose $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is K-polystable, then $(D_1,...,D_k)$ is GIT-semistable as we have shown. Let $\rho: \mathbb{C}^*\to {\rm {Aut}}(X)$ be any one parameter subgroup such that the limit point  $\lim_{t\to 0}\rho(t)\cdot (D_1,...,D_k)$ is GIT-polystable, then the GIT-weight corresponding to the $\mathbb{C}^*$-action is zero, which implies that the Futaki invariant of the test configuration induced by the $\mathbb{C}^*$-action vanishes. By \cite{LWX21}, we see that 
$$\lim_{t\to 0}\rho(t)\cdot (D_1,...,D_k)\cong (D_1,...,D_k).$$ 
Thus $(D_1,...,D_k)$ is GIT-polystable.





Conversely, suppose $(D_1,...,D_k)$ is GIT-semistable under ${\rm {Aut}}(X)$-action with respect to the linearization $\mathcal{O}(c_1,...,c_k)$, we aim to show that $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is K-semistable.
First, observe that there is a family $(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^k \mathcal{G}_j)\to C$ over a smooth pointed curve $0\in C$ such that 
\begin{enumerate}
\item there is a morphism $C\setminus{0}\to \mathbb{P}_j$ for each $j$;
\item $(\mathcal{X}\setminus \mathcal{X}_0,\mathcal{G}_j\setminus \mathcal{G}_{j,0})$ is obtained via pulling back $(X\times \mathbb{P}_j,\mathcal{D}_j)$ under the morphism $C\setminus \{0\}\to \mathbb{P}_j$;
\item $(\mathcal{X}_0, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{G}_{j,0})\cong (X, \sum_{j=1}^kD_j)$;
\item $(\mathcal{X}_t, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_j\mathcal{G}_{j,t})$
is K-semistable for any $t\in C\setminus \{0\}$.
\end{enumerate}
By the properness of K-moduli, up to a finite base change, one could replace the central fiber $(\mathcal{X}_0, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_j\mathcal{G}_{j,0})\cong (X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ with a K-semistable log Fano pair $(X', \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_j D_j')$ and we denote the new family by $(\mathcal{X}', \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_j \mathcal{G}_j')\to C$ for convenience. We claim that it suffices to show that $\mathcal{X}'_0\cong X$. Suppose $\mathcal{X}_0'\cong X$, then $(D_1',...,D_k')\in \mathbb{P}_1\times ...\times \mathbb{P}_k$ and it is GIT-semistable. By the separatedness of GIT moduli space, we know that $(D_1,...,D_k)$ and $(D_1',...,D_k')$ lie on the same $S$-equivalence class under ${\rm {Aut}}(X)$-action. Thus, $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is K-semistable. 
Next, we turn to prove that $\mathcal{X}_0'\cong X$. Since $\epsilon$ is sufficiently small, by Theorem \cite[Theorem 3.3]{Zhou21a}, we know that $\mathcal{X}_0'$ is K-semistable. Note that $X$ is K-polystable, thus $\mathcal{X}_0'\cong X$ by \cite[Theorem 3.4]{Zhou21a}. By now, we have finished the proof from GIT-semistability to K-semistability.
Finally, we suppose $(D_1,...,D_k)$ is GIT-polystable, then $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is K-semistable as we have shown. To show that it is K-polystable, let $(X_0, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_{j,0})$ be its K-polystable degeneration. By the same approach as above, we know that the ambient space of the K-polystable degeneration is preserved.
Thus 
$$(D_{1,0},...,D_{k,0})\in \mathbb{P}_1\times...\times \mathbb{P}_k$$ is GIT-polystable by the previous paragraph. By the separatedness of GIT-moduli space, we see $(D_1,...,D_k)\cong (D_{1,0},...,D_{k,0})$.
Therefore, $(X, \epsilon\sum_{j=1}^kc_jD_j)$ is itself K-polystable.

\label{prop: good S}
Suppose $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair (here $S_j$'s are not necessarily smooth), then every $S_j$ is irreducible and $S_j$'s are mutually distinct from each other.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By the choice of $a_j, j=1,...,k$, we know
$$1-a_i-S_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(S_i)=0 $$
for any $1\leq i\leq k$. We show that $S_1$ is irreducible. Suppose not, there is an irreducible component of $S_1$, denoted by $D$, satisfying
$$\beta_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(D)=1-a_1-S_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(D) <0.$$
The inequality holds since 
$$S_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(D)>S_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(S_1).$$
Thus, we get a contradiction to the K-semistability. Similarly, all other $S_j$'s are irreducible. Next we show that $S_j$'s are mutually different. Otherwise we may suppose $S_1=S_2$ up to reordering, then 
$$\beta_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(S_1)=1-(a_1+a_2)-S_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(S_1)<0, $$
which is again a contradiction to the K-semistability. The proof is finished.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop: only if}
Given a log smooth pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^kS_j)$, where $S_j$'s are distinct smooth hypersurfaces of degrees $d_j$ with $\sum_{j=1}^k d_j<n+1$. If $\cap_{j=1}^k S_j$ is a K-semistable  Fano variety of dimension $n-k$, then $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
In the log smooth setting, the proof of Proposition \ref{prop: if} can be reversed by Lemma \ref{lem:cone stability}. To see this, first note that 
$$(S_{k-1}\cap...\cap S_1, a_kS_k\cap S_{k-1}\cap...\cap S_1)$$
can be degenerated to a projective cone over $S_k\cap S_{k-1}\cap...\cap S_1$, denoted by $(\mathcal{X}''_0, a_k\mathcal{G}''_{k,0})$ (see the notation in Step 3 of the proof of Prop \ref{prop: if}). Since $(\mathcal{X}''_0, a_k\mathcal{G}''_{k,0})$ is K-semistable, we see 
$$(S_{k-1}\cap...\cap S_1, a_kS_k\cap S_{k-1}\cap...\cap S_1)$$
is also a K-semistable log Fano pair. Continuing to reverse the proof of Proposition \ref{prop: if}, we see
$$(S_1\cap S_2\cap...\cap S_l, \sum_{j=l+1}^ka_j S_j\cap S_1\cap S_2\cap ...\cap S_l)$$
is a K-semistable log Fano pair for any $1\leq l\leq k-1$. Finally, reversing Step 1 of the proof of Prop \ref{prop: if},  we see that $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$ can be degenerated to a projective cone over $(S_1, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j\cap S_1)$, denoted by $(\mathcal{X}_0, \sum_{j=1}^k a_j\mathcal{G}_{j,0})$. Since $(\mathcal{X}_0, \sum_{j=1}^k a_j\mathcal{G}_{j,0})$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair, it is deduced that $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$ is itself a K-semistable log Fano pair.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor: iff}
Given a log smooth pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^kS_j)$, where $S_j$'s are distinct smooth hypersurfaces of degrees $d_j$ with $\sum_{j=1}^k d_j<n+1$. Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair;
\item $S_1\cap S_2\cap...\cap S_k$ is a K-semistable Fano variety of dimension $n-k$;
\item $(S_1\cap S_2\cap...\cap S_l, \sum_{j=l+1}^ka_j S_j\cap S_1\cap S_2\cap ...\cap S_l)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair for any $1\leq l\leq k$ (the log pair is identical to $S_1\cap S_2\cap...\cap S_k$ for $l=k$). 
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We can clearly see these equivalences from the proofs of Proposition \ref{prop: if} and Proposition \ref{prop: only if}.

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def: deg}
We say that the log pair $(X, \sum_{j=1}^kB_j)$ is a degeneration of log smooth pairs of the form $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^kS_j)$ if there exists a $\mathbb{Q}$-Gorenstein flat family over a smooth pointed curve $0\in C$, denoted by $(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{B}_j)\to C$, such that 
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{X}\to C$ is a flat family such that $-K_{\mathcal{X}/C}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and relatively ample;
\item each $\mathcal{B}_j$ is a Weil divisor on $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{B}_j$ is proportional to $-K_{\mathcal{X}/C}$ over $C$;
\item $(\mathcal{X}_0, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{B}_{j,0})\cong (X, \sum_{j=1}^kB_j)$;
\item $(\mathcal{X}_{t}, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{B}_{j, t})$ is of the form $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^kS_j)$ for $t\in C\setminus \{0\}$, where $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^kS_j)$ is log smooth and $S_j$'s are mutually distinct smooth hypersurfaces of degrees $d_j$'s.
\end{enumerate}
We say $(X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)$ is a K-semistable degeneration if $(X, \sum_{j=1}^kB_j)$ is a degeneration as above and $(X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem: smoothable deg}
Let $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{B})\to C$ be a projective family of pairs over a smooth pointed curve $0\in C$ such that the family over $C\setminus 0$ is a log smooth, where $\mathcal{B}$ is an effective prime divisor on $\mathcal{X}$. Suppose (a) the degeneration $\mathcal{X}_0$ has klt singularities;  (b) $-K_{\mathcal{X}/C}$ is ample over $C$; (c) $\mathcal{B} \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} -rK_{\mathcal{X}/C}$ for some rational $0<r<1$; (d) $\mathcal{B}_0:=\mathcal{B}|_{\mathcal{X}_0}$ is normal. Then we have
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{X}\to C$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Gorenstein family of Fano varieties;
\item if $\mathcal{B}_0$ has klt type singularities, then $\mathcal{B}\to C$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Gorenstein family of Fano varieties;
\item $(K_{\mathcal{X}_0}+\mathcal{B}_0)|_{\mathcal{B}_0}=K_{\mathcal{B}_0}$, i.e. there is no different part.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Note that the family $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{B})\to C$ is log smooth over $C\setminus 0$, we easily see that $\mathcal{X}\to C$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Gorenstein family of Fano varieties by conditions (a) and (b). Since $\mathcal{X}$ is smooth in codim 2, we have the following adjunction formulas
$$ {\rm (\star)}\ \ (K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{B})|_{\mathcal{B}}=K_{\mathcal{B}}, \ \ \ {\rm (\star\star)}\ \  (K_\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{B}_0)|_{\mathcal{B}_0}=K_{\mathcal{B}_0}. $$
By condition (c), we see that $-K_\mathcal{B}$ is ample over $C$. Combining condition (d), we see that $\mathcal{B}\to C$ is also a $\mathbb{Q}$-Gorenstein family of Fano varieties if $\mathcal{B}_0$ has klt type singularities, which concludes (2). By adjunction, we have
$$(K_{\mathcal{X}_0}+\mathcal{B}_0)|_{\mathcal{B}_0}=K_{\mathcal{B}_0}+{\rm {Diff}}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(0). $$
We aim to show that ${\rm {Diff}}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(0)=0$ which concludes (3). To see this, first note that ${\rm {Diff}}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(0)\geq 0$ (e.g. \cite[Proposition 4.5]{Kollar13}). Observe the following:
$$-(K_{\mathcal{X}_0}+\mathcal{B}_0)=-(K_\mathcal{X}+\mathcal{X}_0+\mathcal{B})|_{\mathcal{X}_0}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}, C} -(1-r)K_{\mathcal{X}_0}, $$
which implies the following:
$$-(K_{\mathcal{B}_0}+{\rm {Diff}}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(0))\sim_\mathbb{Q} -(1-r)K_{\mathcal{X}_0}|_{\mathcal{B}_0}.$$
On the other hand, by formulas $(\star)$ and $(\star\star)$, we have
$$-K_{\mathcal{B}_0}=-(K_{\mathcal{B}}+\mathcal{B}_0)|_{\mathcal{B}_0}=-(K_\mathcal{X}+\mathcal{B})|_{\mathcal{B}_0}\sim_\mathbb{Q} -(1-r)K_{\mathcal{X}_0}|_{\mathcal{B}_0}. $$
Thus, we derive $-(K_{\mathcal{B}_0}+{\rm {Diff}}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(0))\sim_\mathbb{Q} -K_{\mathcal{B}_0}$ and it is clear that ${\rm {Diff}}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(0)=0$.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop: deg}
Notation as in Definition \ref{def: deg}, let $(X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)$ be a K-semistable degeneration. Denoting by $B_0:=X$ and $B^{(l)}:=B_0\cap B_1\cap...\cap B_l$, then we have the following conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B^{(l)}$ is a Fano variety for any $0\leq l\leq k$;
\item $K_{B^{(l)}}+B^{(l+1)}=K_{B^{(l+1)}}$ for any $0\leq l\leq k-1$, i.e. there is no different part;
\item $B^{(k)}=B_1\cap B_2\cap...\cap B_k$ is a K-semistable Fano variety. 
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}_j)\to C$ be the degeneration family over a pointed curve $0\in C$ as in Definition \ref{def: deg}, for which we have $(\mathcal{X}_0, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}_{j,0})\cong (X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)$. Note that the degenerating family is log smooth over $C\setminus 0$, and each $\mathcal{B}_j$ is a prime divisor on $\mathcal{X}$ satisfying $\mathcal{B}_j\sim_{\mathbb{Q}, C}-r_jK_{\mathcal{X}/C}$ for $r_j=\frac{d_j}{n+1}<1$.

\

\textit{Step 1.} 
Since  $(X, \sum_{j=1}^k a_j B_j)$ is K-semistable, then the Proposition \ref{prop: good S} stated for $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$ also applies to $(X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)$. Indeed, we observe that $(a_1,...,a_k)$ is still the unique solution of the following linear equations
$$1-a_i-S_{X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j}(B_i)=0,\ \ \ i=1,2,...,k, $$
since we still have the following for a general fiber $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)$:
$$S_{X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j}(B_i)=S_{\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j}(S_i).$$
Thus it is similar for $(X, \sum_{j=1}^kB_j)$ to derive that $B_j$'s are irreducible and mutually different. Since $\beta_{X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j}(B_1)=0$, by \cite{BLZ22}, we know that $B_1$ induces a special test configuration of $(X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)$, denoted by
$$(\mathcal{X}', \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}'_j)\to \mathbb{A}^1,$$ 
where the central fiber is also a K-semistable log Fano pair and $(\mathcal{X}'_0, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}'_{j,0})$ is the projective orbifold cone over $B_1$. Hence $B_1$ is a klt type divisor on $X$ (e.g. \cite[Section 3.1]{Kollar13}). Applying Lemma \ref{lem: smoothable deg} to $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{B}_1)\to C$, we see that $B_1\cong \mathcal{B}_{1,0}$ is a Fano variety, and $(K_X+B_1)|_{B_1}=K_{B_1}$. Since there is no different part, we see that $X$ is smooth along the codimension two points of $B_1$, which implies that $B_j|_{B_1}=B_j\cap B_1$ are all prime divisors on $B_1$ for $2\leq j\leq k$. By the orbifold cone construction (e.g. \cite[Section 4]{Kollar04seifert} or \cite[section 3.1]{Zhuang24}), we see that $(\mathcal{X}'_0, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}'_{j,0})$ is the projective cone over $(B_1, \sum_{j=2}^k a_j B_j\cap B_1)$. By Lemma \ref{lem:cone stability}, we see that $(B_1, \sum_{j=2}^ka_j B_j\cap B_1)$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair. 


\

\textit{Step 2.} 
Now, we consider the degenerating family $(\mathcal{B}_1, \sum_{j=2}^k a_j \mathcal{B}_j\cap \mathcal{B}_1)\to C$, which is also a family of K-semistable log Fano pairs. Similarly as in Step 1 (see Step 2 of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop: if}), we see that $\mathcal{B}_{j,0}\cap \mathcal{B}_{1,0}=B_j\cap B_1$ are mutually different prime divisors on $B_1$ for $2\leq j\leq k$, and $B_2\cap B_1$ induces a special test configuration of $(B_1, \sum_{j=2}^ka_j B_j\cap B_1)$ with vanishing generalized Futaki invariant. Using the same argument as in Step 1, we conclude that $B_2\cap B_1$ is a Fano variety and $K_{B_1}+B_2\cap B_1=K_{B_2\cap B_1}$. Continuing the process, we clearly complete the proofs of (1) and (2). Applying Lemma \ref{lem:cone stability} in the final step, we conclude (3).

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By Corollary \ref{cor: iff}, we directly conclude the first statement in Theorem \ref{thm: main2}. We divide the rest proof into two steps. First, we construct the connecting map $\mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d},\vec{a}}\to \mathcal{M}^K_{n,\vec{d}}$. Then we show that it descends to a surjective map between moduli spaces. 

\

\textit{Step 1.}
By Proposition \ref{prop: deg}, given $(X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)\in \mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}$, we have that $\cap_{j=1}^k B_j$ is also K-semistable of dimension $n-k$. Thus we can define the morphism
$$\mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}\to \mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}},\ \  [(X, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jB_j)]\mapsto [\cap_{j=1}^k B_j].$$
Actually, the above morphism is only defined on $\mathbb{C}$-points, however, we could extend it to be a morphism between stacks as follows. For convenience, we only define the morphism for $\mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}(W)\to \mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}}(W)$, where $W$ is a normal scheme\footnote{For arbitrary base, we take the concept of K-flatness introduced by \cite{Kollar19families}. Refer to \cite[Section 2.6]{XZ20b} for the definition of the family.}. Take $[(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}_j)\to W]\in \mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}(W)$, i.e.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{X}\to W$ is a flat family such that $-K_{\mathcal{X}/W}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier and ample over $W$;
\item $\mathcal{B}_j$ is a prime divisor on $\mathcal{X}$ with $\mathcal{B}_j\sim_{\mathbb{Q}, W} -r_jK_{\mathcal{X}/W}$ and $r_j=\frac{d_j}{n+1}$;
\item $(\mathcal{X}_t, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}_{j,t})\in \mathcal{M}^K_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}(\mathbb{C})$ for every closed point $t\in W$.
\end{enumerate}
By Proposition \ref{prop: deg} and Remark \ref{rem: kss to fano'}, we see that $\mathcal{B}_{i_1,t}\cap \mathcal{B}_{i_2,t}\cap...\cap \mathcal{B}_{i_p,t}$ is a Fano variety for any closed point $t\in W$ and non-repeating subset $\{i_1,...,i_p\}\subset\{1,2,...,k\}$, thus $\mathcal{B}_{i_1}\cap \mathcal{B}_{i_2}\cap...\cap \mathcal{B}_{i_p}\to W$ is flat by \cite[Lemma 10.58]{Kollar23}. By Proposition \ref{prop: deg} (2), we see that the family $\mathcal{B}_1\cap \mathcal{B}_2\cap...\cap \mathcal{B}_k\to W$ could be obtained by taking $k$ times adjunction, and every time we take the adjunction, there is no difference part.  
By Proposition \ref{prop: deg} (3), we see that $\mathcal{B}_1\cap \mathcal{B}_2\cap...\cap \mathcal{B}_k\to W$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Gorenstein family of K-semistable Fano varieties. Thus the connecting morphism could be defined by sending $[(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{B}_j)\to W]$ to $[\mathcal{B}_1\cap \mathcal{B}_2\cap...\cap \mathcal{B}_k\to W]$.


\

\textit{Step 2.}
The morphism between stacks naturally descends to a morphism between K-moduli spaces by the universal property of good moduli spaces (e.g. \cite[Theorem 4.16 (vi)]{Alper13}):
$$\phi: M^K_{n, \vec{d}, \vec{a}}\to M^K_{n, \vec{d}}. $$
We aim to show that $\phi$ is surjective. Suppose $[Y]\in M^K_{n, \vec{d}}$  is of the form $S_1\cap...\cap S_k$ deduced by a log smooth pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^k S_j)$,  by 
Corollary \ref{cor: iff}, $[(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^ka_jS_j)]$ is a preimage. Suppose $[Y]$ is not of the form $S_1\cap...\cap S_k$ deduced by a log smooth pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^k S_j)$. Then there is a flat family of K-semistable Fano varieties $\mathcal{Y}\to C$ over a smooth pointed curve $0\in C$ such that $-K_{\mathcal{Y}/C}$ is ample over $C$, $\mathcal{Y}_0\cong Y$, and $\mathcal{Y}_t$ is of the form $S_1\cap...\cap S_k$ deduced by a log smooth pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^k S_j)$. Let $\mathcal{Y}^*\to C^*:=C\setminus \{0\}$ be the family obtained via base change under the inclusion $C^*\to C$. We claim that there exist an \'etale morphism $C'^*\to C^*$ and a family 
$$(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{S}_j)\to C'^*$$
such that for any $t\in C'^*$, the fiber $(\mathcal{X}_t, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{S}_{j, t})$ is isomorphic to some log smooth pair $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^k S_j)$ and $\cap_{j=1}^k \mathcal{S}_{j, t}\cong \mathcal{Y}'^*_t$, where $\mathcal{Y}'^*\to C'^*$ is the pulling back of $\mathcal{Y}^*\to C^*$ along $C'^*\to C^*$. To see this, we denote by $\mathbb{P}_j:=|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(d_j)|$ and $\mathcal{E}_j\subset \mathbb{P}^n\times \mathbb{P}_j$ the universal divisor.
Putting $\mathcal{D}_j:= \mathbb{P}_1\times...\mathbb{P}_{j-1}\times\mathcal{E}_j\times \mathbb{P}_{j+1}\times ...\times \mathbb{P}_k$ and $T:=\mathbb{P}_1\times \mathbb{P}_2\times...\times \mathbb{P}_k$,
we consider the following universal family
$$(\mathbb{P}^n\times T, \sum_{j=1}^k \mathcal{D}_j)\to T. $$
Let $Z\subset T$ be the subset of $T$ parametrizing those log smooth fibers $(\mathbb{P}^n, \sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{j,t})$ where $\mathcal{D}_{j,t}, j=1,...,k,$ are mutually different. We denote the family by 
$$(\mathbb{P}^n\times Z, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{D}_j\times_T Z)\to Z.$$
                                                                                                                                                                                          
Let $\mathcal{U}\to U$ be the universal family of complete intersections of $k$ different hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{P}^n$ of degrees $d_1, d_2,...,d_k$ respectively, then we naturally have the following commutative diagram

\begin{center}
	\begin{tikzcd}[column sep = 2em, row sep = 2em]
	 (\mathbb{P}^n\times Z, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{D}_j\times_T Z) \arrow[d,"", swap] \arrow[rr,""]&& \bigcap_{j=1}^k\ \mathcal{D}_j\times_T Z \arrow[d,"",swap] \arrow[rr,""]& & \mathcal{U} \arrow[d,"", swap]  \\
	 Z\arrow[rr,"="]&& Z \arrow[rr,""]	&& U
	 \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
Note that there is a morphism $C^*\to U$ such that $\mathcal{Y}^*\to C^*$ is the pulling back of $\mathcal{U}\to U$ via the base change $C^*\to U$. Taking base change again, we obtain a family of log smooth pairs
$$\pi: (\mathbb{P}^n\times Z\times_U C^*, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{D}_j\times_T Z\times_U C^*)\to Z\times_U C^*. $$ 
Up to shrinking $C$ around $0\in C$, one could find a section $C'^*\to Z\times_U C^*$ such that $C'^*\to C^*$ is an \'etale morphism. Pulling back the family $\pi$ via this section, we get the required family $(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^k\mathcal{S}_j)\to C'^*$.

Note that $(\mathcal{X}, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\mathcal{S}_j)\to C'^*$ is a family of K-semistable log Fano pairs, by the properness of K-moduli space, we could find a complete family $$(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_j)\to C'\ni 0'$$ 
up to a finite base change and the central fiber $(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{0'}, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j,{0'}})$ is also K-semistable. By Proposition \ref{prop: if},  
$\cap_{j=1}^k \tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j,{0'}}$ is K-semistable and $[Y]=[\cap_{j=1}^k \tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j,{0'}}]$ by the separatedness of K-moduli space (e.g. \cite{BX19}). Thus $[(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{0'}, \sum_{j=1}^ka_j\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j,{0'}})]$ is a preimage of $[Y]$ and $\phi$ is surjective. The proof is complete.

Consider the log pair $(\mathbb{P}^4, c\cdot \frac{5}{6}S)$, where $S$ is a cubic threefold and $c\in (0,1)$. We show that the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\mathbb{P}^4, c\cdot \frac{5}{6}S)$ is K-semistable for $0<c\ll 1$;
\item $S$ is ${\rm {GIT}}$-semistable;
\item $S$ is K-semistable;
\item $(\mathbb{P}^4, \frac{5}{6}S)$ is K-semistable;
\item $(\mathbb{P}^4, c\cdot\frac{5}{6}S)$ is K-semistable for any $c\in (0,1)$.
\end{enumerate}
Note that (1) implies (2) by \cite[Theorem 1.4]{ADL19}; (2) implies (3) by \cite{LX19}. To see how (3) implies (4), let $(X, \frac{5}{6}B)$ denote the projective cone over $S$ with respect to the polarization $S|_S$, where $B$ is the infinite section of the projective cone $X$. By Lemma \ref{lem:cone stability}, we see that $(X, \frac{5}{6}B)$ is K-semistable. Note that $(X, \frac{5}{6}B)$ is a degeneration of $(\mathbb{P}^4, \frac{5}{6}S)$ by a special test configuration, so we get $(\mathbb{P}^4, \frac{5}{6}S)$ is K-semistable by \cite{BLX22}. (4) implies (5) by the interpolation property of K-stability and (5) implies (1) clearly.


These equivalences already imply that there is no wall for $c\in (0,1)$. Indeed, suppose $c_1\in (0,1)$ is the first wall, then there exists a K-semistable log Fano pair $(\mathbb{P}^4, c_1\cdot \frac{5}{6}S)$ such that $(\mathbb{P}^4, c\cdot \frac{5}{6}S)$ is not K-semistable for $c>c_1$, which is a contradiction.
