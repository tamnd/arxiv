\label{prop_commutators}
Let $f\in\Omega^p_0({\bm M})$ be a test function, then the following operations
involving $E^\pm$ commute: (a) ${\bf d} E^\pm f = E^\pm {\bf d} f$, and
(b) $\bm{\delta} E^\pm f = E^\pm \bm{\delta} f$.

(a)  Let $f\in\Omega_0^p({\bm M})$.  We know that $\mathcal{A}^\pm = E^\pm f \in \Omega^p({\bm M})$
is the unique solution to $\Box \mathcal{A}^\pm = f$.  Likewise we have $\mathcal{A}'^\pm =  E^\pm
{\bf d} f \in \Omega^{p+1}({\bm M})$ is the unique solution to $\Box \mathcal{A}'^\pm = {\bf d} f $.
Consider
\begin{equation}
\Box{\bf d}\mathcal{A}^\pm = {\bf d}\Box\mathcal{A}^\pm = {\bf d} f = \Box\mathcal{A}'^\pm.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal{A}'^\pm$ is unique we deduce ${\bf d}\mathcal{A}^\pm =\mathcal{A}'^\pm$, therefore
${\bf d} E^\pm f = E^\pm {\bf d} f$.

(b)  Let $f$ and $\mathcal{A}^\pm$ be as defined above.  Set $\mathcal{A}'^\pm = E^\pm\bm{\delta} f\in
\Omega^{p-1}({\bm M})$, which is the unique solution to $\Box \mathcal{A}'^\pm = \bm{\delta} f $.
Then consider
\begin{equation}
\bm{\delta}\Box\mathcal{A}^\pm = \Box\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}^\pm = \bm{\delta} f = \Box\mathcal{A}'^\pm.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal{A}'^\pm$ is unique we deduce $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}^\pm =\mathcal{A}'^\pm$, therefore
$\bm{\delta} E^\pm f = E^\pm \bm{\delta} f$.

\label{Prop:A_with_f}
Let $\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ be a smooth solution of $\Box\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{J}$ with Cauchy
data
\begin{eqnarray}
A_{(0)}&\equiv& \rho_{(0)}\mathcal{A} \in\Omega^p(\Sigma)\nonumber\\
A_{({\bf d})}&\equiv& \rho_{({\bf d})}\mathcal{A} \in\Omega^p(\Sigma)\nonumber\\
A_{(\bm{\delta})}&\equiv& \rho_{(\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{A} \in\Omega^{p-1}(\Sigma)\nonumber\\
A_{(n)}&\equiv& \rho_{(n)}\mathcal{A} \in\Omega^{p-1}(\Sigma).\nonumber
\end{eqnarray}
Then, for any compactly supported test function $f\in\Omega^p_0({\bm M})$
we have
\begin{equation}
\int_{\bm M} \mathcal{A}\wedge*f =
\langle\mathcal{J} , E^- f\rangle_{\Sigma^+} +\langle\mathcal{J} , E^+ f\rangle_{\Sigma^-}
-\langle A_{(0)},\rho_{({\bf d})} E f\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})},\rho_{(n)} E f\rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{({\bf d})},\rho_{(0)} E f\rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{(n)},\rho_{(\bm{\delta})} E f\rangle_\Sigma.
\label{eq:INVprop1}
\end{equation}
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
This is a generalization of the proof for Theorem 5 of Furlani \cite{Furl99}.
Note, Furlani uses a different sign convention for the definition of the propagator $E$.
Using Eq.~\ref{eq:applied_Greens}, set $\mathcal{A}$ equal to the smooth solution of
$\Box\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{J}$ and $\mathcal{B} =  E^\mp f$ for $\Sigma^\pm$ respectively, then
\begin{eqnarray}
\int_{\Sigma^\pm} \left(\mathcal{A}\wedge*\Box E^\mp f - E^\mp f \wedge*\Box\mathcal{A}\right)
&=& \mp\left(\langle\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{A},\rho_{({\bf d})}E^\mp f\rangle_\Sigma
+\langle\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{A},\rho_{(n)}E^\mp f\rangle_\Sigma\right.\nonumber\\
&&\left.-\langle\rho_{(0)}E^\mp f,\rho_{({\bf d})}\mathcal{A}\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}E^\mp f,\rho_{(n)}\mathcal{A}\rangle_\Sigma\right).
\end{eqnarray}
Substituting the Cauchy data, noting that $\mathcal{A}$ is a smooth
solution to the Klein-Gordon equation with source $\mathcal{J}$ and
using $\Box E^\pm = \openone$, we can simplify the above to
\begin{equation}
\int_{\Sigma^\pm} \mathcal{A}\wedge* f = \langle\mathcal{J} , E^\mp f \rangle_{\Sigma^\pm}
\mp\left(\langle A_{(0)},\rho_{({\bf d})}E^\mp f\rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})},\rho_{(n)}E^\mp f\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle \rho_{(0)}E^\mp f, A_{({\bf d})}\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}E^\mp f, A_{(n)}\rangle_\Sigma\right)
\end{equation}
When the two above equations are added for $\Sigma^\pm$, the
result is Eq.~\ref{eq:INVprop1}.  Furthermore, the integrals in this
expression are well defined because $f$ has compact support, thus
$E^\pm f$ and $E f$ have compact support on all other Cauchy surfaces.

\label{prop:box_existence}{\rm (Existence of homogenous $\Box$ solutions)}
Let $A_{(0)}, A_{({\bf d})} \in \Omega^p_0(\Sigma)$ and $A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})}
\in\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)$ specify Cauchy data on $\Sigma$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Distributional_solution}
\mathcal{A}' = E\rho_{({\bf d})}' A_{(0)}+E\rho_{(n)}' A_{(\bm{\delta})}
-E\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}' A_{(n)}-E\rho_{(0)}' A_{({\bf d})}
\end{equation}
is the unique smooth solution of $\Box\mathcal{A}'=0$ with these data.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $f\in\Omega_0^p({\bm M})$ be any compactly supported test form and consider
\begin{eqnarray}
\langle \mathcal{A}', f \rangle_{\bm M} &=&\langle E\rho_{({\bf d})}' A_{(0)},f\rangle_{\bm M}
+\langle E\rho_{(n)}' A_{(\bm{\delta})},f\rangle_{\bm M}-\langle E\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}'
A_{(n)},f\rangle_{\bm M}-\langle E\rho_{(0)}' A_{({\bf d})},f\rangle_{\bm M}\nonumber\\
&=& \langle \rho_{({\bf d})}' A_{(0)}, E'f\rangle_{\bm M}
+\langle \rho_{(n)}' A_{(\bm{\delta})},E'f\rangle_{\bm M}-\langle \rho_{(\bm{\delta})}'
A_{(n)},E' f\rangle_{\bm M}-\langle \rho_{(0)}' A_{({\bf d})}, E' f\rangle_{\bm M}.
\end{eqnarray}
The operator $\Box = -(\bm{\delta}{\bf d} - {\bf d}\bm{\delta})$ is self-adjoint, therefore
the transpose operator $E' = -E$ (see Choquet-Bruhat \cite{Choq67}, corollary
to Theorem II).
The pullback operators are all linear and continuous, thus there exist
transpose operators for the $\rho$'s denoted with the primes and we have
\begin{equation}
\langle \mathcal{A}', f \rangle_{\bm M} = -\langle A_{(0)},  \rho_{({\bf d})}E f\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})},\rho_{(n)}Ef\rangle_\Sigma+\langle A_{(n)},\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}
E f\rangle_\Sigma+\langle A_{({\bf d})}, \rho_{(0)}E f\rangle_\Sigma.
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{Prop:A_with_f}, if $\mathcal{A}$ is a solution to $\Box\mathcal{A}=0$
with the Cauchy data given above, then we have $\langle \mathcal{A}', f \rangle_{\bm M}
= \langle\mathcal{A},f\rangle_{\bm M}$ which implies $\mathcal{A}'=\mathcal{A}$ in a distributional sense.
Thus $\mathcal{A}'$ is identified with the unique smooth solution $\mathcal{A}$.

$\mathcal{A}'$ is continuously dependent on the Cauchy data $\left(A_{(0)},

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The proof is a generalization of Theorem 3.2.12 of B\"{a}r et.\ al.\ \cite{BGP07}.
Define $\mathcal{H}^p({\bm M}) \equiv \left\{ \mathcal{A}\in\Omega^p({\bm M}) | \Box\mathcal{A}=0 \right\}$
as the space of smooth homogeneous Klein-Gordon solutions,
then the mapping $\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{H}^p({\bm M})\rightarrow\Omega^p(\Sigma)
\oplus\Omega^p(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}(\Sigma)$ of
a solution to its Cauchy data is by definition both linear and continuous. Next,
let $K\subset{\bm M}$ be a compact subset of ${\bm M}$.  On $K$ we have the spaces
$\Omega_0^p(K) \subset \Omega^p({\bm M})$ and $\Omega_0^p(K\cap\Sigma)\subset
\Omega^p(\Sigma)$ for all $p\le n$.  We also define the space $\mathcal{V}_K^p
= \mathcal{P}^{-1}\left[ \Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)
\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(K\cap\Sigma)\right]$.
Since $\mathcal{P}$ is continuous and $\Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus
\Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0
(K\cap\Sigma) \subset\Omega^p(\Sigma)\oplus \Omega^p(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}
(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}(\Sigma)$ is a closed subset, this implies
$\mathcal{V}_K^p\subset \mathcal{H}^p({\bm M})$ is also a closed subset.
Furthermore, both $\Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus \Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus
\Omega^{p-1}_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0 (K\cap\Sigma)$ and
$\mathcal{V}_K^p$ are Fr\'{e}chet spaces. Also, the map $\mathcal{P}:
\mathcal{V}_K^p \rightarrow \Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)
\oplus \Omega^{p-1}_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(K\cap\Sigma)$ is linear,
continuous, bijective, and by the open mapping theorem for Fr\'{e}chet
spaces (Theorem V.6 of Reed and Simon \cite{RSvI:80}) it is also an open
mapping.  Since a bijection that is open implies a continuous inverse, we
conclude that $\mathcal{P}^{-1}$ is continuous.

Finally, if we have a convergent sequence of Cauchy data $\left( A_{(0),i},
A_{({\bf d}),i}, A_{(n),i}, A_{(\bm{\delta}),i}\right)\rightarrow \left(A_{(0)},
A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})}\right)$ in $\Omega^p_0(\Sigma)\oplus
\Omega^p_0(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)$,
then we can choose a compact subset $K \subset {\bm M}$ with the property
that $\mbox{supp}\left( A_{(0),i}\right)\cup\mbox{supp}\left( A_{({\bf d}),i}\right)
\cup \mbox{supp}\left( A_{(n),i}\right)\cup\mbox{supp}\left( A_{(\bm{\delta}),i}
\right) \subset K$ for all $i$ and $\mbox{supp}\left( A_{(0)}\right)\cup
\mbox{supp}\left( A_{({\bf d})}\right)\cup \mbox{supp}\left( A_{(n)}\right)\cup
\mbox{supp}\left( A_{(\bm{\delta})}\right)\subset K$.  Thus, $\left( A_{(0),i},
A_{({\bf d}),i}, A_{(n),i}, A_{(\bm{\delta}),i}\right)\rightarrow \left(A_{(0)},
A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})}\right)$ in $\Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus
\Omega^p_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(K\cap\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0
(K\cap\Sigma)$ and we conclude that the inverse mapping $\mathcal{P}^{-1}
\left( A_{(0),i},A_{({\bf d}),i}, A_{(n),i}, A_{(\bm{\delta}),i}\right)\rightarrow
\mathcal{P}^{-1}\left(A_{(0)},A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})}\right)$.

\end{equation}

\label{thm_Maxwell_solns} {\rm (Maxwell Solutions)}
Suppose $\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ solves $\Box\mathcal{A} = 0$ with Cauchy data
$(A_{(0)}, A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})})$; then $-\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0$
if and only if $\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = 0$ and ${\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})}=0$.
<!-- end prop-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
First, suppose that $\mathcal{A}$ solves both $\Box\mathcal{A} = 0$ and $-\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A}=0$,
and evaluate the two conditions,
\begin{eqnarray}
\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} &=& \bm{\delta} \rho_{({\bf d})} \mathcal{A} = \bm{\delta} \rho_{(n)}{\bf d}\mathcal{A}
= (-1)^{n(p+1)}\rho_{(n)}\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0,\\
{\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})} &=& {\bf d} \rho_{(\bm{\delta})} \mathcal{A} = {\bf d} \rho_{(0)} \bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}
= \rho_{(0)}{\bf d}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} = - \rho_{(0)}\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0.
\end{eqnarray}
Alternatively, suppose we have Cauchy data $(A_{(0)}, A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)},
A_{(\bm{\delta})})$ with $\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = 0$ and ${\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})}=0$. By
Theorem~\ref{prop:_Cauchy for Box}, there exists a unique
$\mathcal{A}$ which is a solutions to $\Box\mathcal{A} = 0$.  Next, let $f\in\Omega^p_0({\bm M})$,
then by Proposition~\ref{Prop:A_with_f} we can evaluate
\begin{eqnarray}
\langle -\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A},f\rangle_{\bm M} &=& -\langle \mathcal{A}, \bm{\delta}{\bf d} f\rangle_{\bm M}
\nonumber\\
&=&\langle A_{(0)},\rho_{({\bf d})} E\bm{\delta}{\bf d} f\rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})},\rho_{(n)} E\bm{\delta}{\bf d} f\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle A_{(n)},\rho_{(\bm{\delta})} E\bm{\delta}{\bf d} f\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle A_{({\bf d})},\rho_{(0)} E\bm{\delta}{\bf d} f\rangle_\Sigma\nonumber\\
&=&-\langle A_{(0)},\rho_{({\bf d})} {\bf d}\bm{\delta} E f\rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})},\rho_{(n)} \bm{\delta}{\bf d} E f\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle A_{(n)},\rho_{(\bm{\delta})} \bm{\delta}{\bf d} E f\rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{({\bf d})},\rho_{(0)} {\bf d}\bm{\delta} E f\rangle_\Sigma,
\end{eqnarray}
where we have made use of $\Box\mathcal{A} = 0$ in the first and
fourth terms.  The first and third terms vanish because $\rho_{({\bf d})}{\bf d} = 0$
and $\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}\bm{\delta} = 0$.  For the remaining two terms, we
commute the pullback operators with the first derivative operation to
obtain
\begin{equation}
\langle -\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A},f\rangle_{\bm M} = (-1)^{n(p+1)}\langle {\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})}
, \rho_{({\bf d})} E f \rangle_\Sigma + \langle \bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})}, \rho_{(\bm{\delta})}
E f \rangle_\Sigma = 0,
\end{equation}
where we have made use of the properties of the Cauchy data.  This is true
for all $f$, so we conclude $-\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A}=0$.

{\rm (Lorenz solutions)}\label{prop:Lorenz_soln}
Suppose $\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ solves $\Box\mathcal{A}=0$ with Cauchy data $\left ( A_{(0)},
A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})} \right)$, then $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} = 0$ if and only if
$\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = 0$, $\bm{\delta} A_{(n)} = 0$, and $A_{(\bm{\delta})}=0$.
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
First suppose that $\mathcal{A}$ solves both $\Box\mathcal{A} = 0$ and $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} =0$ and let us
evaluate the three conditions above.  For the forward normal derivative we have
\begin{equation}
\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = \bm{\delta} \rho_{({\bf d})}\mathcal{A} = \bm{\delta} \rho_{(n)}{\bf d} \mathcal{A}.
\end{equation}
Using the identity for $p$-forms that $\bm{\delta}\rho_{(n)} = (-1)^{np} \rho_{(n)}\bm{\delta} $
we find
\begin{equation}
\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = (-1)^{n(p+1)}\rho_{(n)}\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = -(-1)^{n(p+1)}\rho_{(n)}{\bf d}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}=0,
\end{equation}
where we have used the wave equation and the subsidiary condition in the last two
steps. For the divergence of the forward normal we find
\begin{equation}
\bm{\delta} A_{(n)} = \bm{\delta}\rho_{(n)} \mathcal{A} = (-1)^{np}\rho_{(n)}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} =0.
\end{equation}
Finally, for the pullback of the divergence we have
\begin{equation}
A_{(\bm{\delta})} = \rho_{(0)}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} = 0.
\end{equation}

On the other hand, assume $\mathcal{A}$ is a Klein-Gordon solution whose Cauchy
data satisfies $\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = 0=\bm{\delta} A_{(n)}$ and $A_{(\bm{\delta})}=0$.
Let $f\in\Omega_0^{p-1} ({\bm M})$ and evaluate
\begin{eqnarray}
\langle \bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} , f \rangle_{\bm M} &=& \langle \mathcal{A}, {\bf d}  f \rangle_{\bm M}\nonumber\\
&=& -\langle A_{(0)}, \rho_{({\bf d})} E {\bf d} f \rangle_\Sigma
- \langle A_{(\bm{\delta})}, \rho_{(n)} E {\bf d} f \rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{({\bf d})}, \rho_{(0)} E {\bf d} f \rangle_\Sigma
+ \langle A_{(n)}, \rho_{(\bm{\delta})} E {\bf d} f \rangle_\Sigma\nonumber\\
&=& -\langle A_{(0)}, \rho_{({\bf d})} {\bf d}  E f \rangle_\Sigma
- \langle 0, \rho_{(n)} {\bf d}  E f \rangle_\Sigma
+\langle A_{({\bf d})}, \rho_{(0)} {\bf d} E f \rangle_\Sigma
+ \langle A_{(n)}, \rho_{(\bm{\delta})} {\bf d} E f \rangle_\Sigma,
\end{eqnarray}
where we have used the fact that ${\bf d}$ commutes with $E$ on forms of compact
support. The first term vanishes because $\rho_{({\bf d})}{\bf d} = 0$. The second
term trivially vanishes. For the third term we have that $\rho_{(0)}$ and
${\bf d}$ commute. For the fourth term we use $\rho_{(\bm{\delta})} = \rho_{(0)}\bm{\delta}$
and the fact that $E f$ is a solution to the homogeneous Klein-Gordon equation
to swap the order of the derivative operators. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\langle \bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} , f \rangle_{\bm M} =
\langle A_{({\bf d})}, {\bf d} \rho_{(0)} E f \rangle_\Sigma
-\langle  A_{(n)}, \rho_{(0)} {\bf d}\bm{\delta} E f \rangle_\Sigma\
=
\langle  \bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})}, \rho_{(0)}  E f \rangle_\Sigma
- \langle \bm{\delta} A_{(n)}, \rho_{(0)} \bm{\delta} E f \rangle_\Sigma
=0,
\end{equation}
where we have used the conditions on the initial data.
Since this is true for all $f$ we deduce that $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} = 0$.

\label{thm: gauge_soln}{\rm (Closed Solutions)}
Suppose $\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ solves $\Box\mathcal{A} = 0$ with Cauchy data
$(A_{(0)}, A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})})$; then $\mathcal{A}\in{\rm ker}\,
{\bf d}_p$ on ${\bm M}$ if and only if ${\bf d} A_{(0)} = 0$, $A_{({\bf d})}=0$
and ${\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})}=0$.
<!-- end prop-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{A}\in{\rm ker}\,{\bf d}_p$ be a Klein-Gordon solution, then $\mathcal{A}$ is
also a Maxwell solution.  Next, evaluate the three conditions;
\begin{eqnarray}
{\bf d} A_{(0)} &=& {\bf d}\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{A} = \rho_{(0)}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0,\nonumber\\
 A_{({\bf d})} &=& \rho_{(n)}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0,\nonumber\\
{\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})} &=& {\bf d}\rho_{(0)}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} = \rho_{(0)}{\bf d}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}
=-\rho_{(0)}\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0.
\end{eqnarray}

Alternatively, assume Cauchy data $(A_{(0)}, 0, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})})$
where ${\bf d} A_{(0)} = 0 ={\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})}$.  Then, there exists an unique
$\mathcal{A}$ that solves $\Box\mathcal{A} = 0$ with this Cauchy data.  By proposition~ 
\ref{thm_Maxwell_solns} above, it is also a Maxwell solution. Additionally,
let $g\in\Omega^{p+1}_0({\bm M})$ and evaluate
\begin{eqnarray}
\langle {\bf d} \mathcal{A} , g\rangle_{\bm M} &=& \langle \mathcal{A}, \bm{\delta} g \rangle_{\bm M} \nonumber\\
&=& -\langle A_{(0)},\rho_{({\bf d})}E\bm{\delta} g \rangle_\Sigma -\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})}
,\rho_{(n)} E \bm{\delta} g \rangle_\Sigma + \langle A_{(n)}, \rho_{(\bm{\delta})}
E\bm{\delta} g\rangle_\Sigma + \langle 0, \rho_{(0)} E\bm{\delta} g\rangle_\Sigma
\nonumber\\
&=& -\langle A_{(0)},\rho_{(n)}{\bf d}\bm{\delta} E g \rangle_\Sigma -
\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})},\rho_{(n)} \bm{\delta} E g \rangle_\Sigma + \langle
A_{(n)}, \rho_{(\bm{\delta})}\bm{\delta} E g\rangle_\Sigma\nonumber\\
&=&\langle A_{(0)}, \rho_{(n)}\bm{\delta}{\bf d} E g\rangle_\Sigma
-\langle A_{(\bm{\delta})},\rho_{(n)}\bm{\delta} E g\rangle_\Sigma\nonumber\\
&=& (-1)^{n(p+2)}\langle {\bf d} A_{(0)} , \rho_{(n)} {\bf d} E g\rangle_\Sigma-
(-1)^{n(p+1)}\langle {\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})}, \rho_{(n)} E g\rangle_\Sigma\nonumber\\
&=& 0,
\end{eqnarray}
which is true for all $g$, implying that ${\bf d} \mathcal{A} =0$.  Therefore,
$\mathcal{A}\in{\rm ker}\, {\bf d}_p$ on the spacetime ${\bm M}$.

Suppose $\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ solves $\Box\mathcal{A}=0$ with Cauchy data
$(A_{(0)}, A_{({\bf d})}, A_{(n)}, A_{(\bm{\delta})})$; then ${\bf d}\mathcal{A}=\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}=0$

<!-- end prop-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
First, supposed $\mathcal{A}$ satifies ${\bf d}\mathcal{A}=0$ and $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}=0$, then $\mathcal{A}$ trivially satisfies the
Klein-Gordon and Maxwell equations.  We evaluate
\begin{eqnarray}
{\bf d} A_{(0)} &=& {\bf d}\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{A} = \rho_{(0)}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0,\nonumber\\
A_{({\bf d})} &=& \rho_{(n)}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0,\nonumber\\
\bm{\delta} A_{(n)} &=& \bm{\delta}\rho_{(n)}\mathcal{A} = (-1)^{np}\rho_{(n)}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} = 0,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal{A}_{(\bm{\delta})} &=& \rho_{(0)}\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A} = 0.
\end{eqnarray}

with this Cauchy data.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:Lorenz_soln} above, it is also a
Lorenz solution with $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{A}=0$.  By Proposition~\ref{thm: gauge_soln},
$\mathcal{A}\in{\rm ker}\, {\bf d}_p$, i.e., ${\bf d}\mathcal{A}=0$.

<!-- prop-10: body_md -->
{\rm (Existence of Homogenous Maxwell Solutions)}
For any $( A_{(0)}, A_{({\bf d})} )\in\Omega^{p}_0(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p}_0(\Sigma)$
with $\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = 0$, there exists an $\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ such that
\begin{equation}
-\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0,\qquad\qquad\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{A} = A_{(0)}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad
\rho_{({\bf d})}\mathcal{A} = A_{({\bf d})}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end prop-10 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
This is a broader proof than that given by Proposition 2.10 of Pfenning (original version
of this manuscript), which was itself a generalization of the proof given for Dimock's

{\rm (Gauge Equivalence of Maxwell Solutions)}\label{prop: Trad gauge}\\
\indent(a) Let $( A_{(0)}, A_{({\bf d})} )\in\Omega^{p}_0(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p}_0(\Sigma)$
with $\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = 0$ specify reduced Cauchy data on $\Sigma$.  If $\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}'
\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ are Maxwell solutions with this data, then $\mathcal{A}\sim\mathcal{A}'$.

(b) Let $(A_{(0)},A_{({\bf d})})$ and $(A'_{(0)},A'_{({\bf d})})$ with $\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})}
= 0 = \bm{\delta} A'_{({\bf d})}$ specify Cauchy data on a common Cauchy surface $\Sigma$.
If $\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}' \in\Omega^p({\bm M})$ are Maxwell solutions with these Cauchy data, then
$\mathcal{A}'\sim\mathcal{A}$ if and only if $A'_{(0)}\sim A_{(0)}$, $A'_{({\bf d})}=A_{({\bf d})}$.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
(a) In terms of the complete Cauchy data on $\Sigma$, any Maxwell solution will have
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A} \mapsto (A_{(0)},A_{({\bf d})},A_{(n)},A_{(\bm{\delta})})
\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad
\mathcal{A}' \mapsto (A_{(0)},A_{({\bf d})},A'_{(n)},A'_{(\bm{\delta})})
\end{equation}
with the constraints $\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})}=0$ and ${\bf d} A_{(\bm{\delta})}=0={\bf d} A'_{(\bm{\delta})}$.

On the Cauchy surface $\Sigma$, we specify complete initial data $(0, 0, A'_{(n)}-A_{(n)},
A'_{(\bm{\delta})}-A_{(\bm{\delta})})\in\Omega^{p}_0(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p}_0(\Sigma)\oplus
\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:_Cauchy for Box}, there exists a unique smooth $\mathcal{B}\in\Omega^{p}
({\bm M})$ which is a solution to $\Box\mathcal{B}=0$ with this Cauchy data.  By
Propositions~\ref{thm_Maxwell_solns} and \ref{thm: gauge_soln}, $\mathcal{B}$ is a closed solution
to the Maxwell equation.

Define $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}= \mathcal{A} +\mathcal{B}$, which is also a Maxwell solution, and evaluate its Cauchy data;
\begin{eqnarray}
\rho_{(0)} \widetilde{\mathcal{A}} &=& \rho_{(0)}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}) = A_{(0)} + \rho_{(0)}\mathcal{B}
= A_{(0)},\nonumber\\
\rho_{({\bf d})} \widetilde{\mathcal{A}} &=& \rho_{({\bf d})}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}) = A_{({\bf d})} + \rho_{({\bf d})}
\mathcal{B} = A_{({\bf d})},\nonumber\\
\rho_{(n)} \widetilde{\mathcal{A}} &=& \rho_{(n)}(\mathcal{A} + \mathcal{B}) = A_{(n)} + \rho_{(n)}\mathcal{B}
= A_{(n)}+A'_{(n)}-A_{(n)} = A'_{(n)},\nonumber\\
\rho_{(\bm{\delta})} \widetilde{\mathcal{A}} &=& \rho_{(\bm{\delta})}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}) = A_{(\bm{\delta})} +
\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{B} = A_{(\bm{\delta})} + A'_{(\bm{\delta})}-A_{(\bm{\delta})} =
A'_{(\bm{\delta})}.
\end{eqnarray}
We immediately see that $\mathcal{A}'$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}$ are both Maxwell solutions with identical
complete Cauchy data, and by uniqueness of homogenous Klein-Gordon solutions $\mathcal{A}' = \widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
= \mathcal{A} +\mathcal{B}$. Setting the $p$-th deRham cohomology group for the spacetime, $H^p({\bm M})$, to be trivial,
we can then conclude that $\mathcal{B}$ is exact.  More specifically, there exists a $\lambda\in\Omega^{p-1}
({\bm M})$ such that $\mathcal{B}={\bf d}\lambda$.  Upon substitution, we have $\mathcal{A}' = \mathcal{A} +{\bf d}\lambda$, or
more simply, $\mathcal{A}'\sim\mathcal{A}$.

(b)Let $\lambda\in\Omega^{p-1}({\bm M})$ and set $\mathcal{A}' = \mathcal{A} + {\bf d}\lambda$;  then it
immediately follows $A'_{(0)}\sim A_{(0)}$ and $A'_{({\bf d})}=A_{({\bf d})}$.

Alternatively,  assume $A'_{(0)} = A_{(0)} +{\bf d} \alpha$ and $A'_{({\bf d})}=A_{({\bf d})}$,
where $\alpha\in\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)$.  By part (a) of this proposition, $\mathcal{A}\sim
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}$, where $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}$ is the Coulomb solution with complete Cauchy
data $(A_{(0)},A_{({\bf d})},0,0)$.  Likewise, $\mathcal{A}'\sim\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}'$ where $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}'$
is the Coulomb solution with complete Cauchy data $(A_{(0)}+{\bf d}\alpha,A_{({\bf d})},0,0)$.

Next, on the Cauchy surface $\Sigma$, specify initial data $(\alpha, 0,0,0)\in
\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)\oplus \Omega^{p-2}_0(\Sigma)
\oplus\Omega^{p-2}_0(\Sigma)$.  By Theorem~\ref{prop:_Cauchy for Box}, there
exists a unique smooth $\lambda$ which is a solution to $\Box\lambda=0$ with
this Cauchy data.  Furthermore, by Proposition~\ref{prop:Lorenz_soln} and the
text following the proposition, $\lambda$ is a Coulomb solution to the Maxwell
equation.

Define $\mathcal{B} = \widetilde{\mathcal{A}} + {\bf d}\lambda$, then
\begin{equation}
\Box\mathcal{B} = \Box\widetilde{\mathcal{A}} + \Box{\bf d}\lambda = {\bf d}\Box\lambda = 0
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\bm{\delta}\mathcal{B} = \bm{\delta}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}} +\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\lambda = 0.
\end{equation}
Evaluating the initial data for $\mathcal{B}$, we find;
\begin{eqnarray}
\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{B} &=& \rho_{(0)}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}} + {\bf d}\rho_{(0)}\lambda=A_{(0)}
+{\bf d}\alpha, \nonumber\\
\rho_{({\bf d})}\mathcal{B} &=& \rho_{({\bf d})}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}} + \rho_{({\bf d})}{\bf d}\lambda =
A_{({\bf d})}, \nonumber\\
\rho_{(n)}\mathcal{B} &=&  \rho_{(n)}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}} + \rho_{(n)}{\bf d}\lambda =
\rho_{({\bf d})}\lambda = 0,\nonumber\\
\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{B} &=&\rho_{(\bm{\delta})}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}} + \rho_{(\bm{\delta})}{\bf d}\lambda =
\rho_{(0)}\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\lambda = 0.
\end{eqnarray}
Thus, $\mathcal{B}$ is a Coulomb solution with complete Cauchy data identical to that
$\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}'$. By the uniqueness of solutions to the Klein-Gordon equation,
we have $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}' = \mathcal{B} = \widetilde{\mathcal{A}} + {\bf d}\lambda$, or more
simply $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}'}\sim\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}$.  Therefore, we conclude $\mathcal{A}'\sim\mathcal{A}$.

For $\mathcal{J}\in\Omega^p_0({\bm M})$, $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{J} = 0$ we have\\
(a) $\widehat{[{\mathcal{A}}]}(\mathcal{J})$ satisfies the generalized Maxwell equation in the weak sense,

(b) $\left[\widehat{[{\mathcal{A}}]}(\mathcal{J}),\widehat{[{\mathcal{A}}]}(\mathcal{J}')\right]=-i\langle \mathcal{J}, E\mathcal{J}'\rangle_{\bm M}$.

(a)  By definition we have $\widehat{[{\mathcal{A}}]}(\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{J}) =i\hat{a}(\overline{K 
[E\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{J}]}) - i\hat{a}^*(K[E\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{J}])$, so we will show
$[E\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{J}]=0$.  For any $\theta\in\Omega^p_0({\bm M})$, we have
$\Box E\theta = E\Box\theta = 0$, so $[E\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\theta] = -[E{\bf d}\bm{\delta}\theta]

$\widehat{[{\mathcal{A}}]}(\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{J}) = i\hat{a}(0)-i\hat{a}^*(0)=0$.  Notice, this part
of the proposition does not require the co-closed condition $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{J} = 0$.\\
(b)  Substituting the definition of the field operator into the commutator yields

[\widehat{[{\mathcal{A}}]}(\mathcal{J}),\widehat{[{\mathcal{A}}]}(\mathcal{J}')] &=&

\label{Prop:A_exist_to_give_F}
Let $F_{(0)}\in\Omega_0^p(\Sigma)$ and $F_{(n)}\in\Omega^{p-1}_0(\Sigma)$
with ${\bf d} F_{(0)}=0$ and $\bm{\delta} F_{(n)}=0$ specify Cauchy data for the
field strength $\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^p({\bm M})$, with $0<p\le n$, such that
\begin{equation}
\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{F} = F_{(0)}
\qquad\mbox{ and}\qquad
\rho_{(n)}\mathcal{F} = F_{(n)}.
\label{eq:Fcauchy}
\end{equation}
Given this data, there exists a smooth potential $\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^{p-1}({\bm M})$
such that $\mathcal{F} = {\bf d}\mathcal{A}$ satisfies the generalized Maxwell equations  ${\bf d}\mathcal{F}=0$
and $\bm{\delta}\mathcal{F}=0$, as well as the conditions \ref{eq:Fcauchy}.

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
We know that $\mathcal{F} = {\bf d}\mathcal{A}$ will satisfy the Maxwell equations if
$\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = 0$.  To show that such an $\mathcal{A}$ exists we choose as Cauchy
data:
\begin{equation}
{\bf d} A_{(0)} = F_{(0)},\qquad
A_{({\bf d})} = F_{(n)},\label{eq:F_Cauchy_Data}
\end{equation}
while $A_{(n)}$ and $A_{(\bm{\delta})}$ are arbitrary up to ${\bf d}\mathcal{A}_{(\bm{\delta})}=0$.

The first thing to address is the existence of $A_{(0)}$.  For
non-compact Cauchy surface $\Sigma$ we could restrict to only
those manifolds that are contractible. By the Poincar\'{e}
lemma for contractible manifolds (Theorem~6.4.18 of AMR
\cite{AMR88}) all closed $p$-forms (for $p>0$) are exact.
                                                          
                                                                   
Alternatively, we could require that the
compactly supported deRham cohomology group $H^p_c(\Sigma)$ for
$p$-forms on the Cauchy surface be of dimension zero, i.e.
$H^p_c(\Sigma)=\{[0]\}$. This is a restriction on the topology
of the Cauchy surface.  If we do have a trivial deRham
cohomology group then all closed $p$-forms $F_{(0)}$ are exact.
Either the contractible or cohomology condition is sufficient
to allow for the existence of a suitable $A_{(0)}$.

From the initial Cauchy data on $\mathcal{F}$
we have
\begin{equation}
\bm{\delta} A_{({\bf d})} = \bm{\delta} F_{(n)} = 0.
\end{equation}
Our Cauchy data for $\mathcal{A}$ has the properties necessary to use
Proposition~\ref{thm_Maxwell_solns}. So $\mathcal{A}$ is a
solution to $-\bm{\delta}{\bf d}\mathcal{A}=0$ and therefore $\mathcal{F}={\bf d}\mathcal{A}$ is a
solution to the generalized Maxwell equations.

Now we show this also reproduces the Cauchy data.  We evaluate
\begin{equation}
\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{F} = \rho_{(0)}{\bf d}\mathcal{A} = {\bf d}\rho_{(0)}\mathcal{A}
={\bf d} A_{(0)}= F_{(0)}.
\end{equation}
Next we evaluate
\begin{equation}
\rho_{(n)}\mathcal{F} = \rho_{(n)}{\bf d}\mathcal{A}
=\rho_{({\bf d})}\mathcal{A} = A_{({\bf d})} = F_{(n)}.
\end{equation}
The remaining two pullbacks are trivially zero since
\begin{equation}
\rho_{({\bf d})} \mathcal{F}=\rho_{({\bf d})} {\bf d} \mathcal{A} = 0
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\rho_{(\bm{\delta})} \mathcal{F}=\rho_{(\bm{\delta})} {\bf d} \mathcal{A} = \rho_{(0)} \bm{\delta}{\bf d} \mathcal{A}=0.
\end{equation}
