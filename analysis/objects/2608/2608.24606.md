Let $C\geq1$, $t\geq1$ and $\varepsilon_0\in(0,\tfrac14]$. We say that $(s,t)$ satisfies {\rm(M1)--(M3)} with constant $\varepsilon_0$ at scale $C$ if
$$\begin{gathered}
\text{(M1)}\qquad {\mathcal M}_{50C/t}\!\log w\,(s)\ \leq\ \varepsilon_0,\\
\text{(M2)}\qquad {\mathcal M}^2_{50C/t}G(s)\ \leq\ \varepsilon_0\,w(s),\\
\text{(M3)}\qquad M_{50C/t}\,\mu(s)\ \leq\ (1+\varepsilon_0)\,w(s),
\end{gathered}$$
and the same inequalities hold for the Dirichlet data $(\tilde w,\tilde G,\tilde\mu)$ with $\tilde w(s)$ in place of $w(s)$. We refer to the parameter $C$ as the {\it scale}: at time $t$ the natural resolution of the spectral data near $s$ is $1/t$, and all conditions of this section --- the truncations above and the boxes $Q(s,\cdot/t)$ below --- live at distances from $s$ that are bounded multiples of $C/t$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{propU3}
There are absolute constants $K_9$, $K_{11}$ such that the following holds on ${\mathcal{R}}(T,\eta_0,C,\delta)$ for $t\geq T$ and $c\in\{C,2C,4C,8C\}$, provided that $C\geq4\pi$ and $4\delta\leq\eta_0$. (Both conditions hold in all applications below: there $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)\geq4\pi$, with $C_1$ of Lemma \ref{lemPair}, and the thresholds imposed on $\delta$ are always taken smaller than $\eta_0/4$.)

1) {\rm(lattice rigidity)} If $t\notin T_0(s,c)$, then every zero of $E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,8C^\sharp/t)$ has depth at least $c-1$.

2) {\rm(resonance-free models)} If $t\notin T_0(s,c)$ and $v_{\min}\in[c-1,\infty]$ denotes the smallest depth of the zeros of $E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,8C^\sharp/t)$, then there is a unimodular constant $\alpha$ such that
$$\left|E(t,x)-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt w}\,e^{-itx}\right|\ \leq\ K_{11}\left(\delta+e^{-2v_{\min}}\right)\eta_0^{-1}
\qquad\text{ for all }x\in{\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,c/t).$$
In particular, if no zeros of $E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,8C^\sharp/t)$ have depth $\leq(C^\sharp-C)/2$, then (U3) holds at $(s,t)$ on the box $Q(s,c/t)$ with $\varepsilon=K_{11}\,\delta\,\eta_0^{-1}$. The same statements hold for $\tilde E$.

Throughout, (U2) is used at the scale $C^\sharp$, i.e.\ on the box $Q(s,16C^\sharp/t)$, with $\varepsilon=\delta$, as provided by \eqref{eqRtoU}.

1) Suppose $\zeta$ is a zero of $E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,8C^\sharp/t)$ of depth $v\leq c-1$, and put $\beta=A(t,\zeta)$ as in \eqref{eqDslot}. Then $\beta\neq0$: otherwise ${\mathcal D}(t,\zeta,\cdot)\equiv0$ by \eqref{eqDslot}, while (U2) forces $|{\mathcal D}(t,\zeta,z)|\geq|\sin[t(\bar\zeta-z)]|/w-\delta\geq1/(2w)$ at suitable $z$. By the conjugated slot identity (the display \eqref{eqMappE} below, whose derivation uses only (U2) and \eqref{eqDslot} and is valid on the present box), $|i\beta E(t,z)-\sin[t(z-\zeta)]/w|\leq\delta$ there. The function $\sin[t(z-\zeta)]$ vanishes exactly at the lattice points $z_k=\zeta+k\pi/t$, $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, all of depth $v$, and on the circle $|z-z_k|=r/t$, $r\leq1$, its modulus is at least $r/2$. Taking $r=4\delta w$ (so that $r\leq4\delta\eta_0^{-1}\leq1$) and applying Rouch\'e's theorem to $i\beta E$ against $\sin[\cdot]/w$ on that circle, $E(t,\cdot)$ has a zero in each disc $B(z_k,4\delta w/t)$ with $z_k\in Q(s,15C^\sharp/t)$ (so that the discs stay inside the box of (U2)). Since the lattice has step $\pi/t$ and $c\geq4\pi$, some $z_k$ lies in $Q(s,c/(2t))$, producing a zero of $E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,c/t)$ of depth at most $v+4\delta w\leq c$ --- contradicting $t\notin T_0(s,c)$. 

2) Let $I={\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,c/t)$. For real $\lambda=p$, $z=x$, \eqref{eq1004M} gives ${\mathcal D}(t,p,x)=\Im\left[E(t,x)\overline{E(t,p)}\right]$, so (U2) reads
\begin{equation}
\left|\,\Im\left[E(t,x)\overline{E(t,p)}\right]-\frac{\sin[t(p-x)]}{w}\,\right|\ \leq\ \delta,\qquad x,p\in I .
\label{eqRealPair}
\end{equation}
{\it Step 1: the winding of the phase.} We claim that there exist $x_0,x_1\in I$ with $E(t,x_0)\in(0,\infty)$ and $E(t,x_1)\in-i(0,\infty)$.

First, $E(t,\cdot)$ has no zeros on ${\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,8C^\sharp/t)$: at a real zero $x_*$ the real entire functions $A(t,\cdot)$ and $C(t,\cdot)$ would vanish simultaneously, making ${\mathcal D}(t,x_*,\cdot)\equiv0$ by \eqref{eq1004M}, while (U2) gives $|{\mathcal D}(t,x_*,z)|\geq\sinh(1)/w-\delta>0$ at $z=x_*-i/t$. We may therefore write $E(t,x)=|E(t,x)|\,e^{-i\varphi(x)}$ on ${\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,8C^\sharp/t)$ with a real-analytic phase $\varphi$. The phase is strictly increasing: for real $x$ in that interval, the function $g(z)={\mathcal D}(t,x,z)-\sin[t(x-z)]/w$ is analytic and bounded by $\delta$ on the disc $B(x,8C^\sharp/t)\subset Q(s,16C^\sharp/t)$ and vanishes at its center, so $|g'(x)|\leq\delta t/(8C^\sharp)$ by the Schwarz lemma; since ${\mathcal D}(t,x,x)=0$, \eqref{eqKD} gives $K(t,x,x)=-\partial_z{\mathcal D}(t,x,z)|_{z=x}/\pi$, and the standard identity $\pi K(t,x,x)=\varphi'(x)|E(t,x)|^2$ for the diagonal of the reproducing kernel (see \cite{dBr}) yields
\begin{equation}
\varphi'(x)\,|E(t,x)|^{2}\ =\ \frac tw\left(1\pm\frac{\delta w}{8C^\sharp}\right)\ >\ 0
\qquad\text{ on }{\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,8C^\sharp/t):
\label{eqPhaseVel}
\end{equation}
the phase velocity, weighted by $|E|^2$, is that of the free exponential.

Next we show that $\varphi$ advances by exactly $\pi$ on each period. Fix $p\in I$ and consider the entire function
$$z\ \mapsto\ {\mathcal D}(t,p,z)\ =\ \frac1{2i}\left[\,\overline{E(t,p)}\,E(t,z)-E(t,p)\,E^\#(t,z)\,\right],$$
which is \eqref{eq1004M} at real $\lambda=p$. Since $\overline{E(t,z)}=E^\#(t,\bar z)$, we have $\overline{{\mathcal D}(t,p,z)}={\mathcal D}(t,p,\bar z)$: the zero set of ${\mathcal D}(t,p,\cdot)$ is symmetric with respect to ${\mathbb R}$. On the real axis ${\mathcal D}(t,p,x)=|E(t,x)||E(t,p)|\sin[\varphi(p)-\varphi(x)]$, so the real zeros of ${\mathcal D}(t,p,\cdot)$ are exactly the points where $\varphi\equiv\varphi(p)$ modulo $\pi$. On the other hand, (U2) and Rouch\'e's theorem, applied as in part 1) to ${\mathcal D}(t,p,\cdot)$ against $\sin[t(p-z)]/w$ with $r=4\delta w\leq1$, show that ${\mathcal D}(t,p,\cdot)$ has exactly one zero, counted with multiplicity, in each disc $B(p+k\pi/t,\,4\delta w/t)$ whose center lies in $Q(s,15C^\sharp/t)$, and no real zeros in $Q(s,15C^\sharp/t)$ outside these discs: at a real point at distance at least $4\delta w/t$ from the lattice $p+\tfrac\pi t{\mathbb{Z}}$,
$$\left|{\mathcal D}(t,p,x)\right|\ \geq\ \frac{\left|\sin[t(p-x)]\right|}{w}-\delta\ \geq\ \frac{4\delta w}{2w}-\delta\ =\ \delta\ >\ 0 .$$
Each disc is symmetric with respect to ${\mathbb R}$ and the zero set of ${\mathcal D}(t,p,\cdot)$ is conjugation-invariant, so if the unique zero in a disc were not real it would come with its conjugate, giving two zeros in the disc; hence that zero is real and simple. Denote it by $\tau_k$:
$$\tau_k=p+\frac{k\pi}t\pm\frac{4\delta w}t,\qquad \tau_0=p,\qquad \varphi(\tau_k)\equiv\varphi(p)\ \ (\mathrm{mod}\ \pi).$$
The increments $\varphi(\tau_{k+1})-\varphi(\tau_k)$ are positive by \eqref{eqPhaseVel} and are integer multiples of $\pi$. If some increment were $m\pi$ with $m\geq2$, the intermediate values $\varphi(\tau_k)+\pi,\dots,\varphi(\tau_k)+(m-1)\pi$ would be attained, by \eqref{eqPhaseVel} and the intermediate value theorem, at $m-1$ points of the open interval $(\tau_k,\tau_{k+1})$ --- real zeros of ${\mathcal D}(t,p,\cdot)$ distinct from all the $\tau_j$, while the $\tau_j$ are the only zeros of ${\mathcal D}(t,p,\cdot)$ in the discs and there are no real zeros outside them. Hence
\begin{equation}
\varphi(\tau_{k+1})-\varphi(\tau_k)\ =\ \pi\qquad\text{ for all admissible }k:
\label{eqQuant}
\end{equation}
the phase advances by exactly $\pi$ on each step of the perturbed lattice $\{\tau_k\}$ --- $\arg E(t,\cdot)$ can neither lag behind the free exponential $e^{-itx}$ nor outrun it. Since $c\geq4\pi$, the interval $I$, of length $2c/t\geq8\pi/t$, contains at least five of the points $\tau_k$, so $\varphi$ increases by at least $4\pi>2\pi$ along $I$ by \eqref{eqQuant}, and $\arg E(t,\cdot)=-\varphi$ attains on $I$ every value modulo $2\pi$. Choose $x_0,x_1\in I$ with $E(t,x_0)\in(0,\infty)$ and $E(t,x_1)\in-i(0,\infty)$; then $C(t,x_0)=0$ and $A(t,x_1)=0$, proving the claim.

{\it Step 2: slot evaluation.} Evaluating (U2) at the real slots $\lambda=x_0$ and $\lambda=x_1$ (where ${\mathcal D}(t,x_0,z)=-A(t,x_0)C(t,z)$ and ${\mathcal D}(t,x_1,z)=C(t,x_1)A(t,z)$) gives, with $r_0=A(t,x_0)$, $r_1=C(t,x_1)$ and $x_2:=x_1-\pi/(2t)$,
$$C(t,z)=-\frac{\sin[t(x_0-z)]}{r_0\,w}\pm\frac{\delta}{r_0},\qquad
A(t,z)=\frac{\cos[t(z-x_2)]}{r_1\,w}\pm\frac{\delta}{r_1}$$
on $Q(s,16C^\sharp/t)$, while \eqref{eqRealPair} at $(x,p)=(x_0,x_1)$ gives $r_0r_1w=|\cos[t(x_0-x_2)]|\pm\delta w$. Hence
$$\begin{gathered}E(t,z)=A-iC=\frac{1}{r_1w}\left[\,\rho\cos[t(z-x_2)]\,+\,i\rho\,\frac{r_1}{r_0}\sin[t(x_0-z)]\,\right]_{\rho=1}\pm\\ \pm\ K\delta\,\frac{1+\rho}{r_0w},\end{gathered}$$
i.e., after normalization, $r_0wE(t,z)=\rho\cos(\theta-\alpha_1)-i\sin\theta\pm K\delta w(1+\rho)$ with $\theta=t(z-x_0)+\pi/2$-type real coordinates, $\rho=r_0/r_1$ and $\alpha_1=t(x_0-x_2)$ modulo $2\pi$. The bracket equals $\tfrac12e^{i\theta}\left(\rho e^{-i\alpha_1}-1\right)+\tfrac12e^{-i\theta}\left(\rho e^{i\alpha_1}+1\right)$, so with the mismatch $\varepsilon_m:=|\rho e^{-i\alpha_1}-1|$ it has zeros precisely on the lattice $\left\{\theta:\ e^{2i\theta}=-\frac{\rho e^{i\alpha_1}+1}{\rho e^{-i\alpha_1}-1}\right\}$, whose depth is $\frac12\log\frac{|\rho e^{i\alpha_1}+1|}{\varepsilon_m}=\frac12\log\frac{2\pm\varepsilon_m}{\varepsilon_m}$. If $\varepsilon_m\geq K(\delta w+e^{-2v_{\min}})$ with $K$ large, these zeros lie at depth smaller than $v_{\min}-1$. If that depth does not exceed $8C^\sharp-1$, the Rouch\'e transfer of part 1) (run against the two-exponential model, whose modulus on circles of radius $\varepsilon_m/4$ around its zeros exceeds $c\,\varepsilon_m$, above the error level $K\delta w$) forces zeros of $E(t,\cdot)$ of depth smaller than $v_{\min}$ in $Q(s,8C^\sharp/t)$ --- a contradiction with the definition of $v_{\min}$. If instead the depth $\tfrac12\log\frac{2+\varepsilon_m}{\varepsilon_m}$ exceeds $8C^\sharp-1$, then $\varepsilon_m\leq2e^2e^{-16C^\sharp}\leq K\delta^{16}$ by the choice of $K_9$, and the conclusion below holds directly. Hence $\varepsilon_m\leq K(\delta w+e^{-2v_{\min}})$, and then $\rho=1\pm K\varepsilon_m$, $r_0r_1w=1\pm K(\delta w+\varepsilon_m)$, $r_0=(1\pm K\varepsilon_m)/\sqrt w$, and the bracket is $e^{-i\theta}\pm K\varepsilon_m$: unwinding the normalizations, $E(t,x)=\alpha e^{-itx}/\sqrt w\pm K(\delta+e^{-2v_{\min}})\eta_0^{-1}$ on $I$ for a unimodular $\alpha$, which is 2). For the final claim, if no zeros of depth $\leq(C^\sharp-C)/2$ are present, then $e^{-2v_{\min}}\leq e^{-(C^\sharp-C)}\leq\delta$ by the choice of $K_9$. The Dirichlet family is treated identically.

\label{lemBeta}
Let $\zeta=x_0-iy_0$ be a zero of $E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,16C/t)$, $v=ty_0$ its depth, and put $\beta=A(t,\zeta)$. Then
$$A(t,\zeta)=iC(t,\zeta)=\beta,\qquad E^\#(t,\zeta)=2\beta,$$
and
\begin{equation}
\left|\,2|\beta|^2 w(s)-\sinh[2v]\,\right|\ \leq\ \delta\,w(s)\ \leq\ \delta\,\eta_0^{-1}.
\label{eqBetaAdd}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $v\geq\underline{v}_{\delta}$, then
\begin{equation}
2|\beta|^2 w(s)=\theta\,\sinh[2v]\qquad\text{ for some }\theta\in\left[1-\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1},\,1+\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1}\right].
\label{eqBetaNorm}
\end{equation}
The same holds for the Dirichlet family: if $\tilde\zeta_0$ is a zero of $\tilde E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,16C/t)$ of depth $\tilde v$ and $\tilde\beta=B(t,\tilde\zeta_0)$, then $|2|\tilde\beta|^2\tilde w(s)-\sinh[2\tilde v]|\leq\delta\eta_0^{-1}$, and $2|\tilde\beta|^2\tilde w(s)=\tilde\theta\sinh[2\tilde v]$, $\tilde\theta\in[1\pm\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1}]$, whenever $\tilde v\geq\underline{v}_{\delta}$.

Since $E=A-iC$ vanishes at $\zeta$, $A(\zeta)=iC(\zeta)=\beta$ and $E^\#(\zeta)=A(\zeta)+iC(\zeta)=\beta+\beta=2\beta$. Since $A,C$ are real entire, $\bar A(t,\zeta)=\overline{A(t,\zeta)}=\bar\beta$ and $\bar C(t,\zeta)=\overline{C(t,\zeta)}=i\bar\beta$, so by \eqref{eq1004M},
\begin{equation}
{\mathcal D}(t,\zeta,z)=A(t,z)\,\overline{C(t,\zeta)}-C(t,z)\,\overline{A(t,\zeta)}
=i\bar\beta\,E^\#(t,z).
\label{eqDslot}
\end{equation}
Evaluating \eqref{eqDslot} at $z=\zeta$ gives ${\mathcal D}(t,\zeta,\zeta)=i\bar\beta\cdot2\beta=2i|\beta|^2$, while $\sin[t(\bar\zeta-\zeta)]=\sin[2iv]=i\sinh[2v]$. Condition (U2) at $\lambda=z=\zeta\in Q(s,16C/t)$ therefore gives
$$\left|2i|\beta|^2-\frac{i\sinh[2v]}{w(s)}\right|\leq\delta,$$
which is \eqref{eqBetaAdd} after multiplying by $w(s)\leq\eta_0^{-1}$ (see \eqref{eqxy2}). If $v\geq\underline{v}_{\delta}$, then $\sinh[2v]\geq\delta^{1/2}$ by \eqref{eqLayer}, and the additive error $\delta w(s)$ is at most $\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1}\sinh[2v]$, which gives \eqref{eqBetaNorm}. The Dirichlet case is identical, with $\tilde E=B-iD$ and the second half of (U2).

\label{lemRep}
Let $\zeta$ be a zero of $E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,8C/t)$, let $v$ be its depth, and suppose $v\geq\underline{v}_{\delta}$. Then there is a unimodular constant $\alpha=\alpha(s,t)$ such that
\begin{equation}
\sup_{z\in Q(s,8C/t)}\left|E(t,z)-\alpha\,\frac{\gamma(v)}{\sqrt{w}}\,\sin[t(z-\zeta)]\right|\ \leq\ \vartheta\,\gamma(v),
\quad
\vartheta:=K_5\,\delta^{1/2}e^{16C}\eta_0^{-3/2},
\label{eqRepE}
\end{equation}
with an absolute constant $K_5$. The same holds for the Dirichlet family: if $\tilde\zeta_0$ is a zero of $\tilde E(t,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,8C/t)$ of depth $\tilde v\geq\underline{v}_{\delta}$, then, with $\tilde\zeta:=\tilde\zeta_0-\pi/(2t)$ and a unimodular $\tilde\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\sup_{z\in Q(s,8C/t)}\left|\tilde E(t,z)-\tilde\alpha\,\frac{\gamma(\tilde v)}{\sqrt{\tilde w}}\,\cos[t(z-\tilde\zeta)]\right|\ \leq\ \vartheta\,\gamma(\tilde v).
\label{eqRepEt}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By (U2) at $\lambda=\zeta$ and \eqref{eqDslot},
$$\sup_{z\in Q(s,16C/t)}\left|i\bar\beta E^\#(t,z)-\frac{\sin[t(\bar\zeta-z)]}{w}\right|\leq\delta .$$
Taking complex conjugates, replacing $\bar z$ by $z$ (the box is invariant under conjugation) and using $\overline{E^\#(t,\bar z)}=E(t,z)$ and $\overline{\sin[t(\bar\zeta-\bar z)]}=\sin[t(\zeta-z)]$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\sup_{z\in Q(s,16C/t)}\left|i\beta\,E(t,z)-\frac{\sin[t(z-\zeta)]}{w}\right|\leq\delta .
\label{eqMappE}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eqBetaNorm} (here $v\geq\underline{v}_{\delta}$ is used), $\frac1{|\beta|}=(1\pm\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1})\sqrt{\frac{2w}{\sinh[2v]}}=(1\pm\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1})\,\gamma(v)\sqrt w$. Dividing \eqref{eqMappE} by $i\beta$ and putting $\alpha=-i\bar\beta/|\beta|$, we get, on $Q(s,8C/t)$,
$$\left|E(t,z)-(1\pm\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1})\,\alpha\,\frac{\gamma(v)}{\sqrt w}\sin[t(z-\zeta)]\right|\leq(1+\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1})\,\delta\,\gamma(v)\sqrt w .$$
Since $|t\,\Im(z-\zeta)|\leq16C$ on $Q(s,8C/t)$, $\left|\sin[t(z-\zeta)]\right|\leq e^{16C}$, so replacing $(1\pm\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1})$ by $1$ changes the model by at most $\delta^{1/2}\eta_0^{-1}\,\gamma(v)e^{16C}/\sqrt w\leq\delta^{1/2}\,e^{16C}\eta_0^{-3/2}\gamma(v)$; together with $\sqrt w\leq\eta_0^{-1/2}$ from \eqref{eqxy2} this gives \eqref{eqRepE}. The Dirichlet case is identical, using $\cos[t(z-\tilde\zeta)]=\sin[t(z-\tilde\zeta_0)+\pi/2]\cdot(\pm1)$ with the sign absorbed into $\tilde\alpha$.

\label{lemChi}
Suppose that $E(t,\cdot)$ has no zeros in $Q(s,c/t)$ and $\tilde E(t,\cdot)$ has no zeros in $Q(s,c'/t)$, for some $c,c'\in\{C,2C,4C,8C\}$, and let $\alpha,\tilde\alpha$ be the unimodular constants of the exponential models of Proposition \ref{propU3}, 2). Then $\chi:=\alpha\bar{\tilde\alpha}$ satisfies
$$\left|\Im\chi-\sqrt{w\tilde w}\right|\ \leq\ K\,\epsilon_c\!\left(\min(c,c')\right)\eta_0^{-1/2},$$
with $\epsilon_c$ defined in \eqref{eqEpsU3} and an absolute constant $K$.

Note that $\epsilon_{\min(c,c')}\geq\max\left(\epsilon_c,\epsilon_{c'}\right)$; we may assume $\epsilon_{\min(c,c')}\eta_0^{-1/2}\leq1$, the claim being trivial otherwise. Insert the models of Proposition \ref{propU3}, 2) into \eqref{eqImE} at the point $x=s$:

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemPair}
There exist absolute constants $C_0\geq4\pi$, $K_6$ and $K_{12}$ with the following properties.

The constants $C_0$ and $K_{12}$ determine the least admissible scale: given $\eta_0\in(0,\tfrac12]$, we put
$$C_1(\eta_0)\ :=\ C_0+K_{12}\left(1+\log(1/\eta_0)\right)$$
and assume $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$; the constant $K_6$ appears in the estimates \eqref{eqDepthMatch} and \eqref{eqDeltaPin} below. For every such $C$ and $\eta_0$ there is a threshold $\delta_0(C,\eta_0)>0$ such that the following holds whenever $\delta\leq\delta_0(C,\eta_0)$; here and in the similar statements below the thresholds imposed on $\delta$ are always taken smaller than $\eta_0/4$, as required by Proposition \ref{propU3}.

Let $s\in{\mathcal{R}}(T,\eta_0,C,\delta)$ and $t\geq T$, and suppose that the pair $(s,t)$ is not $\delta$-shallow. Let $c$ be one of the values $C$, $2C$, $4C$, and suppose that $E(t,\cdot)$ has a zero $\zeta=x_0-iy_0$ in $Q(s,c/t)$; let $v=ty_0$ be its depth (since $(s,t)$ is not $\delta$-shallow, necessarily $v\geq\underline{v}_{\delta}$). Then:

1) $\tilde E(t,\cdot)$ has zeros in $Q(s,2c/t)$; let $\tilde\zeta_0$ be one nearest to $x_0$ and $\tilde v$ its depth. Then
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{\sinh[2\tilde v]}{\sinh[2v]}-1\right|\ \leq\ K_6\,\vartheta\,\eta_0^{-3},
\label{eqDepthMatch}
\end{equation}

2) with $\delta_p:=t\,(\Re\tilde\zeta-x_0)$, where $\tilde\zeta=\tilde\zeta_0-\pi/(2t)$,
\begin{equation}
\left|\cos^2\delta_p-w\tilde w\right|\ \leq\ K_6\,\vartheta\,\eta_0^{-3}.
\label{eqDeltaPin}
\end{equation}

The same holds with the roles of $E$ and $\tilde E$ exchanged.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose first that $\tilde E(t,\cdot)$ has a zero in $Q(s,8C/t)$, and let $\tilde\zeta_0$ be one nearest to $x_0$. By Lemma \ref{lemRep} for the Dirichlet family and Rouch\'e's theorem, the zeros of $\tilde E$ in $Q(s,8C/t)$ lie within $K\vartheta\eta_0^{-1/2}/t$ of the zero set of the cosine model (near each of its zeros the model has modulus at least $c_1\gamma(\tilde v)\,r/\sqrt{\tilde w}$ on the circle of radius $r/t$, which exceeds the error $\vartheta\gamma(\tilde v)$ once $r\geq K\vartheta\eta_0^{-1/2}$), and the model's consecutive zeros are spaced by $\pi/t$; hence $t|\Re\tilde\zeta_0-x_0|\leq\pi/2+1$ for $\delta\leq\delta_0$. That $\tilde\zeta_0$ in fact lies in $Q(s,2c/t)$ will follow a posteriori: its real part satisfies $|\Re\tilde\zeta_0-s|\leq(\pi/2+1)/t+c/t\leq2c/t$ since $c\geq C\geq4\pi$, and its depth is comparable to $v\leq c$ by \eqref{eqDepthMatch}, which is proved below using only $\tilde\zeta_0\in Q(s,8C/t)$.

By \eqref{eqDet2i} evaluated at $z=\zeta$ (where $E(\zeta)=0$) and at $z=\tilde\zeta_0$ (where $\tilde E(\tilde\zeta_0)=0$),
\begin{equation}
-\tilde E(t,\zeta)\,E^\#(t,\zeta)=2i,\qquad E(t,\tilde\zeta_0)\,\tilde E^\#(t,\tilde\zeta_0)=2i .
\label{eqWzeros}
\end{equation}
By Lemma \ref{lemBeta}, $|E^\#(t,\zeta)|=2|\beta|=(1\pm\delta)\sqrt{2\sinh[2v]/w}$ and $|\tilde E^\#(t,\tilde\zeta_0)|=(1\pm\delta)\sqrt{2\sinh[2\tilde v]/\tilde w}$, so \eqref{eqWzeros} gives
\begin{equation}
|\tilde E(t,\zeta)|=(1\pm2\delta)\,\gamma(v)\sqrt{w},\qquad
|E(t,\tilde\zeta_0)|=(1\pm2\delta)\,\gamma(\tilde v)\sqrt{\tilde w}.
\label{eqSizes}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, evaluating the representations of Lemma \ref{lemRep} of each family at the zero of the other family, with $\delta_p$ as in the statement and $\nu:=\tilde v-v$: since
$t(\zeta-\tilde\zeta)=-\delta_p+i\nu$ and $t(\tilde\zeta_0-\zeta)=\delta_p+\pi/2-i\nu$, and $\sin(\delta_p+\pi/2-i\nu)=\cos(\delta_p-i\nu)$, both evaluations produce the quantity $|\cos(\delta_p-i\nu)|$, $|\cos(\delta_p-i\nu)|^2=\cos^2\delta_p+\sinh^2\nu$:
$$\begin{gathered}|\tilde E(t,\zeta)|=\frac{\gamma(\tilde v)}{\sqrt{\tilde w}}\left|\cos(\delta_p-i\nu)\right|\pm\vartheta\gamma(\tilde v),\\
|E(t,\tilde\zeta_0)|=\frac{\gamma(v)}{\sqrt{w}}\left|\cos(\delta_p-i\nu)\right|\pm\vartheta\gamma(v).\end{gathered}$$
Comparing with \eqref{eqSizes} and writing $R:=\sinh[2\tilde v]/\sinh[2v]=\gamma^2(v)/\gamma^2(\tilde v)$, we obtain the two relations
$$\begin{gathered}\cos^2\delta_p+\sinh^2\nu=(1\pm4\delta)\,R\,w\tilde w\pm K\vartheta\eta_0^{-1}\sqrt R,\\
\cos^2\delta_p+\sinh^2\nu=(1\pm4\delta)\,\frac{w\tilde w}{R}\pm \frac{K\vartheta\eta_0^{-1}}{\sqrt R},\end{gathered}$$
where the additive terms come from the errors $\vartheta\gamma(\tilde v)$, $\vartheta\gamma(v)$ of the cross-evaluations, divided by the model amplitudes. We may assume $R\geq1$, exchanging the roles of the two families in the derivation otherwise. Comparing the two relations and dividing by $w\tilde w\geq\eta_0^2$,
$$(1-4\delta)R^2\ \leq\ (1+4\delta)+K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3}\left(R^{3/2}+\sqrt R\right),$$
which first shows $R\leq K$ (if $R\geq2$, the right-hand side is dominated by $K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3}R^{3/2}$, whence $R\leq K(\vartheta\eta_0^{-3})^2+K$, and $\delta_0$ makes this $\leq K$), and then, with $R$ bounded,
$$R^2=1\pm K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3},$$
hence \eqref{eqDepthMatch}; since $|\log R|\geq2|\nu|$, also $\sinh^2\nu\leq K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3}$, and then $\cos^2\delta_p=w\tilde w\pm K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3}$, which is \eqref{eqDeltaPin}.

It remains to exclude the case when $\tilde E$ has no zeros in $Q(s,8C/t)$. In that case Proposition \ref{propU3}, 2) with $c=8C$ provides the exponential model for $\tilde E$ on ${\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,8C/t)$, with the error $\epsilon_{8C}=K_{11}(\delta+e^{-2(8C-1)})\eta_0^{-1}$, while Lemma \ref{lemRep} provides the sine model for $E$. Insert both into \eqref{eqImE} on the interval ${\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,c/t)$: with $\theta=t(x-x_0)$,
$$\begin{gathered}
E(t,x)\overline{\tilde E(t,x)}=\frac{\alpha\bar{\tilde\alpha}\,\gamma(v)}{\sqrt{w\tilde w}}\,\sin(\theta+iv)\,e^{itx}+O\left(\vartheta\,\gamma(v)\,\eta_0^{-1/2}\right)\\
=\frac{\alpha\bar{\tilde\alpha}\,\gamma(v)}{2i\sqrt{w\tilde w}}\left[e^{i(2tx-tx_0)}e^{-v}-e^{itx_0}e^{v}\right]+O\left(\vartheta\,\gamma(v)\,\eta_0^{-1/2}\right).
\end{gathered}$$
The second term in the bracket does not depend on $x$, while the first is a harmonic of frequency $2t$ whose contribution to $\Im\left(E\bar{\tilde E}\right)$ has amplitude
$$\frac{\gamma(v)e^{-v}}{2\sqrt{w\tilde w}}\ \geq\ \frac{\gamma(v)\,e^{-4C}}{2}\ >\ 0$$
(recall $\sqrt{w\tilde w}\leq1$ and $v\leq4C$, so $e^{-v}\geq e^{-4C}$; adjust $c_2$ accordingly). As $x$ runs over ${\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,c/t)$, an interval of length at least $2C/t>8\pi/t$, the harmonic sweeps full periods, so $\Im\left(E\bar{\tilde E}\right)$ would fluctuate by at least $\gamma(v)e^{-4C}$, while it is identically $1$ and the error terms fluctuate by at most $K\left(\vartheta+\epsilon_{8C}\,e^{4C}\right)\gamma(v)\eta_0^{-1/2}$: both the main amplitude and the errors carry the factor $\gamma(v)$, so the comparison is uniform in the depth. For $\vartheta\leq c_2e^{-4C}\eta_0^{1/2}$ and $\epsilon_{8C}\leq c_2e^{-8C}\eta_0^{1/2}$ --- the first holds for $\delta\leq\delta_0(C,\eta_0)$ small enough, and the second for such $\delta$ together with $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$, which makes $e^{-16C+2}\eta_0^{-1}\leq e^{-8C}\eta_0^{1/2}$ once $K_{12}$ is chosen large enough --- this is a contradiction. Hence $\tilde E$ has zeros in $Q(s,4C/t)$, and the nearest one lies in $Q(s,2c/t)$ as shown above.

The exchanged version is proved by the same argument with the two families swapped.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lemSweep}
After enlarging the absolute constants $C_0$ and $K_{12}$ of Lemma \ref{lemPair} and decreasing the thresholds $\delta_0(C,\eta_0)$, if necessary, the following holds under the standing assumptions and the endpoint configurations described above:
\begin{equation}
\left\langle |b_{t\rightarrow2t}|^2\right\rangle_{J(s)}\ \geq\ \frac{\cosh[2\nu]}{2^8\,S},
\qquad
\sup_{J(s)}|b_{t\rightarrow2t}|^2\ \leq\ \frac{2^5\cosh[2\nu]\,e^{8C}}{\eta\,S},
\label{eqSweepMoments}
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdot\rangle_{J(s)}$ denotes the mean over $J(s)$, and in the free case $v_2=\infty$ the quantity $\cosh[2\nu]/S$ is understood as its limit $e^{-2v_1}/(2\sinh[2v_1])$.

Consequently, on a subset of $J(s)$ of measure at least $2^{-14}\,\eta_0^2\,e^{-8C}\,|J(s)|$,
\begin{equation}
\log|a_{t\rightarrow2t}|\ \geq\ \Phi(v_1,C):=\frac12\log\left(1+\frac{2^{-13}e^{-8C}}{v_1}\right).
\label{eqSweepLevel}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $p\in J(s)$, so that in \eqref{fin11} $E^\#=\bar E$ and $\tilde E^\#=\bar{\tilde E}$. Write
$$\theta_k=t_k(p-\Re\zeta_k),\quad S_k=\sin(\theta_k+iv_k),\quad C_k=\cos(\theta_k-\delta_k+iv_k),\quad k=1,2.$$
By Lemmas \ref{lemRep} and \ref{lemPair}, at each endpoint with presence,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
E(t_k,p)=\alpha_k\,\frac{\gamma(v_k)}{\sqrt{w}}\,S_k+O(\vartheta')\gamma(v_k),\\
\tilde E(t_k,p)=\tilde\alpha_k\,\frac{\gamma(v_k)}{\sqrt{\tilde w}}\,C_k+O(\vartheta')\gamma(v_k),
\end{gathered}
\label{eqEndpApp}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_k,\tilde\alpha_k$ are unimodular constants and
$$\vartheta'\ :=\ K_7\left(\vartheta\eta_0^{-3}\right)^{1/2}e^{4C}:$$
here the Dirichlet depth has been replaced by $v_k$ using \eqref{eqDepthMatch}, which perturbs the depth by $|\nu_k|\leq K(\vartheta\eta_0^{-3})^{1/2}$ and hence the factor $\gamma(\tilde v_k)\cos[\cdot+i\tilde v_k]$ by at most $K(\vartheta\eta_0^{-3})^{1/2}e^{4C}\gamma(v_k)$ (the derivatives of the model in the depth are bounded by $e^{4C}$ times its amplitude on the box), and the Dirichlet center has been written as $\Re\zeta_k+\delta_k/t_k$. If $t_2$ is in the free case, \eqref{eqEndpApp} holds at $t_2$ with $S_2$, $C_2$, $\gamma(v_2)$ replaced by $ie^{-i\theta_2}$, $e^{-i(\theta_2-\delta_2)}$, $1$, where $\theta_2=t_2(p-x_2)$ with an arbitrary reference point $x_2$ (absorbed into the unimodular constants), by Proposition \ref{propU3}, 2) and the definition of $\delta_2$; the error terms of \eqref{eqEndpApp} at the far endpoint are then $O\left(\vartheta'+\epsilon_{2C}\right)$ in place of $O(\vartheta')\gamma(v_2)$, and \eqref{eqRbound} below holds with $\vartheta'$ replaced by $\vartheta'+K\epsilon_{2C}$ and $\gamma(v_2)$ by $1$. Below the two cases are treated together, the free case corresponding to $v_2=\infty$.

Multiplying the relations \eqref{eqEndpApp} and inserting them into \eqref{fin11}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
2ie^{-i(t_2-t_1)p}\,b_{t\rightarrow2t}(p)=\frac{\gamma(v_1)\gamma(v_2)}{\sqrt{w\tilde w}}\,
\left[\chi_2' \,C_2S_1-\chi_1'\, S_2C_1\right]+R(p),
\label{eqModelB}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_2'=\alpha_1\tilde\alpha_2$ and $\chi_1'=\alpha_2\tilde\alpha_1$ are unimodular constants, on which no assumption is made, and $R(p)$, the sum of the products containing at least one error term of \eqref{eqEndpApp}, satisfies, since $|S_k|,|C_k|\leq\cosh v_k\leq e^{4C}$ on $J(s)$,
\begin{equation}
|R(p)|\ \leq\ K\,\vartheta'\,e^{4C}\eta_0^{-1/2}\,\gamma(v_1)\gamma(v_2),\qquad p\in J(s).
\label{eqRbound}
\end{equation}

Write $B(p):=\chi_2'C_2S_1-\chi_1'S_2C_1$ and expand the four factors into exponentials,
$$S_k=\frac i2\left[e^{v_k}e^{-i\theta_k}-e^{-v_k}e^{i\theta_k}\right],\qquad
C_k=\frac 12\left[e^{v_k}e^{-i(\theta_k-\delta_k)}+e^{-v_k}e^{i(\theta_k-\delta_k)}\right].$$
Then $B$ is a trigonometric polynomial in $p$ with the four frequencies $\mp(t_1+t_2)$, $\pm(t_1-t_2)$:
$$B=\frac i4\left[e^{v_1+v_2}b_1e^{-i(\theta_1+\theta_2)}-e^{v_2-v_1}b_2e^{i(\theta_1-\theta_2)}\right.$$
$$\left.+\,e^{v_1-v_2}b_3e^{-i(\theta_1-\theta_2)}-e^{-v_1-v_2}b_4e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)}\right],$$
$$\begin{gathered}b_1=\chi_2'e^{i\delta_2}-\chi_1'e^{i\delta_1},\qquad b_2=\chi_2'e^{i\delta_2}+\chi_1'e^{-i\delta_1},\\
b_3=\chi_2'e^{-i\delta_2}+\chi_1'e^{i\delta_1},\qquad b_4=\chi_2'e^{-i\delta_2}-\chi_1'e^{-i\delta_1}.\end{gathered}$$
The identities
$$b_2-b_1=2\chi_1'\cos\delta_1,\qquad b_3+b_1=2\chi_2'\cos\delta_2,$$
together with $|\cos\delta_k|=\sqrt\eta+O(\vartheta\eta_0^{-4})$ from \eqref{eqDeltaPin} (and $|\cos\delta_2|=\sqrt\eta+O(\epsilon_{2C}\eta_0^{-1/2})$ from Lemma \ref{lemChi} in the free case), give a dichotomy: either $|b_1|\geq\sqrt\eta/2$, or
$$\begin{gathered}|b_2|\ \geq\ 2|\cos\delta_1|-|b_1|\ \geq\ \sqrt\eta+O(\vartheta\eta_0^{-4}),\\
|b_3|\ \geq\ 2|\cos\delta_2|-|b_1|\ \geq\ \sqrt\eta+O(\vartheta\eta_0^{-4}).\end{gathered}$$
Since the coefficient of $B$ multiplying $b_1$ carries the factor $e^{v_1+v_2}\geq e^{|\nu|}$, and those multiplying $b_2$, $b_3$ carry $e^{\nu}$ and $e^{-\nu}$, in either case the largest of the four coefficients of $B$ is at least $\frac{\sqrt\eta}{8}\,e^{|\nu|}$ in absolute value, once $\delta\leq\delta_0(C,\eta_0)$ and $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$ make the $O(\vartheta\eta_0^{-4})$- and $O(\epsilon_{2C}\eta_0^{-1/2})$-terms smaller than $\sqrt\eta/4$; for the latter, $K_{11}e^{-4C+2}\eta_0^{-3/2}\leq\eta_0/4$ once $K_{12}$ is large enough, and the $\delta$-part of $\epsilon_c$ is absorbed into $\delta_0$.

The pairwise gaps of the four frequencies are $2t_1$, $2t_2$, $2(t_1+t_2)$ and $2(t_2-t_1)$, all at least $t_1=t_2/2$. Since $\left|\int_Ie^{i\omega p}\,dp\right|\leq2/\omega$ for every interval $I$, and $|J(s)|=2C/t$, the mean over $J(s)$ of each cross product of two distinct harmonics is at most $2/C$ times the product of the moduli of their coefficients; hence, with $c_1,\dots,c_4$ denoting the coefficients of $B$,
\begin{multline*}\frac1{|J(s)|}\int_{J(s)}|B|^2\,dp\ \geq\ \sum_j|c_j|^2-\frac 2C\Big(\sum_j|c_j|\Big)^2\ \geq\\ \geq\ \Big(1-\frac{8}C\Big)\max_j|c_j|^2\ \geq\ \frac{\eta}{2^7}\,e^{2|\nu|}\ \geq\ \frac{\eta}{2^7}\,\cosh[2\nu]\end{multline*}
for $C\geq16$; in the free case the terms containing $e^{-v_2}$ are absent and the same dichotomy applies to $b_1$, $b_2$ alone, with the same conclusion in the limiting normalization. For the supremum, each $|b_j|\leq2$, so
\begin{multline*}\sup_J|B|\ \leq\ \frac12\left(e^{v_1+v_2}+e^{\nu}+e^{-\nu}+e^{-v_1-v_2}\right)\ \leq\\ \leq\ 2\,e^{v_1+v_2}\ \leq\ 2\,e^{|\nu|}e^{2\min(v_1,v_2)}\ \leq\ 2\,e^{|\nu|}e^{4C},\end{multline*}
since $\min(v_1,v_2)\leq v_1\leq2C$; hence $\sup_J|B|^2\leq8\cosh[2\nu]\,e^{8C}$.

By \eqref{eqModelB} and \eqref{eqRbound}, since $\gamma^2(v_1)\gamma^2(v_2)=4/S$,
$$|b_{t\rightarrow2t}(p)|^2=\frac{\left|B(p)+O(\vartheta'e^{4C}\eta_0^{-1/2})\right|^2}{\eta\,S}.$$
For $\delta\leq\delta_0(C,\eta_0)$ small enough the error term changes the mean and the supremum of $|B|^2$ by relative amounts less than $\tfrac12$ (for the mean, use $\vartheta'e^{4C}\eta_0^{-1/2}\leq\sqrt\eta/2^5$). In the free case the error carries the factor $e^{v_1}$ while the guaranteed coefficient of $B$ may be as small as $\tfrac{\sqrt\eta}4e^{-v_1}$, so the required smallness reads $K\left(\vartheta'+\epsilon_{2C}\right)e^{2v_1}\eta_0^{-1/2}\leq\sqrt\eta/2^5$: since $v_1\leq C$ in the free case, the $\vartheta'$-part follows from $\delta\leq\delta_0$, and the $\epsilon_c$-part from $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$ --- $KK_{11}e^{-4C+2}\eta_0^{-3/2}e^{2C}\leq\eta_0/2^5$ once $K_{12}$ is large enough. Thus \eqref{eqSweepMoments} follows.

Finally, the subset of $J(s)$ where $|b_{t\rightarrow2t}|^2\geq\frac12\langle|b_{t\rightarrow2t}|^2\rangle$ has measure at least
$$\frac{\langle|b|^2\rangle/2}{\sup_J|b|^2}\,|J(s)|\ \geq\ \frac{\cosh[2\nu]/(2^9S)}{2^5\cosh[2\nu]e^{8C}/(\eta S)}\,|J(s)|\ \geq\ 2^{-14}\,\eta_0^2\,e^{-8C}\,|J(s)| ,$$
and on that subset, since
$$\sinh[2v_2]=\sinh[2v_1]\cosh[2\nu]+\cosh[2v_1]\sinh[2\nu]\leq\cosh[2\nu]\,e^{2v_1},$$
$\sinh[2v_1]\leq2v_1e^{2v_1}$ and $v_1\leq2C$,
$$|b_{t\rightarrow2t}|^2\ \geq\ \frac{\cosh[2\nu]}{2^9\,S}\ \geq\ \frac1{2^9\sinh[2v_1]e^{2v_1}}\ \geq\ \frac1{2^{10}v_1e^{4v_1}}\ \geq\ \frac{2^{-13}e^{-8C}}{v_1}$$
(in the free case $e^{-2v_1}/(2^{10}\sinh[2v_1])\geq e^{-8C}/(2^{11}v_1)$, with the same conclusion), whence
$\log|a_{t\rightarrow2t}|=\frac12\log(1+|b_{t\rightarrow2t}|^2)\geq\Phi(v_1,C)$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lemLip} Let $f\in L^1_{loc}({\mathbb R}_+)$ be real. Then for any $0\leq t_1<t_2$ and any $s\in{\mathbb R}$,
\begin{equation}\big|\log|a(t_2,s)|-\log|a(t_1,s)|\big|\leq \int_{t_1}^{t_2}|f(\tau)|\,d\tau.\label{eqLip}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let ${\mathcal E}(t,z)=e^{itz}E(t,z)$ and $\tilde{\mathcal E}(t,z)=e^{itz}\tilde E(t,z)$, so that $a=({\mathcal E}+i\tilde{\mathcal E})/2$ and $b=({\mathcal E}-i\tilde{\mathcal E})/2$ by \eqref{eqab}. By \eqref{eqME}, $\partial_t{\mathcal E}=f e^{itz}E^\#$. At a real spectral point $z=s$ we have $E^\#(t,s)=\overline{E(t,s)}$, and therefore $e^{ist}E^\#(t,s)=e^{2ist}\overline{{\mathcal E}(t,s)}$, so that
$$\partial_t{\mathcal E}(t,s)=f(t)e^{2ist}\overline{{\mathcal E}(t,s)},\qquad \partial_t\tilde{\mathcal E}(t,s)=f(t)e^{2ist}\overline{\tilde{\mathcal E}(t,s)}.$$
Hence
$$\partial_t a(t,s)=f(t)e^{2ist}\overline{b(t,s)},\qquad \partial_t b(t,s)=f(t)e^{2ist}\overline{a(t,s)}.$$
Since $|a|^2-|b|^2\equiv 1$ on ${\mathbb R}$, $|a|\geq\max(1,|b|)$, and therefore for a.e. $t$
$$\big|\partial_t \log|a(t,s)|\big|\leq \frac{|\partial_t a(t,s)|}{|a(t,s)|}= |f(t)|\,\frac{|b(t,s)|}{|a(t,s)|}\leq |f(t)|.$$
Integrating from $t_1$ to $t_2$ we obtain \eqref{eqLip}.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{remFloor}
The computation in the proof of Lemma \ref{lemLip}, applied to $E$ in place of $a$, gives $\partial_t\log|E(t,x)|=f(t)\cos[2\arg E(t,x)]$, whence, since $E(0,\cdot)\equiv1$,
$$e^{-\int_0^t|f|}\ \leq\ |E(t,x)|\ \leq\ e^{\int_0^t|f|}\qquad\text{ for all }x\in{\mathbb R},\ t>0 ;$$
we will refer to this bound, and to its localized form $\big|\log|E(t_2,x)|-\log|E(t_1,x)|\big|\leq\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|f|$, as the {\it value rigidity} of $E$.
Under \eqref{eqDMR}, $\int_0^t|f|\leq M'+M(1+\log_2t)$ for $t\geq1$. If $\zeta=x_0-iy_0$ is a zero of $E(t,\cdot)$ of depth $v=ty_0\leq1$, then, by the Phragm\'en--Lindel\"of principle and Bernstein's inequality applied at the heights $y\in[0,y_0]$, $|E(t,x_0)|=|E(t,x_0)-E(t,\zeta)|\leq y_0\,t\,e^{1+\int_0^t|f|}$, and comparison with the lower bound at $x_0$ gives the following lower bound for the depth, which requires none of the assumptions of Section \ref{secU}:
\begin{equation}
v\ \geq\ c\,e^{-2(M'+M)}\,t^{-2M/\ln 2},
\label{eqFloorCrude}
\end{equation}
it is valid at every point of ${\mathbb R}$ and for every zero. The decay in $t$ in \eqref{eqFloorCrude} cannot be removed in general: potentials of Wigner--von Neumann type, admissible under \eqref{eqDMR}, drive a zero towards their resonant frequency at a polynomial rate.

The bound \eqref{eqFloorCrude} enters the paper only through the threshold $T_\delta$ of \eqref{eqTdelta} below, which guarantees that no zero can be $\delta$-shallow before the time $T_\delta$; the same bound holds for the zeros of $\tilde E$, since $|\tilde E(0,\cdot)|\equiv1$ and the argument is identical. On the regular sets the dynamics of the zeros gives much more --- for $M<\frac{\ln2}2$ the depth of a resonance can only grow in the multiplicative sense while the representations of Section \ref{secRep} apply, and for every $M$ it can shrink by at most a bounded factor per octave --- but none of this is used below: the quantitative statement the paper needs, that the entry of a resonance into the shallow layer requires $L^2$ mass of $f$, is proved directly in Lemma \ref{lemShallow}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lemElev}
There exists an absolute constant $K_4$ --- the constant of the error term \eqref{eqOmega} --- with the following property. Fix $\eta_0\in(0,\tfrac12]$ and $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$, with $C_1$ as in Lemma \ref{lemPair}; then there is a threshold $\delta_1(C,\eta_0)>0$ such that the following holds for all $\delta\leq\delta_1(C,\eta_0)$.

Let $s\in{\mathcal{R}}(T,\eta_0,C,\delta)$ and $t\geq T$, and suppose that the pair $(s,t)$ is not $\delta$-shallow. Then the quantity $\omega=\omega(\delta,\eta_0,C)$ of \eqref{eqOmega} satisfies $\omega\leq1$, and:

1) if $E(t,\cdot)$ has no zeros in $Q(s,2C/t)$, then $|u(t,s)-A(s)|\leq\omega$;

2) if $E(t,\cdot)$ has zeros in $Q(s,2C/t)$ and $v\leq2C$ is the smallest of their depths, then
$$u(t,s)\ \leq\ A(s)+\frac12\log\coth v+\omega\ \leq\ A(s)+\frac12\log\left(1+\frac1v\right)+\omega ;$$

3) if, in the setting of part 2), $\tanh v\geq\eta_0$, then also
$$u(t,s)\ \geq\ A(s)+\frac12\log\tanh v-\omega .$$

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
1) By the exchanged version of Lemma \ref{lemPair} with $c=C$, $\tilde E(t,\cdot)$ has no zeros in $Q(s,C/t)$: a zero of $\tilde E$ there would produce a zero of $E$ in $Q(s,2C/t)$. Hence the exponential models of Proposition \ref{propU3}, 2) apply to both families, at $c=2C$ and $c=C$ respectively, and at the point $s$, with $\epsilon_C$ of \eqref{eqEpsU3},
$$\left|E(t,s)-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{w}}e^{-its}\right|\leq\epsilon_C,\qquad
\left|\tilde E(t,s)-\frac{\tilde\alpha}{\sqrt{\tilde w}}e^{-its}\right|\leq\epsilon_C .$$
Writing $x=w^{-1/2}$, $y=\tilde w^{-1/2}$ and using \eqref{eqAid},
$$e^{2A(s)}-\frac{\epsilon_C(x+y)}2\ \leq\ |a(t,s)|^2\ \leq\ e^{2A(s)}+\frac{\epsilon_C(x+y)}2+\frac{\epsilon_C^2}2 .$$
Since $e^{2A}=\frac14(x^2+y^2+2)\geq\frac18(x+y)^2$ and $x+y\geq2$ by \eqref{eqxy2}, the relative deviation is at most $8\epsilon_C$ (we may assume $\epsilon_C\leq1$: otherwise the claim is weaker than $\omega\leq1$), so $|u(t,s)-A(s)|\leq8\epsilon_C=8K_{11}\left(\delta+e^{-2(C-1)}\right)\eta_0^{-1}\leq\omega$ for $K_4\geq8e^2K_{11}$; the threshold $\delta_1$, together with $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$ after enlarging $K_{12}$ if needed, guarantees $\omega\leq1$.

2) Let $\zeta$ be a zero of the smallest depth $v$. By Lemma \ref{lemRep}, using $|\sin[t(s-\zeta)]|\leq\cosh v$ and $\gamma(v)\cosh v=\sqrt{\coth v}$, and $\gamma(v)\leq\sqrt{\coth v}$,
$$|E(t,s)|\ \leq\ \frac{\sqrt{\coth v}}{\sqrt{w}}\left(1+\vartheta\sqrt w\right).$$
By Lemma \ref{lemPair} with $c=2C$, $\tilde E$ has a matched zero of depth $\tilde v$ with $\sinh[2\tilde v]=(1\pm K_6\vartheta\eta_0^{-3})\sinh[2v]$; since the relative sensitivity of $\coth^2$ with respect to $\sinh[2\cdot]$ is at most $2$, $\coth\tilde v\leq(1+K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3})\coth v$, and the Dirichlet part of Lemma \ref{lemRep} gives
$$|\tilde E(t,s)|\ \leq\ \frac{\sqrt{\coth v}}{\sqrt{\tilde w}}\left(1+K\vartheta\eta_0^{-1}\right).$$
Inserting the two bounds into \eqref{eqAid}, using $\coth v\geq1$ and absorbing the cross terms by \eqref{eqxy2} as in part 1),
$$|a(t,s)|^2\ \leq\ \coth v\ e^{2A(s)}\left(1+K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3}\right),$$
and $\coth v\leq1+1/v$ gives the statement, with $\omega$ absorbing $K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3}\leq K K_5\delta^{1/2} e^{16C}\eta_0^{-9/2}$.

3) We use the bound $|\sin(\theta+iv)|\geq\sinh v$ at real $\theta$, $\gamma(v)\sinh v=\sqrt{\tanh v}$, $\gamma^2(v)\sinh v=1/\cosh v\leq1$, and the elementary inequality $(\max(0,p-q))^2\geq p^2-2pq$: by Lemma \ref{lemRep},
$$|E(t,s)|^2\ \geq\ \frac{\tanh v}{w}-2\vartheta\,\frac{1}{\sqrt w}\ \geq\ \frac{\tanh v}{w}-2\vartheta\,\eta_0^{-1/2},$$
and similarly for $\tilde E$ with $\tanh\tilde v\geq(1-K\vartheta\eta_0^{-3})\tanh v$. Hence, by \eqref{eqAid} and $\tanh v\leq1$,
$$|a(t,s)|^2\ \geq\ \tanh v\cdot e^{2A(s)}-K\vartheta\,\eta_0^{-3}.$$
Since $e^{2A}\geq1$ and $\tanh v\geq\eta_0$, the subtracted term is at most $K\vartheta\eta_0^{-4}\tanh v\,e^{2A}$, and
$$u(t,s)\ \geq\ A(s)+\frac12\log\tanh v+\frac12\log\left(1-K\vartheta\eta_0^{-4}\right)\ \geq\ A(s)+\frac12\log\tanh v-\omega$$
after enlarging $K_4$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lemDescent}
There is an absolute constant $K_6'$ with the following property. Let $s\in{\mathcal{R}}(T,\eta_0,C,\delta)$, let $T\leq t_1<t_2$, and let $\zeta(\tau)$ be a continuous family of zeros of $E(\tau,\cdot)$ lying in $Q(s,16C/\tau)$ for $\tau\in[t_1,t_2]$, with depths $v(\tau)\leq1$. Then
\begin{equation}
\sinh v(t_2)\ \geq\ \tfrac16\,e^{-2\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|f|}\,\sinh v(t_1)\ -\ 2\,\delta\,\eta_0^{-1} .
\label{eqDescent}
\end{equation}
The same holds for the Dirichlet family.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Write $x_0=\Re\zeta(t_2)$, $W={\mathbb R}\cap Q(s,16C/t_1)$, $\int=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|f|$, and let $\beta_i=\beta(t_i)$ be the normalizations of Lemma \ref{lemBeta} at $\zeta(t_i)$. By \eqref{eqMappE}, which holds at every depth, for $p\in W$ and $i=1,2$,
\begin{equation}
\Big|\,|\beta_i|\,|E(t_i,p)|-\frac{\left|\sin[t_i(p-\zeta(t_i))]\right|}{w}\,\Big|\ \leq\ \delta .
\label{eqPattern}
\end{equation}
Four applications of \eqref{eqPattern} and of the value rigidity $|\Delta\log|E(\tau,x)||\leq\int$ (Remark \ref{remFloor}) give the claim. First, at $p=x_0$ and $i=2$, since $|\sin[t_2(x_0-\zeta(t_2))]|=\sinh v(t_2)$,
$$|\beta_2|\,|E(t_2,x_0)|\ \leq\ \frac{\sinh v(t_2)+\delta w}{w}.$$
Second, at $p=x_0$ and $i=1$, since $|\sin[t_1(x_0-\zeta(t_1))]|\geq\sinh v(t_1)$,
$$|\beta_1|\,|E(t_1,x_0)|\ \geq\ \frac{\sinh v(t_1)-\delta w}{w}.$$
Third, taking $p=p^*$, the point of $W$ where $|\sin[t_2(p-\zeta(t_2))]|$ is maximal (that maximum lies in $[1,\cosh1]$ since $v(t_2)\leq1$ and $W$ is longer than the period), $|\beta_2|\sup_W|E(t_2,\cdot)|\geq|\beta_2||E(t_2,p^*)|\geq(1-\delta w)/w\geq1/(2w)$; and similarly, at $i=1$, $|\beta_1|\sup_W|E(t_1,\cdot)|\leq(\cosh 1+\delta w)/w\leq3/w$. Since the value rigidity gives $\sup_W|E(t_2,\cdot)|\leq e^{\int}\sup_W|E(t_1,\cdot)|$ pointwise on $W$, the last two bounds yield $|\beta_2|/|\beta_1|\geq\tfrac16e^{-\int}$. Finally, the rigidity at the fixed point $x_0$ gives $|E(t_2,x_0)|\geq e^{-\int}|E(t_1,x_0)|$, and chaining the four displays,
$$\frac{\sinh v(t_2)+\delta w}{w}\ \geq\ |\beta_2|\,e^{-\int}\,|E(t_1,x_0)|\ \geq\ \tfrac16\,e^{-2\int}\,\frac{\sinh v(t_1)-\delta w}{w},$$
which is \eqref{eqDescent} after $w\leq\eta_0^{-1}$.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lemShallow}
There exist absolute constants $K$ and $K_6''$ with the following property. Fix $\eta_0\in(0,\tfrac12]$, $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$ and $M'\geq0$; then there is a threshold $\delta_5(C,\eta_0,M')>0$ such that the following holds for all $\delta\leq\delta_5$.

\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\left|\left\{s\in{\mathcal{R}}(T,\eta_0,C,\delta):\ (s,t)\text{ is $\delta$-shallow for some }t\geq T_*\right\}\right|\ \leq\\
\leq\ K\left(\frac{M'}\rho+1\right)\frac{e^{8C}\,\eta_0^{-2}}{\log(1/\delta)}\ \|f\|^2_{L^2((T^\sharp,\,\infty))},\qquad T^\sharp:=\max(T,T_\delta)/32 .
\end{gathered}
\label{eqShallowSet}
\end{equation}
Moreover, if in the left-hand side the shallow zero is additionally required to have depth at most $v\leq\underline{v}_{\delta}$, the factor $1/\log(1/\delta)$ improves to $1/\Phi(v^\sharp,C)$ with $v^\sharp:=v\,e^{K_6''(M'+C+1)}$, provided $v^\sharp\leq C$.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $s$ belong to the set in \eqref{eqShallowSet} and let $t^\flat=t^\flat(s)\geq T_*$ be the first time at which $(s,t)$ is $\delta$-shallow; by the choice of $T_\delta$ and continuity, $t^\flat$ is well defined, and at $t^\flat$ some zero $\zeta^0$ of $E(t^\flat,\cdot)$ or of $\tilde E(t^\flat,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,16C/t^\flat)$ has depth exactly $\underline{v}_{\delta}$, while for $t<t^\flat$ no zero of either family in $Q(s,16C/t)$ has depth below $\underline{v}_{\delta}$. We treat the case of a zero of $E$; the Dirichlet case is identical --- the argument below uses only the pattern \eqref{eqPattern} and the value rigidity, both of which hold for the Dirichlet family, and its conclusion, a zero of $\tilde E(t_1,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,2C/t_1)$ of depth between $\underline{v}_{\delta}$ and $v^\sharp$, is converted into a zero of $E(t_1,\cdot)$ in $Q(s,4C/t_1)$ of comparable depth by the exchanged version of Lemma \ref{lemPair} (the pair $(s,t_1)$ is not $\delta$-shallow since $t_1<t^\flat$, and the depths match by \eqref{eqDepthMatch}); Proposition \ref{propFirstEntry} is then applied with $2v^\sharp$ in place of $v^\sharp$.

Let $t_1$ be the largest grid point with $t_1\leq t^\flat/16$; it exists, since $t_0=T/32\leq T_*/16\leq t^\flat/16$. We claim that $E(t_1,\cdot)$ has a zero in $Q(s,2C/t_1)$ of depth between $\underline{v}_{\delta}$ and $v^\sharp:=\underline{v}_{\delta} e^{K_6''(M'+C+1)}$. Indeed, write $x_0=\Re\zeta^0\in Q(s,16C/t^\flat)$; then $t_1|x_0-s|\leq(t_1/t^\flat)\cdot16C\leq C$. The interval $(t_1,t^\flat)$ spans at most five octaves, at most one of which may intersect $(0,1)$, so the mass $\int_{t_1}^{t^\flat}|f|$ is at most $5M+M'\leq K(M'+1)$. $$\begin{gathered}\left|\sin[t_1(x_0-\zeta')]\right|\ \leq\ w\,|\beta'|\,|E(t_1,x_0)|+\delta w\ \leq\\ \leq\ K\sqrt{\sinh[2v']+\delta\eta_0^{-1}}\cdot e^{K(M'+1)}\sqrt{(\underline{v}_{\delta}+\delta\eta_0^{-1})\,\eta_0^{-1}}+\delta\eta_0^{-1},\end{gathered}$$

Since $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$, the powers of $\eta_0^{-1}$ are absorbed into $e^{KC}$, and both the distance $t_1|x_0-\Re\zeta'|$ and the depth $v'$ are at most $\underline{v}_{\delta} e^{K_6''(M'+C+1)}=v^\sharp$; in particular $\zeta'\in Q(s,2C/t_1)$, and, since $t_1<t^\flat$, its depth is at least $\underline{v}_{\delta}$.

Summing \eqref{eqFirstEntry} over the grid points $t_j\geq T^\sharp$ and using \eqref{eqGridMass},
$$\left|\left\{\cdots\right\}\right|\ \leq\ \frac{2^{17}\pi e^{8C}}{\eta_0^{2}\,\Phi(v^\sharp,C)}\left(\frac{2M'}\rho+1\right)\|f\|^2_{L^2((T^\sharp,\infty))} .$$
Finally,
$$\Phi(v^\sharp,C)=\frac12\log\left(1+\frac{2^{-13}e^{-8C}}{v^\sharp}\right)\ \geq\ c\left(\log\frac1\delta-K(M'+C+1)\right)\ \geq\ c'\log\frac1\delta$$
for $\delta\leq\delta_5(C,\eta_0,M')$, which gives \eqref{eqShallowSet}; the graded version is the same estimate with the first-entry threshold $\underline{v}_{\delta}$ replaced by $v$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{propMaxPar}
Let $f\in L^2({\mathbb R}_+)$ be real and satisfy \eqref{eqDMR}. There exists an absolute constant $K$ with the following property. Fix $\eta_0\in(0,\tfrac12]$, $C\geq C_1(\eta_0)$ and $M'\geq0$; then there is a threshold $\delta_2(C,\eta_0,M')>0$ such that the following holds for all $\delta\leq\delta_2(C,\eta_0,M')$.

\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\left|\left\{s\in{\mathcal{R}}(T,\eta_0,C,\delta):\ \sup_{t\geq T}\big|\log|a(t,s)|-A(s)\big|>\lambda+\rho+2\omega\right\}\right|\ \leq\\
\leq\ K\,(M'+1)\,e^{16C}(C+1)\,\eta_0^{-2}\left[\frac{\|f\|^2_{L^2((T/32,\infty))}}{\rho\,\lambda}

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{remExtract}
The only non-quantitative ingredient of the arguments above is the rate at which the kernel-approximation error in (U2) tends to zero, which for a general $f\in L^2$ admits no modulus; this is the reason the estimates are localized to the regular sets ${\mathcal{R}}$. The restriction of Theorem \ref{thmMaxR} to the modulus $|a|$ is essential. \label{lemAMon}
1) If $F\in\text{\rm PW}_t$ has no zeros in ${\mathbb{C}}_+$, then $e^{ty}|F(x+iy)|$ is non-decreasing in $y\geq0$ for every fixed $x\in{\mathbb R}$.

\label{lemAUp}
There is an absolute constant $K_{16}$ with the following property. Let $\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac14]$, $\sigma\in{\mathbb R}$, and $\lambda=x_0+iy/t$ with $y\geq1$ and $|x_0-\sigma|\leq y/t$. Suppose that

\label{eqAupHyp}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
After a translation, $\sigma=0$; multiplying $G$ by a unimodular constant we may take $G(0)=\sqrt{w(0)}>0$. Let $F\in\text{\rm PW}_t$, $\|F\|_\mu\leq1$, maximize $|F(\lambda)|$, so that $K(t,\lambda,\lambda)=|F(\lambda)|^2$; by Lemma \ref{lemAMon}, $F$ has no zeros in ${\mathbb{C}}_+$ and $e^{ty'}|F(x+iy')|$ is non-decreasing in $y'$. Put
$${\mathcal S}(z)=\frac{\text{\rm Sinc}(t,\lambda,z)}{\sqrt{\text{\rm Sinc}(t,\lambda,\lambda)}},\qquad \|{\mathcal S}\|_{L^2({\mathbb R})}=1 .$$
Since $\mu\geq w\,dm$ as measures, $\|FG\|_{L^2({\mathbb R})}\leq\|F\|_\mu\leq1$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
\left|\int_{\mathbb R} F(x)G(x)\overline{{\mathcal S}(x)}\,dx\right|\ \leq\ 1 .
\label{eqAtest}
\end{equation}
For real $x$,
$$\overline{{\mathcal S}(x)}=\frac1{\sqrt{\text{\rm Sinc}(t,\lambda,\lambda)}}\cdot\frac{e^{it(x-\lambda)}-e^{-it(x-\lambda)}}{2\pi i(x-\lambda)},$$
so the integral in \eqref{eqAtest} equals $\text{\rm Sinc}(t,\lambda,\lambda)^{-1/2}(I-II)$ with
$$I=\frac{e^{-it\lambda}}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb R} F(x)G(x)\,\frac{e^{itx}}{x-\lambda}\,dx=F(\lambda)G(\lambda),$$
by Cauchy's formula, since $e^{itz}FG\in H^2\cdot N^+$ with square-summable boundary values, hence in $H^1$-Smirnov sense integrable, and $\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_+$; and
$$II=\frac{e^{it\lambda}}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb R} F(x)G(x)\,\frac{e^{-itx}}{x-\lambda}\,dx .$$
To estimate $II$, note that
$$\begin{gathered}III:=\frac{e^{it\lambda}}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb R} F(x)\overline{G(x)}\,\frac{e^{-itx}}{x-\lambda}\,dx=\\
=\overline{\ \frac{e^{-it\bar\lambda}}{-2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb R} \overline{F(x)}\,G(x)\,\frac{e^{itx}}{x-\bar\lambda}\,dx\ }=0,\end{gathered}$$
because $e^{itz}F^\#G$ is in the Smirnov class of ${\mathbb{C}}_+$ with square-summable boundary values and $\bar\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_-$. Hence, for any $D\geq1$,
$$\begin{gathered}|II|=|II-III|\leq
\underbrace{\left|\int_{|x|\leq Dy/t}\!\!FG_0'\,\frac{e^{-itx}\,dx}{2\pi(x-\lambda)}\right|}_{II_a}
+\underbrace{\left|\int_{|x|>Dy/t}\!\!FG_0'\,\frac{e^{-itx}\,dx}{2\pi(x-\lambda)}\right|}_{II_b},\\
G_0':=G-\overline G .\end{gathered}$$

$$\begin{gathered}II_a\leq\frac1\pi\,\big\|F\big|_{[-Dy/t,Dy/t]}\big\|_{L^2}\left(\int\frac{4|G-G(0)|^2}{|x-\lambda|^2}dx\right)^{1/2}
\leq\\ \leq\ K\,\big\|F\big|_{[-Dy/t,Dy/t]}\big\|_{L^2}\sqrt{\frac{t}{y}\,{\mathcal M}^2_{y/t}G(0)},\end{gathered}$$
where we used $|x-\lambda|^{-2}=\frac{\pi t}{y}P_\lambda(x)$ and $\lambda\in\Gamma_0$ at height $y/t$.

$$\begin{gathered}\log|G(x'+i/t)|=\tfrac12P[\log w](x'+i/t)\geq\\

$$\big\|F\big|_{[-Dy/t,Dy/t]}\big\|_{L^2}\ \leq\ \frac{K}{\sqrt{w(0)}}\,\exp\left[KD^2(1+y)\,{\mathcal M}_{(y+1)/t}\log w(0)\right] .$$

$$\begin{gathered}\text{\rm Sinc}^{-1/2}\,|F(\lambda)|\,|G(\lambda)|\ \leq\ 1+\text{\rm Sinc}^{-1/2}(II_a+II_b)
\ \leq\\ \leq\ 1+K\sqrt{\frac1{D\sinh2y}}+K\,e^{KD^2(1+y){\mathcal M}}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal M}^2_{y/t}G(0)}{w(0)\sinh2y}} .\end{gathered}$$
Choose $D=K'/\varepsilon^2$ with $K'$ absolute so large that the middle term is at most $\varepsilon$ (recall $y\geq1$). Then $KD^2(1+y){\mathcal M}_{(y+1)/t}\log w(0)\leq K K'^2\varepsilon^{-4}(1+y)\cdot\frac{\varepsilon^4}{K_{16}(1+y)}\leq\log2$ for $K_{16}$ large, and by \eqref{eqAupHyp} the last term is at most $2K\sqrt{\varepsilon^2/(K_{16}\sinh2)}\leq\varepsilon$ for $K_{16}$ large. Altogether $\sqrt{w(0)}|F(\lambda)|\leq\frac{1+2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\,\text{\rm Sinc}^{1/2}$, and squaring gives the claim.

\label{lemAL2}
There is an absolute constant $K_{18}$ such that, under {\rm(M1)--(M3)} with $\varepsilon_0\leq\varepsilon^4/\left(K_{18}(1+C)\right)$, for all $z\in\partial Q(s,C_t/t)$:
$$w(s)\,K(t,z,z)\ \leq\ (1+K\varepsilon)\,\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)\,t,$$
and
$$\left\|K(t,z,\cdot)-\frac1{w(s)}\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,\cdot)\right\|^2_\mu\ \leq\ \frac{K\left(\varepsilon+\sqrt{\varepsilon_0}\right)}{w(s)}\,\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)\,t .$$

{\it The norm bound.} By the symmetry $K(t,\bar z,\bar z)=K(t,z,z)$ it suffices to take $z\in\partial Q\cap\bar{\mathbb{C}}_+$. Such $z$ lies either on the top edge, where $z\in\bar\Gamma_s$ at height $C_t/t$, or on a lateral edge, where $z$ lies on the vertical segment over $\sigma_+$ or $\sigma_-$; in the latter case with $y=t\Im z\geq1$ the point is in $\bar\Gamma_{\sigma_\pm}$ and Lemma \ref{lemAUp} applies at the base point $\sigma_\pm$ (its hypotheses hold by Lemma \ref{lemAShift} and $\varepsilon_0\leq\varepsilon^4/(K_{18}(1+C))$, since $1+y\leq1+48C$); for $y<1$, if $F$ is the maximizer at $z_1=\Re z+i/t$, monotonicity (Lemma \ref{lemAMon}) gives $|F(z)|\leq e|F(z_1)|$ while $\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z)\geq c\,\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z_1,z_1)$, so the bound at height $1$ transfers with an absolute loss. On the lateral edges the density at the base point is exchanged for $w(s)$ with a multiplicative loss of at most $e^{|\log w(\sigma_\pm)-\log w(s)|}\leq1+K\varepsilon_0$ by Lemma \ref{lemAShift}. Finally $\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z)\leq\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)t$ on $\partial Q(s,C_t/t)$ since $\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iy,iy)$ increases in $y$.

{\it The $L^2(\mu)$-bound.} Since $\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,\cdot)\in\text{\rm PW}_t$ and $K$ reproduces on $\text{\rm PW}_t$,
$$\begin{gathered}\left\|K(t,z,\cdot)-\frac{\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,\cdot)}{w(s)}\right\|^2_\mu
=\\ = K(t,z,z)-\frac{2\,\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z)}{w(s)}+\frac1{w^2(s)}\int_{\mathbb R}|\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,x)|^2\,d\mu(x).\end{gathered}$$
We claim that
\begin{equation}
\int_{\mathbb R}|\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,\cdot)|^2\,d\mu\ \leq\ \left(1+K\sqrt{\varepsilon_0}\right)w(s)\,\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z) .
\label{eqAsharp}
\end{equation}
Write $\phi=|\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,\cdot)|^2/\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z)$, so $\int\phi\,dm=1$ and, by Lemma \ref{lemAPS} applied at the base point of $z$ (with $h=\max(t\Im z,1)$), $\phi\leq8P_{z'}$ for a point $z'$ lying in the truncated cone of $s$ or of $\sigma_\pm$. Decompose $d\mu=w(s)dm+(w-w(s))dm+d\mu_{\rm sing}$. The first part contributes exactly $w(s)$ to $\int\phi\,d\mu$. For the second, by Cauchy--Schwarz and $|w-w(s)|\leq|G-G(s)|\left(|G|+|G(s)|\right)$,
$$\begin{gathered}\int\phi\,|w-w(s)|\,dm\leq8\left(\int P_{z'}|G-G(s)|^2dm\right)^{1/2}\times\\
\times\left(\int P_{z'}\left(|G|+|G(s)|\right)^2dm\right)^{1/2}
\leq\ K\sqrt{\varepsilon_0\,w(s)}\cdot\sqrt{w(s)}\,,\end{gathered}$$
since $\int P_{z'}|G|^2dm\leq P\mu(z')\leq K w(s)$ and ${\mathcal M}^2$-quantities at the base points are $\leq K\varepsilon_0w(s)$ by Lemma \ref{lemAShift}. For the singular part,
$$\int\phi\,d\mu_{\rm sing}\leq8P\mu_{\rm sing}(z')=8\left(P\mu(z')-P[w\,dm](z')\right)\leq K\left(\varepsilon_0+\sqrt{\varepsilon_0}\right)w(s),$$
using (M3) transported by Lemma \ref{lemAShift} together with the bound on $\int P_{z'}|w-w(s)|\,dm$ just obtained. Summing the three parts and multiplying back by $\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z)$ gives \eqref{eqAsharp}. Inserting \eqref{eqAsharp} and the norm bound into the expansion,
$$\begin{gathered}\left\|K-\frac{\text{\rm Sinc}}{w(s)}\right\|^2_\mu\leq\left[K(t,z,z)-\frac{\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z)}{w(s)}\right]+\frac{K\sqrt{\varepsilon_0}}{w(s)}\text{\rm Sinc}(t,z,z)
\leq\\ \leq\frac{K(\varepsilon+\sqrt{\varepsilon_0})}{w(s)}\,\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)\,t .\end{gathered}\qedhere$$

Let $\varepsilon_1\in(0,\tfrac14]$ be an auxiliary accuracy, to be chosen at the end, and assume (M1)--(M3) with $\varepsilon_0\leq\varepsilon_1^4/(K_{18}(1+C))$; note $\sqrt{\varepsilon_0}\leq\varepsilon_1$. For $\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}$ put
$$\Delta_\lambda(z)=K(t,\lambda,z)-\frac1{w(s)}\text{\rm Sinc}(t,\lambda,z)\ \in\ \text{\rm PW}_t .$$

{\it Step 1: uniform bound on the box $Q(s,C_t/t)$ for boundary $\lambda$.} Let $\lambda\in\partial Q(s,C_t/t)$. The function $F_\lambda=e^{itz}G\,\Delta_\lambda$ lies in the Smirnov class of ${\mathbb{C}}_+$ with
$$\int_{\mathbb R}|F_\lambda|^2dm=\int_{\mathbb R} w\,|\Delta_\lambda|^2dm\leq\|\Delta_\lambda\|^2_\mu\leq\frac{K\varepsilon_1}{w(s)}\,\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)\,t$$
by Lemma \ref{lemAL2}, hence $F_\lambda\in H^2({\mathbb{C}}_+)$ with that norm bound. On the line $\Im z=C_t/t$, $|F_\lambda(z)|^2\leq\frac{t}{\pi C_t}\|F_\lambda\|^2_{H^2}$, $|e^{itz}|=e^{-C_t}$, and on the top edge of $Q(s,C_t/t)$ the outer function satisfies $|G|^2\geq(1-K\varepsilon_1)w(s)$ (by (M1) at the cone point, as in Lemma \ref{lemAUp}). Hence, on the top edge,
$$|\Delta_\lambda(z)|^2\ \leq\ \frac{K\varepsilon_1\,e^{2C_t}}{w^2(s)}\cdot\frac{\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)}{C_t}\,t^2 .$$
Let $\Delta_\lambda'$ be the reflection of $\Delta_\lambda$ provided by Lemma \ref{lemAMon} 2). Since $|\Delta_\lambda'|=|\Delta_\lambda|$ on ${\mathbb R}$, the function $e^{itz}G\Delta_\lambda'$ is again in $H^2$ with the same norm, so the top-edge bound holds for $\Delta_\lambda'$ as well; by Lemma \ref{lemAMon} 1), $e^{ty'}|\Delta_\lambda'(x+iy')|$ is non-decreasing in $y'$, so for every $z$ in the closed upper half of $Q(s,C_t/t)$,
$$|\Delta_\lambda(z)|\leq|\Delta_\lambda'(z)|\leq e^{C_t}\max_{\text{top edge}}|\Delta_\lambda'|\ \leq\ \frac{K\sqrt{\varepsilon_1}\,e^{2C_t}}{w(s)}\sqrt{\frac{\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)}{C_t}}\ t .$$
The function $\Delta_\lambda^\#(z)=\overline{\Delta_\lambda(\bar z)}$ has the same modulus on ${\mathbb R}$ and the argument applied to it bounds $|\Delta_\lambda|$ on the lower half of the box. Since $\text{\rm Sinc}(1,iC_t,iC_t)\leq e^{2C_t}$ and $C_t\leq48C$,
\begin{equation}
\sup_{z\in Q(s,C_t/t)}|\Delta_\lambda(z)|\ \leq\ \frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_1}\ e^{K_{19}C}}{w(s)}\ t,\qquad K_{19}\ \text{absolute}.
\label{eqAunif}
\end{equation}

{\it Step 2: interior $\lambda$.} Let now $\lambda\in Q(s,C'/t)$ and $z\in\partial Q(s,C_t/t)$. By the Hermitian symmetry of the kernels, $|\Delta_\lambda(z)|=|\Delta_z(\lambda)|$, and \eqref{eqAunif} applies to $\Delta_z$ at the interior point $\lambda$. Thus \eqref{eqAunif} holds for $|\Delta_\lambda|$ on $\partial Q(s,C_t/t)$, and since $\Delta_\lambda$ is entire in $z$, the Cauchy integral over $\partial Q(s,C_t/t)$ (contour length $\leq KC/t$, distance to $Q(s,C'/t)$ at least $C'/t$) extends it, with an absolute constant, to all $z\in Q(s,C'/t)$.

{\it Step 3: conclusion.} For $\lambda,z\in Q(s,C'/t)=Q(s,16C/t)$, $|\bar\lambda-z|\leq64C/t$, so
$$\left|{\mathcal D}(t,\lambda,z)-\frac{\sin[t(\bar\lambda-z)]}{w(s)}\right|=\pi|\bar\lambda-z|\,|\Delta_\lambda(z)|\leq\frac{K\,C\sqrt{\varepsilon_1}\,e^{K_{19}C}}{w(s)}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_1}\,e^{K_{20}C}}{w(s)} .$$
Given the target accuracy $\varepsilon$ of (U2), choose $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon^2w^2(s)e^{-2K_{20}C}$ (and at most $\tfrac14$); then the right-hand side is at most $\varepsilon$, and the hypothesis used above, $\varepsilon_0\leq\varepsilon_1^4/(K_{18}(1+C))$, is provided by \eqref{eqAthr} with $K_8$ large enough (the powers of $w(s)$ are absorbed by the factor $\min(w,\tilde w,1)^{8}$ in \eqref{eqAthr}; we do not optimize the exponents). This proves (U2), and the same argument applies to the Dirichlet family, completing the proof.
