<!-- thm--1: body_md -->
(Theorem \ref{THM:CYBE})
A skew-symmetric $2$-tensor $r\in \otimes^2\mathfrak g$ is a solution to the classical Lie-Yamaguti Yang-Baxter equation if and only if the induced map $r^\sharp:\mathfrak g^*\longrightarrow\mathfrak g$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the coadjoint representation, where $\left\langle r^\sharp(\xi),\eta\right\rangle=\left\langle r,\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle$, for all $\xi,\eta\in \mathfrak g^*$.
<!-- end thm--1 -->
<!-- thm--2: body_md -->
{\rm(Theorem ~\ref{main})}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and $\delta:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^2\mathfrak g$ and $\omega:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^3\mathfrak g$ linear maps. Suppose that a pair of structure maps $(\delta^*,\omega^*)$ defines a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~structure on $\mathfrak g^*$. Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*)$ is a double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra;
\item[(2)] the quadruple $\Big(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*;(\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau)\Big)$ is a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s, where $(\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau)$ and $(\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau)$ are the coadjoint representations of $\mathfrak g$ and $\mathfrak g^*$ on $\mathfrak g^*$ and $\mathfrak g$ respectively;
\item[(3)] the triple $\Big(\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*,\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*\Big)$ is a Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s.
\end{itemize}

\cite{Weinstein}\label{LY}
A {\bf Lie-Yamaguti algebra} is a vector space $\mathfrak g$ together with a bilinear bracket $[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^2  \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g} $ and a trilinear bracket $\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket :\wedge^2\mathfrak g \otimes  \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g} $, such that the following conditions hold
\begin{eqnarray*}
~ &&\label{LY1}[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]+\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  y,z,x\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  z,x,y\right\rrbracket =0,\\
~ &&\left\llbracket  [x,y],z,w\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  [y,z],x,w\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  [z,x],y,w\right\rrbracket =0,\\
~ &&\label{LY3}\left\llbracket  x,y,[z,w]\right\rrbracket =[\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket ,w]+[z,\left\llbracket  x,y,w\right\rrbracket ],\\
~ &&\left\llbracket  x,y,\left\llbracket  z,w,t\right\rrbracket \right\rrbracket =\left\llbracket  \left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket ,w,t\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  z,\left\llbracket  x,y,w\right\rrbracket ,t\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  z,w,\left\llbracket  x,y,t\right\rrbracket \right\rrbracket ,\label{fundamental}
\end{eqnarray*}
for all $x,y,z,w,t \in \mathfrak g$. We denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra by $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$.

\label{defi:representation}
Let $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A {\bf representation} of $\mathfrak g$ is a vector space $V$ endowed with a linear map $\rho:\mathfrak g \to \mathfrak {gl}(V)$ and a bilinear map $\mu:\otimes^2 \mathfrak g \to \mathfrak {gl}(V)$, which satisfies the following conditions for all $x,y,z,w \in \mathfrak g$,
\begin{eqnarray*}
~&&\label{RLYb}\mu([x,y],z)-\mu(x,z)\rho(y)+\mu(y,z)\rho(x)=0,\\
~&&\label{RLYd}\mu(x,[y,z])-\rho(y)\mu(x,z)+\rho(z)\mu(x,y)=0,\\
~&&\label{RLYe}\rho(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket )=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\rho(z)],\\
~&&\label{RYT4}\mu(z,w)\mu(x,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(x,z)-\mu(x,\left\llbracket  y,z,w\right\rrbracket )+D_{\rho,\mu}(y,z)\mu(x,w)=0,\\
~&&\label{RLY5}\mu(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket ,w)+\mu(z,\left\llbracket  x,y,w\right\rrbracket )=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\mu(z,w)],
\end{eqnarray*}
where $D_{\rho,\mu}$ is given by
\begin{eqnarray}
~ &&\label{rep} D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y)=\mu(y,x)-\mu(x,y)+[\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y]),\quad \forall x,y\in \mathfrak g.
\end{eqnarray}
It is easy to see that $D_{\rho,\mu}$ is skew-symmetric. We denote a representation of $\mathfrak g$ by $(V;\rho,\mu)$. In the sequel, we write $D_{\rho,\mu}$ as $D$ for short without confusion.

\label{rmk:rep}
Let $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and $(V;\rho,\mu)$ its representation. If $\rho=0$ and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra $\mathfrak g$ reduces to a Lie tripe system $(\mathfrak g,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$,  then $(V;\mu)$  is a representation of the Lie triple system $(\mathfrak g,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$; If $\mu=0$, $D_{\rho,\mu}=0$ and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra $\mathfrak g$ reduces to a Lie algebra $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot])$, then $(V;\rho)$ is a representation  of the Lie algebra $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot])$. So the notion of representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s is also a natural generalization of that of Lie algebras and Lie triple systems.

\label{ad}
Let $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. We define linear maps $\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak g \to \mathfrak {gl}(\mathfrak g)$ and $\mathcal{R} :\otimes^2\mathfrak g \to \mathfrak {gl}(\mathfrak g)$ to be $x \mapsto \mathrm{ad}_x$ and $(x,y) \mapsto \mathcal{R}(x,y)$ respectively, where $\mathrm{ad}_xz=[x,z]$ and $\mathcal{R}(x,y)z=\left\llbracket  z,x,y\right\rrbracket $ for all $z \in \mathfrak g$. Then $(\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})$ forms a representation of $\mathfrak g$ on itself, where $\mathcal{L}:= D_{\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R}}$ is given by
$$\mathcal{L}(x,y)z=\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket , \quad \forall z\in \mathfrak g.$$
The representation $(\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})$ is called the {\bf adjoint representation}. If $(\mathfrak g^*,[\cdot,\cdot]_*,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _*)$ is also a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, then the adjoint representation is denoted by $(\mathfrak g^*;\mathfrak{ad},\mathfrak R)$ in this paper, where 
$\mathfrak L:=D_{\mathfrak{ad},\mathfrak R}.$

\label{dual}{\rm (\cite{SZ1})}
Let $(V;\rho,\mu)$ be a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$. Then
\begin{eqnarray*}
\big(V^*;\rho^*,-\mu^*\tau\big)
\end{eqnarray*}

Let $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and $(\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})$ its adjoint representation, where $\mathrm{ad}, \mathcal{R}$ are given in Example \ref{ad}.  If $(\mathfrak g^*,[\cdot,\cdot]_*,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _*)$ is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and $(\mathfrak g^*,\mathfrak{ad},\mathfrak R)$ is its adjoint representation, then the coadjoint representation of $(\mathfrak g^*,\mathfrak{ad},\mathfrak R)$ is $(\mathfrak g;\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau)$, where $\mathfrak L^*=D_{\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau}$.

\cite{Zhang1}
Let $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and $V$ a vector space. Suppose that $\rho:\mathfrak g \to \mathfrak {gl}(V)$ and $\mu:\otimes^2 \mathfrak g \to \mathfrak {gl}(V)$ are linear maps. Then $(V;\rho,\mu)$ is a representation of $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ if and only if there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure $([\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes},\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _{\ltimes})$ on the direct sum $\mathfrak g \oplus V$ which is defined to be
\begin{eqnarray*}
\label{semi1}[x+u,y+v]_{\ltimes}&=&[x,y]+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u,\\
\label{semi2}~\left\llbracket  x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket _{\ltimes}&=&\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket +D(x,y)w+\mu(y,z)u-\mu(x,z)v,
\end{eqnarray*}
for all $x,y,z \in \mathfrak g, ~u,v,w \in V$. This Lie-Yamaguti algebra $(\mathfrak g \oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes},\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _{\ltimes})$ is called the {\bf semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra}, and is denoted by $\mathfrak g \ltimes_{\rho,\mu} V$.

Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $(V;\rho,\mu)$ a representation of $\mathfrak g$. A linear map $f:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow V$ is called a {\bf $1$-cocycle} of $\mathfrak g$ with respect to $(V;\rho,\mu)$ if $f$ satisfies
\begin{eqnarray*}
f([x,y])&=&\rho(x)f(y)-\rho(y)f(x),\\
f(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket )&=&D(x,y)f(z)+\mu(y,z)f(x)-\mu(x,z)f(y),\quad\forall x,y,z\in \mathfrak g.
\end{eqnarray*}

A {\bf derivation} on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ is a linear map $\Delta:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\mathfrak g$ such that
 \begin{eqnarray*}
 \Delta([x,y])&=&[\Delta(x),y]+[x,\Delta(y)],\\
 \Delta(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket )&=&\left\llbracket  \Delta(x),y,z\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  x,\Delta(y),z\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  x,y,\Delta(z)\right\rrbracket ,\quad\forall x,y,z \in \mathfrak g.
 \end{eqnarray*}
Thus a derivation is a $1$-cocycle of $\mathfrak g$ with respect to the adjoint representation $(\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})$.

{\rm (\cite{SZ1})}
Let $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with a representation $(V;\rho,\mu)$ and $T:V\to \mathfrak g$ a linear map. If $T$ satisfies
\begin{eqnarray*}
[Tu,Tv]&=&T\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\Big),\\
\left\llbracket  Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket &=&T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\Big), \quad\forall u,v,w \in V,
\end{eqnarray*}
then we call $T$ a {\bf relative Rota-Baxter operator} on $\mathfrak g$ with respect to the representation $(V;\rho,\mu)$.

{\rm (\cite{SZ1})}
A {\bf pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra} is a vector space $A$ with a bilinear operation $*:\otimes^2A \to A$ and a trilinear operation $\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\} :\otimes^3A \to A$ such that for all $x,y,z,w,t \in A$
\begin{eqnarray}
~ &&\label{pre2}\{z,[x,y]_C,w\}-\{y*z,x,w\}+\{x*z,y,w\}=0,\\
~ &&\label{pre4}\{x,y,[z,w]_C\}=z*\{x,y,w\}-w*\{x,y,z\},\\
~ &&\label{pre5}\{\{x,y,z\},w,t\}-\{\{x,y,w\},z,t\}-\{x,y,\{z,w,t\}_D\}-\{x,y,\{z,w,t\}\}\\
~ &&\nonumber+\{x,y,\{w,z,t\}\}+\{z,w,\{x,y,t\}\}_D=0,\\
~ &&\label{pre6}\{z,\{x,y,w\}_D,t\}+\{z,\{x,y,w\},t\}-\{z,\{y,x,w\},t\}+\{z,w,\{x,y,t\}_D\}\\
~ &&\nonumber+\{z,w,\{x,y,t\}\}-\{z,w,\{y,x,t\}\}=\{x,y,\{z,w,t\}\}_D-\{\{x,y,z\}_D,w,t\},\\
~&&\label{pre7}\{x,y,z\}_D*w+\{x,y,z\}*w-\{y,x,z\}*w=\{x,y,z*w\}_D-z*\{x,y,w\}_D,
\end{eqnarray}
where the commutator
$[\cdot,\cdot]_C:\wedge^2\mathfrak g \to \mathfrak g$ and $\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_D: \otimes^3 A \to A$ are defined by for all $x,y,z \in A$,
\begin{eqnarray}
~[x,y]_C:=x*y-y*x, \quad \forall x,y \in A,\label{pre10}
\end{eqnarray}
and
\begin{eqnarray}
\{x,y,z\}_D:=\{z,y,x\}-\{z,x,y\}+(y,x,z)-(x,y,z), \label{pre3}
\end{eqnarray}
respectively. Here $(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes the associator: $(x,y,z):=(x*y)*z-x*(y*z)$. It is obvious that $\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_D$ is skew-symmetric with respect to the first two variables. We denote a pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra by $(A,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})$.

\label{skew}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $r\in \otimes^2\mathfrak g$. Suppose that $r$ is skew-symmetric, and that $\delta$ and $\omega$ are induced by $r$ as in Eqs. \eqref{cocycle1} and \eqref{cocycle2}. Then $\delta^*:\otimes^2\mathfrak g^*\longrightarrow\mathfrak g^*$ is skew-symmetric and $\omega^*:\otimes^3\mathfrak g^*\longrightarrow\mathfrak g^*$ is skew-symmetric in the first two variables.

Indeed, for any $x\in \mathfrak g$, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\sigma_{12}\omega_1(x)&=&\sum_{ij}y_j\otimes \llbracket x,x_i,x_j \rrbracket\otimes y_i=-\sum_{ij}y_j\otimes\llbracket x_i,x,x_j \rrbracket\otimes y_i=-\omega_2(x),\\

\end{eqnarray*}
This shows that $\omega^*$ is skew-symmetric in the first two variables. Moreover, since $r$ is skew-symmetric, we have $\sigma_{12}\delta_1(x)=-\delta_2(x)$ and  $\sigma_{12}\delta_2(x)=-\delta_1(x)$ for any $x\in \mathfrak g$, and thus $\delta^*$ is skew-symmetric. This finishes the proof.

\label{pro:fund}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra~ and $r=\sum_ix_i\otimes y_i$. Suppose that $r$ is skew-symmetric, and that $\delta:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^2\mathfrak g$ and $\omega:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^3\mathfrak g$ are defined by $r$ as in Eqs. \eqref{cocycle1} and \eqref{cocycle2} respectively. Then we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
[\xi,\eta]_*&=&\mathrm{ad}^*_{r^\sharp(\xi)}\eta-\mathrm{ad}^*_{r^\sharp(\eta)}\xi,\\
\left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket _*&=&\mathcal{L}^*(r^\sharp(\xi),r^\sharp(\eta))\zeta-\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\eta))\xi+\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\xi))\eta,
\quad \forall \xi,\eta,\zeta\in \mathfrak g^*.
\end{eqnarray*}
<!-- end pro-6 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
It is sufficient to prove that
\begin{eqnarray}
\label{br1}\langle\delta(x),\xi\otimes\eta\rangle&=&\langle x,[\xi,\eta]_*\rangle,\\
\label{br2}\langle\omega(x),\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\rangle&=&\langle x,\left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket _*\rangle,\quad \forall x\in \mathfrak g^*,\xi,\eta,\zeta\in \mathfrak g^*.
\end{eqnarray}
Let $r=\sum_ix_i\otimes y_i$. Since $r$ is skew-symmetric, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\langle x,\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\eta))\xi\rangle&=&-\langle\left\llbracket  x,r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\eta)\right\rrbracket ,\xi\rangle=-\langle r^\sharp(\zeta),\mathcal{R}^*(x,r^\sharp(\eta))\xi\rangle\\
~ &=&-\langle r,\zeta\otimes\mathcal{R}^*(x,r^\sharp(\eta))\xi\rangle=-\sum_i\langle y_i,\zeta\rangle\langle x_i,\mathcal{R}^*(x,r^\sharp(\eta))\xi\rangle\\
~ &=&-\sum_i\langle y_i,\zeta\rangle\langle r^\sharp(\eta),\mathcal{L}^*(x_i,x)\xi\rangle=-\sum_i\langle y_i,\zeta\rangle\langle r,\eta\otimes \mathcal{L}^*(x_i,x)\xi\rangle\\
~ &=&\sum_{ij}\langle y_i,\zeta\rangle\langle y_j,\eta\rangle\langle x_j,\mathcal{L}^*(x_i,x)\xi\rangle\\
~ &=&-\langle\sum_{ij}\left\llbracket  x,x_i,x_j\right\rrbracket \otimes y_j\otimes y_i,\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\rangle\\
~ &=&-\langle\omega_1(x),\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\rangle.
\end{eqnarray*}
Hence, we obtain that
$$-\langle x,\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\eta))\xi\rangle=\langle\omega_1(x),\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\rangle.$$
Moreover, we also have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\langle x,\mathcal{L}^*(r^\sharp(\xi),r^\sharp(\eta))\zeta\rangle&=&-\left\langle \left\llbracket  r^\sharp(\xi),r^\sharp(\eta),x\right\rrbracket ,\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle r^\sharp(\xi),\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\eta),x)\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle r,\xi\otimes\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\eta),x)\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\sum_j\left\langle y_j,\xi\right\rangle\left\langle x_j,\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\eta),x)\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\sum_j\left\langle y_j,\xi\right\rangle\left\langle r^\sharp(\eta),\mathcal{R}^*(x_j,x)\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\sum_j\left\langle y_j,\xi\right\rangle\left\langle r,\eta\otimes\mathcal{R}^*(x_j,x)\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\sum_{ij}\left\langle y_j,\xi\right\rangle\left\langle y_i,\eta\right\rangle\left\langle x_i,\mathcal{R}^*(x_j,x)\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle \sum_{ij}y_j\otimes y_i\otimes\left\llbracket  x_i,x_j,x\right\rrbracket ,\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle \omega_3(x),\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\right\rangle,\\
~ \left\langle x,\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\xi))\eta\right\rangle&=&-\left\langle \left\llbracket  x,r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\xi)\right\rrbracket ,\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\left\langle r^\sharp(\zeta),\mathcal{R}^*(x,r^\sharp(\xi))\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\left\langle r,\zeta\otimes\mathcal{R}^*(x,r^\sharp(\xi))\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\sum_i\left\langle y_i,\zeta\right\rangle\left\langle x_i,\mathcal{R}^*(x,r^\sharp(\xi))\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\sum_i\left\langle y_i,\zeta\right\rangle\left\langle r^\sharp(\xi),\mathcal{L}^*(x_i,x)\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\sum_i\left\langle y_i,\zeta\right\rangle\left\langle r,\xi\otimes\mathcal{L}^*(x_i,x)\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\sum_{ij}\left\langle y_i,\zeta\right\rangle\left\langle y_j,\xi\right\rangle\left\langle x_j,\mathcal{L}^*(x_i,x)\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle \sum_{ij}y_j\otimes\left\llbracket  x_i,x,x_j\right\rrbracket \otimes y_i,\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle \omega_2(x),\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\right\rangle.
\end{eqnarray*}
                                                                                                                         
                                                                                                                    
This gives Eq. \eqref{br2}. And Eq. \eqref{br1} can be proved similarly, so we omit the details. This finishes the proof.

<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{THM:CYBE}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $r\in \otimes^2\mathfrak g$ skew-symmetric and nondegenerate. Then $r$ satisfies
\begin{eqnarray}\label{CYBE}
\begin{cases}
~[r,r]&=0,\\
~\left\llbracket  r,r,r\right\rrbracket &=0,
\end{cases}
\end{eqnarray}
if and only if $r^\sharp:\mathfrak g^*\longrightarrow\mathfrak g$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ with respect to the coadjoint representation $(\mathfrak g^*;\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau)$, where $[r,r]$ and $\left\llbracket  r,r,r\right\rrbracket $ are defined as in Eqs. \eqref{r1} and \eqref{r2} respectively.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\xi,\eta,\zeta\in \mathfrak g^*$ and $r=\sum_ix_i\otimes y_i$. Then we compute that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle&=&\sum_i\langle\xi,x_i\rangle\langle\eta,y_i\rangle=\langle\xi,\sum_i\langle\eta,y_i\rangle x_i\rangle.
\end{eqnarray*}
The skew-symmetry of $r$ yields that
$$T(\xi)=\sum_i\langle\xi,y_i\rangle x_i.$$
Now we compute that
\begin{eqnarray*}
T\Big(\mathcal{L}^*(T(\xi),T(\eta))\zeta\Big)&=&T\Big(\mathcal{L}^*\big(\sum_i\langle\xi,y_i\rangle x_i,\sum_j\langle\eta,y_j\rangle x_j\big)\zeta\Big)\\
~ &=&\sum_{ij}\langle\xi,y_i\rangle\langle\eta,y_j\rangle T\Big(\mathfrak L^*(x_i,x_j)\zeta\Big)\\
~ &=&\sum_{ij}\langle\xi,y_i\rangle\langle\eta,y_j\rangle \sum_k\langle\mathcal{L}^*(x_i,x_j)\zeta,y_k\rangle x_k\\
~ &=&-\sum_{ijk}\langle\xi,y_i\rangle\langle\eta,y_j\rangle \langle\zeta,\left\llbracket  x_i,x_j,y_k\right\rrbracket \rangle x_k\\
~ &=&-\Big(\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\eta,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\zeta,\cdot\rangle\otimes1\Big)\left\llbracket  r_{31},r_{32},r_{43}\right\rrbracket ,
\end{eqnarray*}
Similarly, we also have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
-T\Big(\mathcal{R}^*(T(\zeta),T(\eta))\xi\Big)&=&-\sum_{ij}\langle\zeta,y_i\rangle\langle\eta,y_j\rangle T\Big(\mathcal{R}^*(x_i,x_j)\xi\Big)\\
~ &=&\sum_{ijk}\langle\zeta,y_i\rangle\langle\eta,y_j\rangle \langle\xi,\left\llbracket  y_k,x_i,x_j\right\rrbracket \rangle x_k\\
~ &=&-\Big(\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\eta,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\zeta,\cdot\rangle\otimes1\Big)\left\llbracket  r_{13},r_{41},r_{12}\right\rrbracket ,\\
T\Big(\mathcal{R}^*(T(\zeta),T(\xi))\eta\Big)&=&\sum_{ij}\langle\zeta,y_i\rangle\langle\xi,y_j\rangle T\Big(\mathcal{R}^*(x_i,x_j)\eta\Big)\\
~ &=&\sum_{ijk}\langle\zeta,y_i\rangle\langle\xi,y_j\rangle \langle\eta,\left\llbracket  y_k,x_i,x_j\right\rrbracket \rangle x_k\\
~ &=&-\Big(\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\eta,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\zeta,\cdot\rangle\otimes1\Big)\left\llbracket  r_{42},r_{23},r_{21}\right\rrbracket ,\\
\left\llbracket  T(\xi),T(\eta),T(\zeta)\right\rrbracket &=&\sum_{ijk}\left\llbracket  \langle\xi,y_i\rangle x_i,\langle\eta,y_j\rangle x_j,\langle\zeta,y_k\rangle x_k\right\rrbracket \\
~&=&\sum_{ijk}\langle\xi,y_i\rangle \langle\eta,y_j\rangle\langle\zeta,y_k\rangle\left\llbracket  x_i,x_j,x_k\right\rrbracket \\
~ &=&\Big(\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\eta,\cdot\rangle\otimes\langle\zeta,\cdot\rangle\otimes1\Big)
\left\llbracket  r_{41},r_{42},r_{43}\right\rrbracket .
\end{eqnarray*}
Thus we obtain that
$$\left\langle \kappa,\left\llbracket  T(\xi),T(\eta),T(\zeta)\right\rrbracket -T\Big(\mathcal{L}^*(T(\xi),T(\eta))\zeta-\mathcal{R}^*(T(\zeta),T(\eta))\xi+\mathcal{R}^*(T(\zeta),T(\xi))\eta\Big)\right\rangle
=\left\langle \xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta\otimes\kappa,\left\llbracket  r,r,r\right\rrbracket \right\rangle.$$
Similarly, we also have the following relation
$$\left\langle \kappa,[T(\xi),T(\eta)]-T\Big(\mathrm{ad}^*_{T(\xi)}\eta-\mathrm{ad}^*_{T(\eta)}\xi\Big)\right\rangle=\left\langle \xi\otimes\eta\otimes\kappa,[r,r]\right\rangle.$$
The conclusion thus follows.

\label{symplectic}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $r\in \otimes^2\mathfrak g$. Suppose that $r$ is skew-symmetric and nondegenerate. Then $r$ is a classical $r$-matrix of $\mathfrak g$ if and only if the nondegenerate, skew-symmetric bilinear form $\omega\in \wedge^2\mathfrak g^*$ defined to be
$$\omega(x,y):=\langle (r^\sharp)^{-1}(x),y\rangle,\quad \forall x,y\in \mathfrak g$$
is a symplectic structure\footnote{The notion of symplectic structures was introduced in \cite{SZ1}}, i.e., $\omega$ satisfies
\begin{eqnarray*}
 \omega(x,[y,z])+\omega(y,[z,x])+\omega(z,[x,y])&=&0,\\
 ~\omega(z,\left\llbracket  x,y,w\right\rrbracket )-\omega(x,\left\llbracket  w,z,y\right\rrbracket )+\omega(y,\left\llbracket  w,z,x\right\rrbracket )-\omega(w,\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket )&=&0,
 \end{eqnarray*}
for all $x,y,z,w\in \mathfrak g$.
<!-- end pro-7 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since $r\in \otimes^2\mathfrak g$ is nondegenerate, for all $\xi,\eta,\zeta\in \mathfrak g^*$, there exists $x,y,z\in \mathfrak g$, such that $r^\sharp(\xi)=x,r^\sharp(\eta)=y,r^\sharp(\zeta)=z$. Then it follows from Theorem \ref{THM:CYBE} that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\omega(w,\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket )&=&-\langle(r^\sharp)^{-1}(\llbracket r^\sharp(\xi),r^\sharp(\eta),r^\sharp(\zeta)\rrbracket,w)\rangle\\
~ &=&-\langle\mathcal{L}^*(r^\sharp(\xi),r^\sharp(\eta))\zeta-\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\eta))\xi+\mathcal{R}^*(r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\xi))\eta,w\rangle\\
~ &=&\langle\zeta,\llbracket r^\sharp(\xi),r^\sharp(\eta),w\rrbracket\rangle-\langle\xi,\llbracket w,r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\eta)\rrbracket\rangle
+\langle\eta,\llbracket w,r^\sharp(\zeta),r^\sharp(\xi)\rrbracket\rangle\\
~ &=&\omega(z,\left\llbracket  x,y,w\right\rrbracket )-\omega(x,\left\llbracket  w,z,y\right\rrbracket )+\omega(y,\left\llbracket  w,z,x\right\rrbracket )
\end{eqnarray*}
and
\begin{eqnarray*}
\omega(z,[x,y])&=&-\langle(r^\sharp)^{-1}([r^\sharp(\xi),r^\sharp(\eta)]),z\rangle\\
~ &=&-\langle\mathrm{ad}^*_{r^\sharp(\xi)}\eta-\mathrm{ad}^*_{r^\sharp(\eta)}\xi,z\rangle\\
~ &=&\langle\eta,[r^\sharp(\xi),z]\rangle-\langle\xi,[r^\sharp(\eta),z]\rangle\\
~ &=&\omega(y,[x,z])-\omega(x,[y,z]).
\end{eqnarray*}
This finishes the proof.

<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:O}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $(V;\rho,\mu)$ its representation. Then with the above notations, $T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak g$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ with respect to $(V;\rho,\mu)$ if and only if $$r=\overline{T}-\sigma_{12}(\overline{T})$$
is an $r$-matrix of the semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$\mathfrak g\ltimes_{\rho^*,-\mu^*\tau}V^*$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $\{v_1,\cdots,v_n\}$ be a basis for the vector space $V$ and $\{v_1^*,\cdots,v_n^*\}$ its dual basis. Then we have
$$\overline T=\sum_iv_i^*\otimes Tv_i\in V^*\otimes \mathfrak g\subset \otimes^2\Big(\mathfrak g\ltimes_{\rho^*,-\mu^*\tau}V^*\Big).$$
By a direct computation, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\left\llbracket  r_{13},r_{41},r_{12}\right\rrbracket &=&\sum_{ijk}\Big(\left\llbracket  Tv_i,Tv_k,Tv_j\right\rrbracket \otimes v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes v_k^*-\left\llbracket  Tv_i,Tv_k,v_j^*\right\rrbracket \otimes v_i^*\otimes Tv_j\otimes v_k^*\\
~ &&+\left\llbracket  v_i^*,Tv_k,Tv_j\right\rrbracket \otimes Tv_i\otimes v_j^*\otimes v_k^*+\left\llbracket  Tv_i,v_k^*,Tv_j\right\rrbracket \otimes v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes Tv_k\Big),\\
\left\llbracket  r_{42},r_{23},r_{21}\right\rrbracket &=&\sum_{ijk}\Big(v_j^*\otimes\left\llbracket  Tv_k,Tv_i,Tv_j\right\rrbracket \otimes v_i^*\otimes v_k^*-Tv_j\otimes\left\llbracket  Tv_k,Tv_i,v_j^*\right\rrbracket \otimes v_i^*\otimes v_k^*\\
~ &&-v_j^*\otimes\left\llbracket  v_k^*,Tv_i,Tv_j\right\rrbracket \otimes v_i^*\otimes Tv_k-v_j^*\otimes\left\llbracket  Tv_k,v_i^*,Tv_j\right\rrbracket \otimes Tv_i\otimes v_k^*\Big),\\
\left\llbracket  r_{31},r_{32},r_{43}\right\rrbracket &=&\sum_{ijk}\Big(v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes \left\llbracket  Tv_i,Tv_j,Tv_k\right\rrbracket \otimes v_k^*-v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes \left\llbracket  Tv_i,Tv_j,v_k^*\right\rrbracket \otimes Tv_k\\
~ &&-Tv_i\otimes v_j^*\otimes \left\llbracket  v_i^*,Tv_j,Tv_k\right\rrbracket \otimes v_k^*-v_i^*\otimes Tv_j\otimes \left\llbracket  Tv_i,v_j^*,Tv_k\right\rrbracket \otimes v_k^*\Big),\\
\left\llbracket  r_{41},r_{42},r_{43}\right\rrbracket &=&\sum_{ijk}\Big(-v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes v_k^*\otimes\left\llbracket  Tv_i,Tv_j,Tv_k\right\rrbracket +v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes Tv_k\otimes \left\llbracket  Tv_i,Tv_j,v_k^*\right\rrbracket \\
~ &&-Tv_i\otimes v_j^*\otimes v_k^*\otimes \left\llbracket  v_i^*,Tv_j,Tv_k\right\rrbracket +v_i^*\otimes Tv_j\otimes v_k^*\otimes\left\llbracket  Tv_i,v_j^*,Tv_k\right\rrbracket \Big).
\end{eqnarray*}
Moreover, we also have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\sum_i Tv_i\otimes \left\llbracket  Tv_i,Tv_j,v_k^*\right\rrbracket &=&\sum_iTv_i\otimes D^*(Tv_i,Tv_j)v_k^*=\sum_iTv_i\otimes \sum_m\langle D^*(Tv_i,Tv_j)v_k^*,v_m\rangle v_m^*\\
~ &=&\sum_{im}Tv_i\otimes \Big(-\langle D(Tv_i,Tv_j)v_m,v_k^*\rangle v_m^*\Big)=-\sum_mT\Big(D(Tv_i,Tv_j)v_m\Big)\otimes v_m^*,
\end{eqnarray*}
and
\begin{eqnarray*}
\sum_i Tv_i\otimes \left\llbracket  v_i^*,Tv_j,Tv_k\right\rrbracket &=&\sum_iTv_i\otimes (-\mu^*(Tv_k,Tv_j)v_i^*)=\sum_iTv_i\otimes \sum_m(-\langle \mu^*(Tv_k,Tv_j)v_i^*,v_m\rangle v_m^*)\\
~ &=&\sum_{im}Tv_i\otimes \Big(\langle \mu(Tv_k,Tv_j)v_m,v_i^*\rangle v_m^*\Big)=\sum_mT\Big(\mu(Tv_k,Tv_j)v_m\Big)\otimes v_m^*.
\end{eqnarray*}
Denote by
$${\mathcal{O}}_1(u,v,w)=\left\llbracket  Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket -T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\Big),\quad \forall u,v,w\in V.$$
Therefore, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\llbracket r,r,r\rrbracket&=&\left\llbracket  r_{13},r_{41},r_{12}\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  r_{42},r_{23},r_{21}\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  r_{31},r_{32},r_{43}\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  r_{41},r_{42},r_{43}\right\rrbracket \\
~ &=&\sum_{ijk}\Big({\mathcal{O}}_1(v_i,v_j,v_k)\otimes v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes v_k^*+v_j^*\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_1(v_k,v_i,v_j)\otimes v_i^*\otimes v_k^*\\
~ &&+v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes {\mathcal{O}}_1(v_i,v_j,v_k)\otimes v_k^*-v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes v_k^*\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_1(v_i,v_j,v_k)\Big).
\end{eqnarray*}
Moreover, we also have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
[r,r]&=&[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]\\
~ &=&\sum_{ij}\Big({\mathcal{O}}_2(v_i,v_j)\otimes v_i^*\otimes v_j^*-v_i^*\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_2(v_i,v_j)\otimes v_k^*+v_i^*\otimes v_j^*\otimes {\mathcal{O}}_2(v_i,v_j)\Big),
\end{eqnarray*}
where
$${\mathcal{O}}_2(u,v):=[Tu,Tv]-T\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\Big),\quad \forall u,v\in V.$$
Hence, $r$ is an $r$-matrix, i.e.,
$$[r,r]=0,\quad{\rm and}\quad \left\llbracket  r,r,r\right\rrbracket =0$$
if and only if
$${\mathcal{O}}_1(v_i,v_j,v_k)=0,\quad{\rm and}\quad {\mathcal{O}}_2(v_i,v_j)=0,$$
for all $i,j,k$, which implies that $T$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator. This finishes the proof.

Let $(A,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})$ be a pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $\{e_i\}_{i=1}^n$ a basis for $A$ and $\{e_i^*\}_{i=1}^n$ its dual basis. Then

is a skew-symmetric $r$-matrix for the Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$A\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}^*,-R^*\tau}A^*$. Moreover, $r$ is nondegenerate and the induced bilinear form $\mathcal{B}$ on $A\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}^*,-R^*\tau}A^*$ is given by \eqref{bilinear}.

<!-- defi-8: body_md -->
{\rm(\cite{Kikkawa})}
 A {\bf quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra} is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra $(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form $\mathcal{B} \in \otimes^2\mathfrak g^*$ satisfying the following invariant conditions
\begin{eqnarray}
\label{invr1}\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)&=&-\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]),\\
\label{invr2}\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket ,w)&=&\mathcal{B}(x,\left\llbracket  w,z,y\right\rrbracket ), \quad \forall x,y,z \in \mathfrak g.
\end{eqnarray}
We denote a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~by $\Big((\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket ),\mathcal{B}\Big)$.
<!-- end defi-8 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:qua}
Let $\Big((\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket ),\mathcal{B}\Big)$ be a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Then the induced map $\mathcal{B}^\natural:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\mathfrak g^*$ defined by \eqref{Bna} is an isomorphism from the adjoint representation $(\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})$ to the coadjoint representation $(\mathfrak g^*;\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau)$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For all $x,y,z,w\in \mathfrak g$, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\langle\mathcal{B}^\natural(\mathrm{ad}_xy)-\mathrm{ad}^*_x\mathcal{B}^\natural(y),z\rangle&=&\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)+\langle\mathcal{B}^\natural(y),[x,z]\rangle\\
~ &=&\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)+\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z])\\
~ &=&0.
\end{eqnarray*}
Since $z$ is arbitrary, we deduce that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathcal{B}^\natural(\mathrm{ad}_xy)=\mathrm{ad}^*_x\mathcal{B}^\natural(y), \quad \forall x,y\in \mathfrak g.
\end{eqnarray*}
Similarly, we also have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\langle\mathcal{B}^\natural(\mathcal{R}(x,y)z)+\mathcal{R}^*(y,x)\mathcal{B}^\natural(z),w\rangle&=&\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket  z,x,y\right\rrbracket ,w)+\langle\mathcal{R}^*(y,x)\mathcal{B}^\natural(z),w\rangle\\
~ &=&\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket  z,x,y\right\rrbracket ,w)-\mathcal{B}(z,\left\llbracket  w,y,x\right\rrbracket )\\
~ &=&0.
\end{eqnarray*}
Since $w$ is arbitrary, we deduce that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathcal{B}^\natural(\mathcal{R}(x,y)z)=-\mathcal{R}^*(y,x)\mathcal{B}^\natural(z), \quad \forall x,y,z\in \mathfrak g.
\end{eqnarray*}
Hence, $\mathcal{B}^\natural$ is an isomorphism between adjoint representation and coadjoint representation. This completes the proof.

Let $\Big((\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket ),\mathcal{B}\Big)$ be a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Then $\mathcal{B}^\natural:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\mathfrak g^*$ satisfies
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathcal{B}^\natural(\mathcal{L}(x,y)z)=\mathcal{L}^*(x,y)\mathcal{B}^\natural(z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in \mathfrak g.
\end{eqnarray*}
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The proof is a direct computation and is similar to that of Lemma \ref{lem:qua}.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{sts}
Let $\Big((\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket ),\mathcal{B}\Big)$ be a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $T:\mathfrak g^*\longrightarrow\mathfrak g$ a linear map. Then $T$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ with respect to the coadjoint representation $(\mathfrak g^*;\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau)$ if and only if $T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ with respect to the adjoint representation $(\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})$.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For all $x,y,z\in \mathfrak g$, by Lemma \ref{lem:qua}, we have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
(T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural)\Big([T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(x),y]+[x,T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(y)]\Big)&=&T\Big(\mathcal{B}^\natural(\mathrm{ad}_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(x)}y)
-\mathcal{B}^\natural(\mathrm{ad}_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(y)}x)\Big)\\
~ &=&T\Big(\mathrm{ad}^*_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(x)}\mathcal{B}^\natural(y)-\mathrm{ad}^*_{T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(y)}\mathcal{B}^\natural(x)\Big),
\end{eqnarray*}
and
\begin{eqnarray*}
~ &&(T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural)\Big(\left\llbracket  T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(x),T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(y),z\right\rrbracket +\left\llbracket  x,T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(y),T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(z)\right\rrbracket 
-\left\llbracket  y,T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(x),T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(z)\right\rrbracket \Big)\\
~ &=&T\Big(\mathcal{L}^*(T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(x),T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(y))\mathcal{B}^\natural(z)
-\mathcal{R}^*(T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(z),T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(y))\mathcal{B}^\natural(x)+\mathcal{R}^*(T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(z),T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural(x))\mathcal{B}^\natural(y)\Big).
\end{eqnarray*}
Thus we obtain that $T$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ with respect to the coadjoint representation $(\mathfrak g^*;\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau)$ if and only if $T\circ\mathcal{B}^\natural$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ with respect to the adjoint representation $(\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad},\mathcal{R})$. This finishes the proof.

\label{local}
A {\bf local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra} is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ together with two linear maps $\delta=\delta_1+\delta_2:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^2\mathfrak g$ and $\omega=\omega_1+\omega_2+\omega_3:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^3\mathfrak g$ such that $(\delta^*,\omega^*)$ defines a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~structure on $\mathfrak g^*$, and the following conditions are satisfied:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\delta_1$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^2\mathfrak g;1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes\mathcal{R})$;
\item $\delta_2$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^2\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad}\otimes1,\mathcal{R}\otimes1)$;
\item $\omega_1$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad}\otimes1\otimes1,\mathcal{R}\otimes1\otimes1)$;
\item $\omega_2$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes1,1\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes1)$;
\item $\omega_3$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes1\otimes\mathcal{R})$.
\end{itemize}

\label{ltpbi}
When a given Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ reduces to a Lie triple system $(\mathfrak g,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$, we obtain the local cocycle bialgebra structure of a Lie triple system: there exists a coalgebra structure $\omega=\omega_1+\omega_2+\omega_3:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^3\mathfrak g$ on the Lie triple system $(\mathfrak g,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ such that the following conditions are satisfied:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\omega_1$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad}\otimes1\otimes1,\mathcal{R}\otimes1\otimes1)$;
\item $\omega_2$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes1,1\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes1)$;
\item $\omega_3$ is a $1$-cocycle with respect to the representation $(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes1\otimes\mathcal{R})$,
\end{itemize}
where $(\mathfrak g;\mathcal{R})$ is the adjoint representation of the Lie triple system $\mathfrak g$.
<!-- end rmk-2 -->
<!-- rmk-3: body_md -->
We would like to point out that $\delta_i$ and $\omega_j$ $(1\leqslant i\leqslant 2,1\leqslant j\leqslant 3)$ as in Eqs. \eqref{c1} and \eqref{c2} are not $1$-cocycles of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$\mathfrak g$ in general, thus a solution to the classical Lie-Yamaguti Yang-Baxter equation can not give rise to a local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra structure.
Unlike $3$-Lie algebras, even for a Lie triple system, these ${\omega_j}$'s are not $1$-cocycles any more, which implies that a solution to the classical Yang-Baxter equation does not
produce a local cocycle bialgebra structure in the context of Lie triple systems. This illustrates that there is a huge difference between $3$-Lie algebras and Lie triple systems.

<!-- defi-10: body_md -->
\label{Manin}
Let $\mathfrak g_1$ and $\mathfrak g_2$ be two Lie-Yamaguti algebra s. A {\bf Manin triple} of $\mathfrak g_1$ and $\mathfrak g_2$ is a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$\Big((\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket ),\mathcal{B}\Big)$ such that
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_1\oplus\mathfrak g_2$ as vector spaces;
\item[(ii)] $\mathfrak g_1$ and $\mathfrak g_2$ are subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$ which  are isotropic, i.e., $\mathcal{B}(x_1,y_1)=\mathcal{B}(x_2,y_2)=0$, for any $x_1,y_1\in \mathfrak g_1$ and $x_2,y_2\in \mathfrak g_2$;
\item[(iii)] For all $x_1,y_1\in \mathfrak g_1$ and $x_2,y_2\in \mathfrak g_2$, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathrm{pr}_1\left\llbracket  x_1,y_1,x_2\right\rrbracket =0,\quad \mathrm{pr}_1\left\llbracket  x_1,x_2,y_1\right\rrbracket =0,\quad \mathrm{pr}_2\left\llbracket  x_2,y_2,x_1\right\rrbracket =0,\quad \mathrm{pr}_2\left\llbracket  x_2,x_1,y_2\right\rrbracket =0,
\end{eqnarray*}
where $\mathrm{pr}_1$ and $\mathrm{pr}_2$ are projections from $\mathfrak g_1\oplus\mathfrak g_2$ to $\mathfrak g_1$ and $\mathfrak g_2$ respectively.
\end{itemize}
We denote a Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s by $\Big((\mathfrak g,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2\Big)$ or simply by $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2)$.
<!-- end defi-10 -->
<!-- rmk-4: body_md -->
Recall that a product structure on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ is a Nijenhuis operator $E:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\mathfrak g$ satisfying $E^2={\rm{Id}}$.                                          

There exists a product structure $E$ on $\mathfrak g$ if and only if $\mathfrak g$ admits a decomposition into two subalgebras: $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_1\oplus\mathfrak g_2$. Moreover, Condition (iii) in Definition \ref{Manin} is just the condition that makes the product structure perfect. See \cite{Sheng Zhao} for more details about product structures and complex structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebra s. Thus a Manin triple $\Big((\mathfrak g,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2\Big)$ of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s is in fact the quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$(\mathfrak g,\mathcal{B})$ such that there is a perfect product structure on $\mathfrak g$ whose decomposed subalgebras are isotropic.
<!-- end rmk-4 -->
<!-- pro-9: body_md -->
\label{stand}
Any Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s is isomorphic to a standard one.
<!-- end pro-9 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $\mathfrak g_1$ and $\mathfrak g_2$ be Lie-Yamaguti algebra s. If $\Big((\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_1\oplus\mathfrak g_2,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2\Big)$ is a Manin triple of $\mathfrak g_1$ and $\mathfrak g_2$, then $\mathfrak g_2$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak g_1^*$ as vector spaces via
$$\langle \alpha,x\rangle:=\mathcal{B}(\alpha,x),\quad\forall \alpha\in \mathfrak g_2,~x\in \mathfrak g_1.$$
Moreover, $\mathfrak g_1^*$ is equipped with a Lie-Yamaguti algebra  ~structure from $\mathfrak g_2$ via this isomorphism. Then $\Big((\mathfrak g_1\oplus\mathfrak g_2,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2\Big)$ is isomorphic to the standard Manin triple $\Big((\mathfrak g_1\oplus\mathfrak g_1^*,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_1^*\Big)$. This completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- rmk-5: body_md -->
By the proof of Proposition \ref{stand}, we obtain that any Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s $\Big((\mathfrak g,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2\Big)$ is also isomorphic to the standard Manin triple $\Big((\mathfrak g_2^*\oplus\mathfrak g_2,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g_2^*,\mathfrak g_2\Big)$ of $\mathfrak g_2$ and $\mathfrak g_2^*$. So the statement of the proposition is that any Manin triple of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s is isomorphic to ``a" standard one, not ``the".

\label{thm:mt}

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $\Big(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*;(\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau)\Big)$ be  a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s. Then $(\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*},\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})$ is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, where $([\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*},\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})$ is given by \eqref{double1} and \eqref{double2}. We only need to prove that $\mathcal{B}$ satisfies the invariant condition \eqref{invr1} and \eqref{invr2}. Indeed, for all $x,y,z,w\in \mathfrak g$ and $\xi,\eta,\zeta,\delta\in \mathfrak g^*$, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
~ &&\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,[y+\eta,z+\zeta]_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})\\
~ &=&\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,[y,z]+\mathrm{ad}_y^*\zeta-\mathrm{ad}_z^*\eta+[\eta,\zeta]_*+\mathfrak{ad}_\eta^*z-\mathfrak{ad}_\zeta^*y)\\
~ &=&\left\langle x,\mathrm{ad}_y^*\zeta-\mathrm{ad}_z^*\eta+[\eta,\zeta]_*\right\rangle+\left\langle \xi,[y,z]+\mathfrak{ad}_\eta^*z-\mathfrak{ad}_\zeta^*y\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\left\langle [y,x],\zeta\right\rangle+\left\langle [z,x],\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle x,[\eta,\zeta]_*\right\rangle\\
~ &&+\left\langle \xi,[y,z]\right\rangle-\left\langle [\eta,\xi]_*,z\right\rangle+\left\langle [\zeta,\xi]_*,y\right\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
on the other hand, we also have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
~ &&\mathcal{B}([x+\xi,y+\eta]_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*},z+\zeta)\\
~ &=&\mathcal{B}([x,y]+\mathrm{ad}_x^*\eta-\mathrm{ad}_y^*\xi+[\xi,\eta]_*+\mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*y-\mathfrak{ad}_\eta^*x,z+\zeta)\\
~ &=&\left\langle \mathrm{ad}_x^*\eta-\mathrm{ad}_y^*\xi+[\xi,\eta]_*,z\right\rangle+\left\langle [x,y]+\mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*y-\mathfrak{ad}_\eta^*x,\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\left\langle \eta,[x,z]\right\rangle+\left\langle \xi,[y,z]\right\rangle+\left\langle [\xi,\eta]_*,z\right\rangle\\
~ &&+\left\langle [x,y],\zeta\right\rangle-\left\langle y,[\xi,\zeta]_*\right\rangle+\left\langle x,[\eta,\zeta]_*\right\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
which implies that
$$\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,[y+\eta,z+\zeta]_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})=\mathcal{B}([x+\xi,y+\eta]_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*},z+\zeta).$$
Moreover, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
~ &&\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket  x+\xi,y+\eta,z+\zeta\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*},w+\delta)\\
~ &=&\mathcal{B}\Big(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket +\mathcal{L}^*(x,y)\zeta-\mathcal{R}^*(z,y)\xi+\mathcal{R}^*(z,x)\eta\\
~ &&+\left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket _*+\mathfrak L^*(\xi,\eta)z-\mathfrak R^*(\zeta,\eta)x
+\mathfrak R^*(\zeta,\xi)y,w+\delta\Big)\\
~ &=&\left\langle \left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket +\mathfrak L^*(\xi,\eta)z-\mathfrak R^*(\zeta,\eta)x+\mathfrak R^*(\zeta,\xi)y,\delta\right\rangle\\
~ &&+\left\langle \left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket _*+\mathcal{L}^*(x,y)\zeta-\mathcal{R}^*(z,y)\xi+\mathcal{R}^*(z,x)\eta,w\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle \left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket ,\delta\right\rangle-\left\langle z,\left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\delta\right\rrbracket _*\right\rangle+\left\langle x,\left\llbracket  \delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket _*\right\rangle-\left\langle y,\left\llbracket  \delta,\zeta,\xi\right\rrbracket _*\right\rangle\\
~ &&+\left\langle \left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket _*,w\right\rangle-\left\langle \zeta,\left\llbracket  x,y,w\right\rrbracket _*\right\rangle+\left\langle \xi,\left\llbracket  w,z,y\right\rrbracket \right\rangle-\left\langle \eta,\left\llbracket  w,z,x\right\rrbracket \right\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
on the other hand, we also have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
~ &&\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,\left\llbracket  w+\delta,z+\zeta,y+\eta\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})\\
~ &=&\mathcal{B}\Big(x+\xi,\left\llbracket  w,z,y\right\rrbracket +\mathcal{L}^*(w,z)\eta-\mathcal{R}^*(y,z)\delta+\mathcal{R}^*(y,w)\zeta\\
~ &&+\left\llbracket  \delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket _*+\mathfrak L^*(\delta,\zeta)y-\mathfrak R^*(\eta,\zeta)w+\mathfrak R^*(\eta,\delta)z\Big)\\
~ &=&\left\langle x,\left\llbracket  \delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket _*+\mathcal{L}^*(w,z)\eta-\mathcal{R}^*(y,z)\delta+\mathcal{R}^*(y,w)\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &&+\left\langle \xi,\left\llbracket  w,z,y\right\rrbracket +\mathfrak L^*(\delta,\zeta)y-\mathfrak R^*(\eta,\zeta)w+\mathfrak R^*(\eta,\delta)z\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle x,\left\llbracket  \delta,\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket _*\right\rangle-\left\langle \left\llbracket  w,z,x\right\rrbracket ,\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle \left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket ,\delta\right\rangle-\left\langle \left\llbracket  x,y,w\right\rrbracket ,\zeta\right\rangle\\
~ &&+\left\langle \xi,\left\llbracket  w,z,y\right\rrbracket \right\rangle-\left\langle \left\llbracket  \delta,\zeta,\xi\right\rrbracket _*,y\right\rangle+\left\langle \left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\zeta\right\rrbracket _*,w\right\rangle-\left\langle \left\llbracket  \xi,\eta,\delta\right\rrbracket _*,z\right\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
which implies that
$$\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket  x+\xi,y+\eta,z+\zeta\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*},w+\delta)=\mathcal{B}(x+\xi,\left\llbracket  w+\delta,z+\zeta,y+\eta\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*}).$$

Conversely, if $\Big((\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*\Big)$ is a Manin triple, where $\mathcal{B}$ is an invariant bilinear form given by \eqref{bilinear}. For all $x\in \mathfrak g$ and $\xi,\eta,\zeta\in \mathfrak g^*$, by \eqref{invr1}, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\left\langle \eta,\rho_2(\xi)x\right\rangle=\mathcal{B}(\eta,[\xi,x]_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})=-\mathcal{B}([\xi,\eta]_*,x)=-\left\langle [\xi,\eta]_*,x\right\rangle=\langle \eta,\mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*x\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
which implies that $\rho_2=\mathfrak{ad}^*.$ Moreover, by \eqref{invr2}, we also have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\left\langle \zeta,\mu_2(\xi,\eta)x\right\rangle=\mathcal{B}(\zeta,\left\llbracket  x,\xi,\eta\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})=\mathcal{B}(\left\llbracket  \zeta,\eta,\xi\right\rrbracket _*,x)=\left\langle \left\llbracket  \zeta,\eta,\xi\right\rrbracket _*,x\right\rangle
=-\left\langle \zeta,\mathfrak R^*(\eta,\xi)x\right\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
which implies that $\mu_2=-\mathfrak R^*\tau.$ Similarly, we have that $\rho_1=\mathrm{ad}^*$ and $\mu_1=-\mathcal{R}^*\tau$. Thus we obtain that $\Big(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*,(\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau)\Big)$ is a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s. This completes the proof.

\label{LYbi}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~endowed with two linear maps $\delta:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^2\mathfrak g$ and $\omega:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^3\mathfrak g$. Then $\Big(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*;(\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau)\Big)$ is a matched pair of $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ and $(\mathfrak g^*,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   {_*},\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _*)$ if and only if the following conditions are satisfied
\begin{itemize}
\item[\rm(i)] $(\mathfrak g,\delta,\omega)$ is a Lie-Yamaguti coalgebra;
\item[\rm(ii)] the following compatibility conditions are satisfied: $\forall x,y,z\in \mathfrak g,$
\begin{eqnarray}
~\label{LYbi1} \delta([x,y])&=&\Big(\mathrm{ad}_x\otimes1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_x\Big)\delta(y)-\Big(\mathrm{ad}_y\otimes1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_y\Big)\delta(x),\\
~\label{LYbi2}\Big(1\otimes \mathcal{R}(y,z)\Big)\delta(x)&=&\Big(1\otimes \mathcal{R}(x,z)\Big)\delta(y),\\
~\label{LYbi3} \omega([x,y])&=&\Big(1\otimes1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_x\Big)\omega(y)-\Big(1\otimes1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_y\Big)\omega(x),\\
~\delta(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket )&=&\Big(\mathcal{L}(x,y)\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathcal{L}(x,y)\Big)\omega(z),\\
~\Big(\mathcal{R}(y,z)\otimes1\Big)\delta(x)&=&\Big(\mathcal{R}(x,z)\otimes1\Big)\delta(y),\\
~\omega(\left\llbracket  x,y,z\right\rrbracket )&=&\Big(\mathcal{L}(x,y)\otimes1\otimes1+1\otimes\mathcal{L}(x,y)\otimes1+1\otimes1\otimes\mathcal{L}(x,y)\Big)\omega(z),
\end{eqnarray}
\begin{eqnarray}
~\label{LYbi7} \Big(1\otimes \mathcal{R}(y,x)\otimes1-R(x,y)\otimes1\otimes1\Big)\omega(z)&=&\sigma_{12}\sigma_{23}\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(x,z)\otimes1\Big)\omega(y)\\
~\nonumber&&+\sigma_{23}\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(y,z)\otimes1\Big)\omega(x).
\end{eqnarray}
\end{itemize}

It is sufficient to show that Eqs. \eqref{mtp1}-\eqref{mtp18} are equivalent to Condition (ii). Note that when $\rho_1=\mathrm{ad}^*, ~\mu_1=-\mathcal{R}^*\tau$ and $\rho_2=\mathfrak{ad}^*,~\mu_2=-\mathfrak R^*\tau$, Eqs. \eqref{mtp1}-\eqref{mtp4} and Eqs. \eqref{mtp6}-\eqref{mtp8} are equivalent to Conditions \eqref{LYbi1}-\eqref{LYbi7} respectively, Eq. \eqref{mtp5} is equivalent to that $\omega$ is skew-symmetric with respect to the first two variables, and moreover Eq. \eqref{mtp8} and \eqref{mtp10} are equivalent.
Indeed, for all $x,y,z\in \mathfrak g$ and $\xi,\eta\in \mathfrak g^*$, let us now compute that
\begin{eqnarray*}
~ &&\left\langle [\mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*x,y]-\mathfrak{ad}_{\mathrm{ad}_x^*\xi}^*y-\mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*[x,y]-[\mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*y,x]+\mathfrak{ad}_{\mathrm{ad}_y^*\xi}^*x,\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\left\langle \delta(x),\xi\otimes\mathrm{ad}_y^*\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle \delta(y),\mathrm{ad}_x^*\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle \delta([x,y]),\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle \delta(y),\xi\otimes\mathrm{ad}_x^*\eta\right\rangle
-\left\langle \delta(x),\mathrm{ad}_y^*\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle \delta([x,y])-(\mathrm{ad}_x\otimes1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_x)\delta(y)+(\mathrm{ad}_y\otimes1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}_y)\delta(x),\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
which implies that Eq. \eqref{mtp1} is equivalent to Eq. \eqref{LYbi1}. Moreover, we have that
\begin{eqnarray*}
\left\langle \left\llbracket  \mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*x,y,z\right\rrbracket -\left\llbracket  \mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*y,x,z\right\rrbracket ,\zeta\right\rangle&=&-\left\langle \mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*x,\mathcal{R}^*(y,z)\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle \mathfrak{ad}_\xi^*y,\mathcal{R}^*(x,z)\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle \delta(x),\xi\otimes\mathcal{R}^*(y,z)\eta\right\rangle-\left\langle \delta(y),\xi\otimes\mathcal{R}^*(x,z)\eta\right\rangle\\
~ &=&\left\langle -\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(y,z)\Big)\delta(x)+\Big(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(x,z)\Big)\delta(y),\xi\otimes\eta\right\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
which implies that Eq. \eqref{mtp2} is equivalent to Eq. \eqref{LYbi2}. Similarly, we obtain that Eqs. \eqref{mtp3}-\eqref{mtp4} and Eqs. \eqref{mtp6}-\eqref{mtp8} are equivalent to Eqs. \eqref{LYbi3}-\eqref{LYbi7}. We only prove the equivalence of Eq. \eqref{mtp8} and Eq. \eqref{mtp16} since others are similar. Indeed, we have that
\begin{eqnarray*}

~ &=&\left\langle \zeta,\left\llbracket  \mathfrak R^*(\eta,\xi)y,z,x\right\rrbracket \right\rangle+\left\langle \zeta,\mathcal{R}(y,x)\mathfrak R^*(\xi,\eta)z\right\rangle-\left\langle \eta,\mathcal{R}(y,z)\mathfrak R^*(\xi,\zeta)x\right\rangle-\left\langle \xi,\mathcal{R}(z,y)\mathfrak R^*(\eta,\zeta)x\right\rangle\\
~ &=&-\left\langle \left\llbracket  \eta,\mathcal{R}^*(z,x)\zeta,\xi\right\rrbracket _*,y\right\rangle-\left\langle \left\llbracket  \xi,\mathcal{R}^*(y,x)\zeta,\eta\right\rrbracket _*,z\right\rangle-\left\langle \left\llbracket  \mathcal{R}^*(y,z)\eta,\xi,\zeta\right\rrbracket _*,x\right\rangle

(1\otimes\mathcal{R}(z,x)\otimes1)\omega(y),\\
~ && \eta\otimes\xi\otimes\zeta\rangle,
\end{eqnarray*}
which gives the equivalence of Eqs. \eqref{mtp8} and \eqref{mtp16}. This completes the proof.

<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{main}
Let $(\mathfrak g,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   ,\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket )$ be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~and $\delta:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^2\mathfrak g$ and $\omega:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\otimes^3\mathfrak g$ linear maps. Suppose that the structure map $(\delta^*,\omega^*)$ defines a Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~structure on $\mathfrak g^*$. Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\rm(1)] the Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$\mathfrak g$ makes $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*)$ into a double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra;
\item[\rm(2)] the quadruple $\Big(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*;(\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau),(\mathfrak{ad}^*,-\mathfrak R^*\tau)\Big)$ is a matched pair of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s;
\item[\rm(3)] the triple $\Big((\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*,\mathcal{B}),\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*\Big)$ is a standard Manin triple, where the invariant bilinear form $\mathcal{B}$ is given by \eqref{bilinear}.
\end{itemize}
In this case, the Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$(\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*,   [ \cdot,    \cdot  ]   {_{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*}},\left\llbracket  \cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket _{\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g^*})$ is called the {\bf double} of the Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g^*)$, and is denoted by $\mathfrak g\bowtie\mathfrak g^*$.

<!-- rmk-7: body_md -->
Since there does not exist the tensor representation in the context of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s, we are not able to study Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras by coboundary methods. In the context of $3$-Lie algebra
$(\mathfrak g,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])$, we have $(\ot^3\mathfrak g;\mathcal{L}\otimes 1\otimes1)$, $(\ot^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes1)$, and $(\ot^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes1\otimes\mathcal{L})$ are representations of the $3$-Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, hence authors studied local cocycle $3$-Lie bialgebras in \cite{B.G.S}. This property even fails in the context of Lie-Yamaguti algebra s. Thus we cannot study the local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras parallel to the $3$-Lie bialgebras, leading to not being able to explore the relationship between the classical Yang-Baxter equation and Lie-Yamaguti bialgebras. We, however, clarify the relationship between the solution to the classical Yang-Baxter equation in Lie-Yamaguti algebra s and relative Rota-Baxter operators in the next section. And one sees that our bialgebra theory for Lie-Yamaguti algebra s enjoys many good properties parallel to that for Lie algebras in the sequel, which justifies its correctness.

Let $(\mathfrak g,\delta,\omega)$ be a double construction Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra. Let $k_1,k_2,k_3$ be complex numbers such  that $k_1=k_2$ and $k_1+k_2+k_3=1$. Denote by $\delta_i=\frac{1}{2}\delta$ and $\omega_j=k_j\omega$, where $i=1,2;~j=1,2,3$. Set $\delta=\delta_1+\delta_2$ and $\omega=\omega_1+\omega_2+\omega_3$. It is obvious that $\delta_1, \delta_2$ are $1$-cocycles of $\mathfrak g$ with respect to the representations $(\otimes^2\mathfrak g;1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes\mathcal{R}),(\otimes^2\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad}\otimes1,\mathcal{R}\otimes1)$ respectively, and $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3$ are $1$-cocycles of $\mathfrak g$ with respect to $(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;\mathrm{ad}\otimes1\otimes1,\mathcal{R}\otimes1\otimes1),
(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes\mathrm{ad}\otimes1,1\otimes\mathcal{R}\otimes1),~(\otimes^3\mathfrak g;1\otimes1\otimes\mathrm{ad},1\otimes1\otimes\mathcal{R})$ respectively. Hence $(\delta,\omega)$ defines a local cocycle Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra on $\mathfrak g$.

<!-- ex-7: body_md -->
For any Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$\mathfrak g$, taking $\delta=0$ and $\omega=0$, then $(\mathfrak g,\delta,\omega)$ is a Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra. In this case, the corresponding Manin triple gives a quadratic Lie-Yamaguti algebra ~$(\mathfrak g\ltimes_{\mathrm{ad}^*,-\mathcal{R}^*\tau}\mathfrak g^*,\mathcal{B})$. Such Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra is called the {\bf trivial Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra}.
