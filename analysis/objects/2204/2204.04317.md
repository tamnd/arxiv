<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:mainintro} Let $({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak m})$ be ${\sf RCD}(K,N)$, $({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})$ be ${\sf CAT}(0)$, $U\subset{\rm X}$ open with ${\mathfrak m}({\rm X}\setminus U)>0$ and $u:U\to {\rm Y}$ harmonic. Then the  Zhang-Zhong-Zhu inequality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ZZZ}\tag{ZZZ}
\Delta\frac{|{\rm d} u|^2}2\geqslant K|{\rm d} u|^2\qquad on\ U
\end{equation}
holds in the sense of distributions and  the map $u$ has a representative, still denoted by $u$, with                                                                     
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mainintro}
{\rm Lip}(u\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{B}$})\leqslant\frac {C(K^-R^2,N)}r\inf_{{\sf o}\in{\rm Y}}\,\sqrt{\fint_{2B}{\sf d}_{\rm Y}^2(u(\cdot),{\sf o})\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}},
\end{equation}
for any ball $B=B_r(x)$ such that $2B=B_{2r}(x)\subset U$ and $r\in(0,R)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Let ${\rm X}$ be ${\sf RCD}(0,N)$ with ${\rm supp}({\mathfrak m})$ unbounded, ${\rm Y}$ be ${\sf CAT}(0)$  and $u:{\rm X}\to{\rm Y}$ be  harmonic with sublinear growth. Then $u$ is constant.

Here `harmonic' means that $u\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{U}$}$ is harmonic for any $U\subset{\rm X}$ open bounded with ${\mathfrak m}({\rm X}\setminus U)>0$ and the growth condition means that  for some $\bar x\in{\rm X}$, ${\sf o}\in{\rm Y}$ and function $f:\varmathbb{R}^+\to\varmathbb{R}^+$ with $\lim_{r\uparrow\infty}f(r)=0$ we have $\sup_{B_r(\bar x)}{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),{\sf o})<rf(r)$ for every $r>0$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By the assumptions  we can pick $B=B_r(\bar x)$ with $r=R$ arbitrarily large in  \eqref{eq:mainintro}. We then conclude from the fact that the right hand side is bounded by $C(0,N)f(2r)$.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
An $L^2$-normed $L^\infty$-module on ${\rm X}$ is a Banach space $(\mathcal M,\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal M})$ that is also a module 
over the commutative ring with unity $L^\infty({\rm X},{\mathfrak m})$ and which possesses a \emph{pointwise $L^2$ norm}, i.e.\ a map $|\cdot|:\mathcal M\to L^2({\rm X},{\mathfrak m})$ such that
\[
\begin{split}
|v|&\geqslant 0,\\
|fv|&=|f|\,|v|,\\
\|v\|_{\mathcal M}^2&=\int|v|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m},
\end{split}
\]
for every $v\in \mathcal M$ and $f\in L^\infty({\rm X},{\mathfrak m})$, the first two identities being intended ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.. We say that $\mathcal M$ is \emph{Hilbertian} if, when seens as Banach space, is an Hilbert space.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
The space
$W^{1,2}(U)$ consists  of those functions $f\in L^2(U)$ for which there is $G\geqslant 0$, $G\in L^2(U)$ such that for any Lipschitz function $\varphi:{\rm X}\to\varmathbb{R}$ with support bounded and contained in $U$ we have $\varphi f\in W^{1,2}({\rm X})$ with $|{\rm d} (\varphi f)|\leqslant G$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ on $\{\varphi=1\}$. The least, in the ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ sense, such function $G$ is denoted $|{\rm d} f|$ and then the $W^{1,2}(U)$-norm is defined as in \eqref{eq:normw12}.

The space $W^{1,2}_0(U)$ is the $W^{1,2}(U)$-completion of the space of functions $f\in W^{1,2}(U)$ with support at positive distance from $\partial U$ (i.e.\ $\inf_{x\in{\rm supp}(f),y\in\partial U}{\sf d}(x,y)>0$).

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $K\in\varmathbb{R}$, $N\in[1,\infty]$ and $({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak m})$ a complete and separable metric space equipped with a non-negative, and non-zero, Borel measure finite on bounded sets. We say that ${\rm X}$ is an ${\sf RCD}(K,N)$ space provided:
\begin{itemize}
\item[i)] For some $C>0$ and $\bar x\in{\rm X}$ we have ${\mathfrak m}(B_r(\bar x))\leqslant Ce^{Cr^2}$ for any $r\geqslant0$.
\item[ii)] Any $f\in W^{1,2}({\rm X})$ with $|{\rm d} f|\leqslant 1$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ admits a 1-Lipschitz representative.
\item[iii)] The space is infinitesimally Hilbertian.
\item[iv)] For any $f,g\in D(\Delta)$ with $\Delta f\in W^{1,2}({\rm X})$, $g,\Delta g\in L^\infty({\rm X})$, $g\geqslant 0$ we have
\[
\tfrac12\int |{\rm d} f|^2\Delta g\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\geqslant\int g\big(\tfrac1N(\Delta f)^2+\langle{\rm d} f,{\rm d}\Delta f\rangle+K|{\rm d} f|^2\big)\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}.
\]
\end{itemize}

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
A ${\sf CAT}(0)$ space is a complete geodesic space $({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})$ such that for any geodesic $\gamma$ and any $z\in{\rm Y}$ we have

{\sf d}_{\rm Y}^2(z,\gamma_t)\leqslant(1-t){\sf d}_{\rm Y}^2(z,\gamma_0)+t{\sf d}^2_{{\rm Y}}(z,\gamma_1)-t(1-t){\sf d}^2_{\rm Y}(\gamma_0,\gamma_1)\qquad\forall t\in[0,1].
\]

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:paseHL}
With the above notation and assumptions, for every $x\in{\rm X}$ the following holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item[i)] The function $t\mapsto f_t(x)$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$, locally Lipschitz and locally semiconcave on $(0,\infty)$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:derdshl}
\frac{{\rm d}^-}{{\rm d} t}f_t(x)=-\frac{(D^-_t(x))^2}{2t^2}\qquad\qquad\frac{{\rm d}^+}{{\rm d} t}f_t(x)=-\frac{(D^+_t(x))^2}{2t^2}.
\end{equation}
\item[ii)] The function $t\mapsto D^+_t(x)$ (resp.\ $D^-_t(x)$) is upper (resp.\ lower) semicontinuous, converge to 0 as $t\downarrow0$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mondpm}
D^-_t(x)\leqslant D^+_t(x)\leqslant D^-_s(x)\qquad\forall 0\leqslant t< s.
\end{equation}
Moreover, the identity $D^+(t)=D^-(x)$ holds for every $t$ except at most a countable number.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Considering the function $g(\cdot):=f(x,\cdot)$, all the stated results follow from the analysis carried out in \cite[Section 3]{AmbrosioGigliSavare11}. Specifically, 
the monotonicity \eqref{eq:mondpm} and the last claim in item $(ii)$ are proved in \cite[Proposition 3.1]{AmbrosioGigliSavare11}, the semicontinuity in  \cite[Proposition 3.2]{AmbrosioGigliSavare11} and the convergence to 0 is obvious from the fact that $f$ is bounded from below (see also the explicit bound \eqref{eq:stimadqt2}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{le:stimalipa}
We have
\[
\frac{D^+_t(x)}t\leqslant |\partial^- f_t|(x)+{\sf tilt}(f)(x)\qquad\forall t>0,\ x\in{\rm X}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
If $D_t^+(x)=0$ there is nothing to prove, thus we assume $D_t^+(x)>0$. Let $x\in{\rm X}$, $t>0$ and $(y_n)\subset{\rm X}$ a minimizing sequence for $F(t,x;\cdot)$ such that ${\sf d}(x,y_n)\to D_t^+(x)$. For every $n\in\varmathbb{N}$, let $\gamma_n:[0,1]\to{\rm X}$ be with constant speed equal to ${\sf d}(x,y_n)(1+\frac1n)$ such that $\gamma_n(0)=x$, $\gamma_n(1)=y_n$. Then ${\sf d}(\gamma_n(s),y_n)\leqslant (1-s)(1+\frac1n){\sf d}(x,y_n)$ for every $s,n$ and thus
\[
\begin{split}
\varlimsup_n  f_t(x)-f_t(\gamma_n(s))&\geqslant \varlimsup_n \big(F(t,x;y_n)-F(t,\gamma_n(s);y_n)\big)\\
&=\varlimsup_n\big( f(x,y_n)- f(\gamma_n(s),y_n)  +\frac1{2t}\big({\sf d}^2(x,y_n)-{\sf d}^2(\gamma_n(s),y_n)\big)\big)\\
\text{(by \eqref{eq:rti})}\qquad&\geqslant \varlimsup_n f(x,\gamma_n(s))  +\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y_n)}{t}\big(s-\tfrac{s^2}2\big)\\
&\geqslant\varliminf_n f(x,\gamma_n(s))  +\frac{D_t^+(x)^2}{t}\big(s-\tfrac{s^2}2\big)\geqslant -\varlimsup_n f(x,\gamma_n(s))^-  +\frac{D_t^+(x)^2}{t}\big(s-\tfrac{s^2}2\big)
\end{split}
\]
Rearranging, dividing by $sD_t^+(x)>0$ and recalling that ${\sf d}(x,\gamma_n(s))\leqslant s{\sf d}(x,y_n)(1+\frac1n)\to sD_t^+(x)$ we get
\[
\frac{D_t^+(x)}{t}\leqslant \varlimsup_n\frac{ f_t(x)-f_t(\gamma_n(s))}{{\sf d}(x,\gamma_n(s))}+\varlimsup_n \frac{f(x,\gamma_n(s))^- }{{\sf d}(x,\gamma_n(s))}+\frac s2\frac{D_t^+(x)}{t}\qquad\forall s\in(0,1).
\]
By diagonalization we can find sequences $n_j,m_j\uparrow+\infty$ such that
\[
\frac{D_t^+(x)}{t}\leqslant \varlimsup_j\frac{ f_t(x)-f_t(\gamma_{n_j}(\tfrac1j))}{{\sf d}(x,\gamma_{n_j}(\tfrac1j))}+\varlimsup_j \frac{f(x,\gamma_{m_j}(\tfrac1j))^- }{{\sf d}(x,\gamma_{m_j}(\frac1j))}\leqslant |\partial^- f_t|(x)+{\sf tilt}(f)(x),
\]
as desired.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:dualtilt} For any $x\in{\rm X}$ we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dualtilt}
\frac12{\sf tilt}(f)^2(x)=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}-\frac{f_t(x)}t=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\frac{D^\pm(t,x)^2}{2t^2}
\end{equation}
and a sequence $t_n\downarrow0$ realizes the $\varlimsup$ of $-\frac{f_t(x)}t$ if and only if it does so for $\frac{D^\pm(t,x)^2}{2t^2}$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Letting $y_t$ be (almost) minimizers for $f_t(x)$ we get
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:at}
\begin{split}
0&\leqslant\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}-\frac{f_t(x)}{t}=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\Big(-\frac{f(x,y_t)}{t}-\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y_t)}{2t^2}\Big)\\
&\leqslant \varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\underbrace{\Big({\sf tilt} (f)(x)\frac{{\sf d}(x,y_t)}{t}-\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y_t)}{2t^2}\Big)}_{=a_t}\stackrel *\leqslant\frac{{\sf tilt}(f)^2(x)}2,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where the last inequality comes from Young's inequality (and we shall use at the end of the proof that $*$ is actually an equality). Similarly with the $\varliminf$. If ${\sf tilt}(f)(x)=0$ this gives   the first equality in \eqref{eq:dualtilt}, that  $a_t=-\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y_t)}{2t^2}$ and that all the $\varlimsup$ must be limits. Thus by  \eqref{eq:mondpm} we get the second inequality in  \eqref{eq:dualtilt}.

 
 We thus we assume ${\sf tilt}(f)(x)>0$ and  notice that for $a,c\geqslant 0$ the quantity  $\sup_{b>c}ab-\frac{b^2}2$ is equal to $\frac{a^2}2$ if $a\geqslant c$ and to $ac-\frac{c^2}2$ if $a\leqslant c$. It follows that for any $x,y\in{\rm X}$ and $\varepsilon>0$ we have
\[
\sup_{\tau\in(0,\varepsilon)}\Big(\frac{f(x,y)^-}{{\sf d}(x,y)}\frac{{\sf d}(x,y)}{\tau}-\frac12\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{\tau^2}\Big)=\left\{\begin{array}{ll}
\frac12\frac{(f(x,y)^-)^2}{{\sf d}^2(x,y)},&\qquad\text{ if }\frac{f(x,y)^-}{{\sf d}(x,y)}\geqslant\frac{{\sf d}(x,y)}\varepsilon\\
&\\
\frac{f(x,y)^-}{\varepsilon}-\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{2\varepsilon^2},&\qquad\text{ if }\frac{f(x,y)^-}{{\sf d}(x,y)}\leqslant\frac{{{\sf d}(x,y)}}\varepsilon\\
\end{array}\right.
\]
Taking $\varlimsup_{y\to x}$  and noticing that by ${\sf tilt}(f)(x)>0$  we are eventually in the first case we obtain
\[
\begin{split}
\frac12{\sf tilt}(f)^2(x)&=\varlimsup_{y\to x}\sup_{\tau\in(0,\varepsilon)}\Big(\frac{f(x,y)^-}{\tau} -\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{2\tau^2}\Big)\leqslant \sup_{\tau\in(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{\sup_{y\neq  x}\big(f(x,y)^- -\frac12\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{\tau}\big)}\tau\\
&= \sup_{\tau\in(0,\varepsilon)}-\frac{\inf_{y\in{\rm X}}\big(f(x,y) +\frac12\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{\tau}\big)}\tau= \sup_{\tau\in(0,\varepsilon)}-\frac{f_t(x)}{t}
\end{split}
\]
and the arbitrariness of $\varepsilon>0$ gives $\frac12{\sf tilt}(f)^2(x)\leqslant \varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}-\frac{f_t(x)}{t}$.
                                  
Hence the inequalities in  \eqref{eq:at} must all be equalities: then the first in \eqref{eq:dualtilt} follows, while the second is a consequence of the equality in the starred inequality in \eqref{eq:at} and of \eqref{eq:mondpm}. The last claim also follows from these arguments.

<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
Let $({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak m})$ be an ${\sf RCD}(K,\infty)$ space.

The space $D(\Delta_{loc})$ is the collection of functions  $f\in L^1+L^\infty({\rm X})$ for which there is $g\in L^1+ L^\infty({\rm X})$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:defdeltaloc}
\int  f\Delta \varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\int g\,\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\qquad\forall\varphi\in \widetilde{\rm Test}({\rm X}).
\end{equation}
In this case the function $g$ (that is clearly unique by \eqref{eq:denstvar}) will be denoted $\Delta f$.

For $f,g\in L^1+L^\infty({\rm X})$ we   say that the Laplacian of $f$ is bounded above by $g$ if  
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deflub}
\int f\,\Delta\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\leqslant \int g\, \varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m},\qquad\forall \varphi\in \widetilde{\rm Test}^+({\rm X}).
\end{equation}
In this case we write ${\bf \Delta} f\leqslant g{\mathfrak m}$. Finally, for $f:{\rm X}\to\varmathbb{R}$ bounded and Borel we define                                              
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deftd}
\tilde\Delta f(x):=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\frac{{\sf h}_tf(x)-f(x)}{t}\qquad\forall x\in{\rm X}.
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{le:lapcomb}
Let $({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak m})$ be  ${\sf RCD}(K,\infty)$  and assume that ${\bf \Delta} f\leqslant g{\mathfrak m}$ with $g\in L^\infty({\rm X})$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[i)] For every $t\geqslant 0$ the bound $ {\sf h}_tf-f\leqslant \int_0^t{\sf h}_sg\,{\rm d} s$ holds ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e..
\item[ii)] It holds  ${\bf \Delta} f\leqslant\tilde\Delta f{\mathfrak m}$.
\item[iii)] If  $f\in{\mathcal AC}^-({\rm X})$ and $g$ is upper semicontinuous, then $\tilde\Delta f\leqslant g$ everywhere on ${\rm X}$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For $(i)$ let $\varphi\in\widetilde{\rm Test}^+({\rm X})$ and recall  \eqref{eq:stabtvar} to get
\[
\int ({\sf h}_tf-f)\varphi=\int f({\sf h}_t\varphi-\varphi)=\iint_0^tf\Delta{\sf h}_s\varphi\leqslant \iint_0^tg{\sf h}_s\varphi=\int\varphi\Big(\int_0^t{\sf h}_sg\,{\rm d} s\Big)\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m},
\]
so the claim   follows from the density result \eqref{eq:denstvar}.  For $(ii)$  we pick $\varphi\in \widetilde{\rm Test}^+({\rm X})$ and notice that
\[
\int f\Delta\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\int f\frac{{\sf h}_t\varphi-\varphi}t\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\int \frac{{\sf h}_tf-f}t\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m},
\]
thus  $(i)$, the weak maximum principle, the fact that $\varphi\in L^1({\rm X})$ and  (reverse) Fatou's lemma give                 
\[
\int f\Delta\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\leqslant \int \varlimsup_{t\downarrow0} \frac{{\sf h}_tf-f}t\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\int \tilde\Delta f\,\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m},
\]
as desired. For $(iii)$, let $A\subset {\rm X}$ be as in \eqref{eq:Acont}. Then $(i)$ and the continuity of ${\sf h}_tf$ (recall \eqref{eq:lintylip}) and $ \int_0^t{\sf h}_sg\,{\rm d} s$ (that follows  from the representation formula \eqref{eq:reprht} and dominated convergence) ensure that ${\sf h}_t f(x)\leqslant f(x)+(\int_0^t{\sf h}_sg\,{\rm d} s)(x)$ for every $x\in A$. Then  \eqref{eq:acmc}, again the continuity of ${\sf h}_tf$ and $\varlimsup_t\fint_0^t{\sf h}_s g\,{\rm d} s\leqslant g$ (from \eqref{eq:reprht} and upper semicontinuity) yield the  claim.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{le:inflap}
Let $({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak m})$ be ${\sf RCD}(K,\infty)$, $K\in\varmathbb{R}$, $I$ is a set of indexes, not necessarily countable, and $g\in L^1+L^\infty({\rm X})$. Assume that $\sup_{i\in I}\|f_i\|_{L^\infty}<\infty$, that ${\bf \Delta} f_i\leqslant g{\mathfrak m}$ for every $i\in I$  and let $\bar f:=\operatorname*{\rm ess-inf}_{i\in I} f_i$. Then $ {\bf \Delta}\bar f\leqslant g{\mathfrak m}$.

Assume also that for some $E\subset{\rm X}$ Borel with ${\mathfrak m}({\rm X}\setminus E)=0$ we have that $f_i\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{E}$}$ is upper semicontinuous for every $i\in I$.  Then the infimum $f:=\inf_i f_i$ coincides ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ with  $\bar f$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Since the essential infimum can be realized as infimum of a suitably chosen countable subfamily, recalling the stability result \eqref{eq:stablub} it is sufficient to prove the claim for $I=\{1,2\}$. Thus let $f_1,f_2\in L^\infty({\rm X})$ be with ${\bf \Delta} f_1,{\bf \Delta} f_2\leqslant C{\mathfrak m}$ and  recall  \eqref{eq:l1loc} to see that $\tilde{\sf h}_nf_1\wedge \tilde{\sf h}_nf_2\to  f_1\wedge f_2$ and $\tilde{\sf h}_ng\to g$ in $L^1_{loc}({\rm X})$. Thus from \eqref{eq:stabub2} and \eqref{eq:stablub} (possibly after passing to a suitable ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ converging sequences) we see that we can replace $f_i$ with $\tilde{\sf h}_nf_i$, $i=1,2$, in our claim. 

By \eqref{eq:extappr} we thus reduced to proving that for $f_1,f_2\in{\rm Lip}_{\sf b}({\rm X})\cap D(\Delta_{loc})$ with $\Delta f_1,\Delta f_2\leqslant g$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ we have ${\bf \Delta}(f_1\wedge f_2)\leqslant g{\mathfrak m}$. Since the functions $h_n:=-\frac1n\log(e^{-nf_{1}}+e^{-nf_{2}})$ are uniformly bounded and pointwise converge to $f_1\wedge f_2$, by \eqref{eq:stablub} again it is sufficient to show that $\Delta h_n\leqslant g$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ for every $n\in\varmathbb{N}$. This follows  by direct computation,  as  \eqref{eq:calclap} gives
\[
\begin{split}
                                                                      
\Delta h_n&=-n\underbrace{\Big(\frac{e^{-nf_1}|{\rm d} f_1|^2+e^{-nf_2}|{\rm d} f_2|^2}{e^{-nf_1}+e^{-nf_2}}-\frac{|e^{-nf_1}{\rm d} f_1+e^{-nf_2}{\rm d} f_2|^2}{|e^{-nf_1}+e^{-nf_2}|^2}\Big)}_{\geqslant 0\quad{\mathfrak m}-a.e.}+\frac{e^{-nf_1}\Delta f_1+e^{-nf_2}\Delta f_2}{e^{-nf_1}+e^{-nf_2}},
\end{split}
\]
so that $\Delta f_1,\Delta f_2\leqslant g$ implies $\Delta h_n\leqslant g$ as well, as desired.

For the second claim notice that the inequality $f\leqslant \bar f$ is trivial. For the other start defining, for $\varepsilon>0$, the function $f_\varepsilon(x):=\varepsilon+\sup_{B_\varepsilon(x)}f$ and notice that since $f\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{E}$}$ is upper semicontinuous (as infimum of upper semicontinuous functions) we have that $f_\varepsilon\downarrow f$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ on $E$.

Now fix $\varepsilon>0$ and for every $x\in E$ use the upper semicontinuity of the $f_i$'s to find $i_x\in I$ and $r_x\in(0,\varepsilon)$ such that $f_{i_x}(y)< f(x)+\varepsilon$ for every $y\in B_{r_x}(x)\cap E$. Thus we have $f_{i_x}\leqslant f_\varepsilon$ on $ B_{r_x}(x)$. By the Lindelof property of $E$ (that is a separable, though not complete in general, metric space when equipped with the restricted distance -  see \cite[Chapter I, Thm. 15]{Kelley75}) there is a countable collection $(x_n)\subset E$ such that $\cup_nB_{r_{x_n}}(x_n)\supset E$. Since $\bar f\leqslant f_{i_{x_n}}\leqslant f_\varepsilon$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ on $B_{r_{x_n}}(x_n)$ for every $n\in\varmathbb{N}$, we deduce that $\bar f\leqslant f_\varepsilon$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.. The conclusion follows from the arbitrariness of $\varepsilon>0$.

<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:lapbU} Let $({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak m})$ be a proper ${\sf RCD}(K,\infty)$ space and $U\subset{\rm X}$ open.

The space $D(\Delta_{loc},U)$ is the collection of functions  $f\in L^1+L^\infty(U)$ for which there is $g\in L^1+ L^\infty(U)$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:defdeltalocU}
\int  f\Delta \varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\int g\,\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\qquad\forall\varphi\in \widetilde{\rm Test}(U).
\end{equation}
In this case the function $g$ (that is clearly unique by \eqref{eq:denstvar}, \eqref{eq:tcut}) will be denoted $\Delta f$.

For $f,g\in L^1+L^\infty(U)$ we   say that the Laplacian of $f$ is bounded above by $g$ on $U$, and write ${\bf \Delta} f\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{U}$}\leqslant g{\mathfrak m}$ or ${\bf \Delta} f\leqslant g{\mathfrak m}$ on $U$,  provided
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deflubU}
\int f\,\Delta\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\leqslant \int \varphi\,g\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m},\qquad\forall \varphi\in \widetilde{\rm Test}^+(U).
\end{equation}
Finally, for $f:U\to\varmathbb{R}$ bounded and Borel we define $\tilde\Delta f:U\to\varmathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}$ as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deftdU}
\tilde\Delta f(x):=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\frac{{\sf h}_tf(x)-f(x)}{t},
\end{equation}
where in defining ${\sf h}_tf$ we are extending $f$ outside $U$  by setting it to 0.

\label{def:rlf}
Let $({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak m})$ be an ${\sf RCD}(K,\infty)$ space, $K\in\varmathbb{R}$ and $(v_t)\in L^1([0,1], L^\infty(T{\rm X}))$.  Then ${\sf Fl}:[0,1]\times {\rm X}\to {\rm X} $  is said to be a \emph{Regular Lagrangian Flow} for $(v_t)$ provided it is Borel and the following properties are verified:

\item[ii)] For every $x\in{\rm X}$ the curve $[0,1]\ni t \mapsto {\sf Fl}_t(x)\in{\rm X}$

\begin{equation}\label{eq:RLF}
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d} t}\,(f\circ {\sf Fl}_t)(x)={\rm d} f(v_t)\big({\sf Fl}_t(x)\big)

\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\label{prop:unique}

\]

\end{equation}

${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ represents the gradient of $-Q_1f$ (in the sense of  \cite[Definition 3.7]{Gigli12} with $q=2$).

For arbitrary $\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})$ the definition of $Q_1f$ gives
\[
Q_1f(\gamma_0)- f(\gamma_{1})\stackrel{(*)}\leqslant \frac{{\sf d}^2(\gamma_0,\gamma_{1})}{2}.
\]
On the other hand, the assumption \eqref{eq:perunicog} tells
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stimalungh}
\int Q_1f\circ{\rm e}_0- f\circ{\rm e}_1\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}\geqslant \frac12\iint_0^{1}|\dot\gamma_t|^2\,{\rm d} t\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}(\gamma)\stackrel{(**)}\geqslant \int \frac{{\sf d}^2(\gamma_0,\gamma_{1})}{2}\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}(\gamma)
\end{equation}
thus forcing $(**)$ to be an equality and  $(*)$ to be an equality for ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$-a.e.\ $\gamma$. Equality in $(**)$ holds iff ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$-a.e.\ $\gamma$ is a (constant speed) geodesic, and since the collection of geodesics is a closed subset of $C([0,1],{\rm X})$, property $(ii)$ follows (the estimate on the length also comes from \eqref{eq:stimalungh}). \end{equation}
This is sufficient to ensure that $({\rm e}_0,{\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ is $c$-optimal and since we already established that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ is concentrated on geodesics, $(iv)$ follows.  thus letting first $t\downarrow0$ and then $R_1\uparrow R_2$ we deduce that ${\mathfrak m}(A_1\cap B_{R_2})={\mathfrak m}(B_{R_2})$. By the arbitrariness of $R_2$, this proves \eqref{eq:slopedqtf} for $t=1$. The general case follows by scaling.
 

We turn to the rest of the proof and observe that for ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_{R_1}$ built as above the plan ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}':=\sum_{n}2^{-n}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_{R_{1,n}}$ for some $R_{1,n}\uparrow\infty$ satisfies  $({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}},({\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}\ll{\mathfrak m}$ and \eqref{eq:perunicog}, thus   Proposition \ref{prop:unique} ensures that   is induced by a map $F$. The fact that $F$ takes values on geodesics of length $\leqslant \sqrt{2{\sf Osc}(f)}$ and satisfies \eqref{eq:flussoQt} (for $T=1$) follows from the analogue statements in Proposition \ref{prop:unique}. Now observe that   from Lemma \ref{le:lapcomb} and the assumptions $f\in{\mathcal AC}^-({\rm X})$, ${\bf \Delta} f\leqslant C{\mathfrak m}$ we have that $\tilde\Delta f(y)\leqslant  C$ for every $y\in{\rm X}$. Hence by \eqref{eq:flussoQt} we are in position to apply  Lemma \ref{le:lappunt} above in conjunction with the trivial bound \eqref{eq:stimadqt2} to deduce that
\[
\tilde\Delta Q_1f(x)\leqslant C+2K^-{\sf Osc}(f)\qquad{\mathfrak m}-a.e.\ x\in{\rm X}.
\] 
This  bound and the distributional inequality \eqref{eq:corelap} give, via  Lemma \ref{le:lapcomb},  estimate \eqref{eq:stimalapqtf}.

Now that we have \eqref{eq:stimalapqtf} we can apply once again Proposition \ref{prop:exist}, this time  with $C+2K^-{\sf Osc}(f)$ in place of $C$ to conclude, by \eqref{eq:regestpi} and the construction, that \eqref{eq:stimabella} holds.

The validity of \eqref{eq:flussoQt}, the very definition of $Q_1f$ and the fact that $\int_0^1|\dot\gamma_t|^2\,{\rm d} t\geqslant{\sf d}^2(\gamma_0,\gamma_1)$ with equality iff $\gamma$ is a constant speed geodesic, ensure that $F_\cdot(x)$ is a minimizer for \eqref{eq:prob}. Thus existence is proved. Uniqueness follows from Proposition \ref{prop:unique}: if $\tilde F:{\rm X}\to C([0,1],{\rm X})$ is also a selection of minimizers with $(\tilde F_1)_*{\mathfrak m}\ll{\mathfrak m}$, then for $\mu\in\mathscr P({\rm X})$ with ${\mathfrak m}\ll\mu\ll{\mathfrak m}$ the plan ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}:=\frac12((F_\cdot)_*\mu+(\tilde F_\cdot)_*\mu)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:perunicog} with $({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}=\mu$ and $({\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}\ll{\mathfrak m}$. By Proposition \ref{prop:unique} this is enough to ensure that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ is induced by a map, i.e.\ that $F=\tilde F$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.. The last claim in Proposition  \ref{prop:unique} also gives ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ uniqueness of minimizers of \eqref{eq:minHL} if ${\rm X}$ is ${\sf RCD}(K,N)$.

It remains to prove that $(F_t)$ is the only Regular Lagrangian Flow of $v_t:=-\nabla Q_{1-t}f$.  To see that it is a RLF, since $F$ takes values in continuous curves, it is sufficient to show that for any $\delta\in(0,1)$ it is the RLF of $(v_t)$ on $[0,1-\delta]$.  In this direction notice that \eqref{eq:lipQt} tells
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:estvtagain}
|v_t|\leqslant C(\delta)\qquad{\mathfrak m}-a.e.\qquad \forall t\in[0,1-\delta],\qquad\forall \delta\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
Then    let $\rho$ be a bounded probability density with bounded support, put ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}:=(F_\cdot)_*(\rho{\mathfrak m})$ as above and use  \eqref{eq:estvtagain} to apply Proposition \ref{prop:RLF} on $[0,1-\delta]$ and deduce that it is sufficient to prove that for any   $\varphi\in W^{1,2}({\rm X})$ the map $[0,1-\delta]\ni t\mapsto \int\varphi \circ{\rm e}_t\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ is absolutely continuous with derivative equal to $-\int{\rm d}\varphi(\nabla Q_{1-t}f)\circ{\rm e}_t\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ for a.e.\ $t$. Absolute continuity follows from the Sobolev regularity of $\varphi$ and the fact that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ is a test plan (see e.g.\ \cite[Theorem 2.1.21]{GP19}). Then recalling that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ is an optimal geodesic test plan and by  a simple scaling argument we see that it is sufficient to prove the formula for the derivative for $t=0$, i.e.\ to prove that
\[
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\int\frac{\varphi(\gamma_t)-\varphi(\gamma_0)}t\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}(\gamma)=-\int{\rm d}\varphi(\nabla Q_{1}f)\circ{\rm e}_0\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}.
\]
This follows recalling that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ represents the gradient of $-Q_1f$ in the sense of \cite[Definition 3.7]{Gigli12} (recall item $(vii)$ in Proposition \ref{prop:unique}) and the lemma about `horizontal and vertical derivatives', see \cite[Theorem 3.10]{Gigli12} (and \cite[Lemma 4.5]{AmbrosioGigliSavare11-2}).

We pass to uniqueness, thus let $\rho$ as before, $\tilde {\sf Fl}$ be a RLF of the $v_t$'s, put $\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}:=(\tilde {\sf Fl}_\cdot)_*\mu$, let $f_t:=Q_{t}f$ and notice that - by \eqref{eq:HLHJ2} and the assumption \eqref{eq:slopedqtf} -  the identity $\partial_tf_t=-\frac12|{\rm d} f_t|^2$ holds ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ for a.e.\ $t\in[0,1]$. Then from \eqref{eq:estvtagain} and \eqref{eq:spRLF} and arguing as for  \eqref{eq:integr} we obtain
\[
\begin{split}
\int Q_1f\,{\rm d}\mu-\int Q_\delta f\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_{1-\delta})_*\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}&=\iint_0^{1-\delta}\big(|{\rm d} f_t|^2-\tfrac12|{\rm d} f_t|^2)\circ\tilde {\sf Fl}_t\,{\rm d} t\,{\rm d}\mu   
                                                                     
\stackrel{\eqref{eq:spRLF}}=\frac12\iint_0^{1-\delta}  |\dot\gamma_t|^2\,{\rm d} t\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}(\gamma).
\end{split}
\]
Now notice that the continuity of  $t\mapsto\tilde {\sf Fl}_t(x)$ for ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ $x\in {\rm X}$ implies the weak convergence of  $({\rm e}_{1-\delta})_*\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ to $({\rm e}_1)_*\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$, hence the lower semicontinuity stated in \eqref{eq:contqt} and Fatou's lemma for weakly converging measures (see e.g.\ \cite[Lemma 2.5]{GDP17}) imply $\varliminf_{\delta\downarrow0}\int Q_\delta f\,{\rm d}(\tilde {\sf Fl}_{1-\delta})_*\mu\geqslant \int f\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_1)_*\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$.

We can therefore pass to the limit in the above and conclude that $\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:perunicog}, so that the uniqueness claim follows again by Proposition \ref{prop:unique}.

\noindent{\bf  Step 2: the approximation argument} Let $f\in{\mathcal AC}^-({\rm X})$ be   such that ${\bf \Delta} f\leqslant C{\mathfrak m}$ and assume that $(f_n)\subset{\mathcal AC}^-({\rm X})$  is a sequence that is $\Gamma$-converging to  $f$  (see \cite{DalMaso93}, \cite{Braides02}), i.e.\ so that
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:glim}
\begin{align}
\label{eq:glimi}
&\text{for every $x_n\to x$ we have  $\varliminf_nf_n(x_n)\geqslant f(x)$,}\\
\label{eq:glims}
&\text{for every $x\in{\rm X}$ there is $x_n\to x$ such that $\varlimsup_nf_n(x_n)\leqslant f(x)$.}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Assume also that for some $C''>0$ the uniform bounds
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:boundbrutti}
\begin{align}
\label{eq:lapbrutto}
{\bf \Delta} f_n&\leqslant C''{\mathfrak m}\\
\label{eq:oscbound}
{\sf Osc}(f_n)&\leqslant C''
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
hold for every $n\in\varmathbb{N}$ and that the conclusions of the theorem are true for the $f_n$'s (with the estimate \eqref{eq:stimabella} depending on $C''$). We claim that in this case the conclusions of the statement hold for $f$ (with the correct estimate \eqref{eq:stimabella} depending on $C,{\sf Osc}(f)$ only). According to the previous step to this aim it is sufficient to show that the properties \eqref{eq:core} hold. In proving this we shall frequently use the fact  that \eqref{eq:glim} and \eqref{eq:oscbound} give that the $f_n$'s - and thus also the $Q_tf_n$'s - are equibounded.
 

We start claiming that 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:daglims}
\varlimsup_{n}Q_tf_n(x)\leqslant Q_tf(x)\qquad\forall t>0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $x,y\in{\rm X}$ we use \eqref{eq:glims} to find $y_n\to y$ with $\varlimsup_nf_n(y_n)\leqslant f(y)$ and observe that
\[
\varlimsup_{n}Q_tf_n(x)\leqslant\varlimsup_n\Big( f_n(y_n)+\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y_n)}{2t}\Big)\leqslant f(y)+\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{2t},
\]
so that  the arbitrariness of $y\in{\rm X}$ gives \eqref{eq:daglims}. 

To get (a sort of) the $\Gamma-\varliminf$ inequality analogue to \eqref{eq:daglims} we need to overcome the  lack  of compactness by using the uniform estimates \eqref{eq:boundbrutti}. 


We assumed that for the $f_n$'s the conclusions are true, thus let $F^n:{\rm X}\to C([0,1],{\rm X})$ be the corresponding maps as in the statement, fix a bounded probability density $\rho$ with bounded support, define ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n:=(F^n)_*(\rho{\mathfrak m})\in\mathscr P(C([0,1],{\rm X}))$ and notice that by \eqref{eq:oscbound} and the last claim in the statement   the ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n$'s are concentrated on a bounded set of geodesics. Also, by \eqref{eq:boundbrutti} and \eqref{eq:stimabella}, they satisfy 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unipin}
({\rm e}_t)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n\leqslant e^{t(C''+2K^-C'')}{\mathfrak m},\qquad\forall n\in\varmathbb{N},\ t\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
Arguing as in the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:exist}, this is enough to conclude that the family  $({\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n)$ is tight, so that passing to a non-relabeled subsequence we can assume that it converges to a limit plan ${\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$. Now observe that integrating \eqref{eq:flussoQt} we get
\[
\int Q_1f_n\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n=\int f_n\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n+\frac12\iint_0^1|\dot\gamma_t|^2\,{\rm d} t\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n(\gamma)\qquad\forall n\in\varmathbb{N}.
\]
The bound \eqref{eq:daglims},  identity $({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n=({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}=\rho{\mathfrak m}$   and (reverse) Fatou's lemma give $$\varlimsup_n\int Q_1f_n\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n\leqslant \int Q_1f\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}\,,$$ while \eqref{eq:glimi} and  Fatou's lemma for weakly converging sequences of measures (see e.g.\ \cite[Lemma 2.5]{GDP17}) give $\varliminf_n\int f_n\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n\geqslant \int f\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}$. Thus by  the lower semicontinuity of the kinetic energy we can pass to the limit in the above and obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tantiug2}
\begin{split}
\int Q_1f\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}&\geqslant \varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int Q_1f_n\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_0)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_n\geqslant \int f\,{\rm d}({\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}+\frac12\iint_0^1|\dot\gamma_t|^2\,{\rm d} t\,{\rm d}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}(\gamma).
                                                                                                                                 
                                                                               
                                                                                                                                           
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Since  \eqref{eq:unipin}  implies   $({\rm e}_1)_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}\ll{\mathfrak m}$,  we can apply Proposition \ref{prop:unique} and deduce that
\[
|{\rm d} Q_tf|={\rm lip}(Q_tf)\qquad ({\rm e}_{1-t})_*{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}-a.e..
\]
Then the arbitrariness of $\rho$ and the very same arguments used at the beginning of the previous step (based on the fact that the estimate \eqref{eq:unipin} passes to the limit in place of \eqref{eq:regestpi}) show that \eqref{eq:slopedqtf}, and in particular {\eqref{eq:coresl}, holds.} 

Also, since the inequality $f(\gamma_1)+\frac12\int_0^1|\dot\gamma_t|^2\,{\rm d} t\geqslant f(\gamma_1)+\frac{{\sf d}^2(\gamma_0,\gamma_1)}2\geqslant Q_1f(\gamma_0)$ is true for any $\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})$, the bound \eqref{eq:tantiug2}  gives  $\varliminf_n\int Q_1f_n\,{\rm d}(\rho{\mathfrak m})\geqslant \int Q_1f\,{\rm d}(\rho{\mathfrak m})$. Now   put $g_n:=\sup_{k\geqslant n}Q_1f_k$, $g:=\inf_n g_n=\varlimsup_nQ_1f_n$ and observe that,  by dominated convergence,  $g_n\to g$ in $L^1({\rm X},\rho{\mathfrak m})$. On the other hand
\[
\varlimsup_n\|g_n-Q_1f_n\|_{L^1(\rho{\mathfrak m})}=\varlimsup_n\int g_n-Q_1f_n\,{\rm d}(\rho{\mathfrak m})\leqslant \int \underbrace{ g-Q_1f}_{\leqslant 0\text{ by \eqref{eq:daglims}}}\,{\rm d}(\rho{\mathfrak m})\leqslant 0.
\]
This forces at once $Q_1f_n\to g$ in $L^1(\rho{\mathfrak m})$ and $g=Q_1f$ $\rho{\mathfrak m}$-a.e., hence  the arbitrariness of $\rho$ tells that $(Q_1f_n)$ converges to $Q_1f$ in $L^1_{loc}({\rm X})$. Now from \eqref{eq:boundbrutti} and    the validity  of \eqref{eq:stimalapqtf} for $f_n$ we get  ${\bf \Delta} Q_1f_n\leqslant (C''+2K^-C''){\mathfrak m}$ and according to the stability property \eqref{eq:stablub} we can conclude that ${\bf \Delta} Q_1f\leqslant (C''+2K^-C''){\mathfrak m}$. Since by a scaling argument the same inequality is true for $Q_tf$, \eqref{eq:corelap} holds. We thus established \eqref{eq:core}, as desired.

\noindent{\bf  Step 3: wrapping everything up}

\noindent\underline{The case of test functions}  Let $f\in{\rm  Test}^\infty({\rm X})$. Then   clearly $f\in{\mathcal AC}^-({\rm X})$. Also, the properties  \eqref{eq:core} are granted by  Lemma \ref{prop:GTT}, hence in this case the conclusion follows from the first step.

\noindent\underline{Intermediate regularity} Assume that $f$ is in ${\rm Lip}_{\sf b}({\rm X})\cap D(\Delta_{\rm loc})$ with $\Delta f\in {\rm Lip}_b({\rm X})$. Pick $\bar x\in{\rm X}$, let $\phi_n:=(1-{\sf d}(\cdot,B_n(\bar x)))^+$, define $\eta_n:=\tilde{\sf h}_1\phi_n\in\widetilde{\rm Test}({\rm X})$ and then $f_n:=\eta_nf$. It is clear from \eqref{eq:calclap} that $f_n\in{\rm  Test}^\infty({\rm X})$ for every $n\in\varmathbb{N}$, so by the previous step the conclusions of the theorem are true for it. We claim that the bounds \eqref{eq:boundbrutti} and the $\Gamma-$convergence \eqref{eq:glim} are in place.

The bound \eqref{eq:oscbound} is trivial and since \eqref{eq:apprf}  gives
\[
\sup_n{\sf Osc}(\eta_n)+{\rm Lip}(\eta_n)+\|\Delta\eta_n\|_\infty<\infty,
\]
the bound  \eqref{eq:lapbrutto}  follows from the Leibniz rule for the Laplacian \eqref{eq:calclap}. We pass to \eqref{eq:glim} and   notice that the monotonicity of the heat flow grants that $(\eta_n(x))$ is a bounded non-decreasing sequence for every $x\in{\rm X}$, hence it converges to a limit $\eta(x)$. In particular  for $\varphi\in\widetilde{\rm Test}({\rm X})$ by dominated convergence we have $\int\eta_n\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\to\int\eta\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}$. On the other hand we have
\[
\int \varphi\eta_n\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\int \varphi\tilde{\sf h}_1\phi_n\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\int \tilde{\sf h}_1\varphi\,\phi_n\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\qquad\to\qquad\int \tilde{\sf h}_1\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\int \varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}
\]
and by the density property \eqref{eq:denstvar} we conclude that $\eta=1$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e., and thus everywhere because it is a Lipschitz function (being limit of equiLipschitz functions). It follows that for $x_n\to x$ we have
\[
|\eta_n(x_n)-\eta(x)|\leqslant {\sf d}(x_n,x)\sup_n{\rm Lip}(\eta_n)+|\eta_n(x)-\eta(x)|\quad\to\quad 0\qquad\text{ as $n\to\infty$}
\]
and thus, since $f\in{\rm Lip}_{\sf b}({\rm X})$, that $ f_n(x_n)\to f(x)$. This property is clearly stronger than \eqref{eq:glim}, which therefore is proved. Hence the desired conclusion for $f$ follows from Step 2.


\noindent\underline{The general case}  Let $f:{\rm X}\to\varmathbb{R}$ be as in the assumptions, put $f_n:=\tilde{\sf h}_n f$ and notice that by \eqref{eq:extappr} and \eqref{eq:lintylip} we have $f_n\in {\rm Lip}_{\sf b}({\rm X})\cap D(\Delta_{\rm loc})$ with $\Delta f_n\in{\rm Lip}_b({\rm X})$. Then by the previous set the conclusions of the theorem are true for $f_n$, whereas by  \eqref{eq:stabub2}  and the weak maximum principle we see  that the uniform bounds \eqref{eq:boundbrutti} are in place.

According to Step 2 to conclude it is therefore sufficient to show that the $\Gamma$-convergence    \eqref{eq:glim} is in place. To prove \eqref{eq:glimi} we  let $g\in{\rm Lip}_{{\sf b}}({\rm X})$, $g_n:=\tilde{\sf h}_n g$ and notice that \eqref{eq:BElip} implies $L:=\sup_n{\rm Lip}(g_n)<\infty$. Thus if  $g\in {\rm Lip}_{\sf b}({\rm X})$, for any   $x_n\to x$  the monotonicity of the heat flow gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:perlimi}
\varliminf_{n\to\infty}f_n(x_n)\geqslant \varliminf_{n\to\infty} g_n(x_n)\geqslant \varliminf_{n\to\infty} (g_n(x)-L{\sf d}(x,x_n))= \varliminf_{n\to\infty}g_n(x)=g(x),
\end{equation}
where in the last equality we used the continuity of $g$, the representation ${\sf h}_tg(x)=\int g\,{\rm d}{\sf h}_t\delta_x$ and the fact that ${\sf h}_t\delta_x\rightharpoonup\delta_x$ as $t\downarrow0$. From the lower semicontinuity of $f$ it is easy to establish that $f=\sup\{g\, :\, g\in {\rm Lip}_b({\rm X}),  g\leqslant f\}$ (see e.g.\ \cite[Section 5.1]{AmbrosioGigliSavare08}),  thus \eqref{eq:glimi} follows from \eqref{eq:perlimi}.


To prove \eqref{eq:glims} we recall that for some Borel set $E\subset{\rm X}$ with ${\mathfrak m}$-negligible complement we have that $f\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{E}$}$ is continuous. Also, using \eqref{eq:acmc} and a diagunalization argument it is clear that it suffices to prove \eqref{eq:glims} for $x\in E$. Fix such $x$, let $\varepsilon>0$, find $r>0$ such that $f\leqslant f(x)+\varepsilon$ on $B_r(x)\cap E$ and notice that since ${\sf h}_t\delta_x\ll{\mathfrak m}$ for every $t>0$ and ${\sf h}_t\delta_x(B_r^c)\to 0$ as $t\downarrow0$ we have
\[
\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}{\sf h}_tf(x)=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\int f\,{\rm d}{\sf h}_t\delta_x=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\int_{B_r(x)\cap E} f\,{\rm d}{\sf h}_t\delta_x\leqslant f(x)+\varepsilon.
\]
Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, the claim \eqref{eq:glims}  follows by picking $x_n\equiv x$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
{\rm
There are few differences between the statement as given above and that of \cite[Theorem 4.18]{gn20}, let us comment on them. 

One concerns the fact that in  \cite[Theorem 4.18]{gn20} we assumed $f$ to be globally Lipschitz, rather than only on an essential image of $u$ as above. The modification we did is inessential in  the proof (just replace ${\rm Y}$ with ${\rm Y}'$ and notice that $u$ remains harmonic) and useful later on, when, after proving that $u$ is locally bounded, we will choose as $f$ the squared distance from a point.

Another is about the use of ${\rm e}_2[u]$ in place of $|{\rm d} u|_{\sf HS}$ that appeared in \cite[Theorem 4.18]{gn20}. There is a strict relation between these two: in \cite[Proposition 6.7]{GT20} we proved that $(d+2){\rm e}_2[u]^2=|{\rm d} u|_{{\sf HS}}^2$ so  that \eqref{eq:fconv} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fconv2}
{\bf \Delta}(f\circ u)\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{U}$}\geqslant \lambda |{\rm d} u|_{{\sf HS}}^2{\mathfrak m}.
\end{equation}
We preferred to state the bound as in \eqref{eq:fconv} because in order to give a meaning to the pointwise Hilbert-Schmidt norm   $|{\rm d} u|_{\sf HS}$ one needs to rely on the  \emph{universally infinitesimally Hilbertian} of ${\sf CAT}(\kappa)$ spaces proved in  \cite{DMGSP18}.  Since, perhaps surprisingly, such result  plays no role in this paper (nor in \cite{gn20}), we decided to stick to the notation ${\rm e}_2[u]$ to emphasize this fact.

The last  difference is made more evident by the writing in \eqref{eq:fconv2}, as in  \cite[Theorem 4.18]{gn20} the right hand side is multiplied by an additional factor $\frac1{d+2}$. This is due to a computational mistake done in \cite{gn20} that made it all the way up to publication: namely, in deriving the bound \cite[(4.12)]{gn20} from \cite[(4.11)]{gn20} we forgot to multiply one of the terms in the right hand side by $\frac1{d+2}$. This results in an incorrect formula, the correct version being
\[
\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\frac{{\sf E^{KS}}(u_t)-{\sf E^{KS}}(u)}t\leqslant -\frac1{d+2}\int_\Omega \lambda g|{\rm d} u|_{{\sf HS}}^2+\langle{\rm d}(f\circ u),{\rm d} g \rangle\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}. 
\]
Using this bound in place of the original \cite[(4.12)]{gn20} in the proof of  \cite[Theorem 4.18]{gn20} we obtain the correct result reported above.

We apply Lemma \ref{le:partle} with $E\subset B'$ chosen as the set of points for which the conclusions of Lemma \ref{le:tdF} hold and then define the functions $f_{t,n}$ accordingly.  By the very definitions of $f_t$ and $f_{t,n}$ and keeping also in mind that for $x\in B''$ a minimizer for $f_{t,n}$  exists in $B'$ (by Lemma \ref{le:partle}), we have that $f_{t,n}\to f_t$ uniformly on $B''$ as $n\to\infty$. 

Hence taking into account the `bad' distributional bound \eqref{eq:subopt}, Lemma \ref{le:lapcomb2} and the stability property \eqref{eq:stablubloc},   to conclude it is sufficient to find $C(K^-R^2,N)>0$  such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:optb}
\tilde\Delta f_{t,n}\leqslant \frac{C(K^-R^2,N)}n-K\frac{D_t^2}{t}\qquad{\mathfrak m}-a.e.\   on\  B''\qquad\forall t\in(0,\bar T),\ n\in\varmathbb{N}.
\end{equation}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         
 
By Lemma \ref{le:partle}  we know  that for ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ $\bar x\in B''\cap E$ there is $\bar y\in E$ minimizer for $f_{t,n}(\bar x)$ (recall the definition \ref{eq:defftn}). Fix such $\bar x,\bar y$ and notice that  for every $x\in B''$ we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bella}
\begin{split}
f_{t,n}(x)&=\inf_{y\in{\rm Y}}\Big( f(x,y)+\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{2t}+\frac{{\sf d}^2(y,\bar z)}n\Big),\\
                                                                                                                             
\text{(by \eqref{eq:fsplit})}\qquad &\leqslant   f(\bar x,\bar y)+F_{\bar x,\bar y}(x)+\inf_{y\in {\rm Y}}\Big(F_{\bar y,\bar x}(y)+\frac{{\sf d}^2(x,y)}{2t}+\frac{{\sf d}^2(y,\bar z)}n\Big)\\

\end{split}
\end{equation}
with equality for $x=\bar x$. By the monotonicity of the heat flow (or the representation formula \eqref{eq:reprht})  it follows that for every $s>0$ we have
\[
{\sf h}_sf_{t,n}(\bar x)\leqslant f(\bar x,\bar y)+{\sf h}_sF_{\bar x,\bar y}(\bar x)+{\sf h}_s\big(Q_t\big(F_{\bar y,\bar x}(\cdot)+\tfrac{{\sf d}^2(\cdot,\bar z)}n\big)\big)(\bar x)
\]
hence subtracting \eqref{eq:bella} written for the equality case $x=\bar x$, after passing to the limit  we obtain:
\[
\tilde\Delta f_{t,n}(\bar x)\leqslant \tilde\Delta F_{\bar x,\bar y}(\bar x)+\tilde\Delta \big(Q_t\big(F_{\bar y,\bar x}(\cdot)+\tfrac{{\sf d}^2(\cdot,\bar z)}n\big)\big)(\bar x).
\]
Now observe that Lemma \ref{le:tdF}  and the fact that $\bar x\in E$ give $ \tilde\Delta F_{\bar x,\bar y}(\bar x)\leqslant 0$. Also, by inspecting the equality case $x=\bar x$ in \eqref{eq:bella} we see that the inf in the definition of $Q_t\big(F_{\bar y,\bar x}(\cdot)+\tfrac{{\sf d}^2(\cdot,\bar z)}n\big)(\bar x)$ is realized in $\bar y$, therefore  Lemma \ref{le:lappunt}, the subadditivity of $\tilde\Delta$ and the definition \eqref{eq:defdtns} give
\[
\begin{split}
\tilde\Delta \big(Q_t\big(F_{\bar y,\bar x}(\cdot)+\tfrac{{\sf d}^2(\cdot,\bar z)}n\big)\big)(\bar x)                                                                                                                        
&\leqslant \tilde\Delta  F_{\bar y,\bar x}(\bar y)+\tfrac1n \tilde\Delta {{\sf d}^2(\cdot,\bar z_t)}(\bar y)-K\frac{D_t^2(\bar x)}{t}.
\end{split}
\]
Now observe that since  $\bar y\in E$ we have $ \tilde\Delta  F_{\bar y,\bar x}(\bar y)\leqslant 0$ by Lemma \ref{le:tdF}. To bound the second term we apply Lemma \ref{le:lapcomb2}  with $g\equiv C$ and $ C=C(K^-R^2,N)$ given by Theorem \ref{thm:lapbd}  (recall that $\bar x.\bar y,\bar z_t\in B'\subset B_R(\hat x)$): we obtain  $\tilde\Delta {\sf d}^2(\cdot,\bar z_t)\leqslant C$ on $B'$. The conclusion \eqref{eq:optb} follows.

<!-- theorem-10: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
With the same assumptions and notations introduced above, the following holds.  

The map $u:U\to{\rm Y}$ satisfies the Zhang-Zhong-Zhu inequality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ZZ}
{\bf \Delta}\frac{|{\rm d} u|^2}2\geqslant K|{\rm d} u|^2{\mathfrak m}\qquad on\ U
\end{equation}
 and  has a representative, still denoted by $u$, that is locally Lipschitz continuous and satisfies 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:main}
{\rm Lip}(u\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{B}$})\leqslant \frac {C(K^-R^2,N)}r\inf_{{\sf o}\in{\rm Y}}\,\sqrt{\fint_{2B}{\sf d}_{\rm Y}^2(u(\cdot),{\sf o})\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}}.
\end{equation}

\ \\
\noindent{\bf  Rough estimates} Let $\tfrac12 B:=B_{\tfrac r2}(\hat x)$, $\tfrac14 B:=B_{\tfrac r4}(\hat x)$. We claim that for some   $C, T>0$ we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:roughclaim}
\big\|\tfrac{f_t}t\big\|_{L^\infty(\frac12B)}+\big\|\tfrac{D_t^2}{t^2}\big\|_{L^\infty(\frac14B)}\leqslant C \qquad\forall t\in(0, T).
\end{equation}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         
To see this, start noticing that $|\partial^-f_t|\leqslant{\rm lip}(f_t)$ holds everywhere, so by \eqref{eq:radelip} for ${\rm Y}=\varmathbb{R}$ (or the analogous result in \cite{Cheeger00}) we get $|\partial^-f_t|\leqslant|{\rm d} f_t|$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.. Then  Theorem \ref{thm:hlvar} and \eqref{eq:tilte2} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:l1contr}
\|f_t\|_{L^1( B)}=\|f_t-f_0\|_{L^1( B)}\leqslant \int_0^t\int _{ B}|{\rm d} f_t|^2+C\,{\rm e}_2^2[u]\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\,{\rm d} t\leqslant tC+\int_0^t\int _{ B}|{\rm d} f_t|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\,{\rm d} t                    
\end{equation}
for every $t\in(0,\bar T)$. Now let  $\varphi:=(1-\tfrac{1}{3r}{\sf d}(\cdot, B))^+$ and notice that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:anchdop}
\int\varphi^2|{\rm d} f_t|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}=\int{\rm d} f_t\cdot{\rm d}(f_t\varphi^2)-f_t\varphi{\rm d} f_t\cdot{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m} \leqslant\int{\rm d} f_t\cdot{\rm d}(f_t\varphi^2)+\tfrac12\varphi^2|{\rm d} f_t|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}+C,
\end{equation}
where in the last inequality we used \eqref{eq:bft22} and the trivial bound $|{\rm d}\varphi|\leqslant \tfrac{1}{3r}{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\chi$}}_{2 B}$. Now observe that Proposition \ref{prop:lapft} and the bounds  \eqref{eq:stimadqt2}, \eqref{eq:fbdasotto}   give 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lapgrezzo}
{\bf \Delta} (-f_t)\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{B''}$}\geqslant -(K^-\bar C\,{\sf Norm}){\mathfrak m}\qquad\forall  t\in(0,\bar T)
\end{equation}
and since $\varphi=0$ outside $B''$ and $f_t\varphi^2\leqslant 0$, by \eqref{eq:bft22} and \eqref{eq:anchdop} we get  the uniform bound
$\int_{ B}|{\rm d} f_t|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}\leqslant C$ for any  $t\in (0,\bar T).$ We use this estimate in \eqref{eq:l1contr} to deduce $\|f_t\|_{L^1(B)}\leqslant tC$ and then apply Theorem \ref{thm:ellrt} using again   \eqref{eq:lapgrezzo} to get the first in \eqref{eq:roughclaim}.

For the second, we notice that arguing as for \eqref{eq:claimg} using the uniform bound \eqref{eq:bft22} we see that, possibly picking $\bar T $ smaller, for   $x\in \tfrac14B$ any minimizer $y_t$ for $f_t(x)$ belongs to $\tfrac12B$. Then the claim follows from what already proved and  the estimate \eqref{eq:stimadqt2}.
 
\noindent{\bf  Core argument} By the continuity in $t$ of $f_t(x)$ (item $(i)$ of Proposition \ref{prop:paseHL}) we see that $\varlimsup_{t\downarrow0}\frac{-f_t}{t}=\inf_{n\in\varmathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in \varmathbb{Q}\cap(0,\frac1n)}\frac{-f_t}{t}$ pointwise on ${\rm X}$. Thus letting, for every $n\in\varmathbb{N}$,  $\{t_{n,i}\}_{i\in\varmathbb{N}}$ be an enumeration of $\varmathbb{Q}\cap(0,\frac1n)$, by Proposition \ref{prop:dualtilt} we have that $\sup_i\frac{-f_{t_{n,i}}}{t_{n,i}}$ converges  to $\tfrac12{\sf tilt}(f)^2$ pointwise. Then by the first estimate in \eqref{eq:roughclaim} we see that the convergence is also in $L^1(\tfrac12B)$, hence   by a diagonalization argument we can find $N_n\in\varmathbb{N}$ so that $\tilde f_n:=\frac{f_{t_{n,1}}}{t_{n,1}}\wedge \cdots\wedge \frac{f_{t_{n,N_n}}}{t_{n,N_n}}$ converge to $-\tfrac12{\sf tilt}(f)^2$ in $L^1(\tfrac12B)$, and then up to pass to a non-relabeled subsequence also pointwise ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e..

Let $A_{n,i}:=\{\frac{f_{t_{n,i}}}{t_{n,i}}=\tilde f_{n}\}\setminus \cup_{j<i}A_{n,j}\subset{\rm X}$, notice that for every $n\in\varmathbb{N}$ the sets $(A_{n,i})_{i=1,\ldots,N_n}$ form a Borel partition of ${\rm X}$ and then put $\tilde g_n:=\sum_i{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\chi$}}_{A_{i,n}}\frac{D_{t_{n,i}}^2}{t_{n,i}^2}$. By the last claim in Proposition \ref{prop:dualtilt} and the construction we see that $\tilde g_n\to {\sf tilt}(f)^2$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ on $B$, so that by the second estimate in \eqref{eq:roughclaim} the convergence is also in $L^1(\frac14B)$.


Now notice that Proposition \ref{prop:lapft} and the last claim in Lemma \ref{le:lapcomb2} (and  induction) give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:perCacc}
{\bf \Delta} \tilde f_n\leqslant -K\tilde g_n{\mathfrak m},\qquad\forall n\in\varmathbb{N},\qquad on \ \tfrac14B,
\end{equation}
so that the $L^1$ convergences, the uniform bounds \eqref{eq:roughclaim} and   the stability property \eqref{eq:stablubloc} imply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ZZlip}
\tfrac12{\bf \Delta}{\rm lip}^2(u)\geqslant  K{\rm lip}^2(u){\mathfrak m}
\end{equation}
on $\frac14B$, having also  used \eqref{eq:liptilt}. By the arbitrariness of $B$ and the locality property \eqref{eq:localappr} we infer that \eqref{eq:ZZlip} holds on $U$. We now claim that ${\rm lip}^2(u)\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(U)$ and to this aim, by the arbitrariness of $B$  it is sufficient to prove that for any $V\subset\subset\frac14 B$ open we have  ${\rm lip}^2(u)\in W^{1,2}(\bar V,{\sf d},{\mathfrak m}\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{V}$})$ (this is a direct consequence of the locality of the concepts of `being Sobolev' and `minimal weak upper gradients', see for instance \cite[Theorem 4.19]{AmbrosioGigliSavare11-2}). Thus let $\varphi\in{\rm Lip}_{{\sf bs}}({\rm X})$ be identically 1 on $V$ with support in $\frac14B$ and notice that \eqref{eq:perCacc} and the same computations in \eqref{eq:anchdop} give
\[
\begin{split}
\int\varphi^2|{\rm d} \tilde f_n|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}&\leqslant \int -K\tilde g_n\varphi^2\tilde f_n +\tfrac12\varphi^2|{\rm d} \tilde f_n|^2+2\tilde f_n^2|{\rm d}\varphi|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}.
\end{split}
\]
Since $\sup_n\|\tilde f_n\|_{L^\infty(\frac14B)},\|\tilde g_n\|_{L^\infty(\frac14B)}<\infty$ by \eqref{eq:roughclaim}, this is sufficient to derive a uniform bound on $\int\varphi^2|{\rm d} \tilde f_n|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}$ and thus on $\int_{V}|{\rm d} \tilde f_n|^2\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}$ so that the lower semicontinuity of the Cheeger energy on the space $(\bar V,{\sf d},{\mathfrak m}\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{V}$})$ gives our claim. 

We can thus apply Theorem \ref{thm:ellrt} to $f:={\rm lip}^2(u)\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(U)$ using \eqref{eq:ZZlip} and conclude that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lipu}
\|{\rm lip}(u)\|_{L^\infty(B'')}\leqslant C\sqrt{\fint_{B'}{\rm lip}^2(u)\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}}\stackrel{\eqref{eq:tilte2}}\leqslant C\sqrt{\fint_{B'}{\rm e}_2^2[u]\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak m}}\stackrel{\eqref{eq:invP}}\leqslant  \frac {\sf C}r\,{\sf Norm},
\end{equation}
for some ${\sf C}={\sf C}(K^-R^2,N)$. Now let $(p_n)$ be a countable set dense in the essential image of $u$, put $u_n:={\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(\cdot),p_n)$ and notice that - trivially -  we have ${\rm lip}(u_n)\leqslant {\rm lip}(u)$ everywhere and  by item $(ii)$ in Proposition \ref{prop:baseKS}  we know that $u_n\in W^{1,2}(U)$. Hence \eqref{eq:lipu} and the simple Lemma \ref{le:lipdf} below ensure that $|{\rm d} u_n|\leqslant \frac {\sf C}r \,{\sf Norm}$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ on $B''$. We can then apply the local Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property (see \cite[Proposition 1.10]{GV21}) and deduce that $u_n$ has a representative $\tilde u_n$ that is locally Lipschitz on $B''$ with local Lipschitz constant uniformly bounded by $\frac {\sf C}r\,{\sf Norm}$. Since any two points $x,y\in B$ can be joined by a curve lying on $B''$ with length bounded by $3{\sf d}(x,y)$ (if a geodesic $\gamma$ from $x$ to $y$ is so that $\gamma_t\notin B''$ for some $t$, its length, and thus ${\sf d}(x,y)$, must be at least  $\tfrac{2r}3$, while a curve passing through the center has length $\leqslant 2r$), we conclude that ${\rm Lip}(\tilde u_n\lower3pt\hbox{$|_{B}$})\leqslant \frac {\sf C}r\,{\sf Norm}$.

Since this is true for any $n\in\varmathbb{N}$, it follows  that for some $N\subset B$ Borel negligible we have 
\[
|{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),p_n)-{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(y),p_n)|\leqslant \frac Cr{\sf d}(x,y)\,{\sf Norm}\qquad\forall x,y\in B\setminus N.
\]
Taking the supremum in $n$ we conclude that the restriction of $u$ to $B\setminus N$ is $\frac {\sf C}r\,{\sf Norm}$-Lipschitz, so that $u:U\to{\rm Y}$ has a locally Lipschitz representative satisfying \eqref{eq:main}, as desired. 

Finally, \eqref{eq:ZZ} follows from \eqref{eq:ZZlip} taking into account the identity \eqref{eq:radelip}.

\label{le:lipdf}
Let $({\rm X}',{\sf d}',{\mathfrak m}')$ be a complete separable metric space equipped with a non-negative and non-zero Radon measure finite on bounded sets. Let $U\subset {\rm X}'$ be open, $f\in W^{1,2}(U)$ and assume that ${\rm lip}(f)\in L^2(U)$. Then $|{\rm d} f|\leqslant {\rm lip}(f)$ ${\mathfrak m}$-a.e.\ on $U$.
