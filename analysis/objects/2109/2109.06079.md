  Firstly note that $(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(i)$ and $(v)\Rightarrow(iv)$ are
  simply by definition.

  \qquad\framebox{$(i)\Rightarrow(iv)$} Assume $\delta$ is virtually Woodin, and fix a
  function $f\colon\delta\to\delta$. Let $\kappa<\delta$ be virtually $({<}\delta,
  f)$-strong and let $\theta<\delta$ be a regular cardinal such that
  $\sup_{\alpha\leq\kappa}f(\alpha)<\theta$. Then there is a generic elementary
  embedding \hbox{$\pi\colon (H_\theta, \in, f\cap H_\theta) \to (\operatorname{\mathcal M}, \in, f^+)$} such
  that $H_\theta\subseteq \operatorname{\mathcal M}$, $f\cap H_\theta=f^+\cap H_\theta$, $\operatorname{\mathcal M}\subseteq V$, and
  $\pi(\kappa)>\theta$. Note that, by our choice of $\theta$, $f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa\in
  H_\theta$ and $\pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)(\kappa)=f^+(\kappa)=f(\kappa)<\theta$.

 \qquad So it suffices to show that $\kappa$ is a closure point for $f$. Let
 $\alpha<\kappa$. Then
  \begin{align*} 
    f(\alpha)=f^+(\alpha)=\pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)(\alpha) =
    \pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)(\pi(\alpha))=\pi(f(\alpha)),
   \end{align*}

  so $\pi$ fixes $f(\alpha)$ for every $\alpha<\kappa$. Now, if $\kappa$ was not a
  closure point of $f$ then, letting $\alpha<\kappa$ be the least such that
  $f(\alpha)\geq\kappa$, we have
  \begin{align*} 
    \theta > f(\alpha) = \pi(f(\alpha)) > \theta,
   \end{align*}

  a contradiction. Note that we used that $\pi(\kappa)>\theta$ here, so this argument
  would not work if we had only assumed $\delta$ to be virtually pre-Woodin.

  \qquad \framebox{$(iv)\Rightarrow(vi)$} Assume $(iv)$ holds, let
  $f\colon\delta\to\delta$ be given and define $g\colon\delta\to\delta$ as
  $g(\alpha):=(2^{<\gamma_\alpha})^+$, where $\gamma_\alpha$ is the least regular
  cardinal above $|f(\alpha)|$. By $(iv)$ there is a $\kappa<\delta$ which is a closure
  point of $g$ (and so also a closure point of $f$), and there is a regular
  $\lambda\in(\kappa, \delta)$ for which there is a generic elementary embedding
  $\pi\colon H_\lambda\to\operatorname{\mathcal M}$ with $\operatorname{crit}\pi=\kappa$, $H_\lambda\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal M}$, $\operatorname{\mathcal M}\subseteq
  V$, and $\pi(g\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)(\kappa)<\lambda$.

  \qquad Let $\theta$ be the least regular cardinal above
  $|\pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)(\kappa)|$, and note that \hbox{$H_\theta\in H_\lambda$} by our
  definition of $g$. Thus, both $H_\theta$ and $H_{\pi(\theta)}^{\operatorname{\mathcal M}}$ are elements of
  $\operatorname{\mathcal M}$. An application of Countable Embedding Absoluteness~\ref{lemm.ctblabs} then
  yields that $\operatorname{\mathcal M}$ has a generic elementary embedding $\pi^*\colon H_\theta^{\operatorname{\mathcal M}}\to
  H_{\pi(\theta)}^{\operatorname{\mathcal M}}$ such that $\operatorname{crit}\pi^*=\kappa$, $\pi^*(\kappa)=\pi(\kappa)$,
  $\pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)\in\operatorname{ran}\pi^*$, and $\pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)(\kappa)<\theta$. By
  elementarity of $\pi$, $H_\lambda$ has an ordinal $\bar\theta<\kappa$ and a generic
  elementary embedding $\sigma\colon H_{\bar\theta}\to H_\theta$ with
  $\sigma(\operatorname{crit}\sigma)=\kappa$, $f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa\in\operatorname{ran}\sigma$ and
  $f(\operatorname{crit}\sigma)<\bar\theta$, which is what we wanted to show.

  \qquad \framebox{$(vi)\Rightarrow(v)$} Assume $(vi)$ holds and let
  $f\colon\delta\to\delta$ be given. Define $g\colon\delta\to\delta$ as
  $g(\alpha):=\langle(2^{<\gamma_\alpha})^+,f(\alpha)\rangle$, where $\gamma_\alpha$ is
  the least regular cardinal above $|f(\alpha)|$. In particular, $g$ codes $f$. By
  $(vi)$ there exist regular $\bar\kappa<\bar\lambda<\kappa<\lambda$ such that $\kappa$
  is a closure point of $g$ (so also a closure point of $f$) and there exists a generic
  elementary embedding $\pi\colon H_{\bar\lambda}\to H_\lambda$ with $\operatorname{crit}\pi =
  \bar\kappa$, $\pi(\bar\kappa)=\kappa$, $g(\bar\kappa)<\bar\lambda$, and
  $g\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa\in\operatorname{ran}\pi$. Since $f$ is definable from $g$ and
  $g\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa\in\operatorname{ran}\pi$, it follows that $f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa\in\operatorname{ran} \pi$. So let
  $\pi(\bar f)=f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa$ with $\bar f\colon\bar\kappa\to\bar\kappa$. Now observe
  that $\bar f=f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\bar\kappa$ since for $\alpha<\bar\kappa$, we have
  $f(\alpha)=\pi(\bar f)(\alpha)=\pi(\bar f(\alpha))=\bar f(\alpha)$.

  \qquad Let $\bar\theta$ be the least regular cardinal above $|f(\bar\kappa)|$. By the
  definition of $g$, we have $H_{\bar\theta}\in H_{\bar\lambda}$. Now, following the
  $(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)$ direction in the proof of Theorem~\ref{theo.rem} we get that
  $H_{\bar\lambda}$ has a generic elementary embedding \hbox{$\sigma\colon
  H_{\bar\theta}\to\operatorname{\mathcal M}$} with $\operatorname{\mathcal M}$ closed under ${<}\bar\theta$-sequences from $V$,
  $\operatorname{crit}\sigma=\bar\kappa$, $\sigma(\bar\kappa)> \bar\theta$, and $\sigma(\bar
  f)(\bar\kappa)<\bar\theta$. Let $\pi(\bar\theta)=\theta$ and $\pi(\operatorname{\mathcal M})=\operatorname{\mathcal N}$. Now by
  elementarity of $\pi$, we get that there is a generic elementary embedding
  \hbox{$\sigma^*:H_\theta\to \operatorname{\mathcal N}$} with $\operatorname{crit}\sigma^*=\kappa$,
  $\sigma^*(\kappa)>\theta$, and $\sigma^*(\pi(\bar
  f))(\kappa)=\sigma^*(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa)(\kappa)<\theta$.

  \qquad \framebox{$(vi)\Rightarrow(iii)$} Let $C$ be the club of all cardinals
  $\alpha$ such that $$(H_\alpha,\in, A\cap H_\alpha)\prec(H_\delta,\in,A).$$ Let
  $f\colon\delta\to\delta$ be given as
  $f(\alpha):=\langle \gamma_0^\alpha,\gamma_1^\alpha\rangle$, where $\gamma_0^\alpha$ is the
  first limit point of $C$ above $\alpha$ and the $\gamma_1^\alpha$ are chosen such
  that $\{\gamma_1^\alpha\mid\alpha<\beta\}$ encodes $A\cap H_\beta$ for inaccessible
  cardinals $\beta<\delta$.

  \qquad Let $\kappa<\delta$ be a closure point of $f$ such that there are regular
  cardinals \hbox{$\bar\theta<\kappa<\theta$} and a generic elementary embedding
  $\pi\colon H_{\bar\theta}\to H_\theta$ such that $\pi(\operatorname{crit}\pi)=\kappa$,
  $f(\operatorname{crit}\pi)<\bar\theta$, and $f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa\in\operatorname{ran}\pi$. Let $\bar\kappa=\operatorname{crit} \pi$.
  The same argument as above gives that $\pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright} \bar\kappa)=f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa$. In
  particular, it follows that $\bar\kappa$ is a closure point of $f$, and hence
  $\bar\kappa\in C$. We claim that $\bar\kappa$ is virtually $({<}\delta,
  A)$-extendible. Since $\kappa\in C$ because it is a closure point of $f$, it suffices
  by the definition of $C$ to show that
  \begin{align*} 
    (H_\kappa,\in,A\cap H_\kappa)\models\ulcorner \text{$\bar\kappa$ is virtually $(A\cap
    H_\kappa)$-extendible} \urcorner.
   \end{align*}

  Let $\beta$ be the least element of $C$ above $\bar\kappa$, and note that $\beta$ is
  below $\bar\theta$ since $f(\bar\kappa)<\bar\theta$, and the definition of $f$ says
  that the first coordinate of $f(\bar\kappa)$ is a limit point of $C$ above
  $\bar\kappa$. It then holds that $$(H_{\bar\kappa},\in,A\cap H_{\bar\kappa}) \prec
  (H_\beta,\in,A\cap H_\beta)$$ as both $\bar\kappa$ and $\beta$ are elements of $C$.
  Since $f$ encodes $A$ in the manner previously described and
  $\pi(f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\bar\kappa)=f\operatorname{\upharpoonright}\kappa$, we get that $\pi(A\cap H_{\bar\kappa}) = A\cap
  H_\kappa$, and thus
  \begin{align*} 
    (H_\kappa,\in,A\cap H_\kappa) \prec (H_{\pi(\beta)},\in,A^*)
   \end{align*}

  for $A^* := \pi(A\cap H_\beta)$. Now, as $(H_\gamma,\in, A\cap H_\gamma)$ and
  $(H_{\pi(\gamma)},\in,A^*\cap H_{\pi(\gamma)})$ are elements of $H_{\pi(\beta)}$ for
  every $\gamma<\beta$, Countable Embedding Absoluteness~\ref{lemm.ctblabs} implies
  that $H_{\pi(\beta)}$ sees that $\bar\kappa$ is virtually $({<}\beta,
  A^*)$-extendible. Since

  \begin{align*} 
  (H_\beta,\in A\cap H_\beta)\prec (H_{\pi(\beta)},\in A^*),
   \end{align*}

  it follows that $(H_\beta,\in A\cap H_\beta)$ satisfies that $\bar\kappa$ is
  virtually $({<}\beta, A\cap H_\beta)$-extendible. Finally, since

  \begin{align*} 
  (H_\beta,\in, A\cap H_\beta)\prec (H_\kappa,\in,A\cap H_\kappa),
   \end{align*}

  it follows that $(H_\kappa,\in, A\cap H_\kappa)$ satisfies that $\bar\kappa$ is
  virtually $({<}\kappa,A\cap H_\kappa)$-extendible.
}

As a corollary of the proof, we now have that virtually Woodin cardinals and faintly
Woodin cardinals are equivalent.

\prop[][prop.weakerwoodin]{
A cardinal $\delta$ is virtually Woodin if and only if it is faintly Woodin.
}

Indeed, the argument would work as well if we only assumed existence of generic
embeddings $\pi:(H_\theta,\in,A\cap H_\theta)\to (\operatorname{\mathcal M},\in,B)$ such that $\operatorname{crit}
\pi=\kappa$, \hbox{$\pi(\kappa)>\theta$}, $H_\theta\subseteq \operatorname{\mathcal M}$ and $A\cap
H_\theta=B\cap H_\theta$. In other words, we do not need the a priori stronger
assumption that $H_\theta=H_\theta^{\operatorname{\mathcal M}}$. Using Proposition~\ref{prop.weakerwoodin}, it
suffices to observe that if $\pi:(H_\theta,\in,A)\to (\operatorname{\mathcal M},\in,B)$ is a faintly
$(\theta,A)$-strong embedding such that $A$ codes the sequence of $H_\lambda$ for
$\lambda<\theta$, then $H_\theta=H_\theta^{\operatorname{\mathcal M}}$.

\qquad We should also observe that if $\delta$ is virtually Woodin, then indeed for
every $A\subseteq H_\delta$, we have stationary many virtually
$({<}\delta,A)$-extendible cardinals by an argument very similar to the proof that
virtually Woodin cardinals are Mahlo.

\qquad Recall that the \textbf{virtual Vop\v enka Principle} states that for every
proper class $\mathcal C$ consisting of structures in a common language, there are
distinct structures $\operatorname{\mathcal M},\operatorname{\mathcal N}\in \mathcal C$ for which there is a generic elementary
embedding $\pi\colon\operatorname{\mathcal M}\to\operatorname{\mathcal N}$. The second author and Hamkins showed in
\cite{GitmanHamkins} that the virtual Vop\v{e}nka\ principle holds if and only if for
every class $A$ there is a proper class of weakly virtually $A$-extendible cardinals
(in our terminology, these are $({<}\textsf{On},A)$-pre-extendible cardinals). It follows from
Theorem~\ref{theo.woodin} that if $\textsf{On}$ is faintly Woodin, then the virtual Vop\v{e}nka\
Principle holds. However, since it is consistent that the virtual Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle
holds and $\textsf{On}$ is not Mahlo \cite{GitmanHamkins}, the two assertions are not
equivalent.

\qquad It turns out, however, that a weakening of the virtual Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle is
equivalent to the assertion that $\textsf{On}$ is faintly pre-Woodin. Our formal setting for
working with classes throughout this article will be the second-order God\"el-Bernays
set theory $\textsf{GBC}$ whose axioms consist of $\textsf{ZFC}$ together with class axioms consisting
of extensionality for classes, class replacement asserting that every class function
when restricted to a set is a set, global choice asserting that there is a class
well-order of sets, and a weak comprehension scheme asserting that every first-order
formula defines a class.

\defi[][defi.VopenkaFinite]{
The \textbf{virtual Vop\v enka Principle for finite languages} states that for every
proper class $\mathcal C$ consisting of structures in a common \emph{finite} language,
there are distinct structures $\operatorname{\mathcal M},\operatorname{\mathcal N}\in \mathcal C$ for which there is a generic
elementary embedding $\pi\colon\operatorname{\mathcal M}\to\operatorname{\mathcal N}$.
}

\qquad The arguments in \cite{GitmanHamkins} show:

\theo[][theo.virtVopFinAext]{
  The virtual Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle for finite languages holds if and only if for every
  class $A$, there is a weakly virtually $A$-extendible cardinal.
}
\qquad The Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle is equivalent to the Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle for finite
languages. Indeed, the Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle can be restated in terms of the existence of
elementary embeddings between elements of \textit{natural sequences} \cite{Kanamori}.
But as we will see in the next section, this equivalence relies once again relies on
Kunen's Inconsistency.

\defi[]{
  Say that a class function $f\colon\textsf{On}\to\textsf{On}$ is an \textbf{indexing function} if it
  satisfies that $f(\alpha)>\alpha$ and $f(\alpha)\leq f(\beta)$ for all
  $\alpha<\beta$.
}

\defi[]{
  Say that an $\textsf{On}$-sequence $\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}=\langle \operatorname{\mathcal M}_\alpha\mid\alpha<\textsf{On}\rangle$ is \textbf{natural}
  if there exists an indexing function $f^{\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}}\colon\textsf{On}\to\textsf{On}$ and unary relations
  \hbox{$R^{\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}}_\alpha\subseteq V_{f^{\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}}(\alpha)}$} such that $\operatorname{\mathcal M}_\alpha =
  (V_{f^{\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}}(\alpha)}, \in, \{\alpha\}, R_\alpha^{\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}})$ for every $\alpha$.
}

\qquad The following theorem shows that the virtual Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle for finite
languages holds if and only if $\textsf{On}$ is virtually pre-Woodin if and only if $\textsf{On}$ is
faintly pre-Woodin if and only if for every class $A$ there is an weakly virtually
$A$-extendible cardinal. In particular, we get that virtually pre-Woodin and faintly
pre-Woodin cardinals $\delta$ are equivalent, and both are equivalent to the assertion
that for every $A\subseteq H_\delta$, there is a $({<}\delta,A)$-pre-extendible
cardinal. In the next section, in Theorem~\ref{theo.VopenkaForFiniteSeparate}, we will
separate the virtual Vop\v{e}nka\ Principle for finite languages from the virtual
Vop\v{e}nka\ principle.

\theo[D.-G.-N.][theo.vopwood]{
  The following are equivalent.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The virtual Vop\v enka Principle for finite languages holds.
    \item For every class $A$, there is a $({<}\textsf{On},A)$-pre-extendible cardinal.
    \item For any natural $\textsf{On}$-sequence $\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}$ there exists a generic elementary
        embedding $\pi\colon\operatorname{\mathcal M}_\alpha\to\operatorname{\mathcal M}_\beta$ for some $\alpha<\beta$.
    \item $\textsf{On}$ is virtually pre-Woodin.
    \item $\textsf{On}$ is faintly pre-Woodin.
  \end{enumerate}
}
\textsc{Proof.} 
By Theorem~\ref{theo.virtVopFinAext}, $(i)$ and $(ii)$ are equivalent.
$(i)\Rightarrow(iii)$ and $(iv)\Rightarrow(v)$ are trivial.

\qquad $(v)\Rightarrow(i)$: Assume that $\textsf{On}$ is faintly pre-Woodin and fix some class
$\mathcal C$ of structures in a common language. Let $\kappa$ be $({<}\textsf{On},\mathcal
C)$-pre-strong. Fix some regular $\theta>\kappa$ such that $H_\theta$ has a structure
$M$ from $\operatorname{\mathcal C}$ of the $\kappa$-th rank among elements of $\mathcal C$, and fix a generic
elementary embedding $$\pi\colon(H_\theta, \in, \mathcal C\cap H_\theta) \to (\operatorname{\mathcal N}, \in,
\mathcal C^*)$$ with $\operatorname{crit} \pi=\kappa$, $H_\theta=H_\theta^{\operatorname{\mathcal N}}$ (note that actually
$H_\theta\subseteq \operatorname{\mathcal N}$ suffices here), and $\mathcal C\cap H_\theta=\mathcal C^*\cap
H_\theta$.  Now we have that \hbox{$\pi:M\to \pi(M)$} is an elementary embedding (note
that the language is finite and therefore can be assumed to be fixed by $\pi$), and
$\pi(M)\neq M$ because $\pi(M)$ is a structure in $\mathcal C^*$ of rank $\pi(\kappa)$
among elements of $\mathcal C^*$. The structure $(\operatorname{\mathcal N},\in,\mathcal C^*)$ believes that
both $M$ and $\pi(M)$ are elements of $\mathcal C^*$, and, by Countable Embedding
Absoluteness~\ref{lemm.ctblabs}, $\operatorname{\mathcal N}$ has a generic elementary embedding between $M$
and $\pi(M)$. Therefore, $(\operatorname{\mathcal N},\in,\mathcal C^*)$ satisfies that there is a generic
elementary embedding between two distinct elements of $\operatorname{\mathcal C}^*$, and hence
$(H_\theta,\in,\mathcal C\cap H_\theta)$ satisfies that there is a generic elementary
embedding between two distinct elements of $\mathcal C$, and it must be correct about
this.

\qquad $(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)$: Assume $(iii)$ holds and assume that $\textsf{On}$ is not
virtually pre-Woodin, which means that there exists some class $A$ for which there are
no virtually $({<}\textsf{On},A)$-pre-strong cardinals. Define a function $f\colon\textsf{On}\to\textsf{On}$ by
setting $f(\alpha)$ to be the least regular $\eta>\alpha$ such that $\alpha$ is not
virtually $(\eta,A)$-pre-strong. Also define $g\colon\textsf{On}\to\textsf{On}$ by setting $g(\alpha)$
to be the  least strong limit cardinal above $\alpha$ which is a closure point of $f$.
Note that $g$ is an indexing function, so we can let $\vec\operatorname{\mathcal M}$ be the natural sequence
induced by $g$ and \hbox{$R_\alpha := A\cap H_{g(\alpha)}$}. $(ii)$ supplies us with
$\alpha<\beta$ and a generic elementary embedding

\begin{align*} 
\pi\colon(H_{g(\alpha)},\in,A\cap H_{g(\alpha)})\to (H_{g(\beta)}, \in, A\cap
H_{g(\beta)}).
 \end{align*}

Let us argue that $\pi$ is not the identity map, so that it must have a critical point.
Since $g(\alpha)$ is a closure point of $f$, the structure $(H_{g(\alpha)},\in,A\cap
H_{g(\alpha)})$ can define $f$ correctly. So it must satisfy that $f$ does not have a
strong limit closure point above $\alpha$, but the structure $(H_{g(\beta)}, \in, A\cap
H_{g(\beta)})$ does have such a closure point, namely $g(\alpha)$. Thus, $\pi$ must
have a critical point. Now since $g(\alpha)$ is a closure point of $f$, it holds that
$f(\operatorname{crit}\pi)<g(\alpha)$, so fixing a regular $\theta\in(f(\operatorname{crit}\pi), g(\alpha))$ we get
that $\operatorname{crit}\pi$ is virtually $(\theta, A)$-pre-strong, contradicting the definition of
$f$. Hence $\textsf{On}$ is virtually pre-Woodin.
$\hfill\blacksquare$\\
